# EMTALA Requirements: Triggers, Screening, Stabilization, Transfer and Administrative Duties

## 1. Who is covered and when the duty starts

- Hospital: a Medicare participating hospital, including a critical access hospital (CAH) and a rural emergency hospital (REH) (Act (e)(5); Reg (b)). The screening duty applies to a "hospital with an emergency department", meaning one with a dedicated emergency department (DED) (Reg (b)).
- "Comes to the emergency department" (Reg (b)) covers four routes, each with its own facts to prove:
  1. Presents at a DED and requests examination or treatment for a medical condition, or a prudent layperson observer would believe from appearance or behavior that it is needed.
  2. Presents elsewhere on hospital property and requests examination or treatment for what may be an emergency medical condition (EMC), or a prudent layperson would believe emergency care is needed.
  3. Is in a ground or air ambulance owned and operated by the hospital, even off hospital grounds, unless the ambulance runs under communitywide EMS protocols directing it elsewhere or at the direction of a physician not affiliated with the owning hospital.
  4. Is in a non-hospital-owned ambulance on hospital property. A call or telemetry from a non-hospital ambulance off property does not trigger the duty; a hospital in diversionary status may redirect it, but if the crew disregards diversion and arrives on property, the individual has come to the ED.
- Not covered: an individual who is already a "patient" (began a scheduled outpatient encounter, or an inpatient) (Reg (b)); an inpatient admitted for elective care (Reg (d)(2)(ii)). The hospital Conditions of Participation protect these patients instead (App. V, A-2406).
- Nonemergency requests at a DED still need screening appropriate to the presentation (Reg (c)).

## 2. Dedicated emergency department, hospital property and the 250-yard campus

- DED (Reg (b)): any department or facility of the hospital, on or off the main campus, that meets at least one test:
  1. Licensed by the State as an emergency room or emergency department.
  2. Held out to the public (name, posted signs, advertising or other means) as providing care for EMCs on an urgent basis without a scheduled appointment.
  3. In the calendar year immediately preceding the year of the determination, based on a representative sample of visits, at least one-third of its outpatient visits were for EMCs on an urgent basis without an appointment.
- Labor and delivery units and psychiatric intake or assessment units can meet the DED tests (App. V, Part I). Test each off-campus urgent care, freestanding ED and walk-in clinic against all three tests; an "urgent care" name does not settle it.
- Hospital property (Reg (b)): the entire main campus as defined in 42 C.F.R. 413.65, including parking lot, sidewalk and driveway, but excluding areas or structures that are not part of the hospital (physician offices, rural health clinics, SNFs and other separately participating entities, restaurants, shops).
- Campus (413.65(a)(2)): the area immediately adjacent to the main buildings, other areas and structures not strictly contiguous but within 250 yards of the main buildings, and any other area the CMS regional office determines case by case. App. V (A-2406) describes hospital property as including hospital-owned buildings within 250 yards.
- Off-campus provider-based departments (413.65(g)(1)): an on-campus department, and an off-campus department that is a DED, must comply with 489.20(l), (m), (q), (r) and 489.24. An off-campus department that is not a DED has no EMTALA screening duty but needs policies for emergencies (for example, calling EMS) (App. V, A-2406; 42 C.F.R. 482.12(f)).

## 3. Medical screening examination

- Duty (Act (a); Reg (a)(1)(i)): an appropriate MSE within the capability of the ED, including ancillary services routinely available, to determine whether an EMC exists.
- Who may perform it: "an individual(s) who is determined qualified by hospital bylaws or rules and regulations and who meets the requirements of § 482.55" (Reg (a)(1)(i)). App. V: the qualified medical personnel (QMP) designation "should be set forth in a document approved by the governing body"; "It is not acceptable for the hospital to allow informal personnel appointments that could frequently change." A department memo, job description or competency checklist that the governing body did not approve does not designate a QMP. A registered nurse can be a QMP for specific screenings when designated and within scope (App. V, A-2406).
- Triage is not the MSE. App. V: triage prioritizes when the individual will be seen; the MSE "is an ongoing process that begins, but typically does not end, with triage", and "It is not appropriate to merely 'log in' an individual and not provide a MSE." The record must show monitoring until an EMC is ruled out, stabilized or appropriately transferred.
- Same screening regardless of payment: the MSE must be the one the hospital would perform for anyone with those signs and symptoms (App. V).
- Registration and payment (Reg (d)(4)): no delay of the MSE or stabilizing treatment to inquire about method of payment or insurance; no seeking or directing the individual to seek insurer authorization until after the MSE and initiation of stabilizing treatment; reasonable registration, including asking whether and how the individual is insured, is allowed only if it does not delay screening or treatment and does not "unduly discourage individuals from remaining for further evaluation" (Reg (d)(4)(iv)). Tag A-2408.
- Leaving before screening or treatment: App. V: if an individual leaves AMA or LWBS "on his or her own free will (no coercion or suggestion) the hospital is not in violation". A suggestion by hospital staff that the individual go elsewhere, or a statement that discourages waiting, removes that protection. Surveyors sample AMA and LWBS entries (App. V, Part I, Task 2).
- Refusal (Reg (d)(3)): the record describes the examination or treatment refused; the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain a written informed refusal stating that risks and benefits were explained.
- Outcome is not the test: App. V says clinical outcome is not a proper basis for judging the MSE; judge on information available at the time. A misdiagnosis after use of all available resources is not a screening violation.

## 4. Emergency medical condition, stabilization and when the duty ends

- EMC (Reg (b)): acute symptoms of sufficient severity (including severe pain, psychiatric disturbances or symptoms of substance abuse) that absence of immediate attention could reasonably be expected to place health (or, for a pregnant woman, the woman or unborn child) in serious jeopardy, seriously impair bodily functions or seriously damage an organ or part; or, for a pregnant woman having contractions, inadequate time for a safe transfer before delivery or a transfer threat to the woman or unborn child.
- Duty (Reg (d)(1)): within the capabilities of the staff and facilities available (including on-call physicians and resources customarily used for overflow, App. V on capacity), further examination and treatment required to stabilize, or an appropriate transfer.
- "Stabilized" (Reg (b)): no material deterioration of the condition is likely, within reasonable medical probability, to result from or occur during the transfer; for a pregnant woman with contractions, she has delivered the child and the placenta. App. V: "To be considered stable the emergency medical condition that caused the individual to seek care in the dedicated ED must be resolved, although the underlying medical condition may persist."
- "Stable for transfer", "stable for discharge" or a checked "stable" box are clinical shorthand, not the regulatory test. A patient who still needs treatment the hospital cannot provide (surgery, PCI, a specialist not on call) has an EMC that is not stabilized, however well the trip is tolerated, and a transfer must meet section 8.
- Admission (Reg (d)(2)(i)): good-faith inpatient admission to stabilize the EMC satisfies the hospital's EMTALA duty for that individual; the CoPs then apply. App. V (A-2407): admission is when the decision to admit is made with the expectation of an overnight stay, typically shown by a signed and dated admission order; an admission used to avoid EMTALA leaves the hospital liable. Observation status is not inpatient admission. Courts differ on private suits after admission (see the enforcement file).
- The duty ends when a physician or QMP decides no EMC exists, the individual is appropriately transferred, or the individual is admitted for further stabilizing treatment (App. V, A-2407).
- Refusal of transfer (Reg (d)(5)): written informed refusal stating the risks and benefits, the reasons for refusal, and a description of the proposed transfer.

## 5. Labor

- Labor (Reg (b)) runs from the latent phase through delivery of the placenta. "A woman experiencing contractions is in true labor unless a physician, certified nurse-midwife, or other qualified medical person acting within his or her scope of practice as defined in hospital medical staff bylaws and State law, certifies that, after a reasonable time of observation, the woman is in false labor."
- A woman in labor is stabilized only when the child and the placenta are delivered. Transfer before that requires her written request or a certification that the benefits outweigh the risks to the woman and the unborn child (Reg (e)(1)(ii)); App. V: a hospital cannot cite State law or practice as the basis for the transfer.
- The L&D log is part of the central log, directly or by reference (App. V, A-2405).
- CMS rescinded QSO-22-22-Hospitals (pregnancy and pregnancy loss) effective May 29, 2025. The statute, regulation and Appendix V still govern obstetric EMCs; questions on State abortion laws go to counsel (see the enforcement file).

## 6. Psychiatric presentations and boarding

- Psychiatric disturbances and substance-abuse symptoms can be EMCs (Reg (b)). App. V: an individual expressing suicidal or homicidal thoughts or gestures, if determined dangerous to self or others, has an EMC; psychiatric patients are stable "when they are protected and prevented from injuring or harming him/herself or others"; chemical or physical restraint for transport may remove the immediate EMC only temporarily, so stability after restraint needs great care.
- Boarding in the ED does not end the EMTALA duty. Until the individual is admitted as an inpatient in good faith, stabilized, or appropriately transferred, the hospital must keep providing stabilizing treatment within its capability and keep monitoring in the record.
- State laws or community plans that route psychiatric or indigent patients to designated facilities are not a defense; the hospital must screen and stabilize or appropriately transfer first (App. V, A-2406, A-2407).
- Involuntary hold criteria are State law and do not decide whether an EMC exists `[VERIFY: State hold statute]`.

## 7. On-call physicians

- The hospital maintains an on-call list of physicians on its medical staff or with privileges (or in a formal community call plan) available to stabilize (489.20(r)(2); App. V, A-2404). The list names individual physicians with contact information; "Physician group names are not acceptable."
- Written policies are required for when a specialty is unavailable or the on-call physician cannot respond for reasons beyond control, and for any election to allow elective surgery while on call, simultaneous call, or a community call plan (Reg (j)). A community call plan needs the six elements in Reg (j)(2)(iii)(A)-(F), including an annual assessment.
- Response time: App. V advises policies that set "the maximum number of minutes" for a reasonable response. If the treating physician requests the on-call physician in person, the on-call physician must appear; both the hospital and the physician who fails or refuses to appear within a reasonable time may be sanctioned (Act (d)(1)(C); App. V, A-2404).
- If the treating physician transfers because the on-call physician failed or refused to appear, the transferring physician is protected from penalty, but the hospital and the on-call physician are not (Act (d)(1)(C)), and the transfer records must name the on-call physician who refused or failed to appear (Reg (e)(2)(iii)).

## 8. Transfer of an individual whose EMC is not stabilized

"Transfer" includes discharge: the movement of an individual outside the hospital's facilities at the direction of hospital-affiliated persons, except a person declared dead or one who leaves without permission (Reg (b)). Each element is tested separately (Reg (e)).

| Element | Requirement | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Basis (one of) | Written request by the individual or legally responsible person after being informed of the hospital's obligations and the risks, stating reasons and awareness of risks and benefits; or a physician certification that, on information available at the time, the expected benefits outweigh the increased risks (to woman and unborn child in labor), with a summary of risks and benefits; or, if no physician is physically present in the ED, a QMP-signed certification after a physician agrees in consultation, countersigned later | Signed request or certification with date and time; App. V: certification must be express and written, "cannot simply be implied" from the record; its date and time "should closely match" the transfer; "Certifications may not be backdated"; countersignature within the hospital's policy timeframe |
| Treatment before transfer | Treatment within capacity that minimizes transfer risk | Orders and MAR before departure |
| Receiving facility | Has space and qualified personnel, and has agreed to accept and treat | App. V: document the date and time of the request and the name and title of the person accepting |
| Records | All records related to the EMC available at the time of transfer (history, observations, preliminary diagnosis, test results, treatment, the request or certification, name and address of any on-call physician who refused or failed to appear); others as soon as practicable | Transfer packet contents against the record as it stood at departure |
| Transport | Qualified personnel and equipment, including medically appropriate life support | Transport log, crew level, equipment, drips continued |

Whistleblower protection: no penalty against a physician or QMP who refuses to authorize the transfer of an unstabilized individual, or an employee who reports a violation (Reg (e)(3); Act (i)).

## 9. Recipient hospital duties and the 72-hour report

- A participating hospital with specialized capabilities or facilities (burn units, shock-trauma units, NICUs, regional referral centers in rural areas) may not refuse an appropriate transfer of an individual who needs them if it has the capacity, whether or not it has a DED (Reg (f), (f)(1); Act (g)). The duty does not apply to an individual already admitted as an inpatient at the referring hospital (Reg (f)(2)).
- Capacity includes whatever the hospital customarily does to accommodate patients beyond occupancy limits (Reg (b); App. V).
- Report (489.20(m)): a hospital must report to CMS or the State survey agency "any time it has reason to believe it may have received an individual who has been transferred in an unstable emergency medical condition from another hospital in violation of" Reg (e). App. V (Part I and A-2401): notification "should occur within 72 hours of the occurrence"; failure to report may subject the receiving hospital to termination (42 C.F.R. 489.53). The duty belongs to the recipient; the sending hospital's conduct is what is reported.
- The recipient's own duties to the arriving individual (screening, stabilization) start when the individual comes to its ED, whatever the sending hospital did.

## 10. Administrative duties under 489.20(l), (m), (q) and (r)

|---|---|---|
| 489.20(l) (A-2400) | Comply with 489.24 | Policies not adopted or not enforced |
