## 2. Survey and termination process

- Complaint investigations are unannounced (App. V, Part I; SOM 5440.2). When the CMS location triages an allegation as immediate jeopardy (IJ), the State survey agency starts within two business days of authorization; Non-IJ High allegations start within 45 business days; on-site work runs on consecutive business days (SOM 5440.2, Rev. 243).
- Surveyors request ED logs (6-12 months), on-call lists (6 months), policies, bylaws and rules, transfer consent forms, QAPI minutes, staffing schedules and committee minutes (App. V, Task 1), and sample LWBS, AMA, refusals, transfers and returns within 48 hours (Task 2).
- IJ termination track (SOM 5470.1): runs from the date of the preliminary determination letter; projected termination on the 23rd calendar day; avoided by an acceptable plan of correction or by showing the deficiency did not exist. Non-IJ cases follow SOM 3012 (not read; `[VERIFY]` the day count before quoting it).
- QIO review: before a termination compliance determination on MSE, stabilization or transfer appropriateness, CMS requests a 5-day QIO review except where delay would jeopardize health or safety (Act (d)(3)); before CMPs, a 60-day QIO review (Reg (h)), not required where there was no screening examination or delay would jeopardize health or safety (Reg (h)(3)). The QIO report gives medical opinion and does not state whether EMTALA was violated (Reg (h)(2)(vi)).
- Referral: CMS refers 489.24 violations to OIG for CMPs and physician exclusion, and to the Office for Civil Rights (SOM 5470.1, 5470.2).

## 3. Civil monetary penalties and exclusion

| Who | Statute and regulation | Statutory maximum | Maximum per violation for penalties imposed after Jan. 15, 2025 (45 C.F.R. 102.3, 2025 column) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hospital with 100 or more State-licensed, Medicare-certified beds on the date the penalty is imposed | Act (d)(1)(A); 42 C.F.R. 1003.510(a) | $50,000 | $136,886 |
| Hospital with fewer than 100 such beds | Same | $25,000 | $68,445 |
| Responsible physician, including on-call physician | Act (d)(1)(B); 1003.510(b) | $50,000 | $136,886 |

- Basis (1003.500): each negligent violation of section 1867 or 489.24 (other than 489.24(j)) by a hospital; for a physician, a negligent violation, a certification the physician knew or should have known was wrong, or a misrepresentation of the individual's condition or the hospital's obligations. Exclusion of a physician for a gross and flagrant or repeated violation (1003.500(c)-(d)).
- Mitigating (1003.520(a)): corrective action completed before CMS starts an investigation, including disclosure to CMS before CMS receives a complaint from another source.
- Aggravating (1003.520(b)): requesting proof of insurance, prior authorization or payment before screening or initiating stabilizing treatment (or payment before stabilizing); patient harm or risk of harm; the individual presented with an EMC.
- Older figures such as $109,491 and $54,746 are superseded. The next adjustment depends on HHS action after the October 2026 CPI-U `[VERIFY]`.

## 5. Private actions and circuit-variable questions

- Individuals harmed and medical facilities with financial loss may sue the hospital (not physicians) under State-law damages rules within 2 years (Act (d)(2)). State tort caps and procedures may apply `[VERIFY: State]`.
- Improper motive is not required for a stabilization claim: Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc., 525 U.S. 249 (1999) (per curiam; read in full).
- Admission: 489.24(d)(2) ends the hospital's EMTALA duty on good-faith admission for CMS and OIG enforcement. The Sixth Circuit declined to apply that rule in a private action, holding EMTALA requires treatment to stabilize and not just admission: Moses v. Providence Hospital & Medical Centers, Inc., 561 F.3d 573 (6th Cir. 2009) (read in full). Whether other circuits follow Moses was not checked for this file, and its deference reasoning predates later Supreme Court deference decisions; treat private-action exposure after admission as circuit-variable `[VERIFY: circuit]` and route to counsel.
- EMTALA and State abortion restrictions: the federal suit against Idaho was dismissed in March 2025 and private litigation continued; treat the interaction as unsettled and State- and circuit-variable `[VERIFY]`; never resolve it in a compliance review.
