## 2. Counting rules for the limits

- **Year of receipt.** Count each item against the calendar year (January 1 to December 31) in which the physician received it, using that year's limit. A fiscal-year tracker, a rolling 12 months, or last year's limit applied to this year's items gives the wrong answer.
- **Per physician.** The (k) and (z) limits are aggregates per physician per calendar year.
- **No cash or cash equivalents** under (k) or (m): gift cards, prepaid cards and cash do not qualify (411.357(k)(1), (m)). Log them separately as findings; they need another exception or go to counsel.
- **Not solicited** by the physician or the practice, including staff ((k)(1)(ii)), and not determined in any manner that takes into account referrals ((k)(1)(i)).
- **Medical staff appreciation event.** An entity with a formal medical staff may hold one local appreciation event a year for the entire medical staff in addition to the limit, but gifts or gratuities given at the event count toward the (k) limit ((k)(4)).
- **Incidental benefits** under (m) are per occurrence, on campus, offered to all medical staff in the same specialty, and only while the physician is serving the hospital or its patients; they do not count toward (k).
- **Excess return rule** ((k)(3)): inadvertent excess is deemed within the limit if the excess is no more than 50 percent of the limit (for 2026, $267.50) and the physician returns it (or its value) by the earlier of the end of that calendar year or 180 consecutive calendar days after receipt; usable once every 3 years per physician. A planned excess is not inadvertent.
- **Limited remuneration** (z) covers items or services the physician provides to the entity, not gifts. No writing, signature or set-in-advance requirement, but FMV, commercial reasonableness and no volume or value apply.
- The temporary noncompliance rule (411.353(f)) is not available where the arrangement relied on (k) or (m) (411.353(f)(4)).

## 3. Clocks, each with its own trigger

Show each computed date with its weekday. Never anchor two clocks to the same event unless the rule does. Before any period is labeled "Potential noncompliance: escalate to counsel", go down this table and state for each cure whether it is open, closed or unavailable, and why. Common reasons a cure is unavailable: the 90-day writing and signature rule needs the arrangement to comply in every other respect, including FMV pay set in advance; 411.353(f) needs 180 consecutive days of full compliance first and a cause beyond the entity's control; correction during the term needs an unintended discrepancy against written terms, so it cannot supply a missing writing; 411.353(h) applies only after expiration or termination.

| Element | Trigger event | Period | Limits | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Writing or signature obtained late | The date the writing or signature was required but not yet obtained (the noncompliance date; for a new arrangement, usually the first day services, space or equipment were furnished) | 90 consecutive calendar days immediately following | Only if the arrangement fully complies with the exception apart from writing or signature (411.354(e)(4)(i)); compensation must still be set in advance during the 90 days (85 FR 77595-77596); never available for the writing of a modified compensation formula (411.354(d)(1)(ii)(C)) | 411.354(e)(4) |
| Modified compensation mid-term | The first day items, services, space or equipment are furnished at the modified rate | The modification must be determined and set in writing before that day | All exception requirements met on the effective date; no 90-day grace | 411.354(d)(1)(ii) |
| Holdover | Expiration of an office space, equipment or personal services arrangement that had a term of at least 1 year | No fixed limit | Met all conditions when it expired; same terms and conditions; continues to meet every condition, including FMV | 411.357(a)(7), (b)(6), (d)(1)(vii) |
| Payment reconciliation | Expiration or termination of the compensation arrangement (for a holdover, the end of the holdover, as stated in stark-law-aks-compliance) | No later than 90 consecutive calendar days following | All discrepancies reconciled so the full amount is paid as the arrangement required; otherwise fully compliant | 411.353(h) |
| Correction during the term | Discovery of an unintended payment discrepancy while the arrangement is ongoing | Before the arrangement ends | Unintended administrative or operational error only; counsel confirms | 85 FR 77581-77582 |
| Temporary noncompliance | Date the relationship fell out of compliance for reasons beyond the entity's control, after at least 180 consecutive days of full compliance | Up to 90 consecutive calendar days | Entity promptly rectifies; once every 3 years per physician; not for (k) or (m) | 411.353(f) |
| Excess nonmonetary compensation return | Physician's receipt of the excess | Earlier of December 31 of that year or 180 consecutive calendar days after receipt | Excess at most 50 percent of the limit; once every 3 years per physician | 411.357(k)(3) |
| Overpayment report and return | Identification of the overpayment (the person knowingly receives or retains it); counsel decides when identification occurred | Later of 60 days after identification or the cost report due date | Suspended during a timely good-faith investigation (up to 180 days after initial identification) and while an acknowledged CMS SRDP or OIG SDP submission is pending; 6-year lookback | 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7k(d); 42 C.F.R. 401.305(a)(2), (b), (f) |
| Retention payment frequency | Date of the prior retention arrangement with the same physician | 5 years | No new retention arrangement within the period | 411.357(t)(3)(ii) |
| Recruitment records | Recruitment payments through a physician practice | At least 6 years | Records of actual costs and passed-through amounts available to the Secretary | 411.357(e)(4)(iv) |
| Early termination re-entry bar | Termination of a space, equipment or services arrangement within its first year | Through the end of the first year of the original arrangement | No new arrangement for the same space, equipment or substantially the same services | 411.357(a)(2), (b)(3), (d)(1)(iv) |

## 4. Disclosure and overpayment facts to hand to counsel

The disclosure decision belongs to counsel (stark-law-aks-compliance). This review assembles the facts counsel needs:

- Arrangement, parties (with family relationships and owners), DHS entity, and the dates each period of potential noncompliance began and ended.
- Payments by month as written, as paid and as performed; documents found and not found.
- The date the issue was first reported to compliance and who reported it (counsel uses this for identification under 401.305(a)(2)).
- Whether any AKS concern exists, because that decides the protocol: Stark-only conduct goes to the CMS Voluntary Self-Referral Disclosure Protocol (SRDP); AKS conduct, with or without Stark, goes to the OIG Self-Disclosure Protocol; never both for the same arrangement (OIG SDP, updated October 15, 2026, as confirmed in stark-law-aks-compliance).
- SRDP forms: CMS states that disclosures submitted on or after November 1, 2026 must use the CMS-10328 forms OMB approved on July 17, 2026 (Revised September 2026); earlier versions cannot be used (CMS Physician Self-Referral Spotlight and SRDP pages, read 2026-10-30).
- Settlement amounts and multipliers are not estimated in this review. CMS may reduce amounts owed under ACA section 6409(b); quantification goes to conducting-false-claims-analysis.

## 5. Date arithmetic

- "90 consecutive calendar days immediately following" a date: add 90 days to the trigger date; the last day of the window is trigger + 90 (June 1, 2026 gives Sunday, August 30, 2026). Weekends and holidays count.
- State "today's date" in the header and compute whether each window is open, closed, or not yet started as of that date.
- Where the trigger date is uncertain (an unsigned agreement with no start record), compute the window under each candidate trigger and show which conclusions change.
