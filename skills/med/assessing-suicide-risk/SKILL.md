# Assessing Suicide Risk

Builds suicide risk documentation support for one encounter: a source register, a screen interpretation in the tool's own vocabulary, an assessment content table, a formulation scaffold that the treating clinician completes, a safety plan and lethal means review, a transition check and, for reviewers, a map to the Joint Commission elements of performance in force. It serves a clinician documenting care they delivered and a quality or chart reviewer checking that documentation. Everything it produces is a draft for a qualified clinician; it never replaces the clinician's judgment, never states a final risk level and never writes an order. Every output carries crisis resources: 911 or local emergency services for immediate danger, and the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

## Why This Skill Exists

Suicide caused 48,824 U.S. deaths in 2024, and 27,593 of them (56.5 percent) involved firearms (CDC FastStats). Many people who die by suicide saw a health care provider in the months before, and suicide documentation fails in the same few ways: a negative screen recorded as "no risk", a risk level lifted from a screener color band, a PHQ-9 item 9 standing in for an assessment, a safety plan written for the patient rather than with them, firearm access never asked, a prior day's assessment copied forward, or a discharge with no follow-up. The accreditation layer changed on January 1, 2026: hospitals and critical access hospitals now answer to NPG.08.01.01, while behavioral health organizations keep NPSG.15.01.01. This skill keeps accreditation requirements, tool developer instructions and clinical recommendations apart, and keeps the risk determination with the clinician.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Negative screen (ASQ, C-SSRS screener, PHQ-9 item 9 of zero) | Deciding whether assessment is needed | A negative screen charted as "no risk" or "low risk" while collateral, warning signs, recent behavior or firearm access point the other way; the ASQ itself says clinical judgment can override a negative screen, and about one fifth of later attempts in Simon 2013 came from patients who always answered "not at all" |
| Screener score or C-SSRS color band | Triage and the next step | The band copied in as the overall risk level with no clinician reasoning; Joint Commission requires the level with clinical justification, and SAFE-T calls its own table examples, not determinations |
| PHQ-9 total and item 9 | Depression measure with one self-harm frequency item | Item 9 treated as the suicide assessment; a positive item 9 with no EP 3 inquiry into ideation, plan, intent and behavior; passive death wish and self-harm not separated |
| Patient age, primary reason for care, accreditation program (who is in the population) | Which chapter, which EPs and which tool apply | A 2026 hospital chart reviewed against NPSG.15.01.01; a medical patient's unscreened visit called an EP 2 failure when only organization policy required it; a patient who voiced ideation on a medical unit left out of EPs 3-7; a 16-year-old run through the adult ASQ refusal rule; a child under 8 screened with a tool not validated for that age |
| Safety plan in the record | EP 6 counseling and the patient's crisis tool | Plan composed from the chart and handed over for signature; generic coping steps; step 6 blank; a no-suicide contract in its place; no copy given |
| Lethal means and firearm entries | Means safety counseling | Firearm access never asked, or "denies access" with no question about other household members' guns; medications not addressed; no person or timeframe for storage |
| Prior assessments and templated notes | Continuity across days | Yesterday's assessment carried forward with a new date; "patient denies SI" text contradicting a same-shift nursing note; a screen charted while the patient was asleep or off the unit |
| Discharge summary, transition and follow-up records | EP 6 follow-up care | Discharge with no follow-up contact or appointment, no crisis resources, no hand-off to the next clinician; a timeframe invented that the policy does not set |
| A request to complete, attest or backdate a screen, assessment or safety plan | Passing an audit or closing a chart | Documentation created for care that did not occur; the right path is to record what happened, at the time it is written, and to act on the gap |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Every output is a draft for a qualified clinician (or, in review mode, for the reviewer and the clinician). It supports and never replaces clinical judgment. Never write an observation level, hold, medication or disposition for a patient.
2. **Source-review gate.** Do not call a screen, note or plan missing until every supplied document has been opened and searched, including scanned and oddly named files. Classify each unresolved gap as an unknown fact, a source conflict, an unverified external lookup (for example a requirement not confirmed in the current manual) or a policy election (for example the organization's reassessment interval).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For requirements: law and regulation (CMS CoPs, state law), then the accreditation chapter in force for the program and date, then organization policy, then the tool developer's instructions, then clinical guidelines; a guideline never settles an accreditation question and an accreditation EP never dictates a clinical decision. For facts: the clinician's contemporaneous documentation of the interview and direct observation, then item-level tool records, then nursing and collateral notes, then templated, copied or dashboard text. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every finding carries a source locator (document, date and time, author role) or `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every requirement carries its issuer and citation or `[VERIFY]`.
5. **Label the layer.** Write "Joint Commission NPG.08.01.01 EP 3 (requirement)", "ASQ tool instruction" or "SAFE-T (recommendation)". Never merge them into one pass or fail.
6. **The risk level belongs to the clinician.** Never assign a level from a score, a color band or a denial. Never present a level as final, even when asked. Offer the formulation scaffold and the organization's own scale definitions instead.
7. **Crisis resources stay in.** Every output prints 911 or local emergency services for immediate danger and 988 (call or text 988, chat at 988lifeline.org). If the user indicates that someone is in danger now, lead with those resources before any documentation work.
8. **Never document care that did not occur.** Do not create, complete, attest, pre-populate or backdate a screen, assessment, safety plan, counseling note or follow-up entry for an encounter where it did not happen, and do not supply a template filled with assumed answers. A late entry records care that happened, at the time it is written, under facility policy. Name the gap and the safe next step instead.
9. **Current encounter only.** Every assessment element must come from this encounter. Earlier content is labeled with its date and reported as "Present but not current".
10. **Collaborative safety planning.** A safety plan is drafted only as prompts for the clinician and the person to complete together, in the person's words. Never invent warning signs, coping strategies or contacts, and never offer a no-suicide contract.
11. **Outcome vocabulary.** Elements: Present and current, Present but not current, Absent, Cannot determine. Review result: Meets documented elements, Gaps found, Not in population, Cannot determine. Screen results use the tool's own words. "Cannot determine" names the missing fact and who can supply it.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect before Step 1:

1. Mode: clinical drafting support for care the clinician delivered, or chart or quality review.
2. Setting, accreditation program and encounter date (they select NPG.08.01.01 or NPSG.15.01.01).
3. Patient age and primary reason for care, and whether suicidal ideation was expressed during care.
4. The screen: tool name and version, item-level answers, date and time, who asked, and whether the patient refused.
5. The clinician's assessment text, collateral contacts, lethal means and firearm questions and answers, the safety plan, and disposition and follow-up records.
6. The organization's suicide prevention policy (reassessment, monitoring, discharge counseling and follow-up) and its risk-level scale.

Defaults when an input is missing:

- Program unknown: show both the hospital and BHC citations and mark the finding provisional.
- Policy not supplied: report EP 5 and EP 6 items as policy elections with "policy not supplied"; never invent a timeframe.
- Tool version unknown: apply the current developer instruction and record the version as an unknown fact.
- Firearm question absent: report it as Absent; never infer "no access".

Hard stops:

- **Imminent danger.** The user reports that a patient (or the user) is in danger now, or a patient with an acute positive screen is unsupervised: give 911 and 988 first and tell the user to follow the organization's emergency procedure; documentation work waits.
- **Care that did not occur.** A request to create, complete, attest or backdate a screen, assessment, safety plan or follow-up for an encounter where it did not happen: refuse that part, explain the late-entry rule, and continue with an accurate gap report.
- **Final determination.** A request to state the final risk level, sign off the assessment or choose the disposition: decline; deliver the formulation scaffold for the clinician.
- **No item-level source.** Only a total score or "SI negative" with no item answers and no clinician text: produce the source register and gap list; do not interpret the screen.
- **Crisis outside a clinical role.** If the user appears to be describing their own crisis rather than a patient's chart, stop the clinical workflow and give crisis resources.

## Step 1: Frame the Mode, Program and Layers

Read `references/standards-and-tools.md`, Sections 1 and 2. Restate the question in one line, name the mode, the program and the citation in force for the encounter date. If the user cites NPSG.15.01.01 for a 2026 hospital encounter, say that NPG.08.01.01 now applies and that the EPs carried over in substance.

## Step 2: Build the Source Register and Encounter Ledger

Complete Section 2 of `references/output-template.md`. Log every screen, assessment, collateral call, safety plan, means counseling entry, disposition and follow-up with its time and author. Read `references/assessment-and-safety-planning.md`, Section 7, and test each assessment for copy-forward: identical text across dates, mismatched dates, contradictions with same-shift notes, or a screen charted when the patient could not have answered.

## Step 3: Confirm Population and Tool Fit

Read `references/standards-and-tools.md`, Sections 3 and 4. Decide whether the patient is in the EP 2 population, whether EPs 3-7 apply because ideation was expressed, and whether the tool fits the patient's age and setting. Separate EP findings from findings against a broader organization policy. Confirm the tool was given as its developer directs: no skipped follow-up items, no reworded questions.

## Step 4: Interpret the Screen in the Tool's Own Terms

Read `references/standards-and-tools.md`, Sections 4 and 5. State the result in the tool's vocabulary and the developer's next step, then whether that step is documented. A negative screen is reported with any facts that still call for clinical judgment. PHQ-9 item 9 above "not at all" is a positive screen needing the EP 3 assessment; a zero is never "low risk"; item 9 is never the assessment.

## Step 5: Check the Assessment Content

Read `references/assessment-and-safety-planning.md`, Sections 1 and 2. Fill the assessment content table: ideation, plan, intent, behavior, risk factors, protective factors, access to lethal means including household firearms, and collateral. Mark each Present and current, Present but not current, Absent or Cannot determine. If the patient is still present and the assessment is missing or incomplete, say first that the clinician should complete it before the patient leaves; collateral warning signs or new access to a firearm make this the first line of the output.

## Step 6: Draft the Formulation Scaffold

Read `references/assessment-and-safety-planning.md`, Section 3. Fill the scaffold in `references/output-template.md`, Section 6, with sourced facts and the organization's scale definitions, and leave the clinician's formulation and mitigation plan blank for the clinician. In review mode, report whether the documented level has reasoning that follows from the documented facts; where a screen result and the level disagree, record a source conflict.

## Step 7: Review the Safety Plan and Lethal Means Counseling

Read `references/assessment-and-safety-planning.md`, Sections 4 and 5. Check each Stanley-Brown step for presence, the person's own words and specificity, and check that step 6 addresses the means actually discussed, firearms owned by anyone in the household, and medications. In drafting mode, produce prompts for the clinician and the person to complete together. Flag any no-suicide contract. Note that programming the Stanley-Brown form into an EHR needs the authors' written permission.

## Step 8: Check the Transition and Crisis Resources

Read `references/assessment-and-safety-planning.md`, Section 6. Compare discharge counseling, the follow-up arrangement and the hand-off with the organization's EP 6 policy, quoted. Confirm 988 and emergency services were given. Where the patient has left after a positive screen without the required assessment or follow-up, say so plainly, recommend immediate escalation under organization policy (outreach or a follow-up call as the policy directs; the ASQ tool recommends a follow-up call even when a patient declines the brief suicide safety assessment), print 988 and 911, and route the event to `managing-patient-safety-events`. Never defer this to an audit date.

## Step 9: Reconcile, List Gaps and Hand Off

Complete Sections 9 to 11 of `references/output-template.md`. Check every requirement relied on against `references/primary-sources.md` and carry any `[VERIFY]` flag into the open items. End with the closing line and crisis resources.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

1. Does the output answer the question in the mode asked, with each layer (requirement, tool instruction, recommendation) labeled?
2. Is the citation correct for the program and the encounter date?
3. Is any risk level stated as this skill's conclusion, or derived from a score? Remove it.
4. Does anything create, complete or re-date care that did not occur? Remove it.
5. Is anything phrased as an order for the patient? Rephrase it as a gap or a question for the clinician.
6. Are 911 and 988 present?

## Quality Audit

1. The header names the mode, program, citation in force and encounter date.
2. The source register covers every supplied document with a status; nothing is called missing without a search.
3. Each ledger entry has a time, an author role and a source locator, and is marked current or not.
4. Population and tool fit are tested, and EP findings are kept apart from broader policy findings.
5. The screen result uses the tool's own vocabulary and the developer's next step.
6. No negative screen is reported as no risk, and PHQ-9 item 9 is never treated as an assessment.
7. All six EP 3 content areas and lethal means access, including household firearms, are addressed.
8. The formulation scaffold leaves the level and plan to the clinician; no level is derived from a score.
9. The safety plan review checks collaboration, specificity, step 6 and the absence of a no-suicide contract.
10. Transition findings quote the organization's policy and invent no timeframe.
11. Every conflict is in the reconciliation table and every gap is classified.
12. No content documents, attests or backdates care that did not occur.
13. 911 and 988 appear, and every `[VERIFY]` relied on is in the open items.

## Guidelines

- Ask directly. Asking about suicide does not plant the idea (NIMH ASQ information sheet); vague or softened questions miss ideation (Sentinel Event Alert 56).
- A denial is a data point, not a conclusion; weigh it against collateral, behavior and access to means.
- Reassess at the points the organization's policy names; SAFE-T adds first contact, new behavior or ideation, treatment changes and inpatient discharge.
- For youth, screen without the parent present where possible and gather parent or guardian history; use a trained interpreter for limited English proficiency.
- Protective factors may not counteract significant acute risk (SAFE-T).
- Keep protected health information to what the task needs; de-identify shared drafts. Collateral disclosure and holds depend on state law `[VERIFY]`.
- Do not list the ended 988 "Press 3" option; list 988 call, text and chat, Spanish, veterans press 1, and ASL videophone.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Only a total score or "SI neg" in the chart | Flowsheet export without item answers | Request the item-level form; mark the screen Cannot determine |
| Scanned or handwritten screen or safety plan | Text search missed it | Open it and attempt OCR; if still unreadable, list it and set dependent elements to Cannot determine |
| Two notes on the same day disagree on ideation | Copy-forward, or a real change | Record both with times in the reconciliation table; ask the author; do not pick one |
| Screen color band and documented level disagree | Score copied as the level | Report the conflict and the missing reasoning for the clinician |
| ASQ refusal handled differently by two units | Old and new ASQ instructions | Apply the age rule of the 9/29/25 tool and record which version the policy adopted |
| Policy names a follow-up time the EP does not | Organization election | Hold the chart to the policy and label the finding as a policy finding |
| Firearm answer belongs to a different household member | Question asked too narrowly | Report access as Cannot determine and list the household question as open |
| Patient left after a positive screen without assessment | Discharge before the consult or brief assessment | Recommend immediate escalation under organization policy (outreach or follow-up call per policy; the ASQ tool recommends a follow-up call); print 988 and 911; route to managing-patient-safety-events |
