# Standards and Screening Tools

Requirements and tool rules checked October 30, 2026. Each statement names its issuer. A Joint Commission element of performance is an accreditation requirement; a tool developer's instruction governs how the tool is used; a clinical guideline is a recommendation. Keep the three apart in every output.

## 2. The seven elements of performance

Hospital wording (NPG.08.01.01). The BHC wording (NPSG.15.01.01) matches in substance, with "individuals served".

| EP | Requirement (paraphrased; quote the manual in a final deliverable) | What a chart or program review looks for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Psychiatric hospitals and units: environmental risk assessment for features usable in a suicide attempt (anchor points, door hinges, hooks), with action to minimize them. Nonpsychiatric units: procedures for patients at high risk, such as one-to-one monitoring, removing objects where medically possible, checking visitor items and safe transport. Nonpsychiatric units need not be ligature resistant. Documentation required | Program level: the environmental assessment and its follow-up. Patient level: the precautions actually in place for a high-risk patient on a medical unit |
| 2 | Screen all patients being evaluated or treated for behavioral health conditions as their primary reason for care, using a validated screening tool. Note: screening with a validated tool is required for patients age 12 and above | A dated screen with item-level answers, the tool's name and version, who asked, and the age of the patient |
| 3 | For patients who screen positive: an evidence-based process for a suicide assessment that directly asks about suicidal ideation, plan, intent, suicidal or self-harm behaviors, risk factors and protective factors. EPs 2 and 3 may be met by one instrument that screens and grades severity | Each of the six named content areas documented for this encounter |
| 4 | Document the overall level of risk for suicide and the plan to mitigate it | A risk level with the clinician's reasoning and a plan that follows from it |
| 5 | Written policies and procedures for patients at risk, including staff training and competence, guidelines for reassessment, and monitoring of patients at high risk. Documentation required | Reassessment at the times the policy names; monitoring at the level the policy names |
| 6 | Written policies and procedures for counseling and follow-up care at discharge for patients identified as at risk | Discharge counseling, the safety plan, crisis resources and a follow-up arrangement, as the policy defines them |
| 7 | Monitor implementation and effectiveness of the screening, assessment and management policies, and act to improve compliance | Program level: audits and action plans |

Joint Commission FAQ on EP 3 (BHC FAQ 000002237, reviewed February 1, 2022, written under the old number): the process must determine a level of risk (for example high, moderate or low), documented "with clinical justification", together with the plans to mitigate it. If an organization does not use an evidence-based tool, it must document how the level was determined, ask directly about the six areas, and show the evidence-based resource behind its process.

Joint Commission FAQ on EP 2 (hospital FAQ 000002240): screening is not required for every hospital patient, though screening patients with secondary behavioral complaints is encouraged. A validated tool must be used as its creators direct and its wording must not be changed; the FAQ gives skipped C-SSRS follow-up questions and a skipped ASQ acuity question as examples that compromise validity. Tools should fit the population, for example by age.

## 3. Who is in the population

| Patient | Hospital or CAH (NPG.08.01.01) | BHC (NPSG.15.01.01) |
|---|---|---|
| Psychiatric distinct part unit, or behavioral health condition as the primary reason for care | EPs 2-7 apply | EPs 2-7 apply to all individuals served |
| Medical or surgical primary reason, no suicidal ideation expressed | EP 2 does not require screening (screening encouraged) | Not applicable |
| Any patient who expresses suicidal ideation during care | EPs 3-7 apply, whatever the primary reason | EPs 3-7 apply |
| Under 12 | The age-12 note sets the floor of the screening requirement; younger patients are screened under organization policy and the tool's own age range | Same note |

An organization may screen more broadly than the EPs require (for example universal screening at age 12 and above, as the AAP recommends). A gap against that broader policy is a policy finding, not an EP finding; say which one it is.

## 4. Screening tools and their populations

| Tool | Questions and logic (developer's instruction) | Population | Result vocabulary |
|---|---|---|---|
| ASQ (NIMH), screening tool dated 9/29/25 | Four yes-or-no items (wished dead, past few weeks; family better off if dead, past few weeks; thoughts of killing yourself, past week; ever tried, with when). Any yes to 1-4: ask item 5, "Are you having thoughts of killing yourself right now?" | Youth and adults in medical settings. Toolkit: medical patients age 8 and above; youth materials ages 8-24; adult materials 18 and above, overlap at clinician discretion. NIMH: no tool is validated under age 8; a full mental health evaluation is recommended instead when risk is suspected | Negative screen; Non-acute positive screen (potential risk); Acute positive screen (imminent or acute risk) |
| ASQ refusal rule (9/29/25 tool) | Youth refusal is a non-acute positive screen. Adult refusal is not a positive screen; document the refusal | As above | Conflict: the older NIMH ASQ information sheet treats any refusal as positive; record which version the organization adopted |
| ASQ next steps (9/29/25 tool) | Acute positive: STAT safety or full mental health evaluation, patient cannot leave until evaluated, keep in sight, remove dangerous objects where possible, alert the responsible clinician. Non-acute positive: brief suicide safety assessment (BSSA); no 1:1 observation required unless other concerns; an adult (or a youth's parent or guardian) who refuses the BSSA may leave unless other safety concerns exist, with the refusal documented and a follow-up call recommended. A positive due only to a lifetime attempt more than a year ago may not need a BSSA for adults; for youth only if a BSSA has been documented since that attempt | As above | The tool also states that clinical judgment can always override a negative screen, and directs giving 988 and the Crisis Text Line (text HOME or HOLA to 741741) to all patients |
| C-SSRS Screen Version (Columbia Lighthouse Project), 2026 forms | Ask 1 (wish to be dead) and 2 (thoughts of killing yourself), past month. Yes to 2: ask 3 (method), 4 (intent), 5 (plan with intent) and 6. No to 2: go to 6 (any preparatory act or attempt, lifetime, then whether within 3 months) | Columbia FAQ: the standard version suits most children and adults; it has been used with children 6-12; a version for very young children and the cognitively impaired (ages 4-5) exists | Columbia: the most worrisome answers are a recent yes to 4 or 5 and any behavior within 3 months. The ED form routes items 1-3 to behavioral health referral at discharge, and items 4, 5 and 6 within 3 months to immediate notification of the physician or behavioral health and patient safety precautions. The primary care form routes 4, 5 and recent 6 to behavioral health consultation and precautions. The generic form carries low, moderate and high color bands `[VERIFY]` for the item-to-band mapping |
| PSS-3 (ED-SAFE Patient Safety Screener) | Named by the Joint Commission (Sentinel Event Alert 56) and the AAP as a brief screener | Questions and age range not read in this review `[VERIFY]` | Use only with the developer's own instructions |
| PHQ-9 | A depression measure; item 9 is one frequency item. See Section 5 | Adults and adolescents in the cited studies | Not a suicide risk level |

Columbia states that the triage next steps on its setting forms are examples that organizations change to fit their own resources and policies. The organization's adopted pathway therefore controls the next step; the questions and their order do not change (Joint Commission FAQ 000002240).

AAP Blueprint for Youth Suicide Prevention (screening page): universal screening at 12 and above; ages 8-11 when clinically indicated (behavioral health complaint, parent or patient concern, history of suicidal ideation or behavior, or warning signs); under 8, screening is not indicated and warning signs prompt assessment. It recommends screening without the parent present where possible and a trained interpreter for limited English proficiency.

NIMH ASQ Youth Emergency Department Script for Nursing Staff: the nurse asks the parent or guardian to step out ("We ask these questions in private"), screens in private, and if the screen is positive tells the parent that someone trained to talk with kids about suicide will come to speak with them. The ASQ toolkit page adds that if the parent refuses to leave or the child insists they stay, screening goes ahead with the parent present.

## 5. PHQ-9 item 9

- Wording (as quoted by Simon 2013): over the last two weeks, how often bothered by "thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way". It mixes passive thoughts of death with self-harm and asks nothing about plan, intent, behavior or access to means.
- Simon 2013 (84,418 outpatients aged 13 and above): one-year risk of attempt rose from about 0.4 percent ("not at all") to about 4 percent ("nearly every day"). About one fifth of suicide attempts and one fifth of suicide deaths occurred among the 70 percent of patients who answered "not at all" every time. The authors caution that no ideation on the PHQ does not rule out later risk.
- VA/DoD 2024 guideline: lists the PHQ-9 among general-population screening tools, removed the 2019 reference to item 9, and notes that the evidence came from administering all nine items, so it cannot address asking item 9 alone.
- Consequences for a draft: any item 9 response above "not at all" is a positive screen that calls for the EP 3 assessment; a zero on item 9 is one data point, never a determination of low risk; item 9 never satisfies EP 3.

## 6. Requirements that are not in the elements of performance

- No EP sets a time for reassessment or for a post-discharge contact; those come from the organization's EP 5 and EP 6 policies. Quote the policy; do not invent a number.
- No EP names a specific tool, a specific risk-level scale or a specific safety plan form.
- Involuntary holds, duty-to-protect disclosures, firearm removal orders and minors' consent are state law `[VERIFY]` for the jurisdiction; route to `managing-involuntary-commitments` or counsel.
