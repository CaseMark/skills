## 1. SAFE-T structure

SAMHSA, SAFE-T (Suicide Assessment Five-step Evaluation and Triage), HHS Publication (SMA) 09-4432, updated 2024 (SAMHSA Library PEP24-01-036, December 2024):

1. Identify risk factors, noting those that can be modified.
2. Identify protective factors, noting those that can be enhanced. Protective factors, even if present, may not counteract significant acute risk.
3. Conduct the suicide inquiry: ideation (frequency, intensity, duration in the last 48 hours, past month and worst ever), plan (timing, location, lethality, availability, preparatory acts), behaviors (past attempts, aborted attempts, rehearsals, as against non-suicidal self-injury) and intent (how far the person expects to act and believes the act lethal; reasons to die against reasons to live). For youth, ask the parent or guardian too.
4. Determine risk level and intervention. "Assessment of risk level is based on clinical judgment, after completing steps 1-3." Reassess as circumstances change. Develop a safety plan for individuals at low, moderate and high risk. The card's risk table is "an example of a range of risk levels and interventions, not actual determinations."
5. Document risk level and rationale, the treatment plan to address current risk (for example safety plan, medication, psychotherapy, contact with significant others, consultation), counseling on access to lethal means, and the follow-up plan. Patients receive a copy of their safety plan; for youth, the plan includes roles for a parent, guardian or supportive adult.

When to assess (2024 card): at first contact; with any later suicidal behavior, increased ideation or pertinent clinical change; before changes in behavioral health treatment; and at inpatient discharge. The 2009 card also named "prior to increasing privileges" for inpatients.

## 2. Assessment content checklist

Each row is completed from this encounter, with a source locator, or marked as a gap.

| Domain | Minimum content | Source of the requirement or recommendation |

| Ideation | Present or absent; passive or active; frequency, intensity, duration; recent and worst | NPG.08.01.01 EP 3; SAFE-T step 3 |
| Plan | Method, timing, location, preparatory acts, rehearsal | EP 3; SAFE-T |
| Intent | Expectation of acting; perceived lethality; ambivalence | EP 3; SAFE-T |

## 3. Risk formulation: what a draft may and may not say

- The level of risk is the treating clinician's determination (SAFE-T step 4; Joint Commission FAQ 000002237 requires "clinical justification"). VA/DoD 2024 Recommendation 4 finds insufficient evidence for any specific tool or method to determine risk level, and the guideline notes that risk assessments have poor predictive value.
- A draft may: list the documented risk factors, protective factors, warning signs and inquiry findings with sources; show how the organization's adopted scale defines each level; state which facts are missing; and offer a sentence frame the clinician completes, such as "Clinician's overall risk formulation: ____ because ____."
- A draft may not: assign a level from a screen score or color band; state a level as final; write "low risk" because the patient denied ideation; choose an observation level, hold or medication for the patient.
- When a screener outcome and a documented level disagree (for example an ASQ acute positive with "low risk" and no reasoning), report it as a source conflict for the clinician; do not resolve it.

## 4. Stanley-Brown Safety Planning Intervention

Source: suicidesafetyplan.com; Stanley and Brown, Cognitive and Behavioral Practice 2012;19(2):256-264; form copyright 2008, 2021, current form dated 05-02-2024.

The intervention is more than the form. Its tasks include a risk assessment with a description of a recent crisis, reviewing the suicide risk curve, giving the rationale, developing the plan collaboratively with the person, explaining how to use it, discussing where it is kept and who it is shared with, and reviewing it later.

Steps on the plan:

1. Warning signs of an impending crisis (the person's own thoughts, feelings, behaviors).
2. Internal coping strategies the person can use alone.
3. People and social settings that provide distraction.
4. People the person can ask for help, who are told the person is in crisis.
5. Professionals and agencies to contact in a crisis, including a crisis line (the form's example is 988) and the nearest emergency department.
6. Making the environment safer: a plan for lethal means safety. The website states this applies even if the person names no specific plan.

The website also shows an optional further step `[VERIFY]` its content.

Form permissions (suicidesafetyplan.com, Forms page): individual use is permitted; written permission from the authors is needed to change wording, translate, or program the form into an electronic medical record or other digital application. Building the form into an EHR is therefore a policy election for the organization, not a drafting decision.

Evidence: Stanley et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2018;75(9):894-900, a cohort comparison in VA emergency departments (1,640 adults; 1,186 intervention, 454 comparison): the Safety Planning Intervention with structured telephone follow-up (first contact attempted within 72 hours of discharge) was associated with 45 percent fewer suicidal behaviors over 6 months (3.03 against 5.29 percent; odds ratio 0.56, 95% CI 0.33-0.95) and doubled odds of attending outpatient mental health care (odds ratio 2.06). The Joint Commission cites this study as the reference for EP 6.

A safety plan is not a no-suicide contract. Sentinel Event Alert 56 (February 24, 2016) states that a no-suicide contract is not recommended and that the plan should be reviewed at every interaction while risk persists.

Integrity checks for a plan in the record: written in the person's words; specific names and numbers; step 6 filled with the means discussed and who will act; dated for this encounter; a copy given; reviewed with the person, not composed for them.

## 5. Lethal means safety counseling

- VA/DoD 2024 Recommendation 21 (weak for): reduce access to lethal means to reduce suicide by firearms, jumping or medication overdose. The discussion notes about 90 percent of suicide attempts involving firearms result in death.
- CDC FastStats (2024 data): 48,824 suicide deaths, of which 27,593 (56.5 percent) were firearm suicides.
- VA/DoD Psychological Health Center of Excellence, "Lethal Means Safety Counseling: Recommendations for Providers" (updated December 2024): raise the issue (how firearms and medications are stored at home); develop a plan with safe storage and the family's help; document roles and timeframe; confirm the plan was implemented. Ask direct questions about firearm access and explain why; learn why the person owns firearms; frame storage as temporary; do not take possession of firearms, tell patients to "give away" firearms or ask them to bring firearms to the clinic.
- Sentinel Event Alert 56: assess access to firearms and other means such as prescription medications and chemicals, and discuss removing or locking them up during crisis periods.
- Training resource: CALM (Counseling on Access to Lethal Means), listed by the Suicide Prevention Resource Center.
- A firearm owned by another household member is still access. "Denies access" without a question about the household is not an answer; record the exact question and reply.
- Temporary transfer of firearms and extreme risk protection orders are state law `[VERIFY]` for the jurisdiction.

## 6. Transitions, follow-up and crisis resources

- EP 6 requires written policies for counseling and follow-up care at discharge; the policy sets the content and timing.
- Sentinel Event Alert 56 recommends direct referral and linkage to outpatient follow-up within one week of the initial assessment for patients at lower risk, and notes elevated risk in the first week after discharge from inpatient psychiatric care.
- VA/DoD 2024 Recommendation 16 (weak for): periodic caring communications for 12 months after hospitalization related to suicide risk. Recommendation 17: insufficient evidence for or against brief contact interventions after ED discharge.
- 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline (SAMHSA FAQ, updated December 16, 2025): call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org, 24/7; English and Spanish call, text and chat, with interpreters in more than 240 languages; Veterans and service members press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line; ASL by videophone; TTY users dial 711 then 988 (988lifeline.org). 988 went live July 16, 2022 (HHS release, July 15, 2022). SAMHSA ended the separate "Press 3" LGBTQI+ youth option on July 17, 2025 (statement of June 17, 2025); do not list it.
- Emergency services: SAMHSA's 988 FAQ names a suicide attempt in progress, or a specific plan the person intends to carry out now with means available, as times to call 911. Every draft keeps both 988 and emergency services (911 in the U.S.).

## 7. Copy-forward and documentation integrity

- Joint Commission Quick Safety Issue 10 (updated July 2021): copy and paste causes note bloat, internal inconsistency, error propagation and wrong-chart entries; copied material should be identifiable and reviewed.
- Signs of a carried-forward assessment: identical text across dates; a date, location or "today" that does not match the encounter; findings that contradict nursing notes from the same shift; a screen answered while the patient was documented as asleep, sedated or off the unit.
- A carried-forward assessment is reported as "Present but not current". The draft never edits, re-dates or completes it.
- Late entries follow organization policy and carry the actual time written. A late entry records care that happened; it never creates care that did not.
