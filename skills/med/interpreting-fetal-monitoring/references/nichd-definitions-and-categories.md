# NICHD Definitions, Windows and the Three-Tier Categories

## 2. Baseline and variability

- Exclude accelerations, decelerations, periods of marked variability and segments differing by more than 25 bpm before estimating the baseline (CPG 10 Table 1).
- A deceleration lasting 10 minutes or more becomes the new baseline; one lasting 2 to under 10 minutes is a prolonged deceleration, not bradycardia.
- Variability classes (amplitude range): absent, undetectable; minimal, detectable but 5 bpm or fewer; moderate, 6 to 25 bpm; marked, more than 25 bpm. No distinction is drawn between short-term and long-term variability in visual reading (NICHD 2008).
- Moderate variability and accelerations reliably predict the absence of fetal metabolic acidemia at the time observed; minimal or absent variability alone does not reliably predict acidemia (NICHD 2008).
- Drug effects on variability and accelerations (opioids, magnesium sulfate, betamethasone, terbutaline) are tabulated in PB 106; they explain a finding but do not change its label.

## 3. Accelerations and decelerations

- Apply the gestational-age rule from the documented gestational age on the tracing date. If gestational age is unknown or straddles 32 weeks, report the result under both thresholds.
- Early and late decelerations are both gradual; the only difference is nadir timing against the contraction peak. Neither has a depth threshold. A variable deceleration is abrupt and needs 15 bpm and 15 seconds.
- Classifying early or late requires a readable contraction channel. With no uterine activity trace, call the deceleration "unclassified (no contraction reference)".
- Quantify each deceleration by nadir depth in bpm and duration from beginning to end (NICHD 2008). Variable decelerations with slow return, overshoots or "shoulders" are listed as Category II examples.
- Count recurrence against contractions, not minutes, and show the fraction (for example 3 of 5 = 60 percent).

## 4. Uterine activity

- Count contractions in each 10-minute block, sum over 30 minutes, divide by 3. A single block above 5 is not tachysystole if the average is 5 or fewer; a 30-minute average above 5 is tachysystole even if only one block exceeds 5.
- Always qualify tachysystole as with or without associated FHR decelerations. It applies to spontaneous and stimulated labor. "Hyperstimulation" and "hypercontractility" are not defined and should be abandoned (NICHD 2008; PB 106).
- Duration, intensity and resting tone also matter; an external tocodynamometer shows frequency and timing but not intensity or resting tone.

## 5. Three-tier categories and Category II features

| Category | Criteria (NICHD 2008; CPG 10 Box 1) |
|---|---|
| I | All of: baseline 110-160; moderate variability; late or variable decelerations absent; early decelerations present or absent; accelerations present or absent |
| III | Absent variability with any of recurrent late decelerations, recurrent variable decelerations or bradycardia; or a sinusoidal pattern |
| II | Everything else. Examples: bradycardia not accompanied by absent variability; tachycardia; minimal variability; absent variability without recurrent decelerations; marked variability; absence of induced accelerations after fetal stimulation; recurrent variable decelerations with minimal or moderate variability; prolonged deceleration 2 to under 10 minutes; recurrent late decelerations with moderate variability (CPG 10 adds "minimal or"); variable decelerations with slow return, overshoots or shoulders |

A category describes the fetus at that point in time; tracings move between categories (NICHD 2008). Assign a category per segment with clock times.

CPG 10 Box 3 (Category II characteristics, drawn from cited studies; one item marked expert opinion):

- Higher-risk features: absent variability; prolonged, otherwise unexplained, minimal variability; unexplained change in baseline from normal to tachycardia; recurrent late, recurrent variable, or more than one prolonged deceleration.
- Protective features: moderate variability; accelerations.

## 6. Is the signal fetal?

- An external Doppler can record the maternal heart rate (MHR). A key indicator is an acceleration with pushing; in the second stage MHR rises into the fetal range (CPG 10). FIGO 2015 adds accelerations coinciding with contractions and expulsive efforts.
- Compare the recorded rate with the maternal pulse at the same clock time from the flowsheet or pulse oximeter. Matching rates, or a sudden rate change when an FSE is applied, mark the earlier segment as signal source not confirmed.
- CPG 10 names a maternal pulse oximeter and, if doubt persists, a fetal scalp electrode as ways to distinguish the two. Note any absolute contraindication in CPG 10 Box 2 if FSE use is questioned.
- Twins: confirm each channel records a different fetus; nearly identical tracings suggest duplicate monitoring of one twin (FIGO 2015).

## 7. Paper speed, scale and signal artifacts

- Paper speed is usually 3 cm/min in North America and Japan and 1 cm/min in many other countries (2 cm/min in the Netherlands); the vertical scale is 20 or 30 bpm/cm (FIGO 2015). Variability looks reduced at 3 cm/min to a reader used to 1 cm/min, and exaggerated the other way (FIGO 2015). A study found 1 cm/min strips were read as having less variability and more accelerations than the same tracings at 3 cm/min (Peleg et al. 2015, abstract).
- Measure seconds and minutes from the printed time marks, never from an assumed paper speed. Record the speed and scale of every strip reviewed.
- External monitoring is prone to signal loss, double-counting and half-counting, especially in the second stage (FIGO 2015). Mark gaps and artifact; do not interpolate.

## 8. Known differences between sources

| Item | NICHD 2008 / PB 106 / PB 116 | CPG 10 (2025) | How to handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recurrent late decelerations in the Category II example list | "with moderate baseline variability" | "with minimal or moderate baseline variability" | Same result: with minimal variability the tracing is Category II by exclusion in both texts. Name the wording difference if a reviewer relies on it |
| Category III response | "Provision of maternal oxygen" listed among efforts | Recommends against routine maternal oxygen for Category II or III without maternal hypoxia | Apply the guidance in force on the date of care |
| Review frequency | Numeric intervals (PB 106, PB 116) | Hospital protocol | Apply the policy in force on the date of care |
| Recurrence wording | NICHD: 50 percent or more in any 20-minute window; PB 106: at least one half of contractions | Not restated | Use the NICHD 20-minute window and show the count |
