# Retrospective Strip Review Rules

Rules for quality, peer review and medical-legal review of a completed labor. Concurrent documentation support uses only Sections 3 and 5.

## Contents

1. Guidance in force on the date of care
2. Outcome-blind first pass
3. Clinical timeline and clock reconciliation

## 1. Guidance in force on the date of care

| Date of care | ACOG guidance in force | Items that changed |
|---|---|---|
| Before July 2009 | Practice Bulletin 70 (Dec. 2005) and the 1997 NICHD definitions; NICHD 2008 published Sept. 2008 | Not reviewed by this skill: `[VERIFY]` before relying on any rule |
| July 2009 to Nov. 2010 | PB 106 (July 2009), adopting NICHD 2008 and the three-tier system | Review frequency stated numerically; maternal oxygen listed among Category III efforts |
| Nov. 2010 to Jan. 2022 | PB 106 and PB 116 (Nov. 2010) | PB 116 management algorithms; tachysystole algorithm; oxygen in the resuscitation table |
| Jan. 2022 to Sept. 2025 | PB 106, PB 116 and the ACOG Practice Advisory on oxygen (Jan. 2022) | Routine oxygen for fetal resuscitation not recommended with normal maternal saturation |
| CPG 10 online Sept. 18, 2025; October 2025 issue; in force since | ACOG CPG No. 10, which replaces PB 106, PB 116 and the 2022 oxygen advisory | Graded recommendations; review frequency set by hospital protocol; recommends against routine maternal oxygen without maternal hypoxia; conditional recommendation for a rapid-acting uterine relaxation agent when tachysystole with Category III or high-risk Category II tracings persists after pausing oxytocin; against routine STAN, fetal pulse oximetry and primary reliance on computerized interpretation |

- For care between Sept. 18 and Oct. 31, 2025, which document a reviewer treats as current is a policy election: show both and state which changes matter.
- ACOG documents say they are not a statement of the standard of care and do not dictate an exclusive course (CPG 10 and PB 106 front matter). Report guideline content as guidance in force, never as the legal standard.
- Facility policy (documentation frequency, oxytocin protocol, escalation chain, nomenclature) is a separate authority. Ask for the version in force on the date of care; a missing policy is a policy-election gap.

## 2. Outcome-blind first pass

Knowledge of a poor neonatal outcome changes how clinicians read identical tracings and judge care: Zain et al. (J Reprod Med 1998) and Ayres-de-Campos et al. (BJOG 2011) found more hypoxia or pathological readings once the outcome was known, and a 2026 study of 128 obstetricians (Am J Perinatol, doi 10.1055/a-2894-7944) found more severe categorization and twice the rate of "below standard" judgments for identical tracings when told of an adverse outcome.

1. If the outcome (cord gases, Apgar scores, neonatal course, imaging) is in the file, set it aside. Interpret the tracing segment by segment first, using only information available at that clock time.
2. Record the outcome-blind result in its own table before opening the outcome.
3. Then compare. Any reading that changes after the outcome is opened is listed in the reconciliation table with the reason; a change needs a tracing feature, not the outcome, to support it.
4. Never infer a feature from the outcome (for example "absent variability must have been present because the pH was 6.9").

## 3. Clinical timeline and clock reconciliation

- Build one timeline: labor stage and cervical exams, membrane rupture, monitoring mode changes, medications with rates and times (oxytocin titration, epidural boluses, opioids, magnesium, terbutaline), maternal vitals including pulse and SpO2, position changes, notifications, decision time and delivery time.
- The EFM archive clock, the EHR clock and handwritten times can differ. Find an anchor event recorded in both (FSE placement, epidural bolus annotation, delivery) and state the offset before aligning anything.
- Annotations typed onto the tracing and flowsheet entries carry an entry time and an event time; record both. A late entry is labeled as such under facility policy.
- Reading a strip without the timeline is a hard limit on interpretation: drug effects, maternal fever, pushing and position changes explain features that look alike.

## 4. Charted interpretation against the tracing

- Compare each charted assessment (baseline, variability, accelerations, decelerations, uterine activity, category) with the tracing for the same clock window. Record agreement or the specific mismatch.
- A charted category is a fact about the record, not about the fetus. If the chart says Category I and the tracing shows recurrent late decelerations, report both and do not choose silently.
- Legacy terms ("reassuring", "nonreassuring", "fetal distress", "hyperstimulation") are quoted as written and mapped to NICHD terms; the mapping is shown, not substituted.
- Documentation frequency is tested only against the policy in force (Section 1).

## 5. Responses recorded on the timeline

CPG 10 Table 2 lists findings, likely pathophysiology and potential interventions. Use it to describe what was done and when, never to direct care.

| Finding | Potential interventions listed in CPG 10 Table 2 |
|---|---|
| Changes with maternal hypotension (often after regional anesthesia) | IV fluid bolus; IV vasopressor; position change; lower basal epidural infusion rate if recurrent hypotension |
| Intermittent or recurrent late decelerations without hypotension | IV fluid bolus; position change; reduce or stop induction or augmentation agents |
| Intermittent or recurrent variable decelerations | Position change; amnioinfusion |
| Fetal tachycardia | Evaluate and treat maternal causes (infection, hyperthyroidism, dehydration, ketoacidosis) |
| Absent or persistent minimal variability without decelerations | Vibroacoustic or scalp stimulation; IV fluid bolus; evaluate the cause |
| Tachysystole with FHR changes | Reduce or stop agents; IV fluid bolus; short-acting uterine relaxant |
| Recurrent decelerations with pushing in the second stage | Change pushing cadence; change position; evaluate for operative vaginal delivery |

- CPG 10 sets no fixed time for each Category II intervention or for observation; decisions are individualized. PB 116 noted that the historical 30-minute decision-to-incision rule lacks supporting evidence.
- For each response, record finding time, recognition time (first charted note), notification time, action time and the tracing after the action. Each interval runs from its own event; do not run all of them from the first abnormal minute.

## 6. Scope limits

- The draft describes the tracing, the timeline and the documentation against the guidance in force. It does not say whether care met or breached the standard of care, and it does not state causation of neonatal injury.
- FHR patterns reflect current acid-base status and cannot predict cerebral palsy (NICHD 2008). Neonatal encephalopathy causation, cord gas interpretation and timing of injury belong to a qualified expert; ACOG's task force report, Neonatal Encephalopathy and Neurologic Outcome (2nd ed., 2014; reaffirmed 2019), is the starting reference to hand off.
- When counsel or a committee asks for a pre-set conclusion, give the actual reading and list the requested conclusion as a conflict.
