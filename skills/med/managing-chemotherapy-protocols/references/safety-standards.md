# Safety Standards for Ordering, Verification and Handling

Organization-level standards an antineoplastic order and its documentation are checked against. These are accreditation-style and professional standards, not clinical dosing recommendations; report them in their own section and never as a clinical conclusion. Standard numbers are from the ASCO-ONS Antineoplastic Therapy Administration Safety Standards for Adult and Pediatric Oncology (Siegel et al., JCO Oncology Practice and Oncology Nursing Forum 51(4):297-320, 2024), the edition still cited by ONS in January 2026.

## 1. Regimens, exception orders and research regimens

- 3.1: the organization defines standard regimens by diagnosis, with references.
- 3.2: the organization verifies institutional review board approval of research regimens.
- 3.4: an order that varies from the standard regimen (an order set used for a disease not assigned, an added drug, a dose or schedule escalated beyond the regimen) is an exception order. 3.4.1: it requires a supporting reference and/or authorization by a second licensed practitioner before ordering, signing or administration. 3.4.2: the rationale is documented in the health record.
- 3.6: standardized regimen-level preprinted or electronic orders for parenteral and oral therapy; 1.16: EMR ordering format when feasible.
- Version check (skill rule, not an ASCO-ONS text): record the order set's version and effective date and the regimen reference it cites. If the published regimen, the order set and a trial protocol amendment differ, list each difference; never pick one silently.

## 2. Required order elements

3.9: patient name and date of birth, plus in the record: 3.9.1 a second identifier; 3.9.2 date signed; 3.9.3 prescriber; 3.9.4 regimen or protocol name and/or number; 3.9.5 cycle and day number; 3.9.6 full generic names; 3.9.7 doses per organization abbreviation policy (no trailing zeros, leading zeros used); 3.9.8 the dose calculation (methodology, variables, re-evaluation frequency, recalculation trigger); 3.9.9 date of administration; 3.9.10 route; 3.9.11 allergies confirmed before administration; 3.9.12 supportive care (premedication, hydration, growth factors, hypersensitivity medications) in the order form; 3.9.13 hold or modify parameters; 3.9.14 sequencing of oral and parenteral drugs; 3.9.15 rate for parenteral drugs; 3.9.16 time limitation (number of cycles the order is valid).

3.5.1: verbal orders are not allowed except to hold or stop therapy. 3.5.2: new orders or changes, including dose and schedule changes told directly to patients, are documented.

## 3. Oral antineoplastics

- 3.10: all oral antineoplastics are ordered in the EMR or on preprinted forms and documented in the record, whether dispensed by the ordering facility, another facility or a specialty pharmacy. Added elements: 3.10.1 quantity or volume to dispense; 3.10.2 number of refills, zero the preferred default; 3.10.3 schedule of administration.
- 3.11.4 oral labels: dosage form, quantity per container, pills per dose, administration schedule including days on and off (cycle length) and follow-up, food and home-medication instructions, storage and handling warnings.
- 1.9: the medication list, including over-the-counter products, herbals and supplements, is updated at every encounter and reviewed by a licensed practitioner when it changes. 4.4 and 4.6: adherence and barriers to adherence are assessed.

## 4. Preparation and labeling

- 3.11.1: prepared by a licensed pharmacist, pharmacy technician or registered nurse with documented education, training and annual competency.
- 3.11.2 labels, placed immediately on preparation: patient name, date of birth, prescriber, date of preparation and expiration, full generic names, dose, route, and a HAZARDOUS DRUG label if applicable. 3.11.3 parenteral: total volume, divided-dose sequence (for example "one of five"), date to be administered, storage and handling warnings.
- 3.7: products prepared offsite (including white- or brown-bagged products) need a quality-control policy documenting the outside pharmacy's compliance.

## 5. The four verifications

3.12.2: personnel approved by the organization perform four separate verifications, in person or by institutionally approved video technology.

| Verification | When | Who | Elements |
|---|---|---|---|
| First (3.12.2.1) | Before preparation | Personnel approved to prepare or administer | Two identifiers; drug; dose; route; rate; dose calculation with its variables; treatment day and cycle |
| Second (3.12.2.2) | Upon preparation | A second licensed clinician approved to prepare | Vials; concentration; volume or weight; diluent type and volume; route, filters and tubing |
| Third (3.12.2.3) | After preparation, before each administration | At least two licensed clinicians, independently | Drug; dose; infusion or syringe volume; rate; route; expiration; appearance and integrity |
| Fourth (3.12.2.4, new in 2024) | In the presence of the patient | At least two licensed clinicians, at least one on site | Two identifiers; drug; dose; rate and duration; route; administration set; pump settings |

3.12.5: the record confirms the four verifications before parenteral administration. 3.12.1: in an organization treating patients under 18, a licensed pharmacist verifies all orders before dispensing parenteral antineoplastics. The standards do not require two pharmacists; a two-pharmacist check is an organization policy and is scored as one. Treat a single signature entered in two fields, or a check performed while viewing the first checker's result, as not independent.

## 6. Intrathecal therapy and vinca alkaloids

- 3.13: intrathecal or intraventricular agents are 3.13.1 prepared separately; 3.13.2 labeled immediately with a unique intrathecal label; 3.13.3 stored apart from other antineoplastics; 3.13.4 delivered only with other medications intended for the central nervous system; 3.13.5 given immediately after a time-out and two-practitioner double check.
- 3.13.6: an organization that gives intrathecal therapy has a policy that intravenous vinca alkaloids are given only by infusion, for example minibags.
- Vincristine label (Hospira): for intravenous use only; intrathecal administration usually results in death; to reduce fatal route errors it should be diluted in a flexible plastic container and labeled "FOR INTRAVENOUS USE ONLY - FATAL IF GIVEN BY OTHER ROUTES".

## 7. ISMP Targeted Medication Safety Best Practices (2026-2027)

- Best Practice 1: dispense vincristine and other vinca alkaloids (vinblastine, vinorelbine, liposomal vincristine) in a minibag of a compatible solution, not a syringe. ISMP gives example volumes too large for intrathecal use (25 mL pediatric, 50 mL adult) and reports no intrathecal vinca errors when minibags were used.
- Best Practice 2: oral methotrexate defaults to weekly dosing; a hard stop requires an oncologic indication for daily orders; discharge education with teach-back.
- Best Practice 3: weigh each patient on admission and at each appropriate encounter; avoid stated, estimated or historical weights; metric units only; alerts for a large change from the last weight; defined weight categories (wet, dry, dosing).

## 8. USP <800> hazardous drug handling

- Official December 1, 2019; compendially applicable, to the extent <795> and <797> apply, from November 1, 2023. No revision was found in the USP pages read on 2026-10-30 or in the August 2026 USP presentation to NABP; FAQs updated January 21, 2026. Enforcement rests with state boards and other regulators `[VERIFY]` for the facility's jurisdiction.
- Scope: drugs on the current NIOSH List of Hazardous Drugs in Healthcare Settings (2024 list, published December 2024). For <800>, "antineoplastic" means drugs in NIOSH Table 1 (revision bulletin official July 1, 2020).
- Full containment applies to hazardous drug active ingredients and to NIOSH Table 1 antineoplastics that require manipulation. Final dosage forms needing only counting or repackaging may follow an assessment of risk, which must list each drug and dosage form individually.
- Sterile hazardous drug compounding: an externally vented C-PEC with ISO Class 5 air, such as a Class II BSC or a CACI. CSTDs are recommended for compounding and required for administering NIOSH Table 1 antineoplastics when the dosage form allows.
- Program-level compliance (facility design, designated person, medical surveillance) is outside this skill; route it.

## 9. Who may do what

| Act | Who, per the standards | Check in the record |
|---|---|---|
| Sign an antineoplastic order | Licensed practitioner the organization deems qualified (3.3) | Prescriber's privileges or role |
| Authorize an exception order | Supporting reference and/or a second licensed practitioner (3.4.1) | Name, time, reference, rationale |
| Hold or stop by verbal order | The only verbal order allowed (3.5.1) | Any other verbal order is a discrepancy |
| Perform verifications | Personnel approved to prepare or administer; licensed clinicians for the second to fourth (3.12.2) | Role of each signer |
| Administer parenteral therapy | Licensed clinician approved by the organization (3.12.4) | Administering clinician's role |
