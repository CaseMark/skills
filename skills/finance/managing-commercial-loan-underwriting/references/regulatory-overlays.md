## 1. Appraisals and evaluations

Rules: 12 CFR 34.43 (OCC), 12 CFR 323.3 (FDIC), 12 CFR 225.63 (Federal Reserve); the three texts match on these points, with different paragraph numbers at the Federal Reserve.

- An appraisal by a state certified or licensed appraiser is required for every real estate-related financial transaction unless an exemption applies. Exemptions most used in commercial lending: a commercial real estate transaction with a transaction value of $500,000 or less (34.43(a)(13); 225.63(a)(14)); a business loan of $1 million or less that is not dependent on the sale of, or rental income from, real estate as the primary source of repayment (34.43(a)(5)); a lien taken in an abundance of caution (a)(2); and certain existing extensions of credit (a)(7).
- Transaction value for a loan is the loan amount (34.42(o)). A commercial real estate transaction is one not secured by a single 1-to-4 family residential property (34.42(e)).
- An evaluation, not an appraisal, is required when the CRE, business-loan, existing-credit or residential exemptions apply (34.43(b)). The other exemptions require neither.
- A state certified appraiser is required for federally related CRE transactions over $500,000 and for any transaction of $1,000,000 or more (34.43(d)).
- Minimum standards: USPAP, written, sufficient to support the decision, subject to appropriate review, market value definition, deductions and discounts for proposed construction, partially leased buildings and non-market leases (34.44).
- Independence: a fee appraiser must be engaged directly by the institution or its agent; an appraisal engaged by another financial services institution may be accepted if the appraiser has no interest and the institution finds it conforms (34.45(b)). The 2010 Interagency Appraisal and Evaluation Guidelines state that use of a borrower-ordered or borrower-provided appraisal violates the agencies' appraisal regulations.
- Review: as part of the credit approval process and before the final credit decision, the institution reviews the appraisal or evaluation for compliance and support; if deficiencies cannot be resolved, it must obtain a compliant one before the decision (Guidelines, Section XV).
- Age: no fixed regulatory age. The institution sets criteria for whether an existing appraisal remains valid; passage of time, market volatility, financing changes and changes in cap rates or lease terms require a new one (Guidelines, Section XIV).
- No change to these thresholds was located for 2025 or 2026; the FDIC's December 4, 2025 threshold-indexing rule did not amend Part 323.
- Nonbank lenders are not subject to these regulations; their policy, investor or agency program rules govern, and the memo says which.

## 2. Supervisory loan-to-value limits

Interagency Guidelines for Real Estate Lending Policies (OCC: Appendix A to 12 CFR 34 Subpart D). Internal limits should not exceed: raw land 65 percent; land development 75 percent; commercial, multifamily and other nonresidential construction 80 percent; 1-to-4 family construction 85 percent; improved property 85 percent. "Value" for a purchase is the lesser of acquisition cost and the appraised or evaluated value. Loans above the supervisory limits should not exceed 100 percent of total capital in aggregate, and within that, commercial, agricultural, multifamily and other non-1-to-4 family loans should not exceed 30 percent of total capital. These are supervisory guidelines for the institution's policy, not borrower-level prohibitions; the institution's own lower limit governs the policy test.

## 3. CRE concentrations and related CRE statements

- 2006 Interagency Guidance on Concentrations in Commercial Real Estate Lending, Sound Risk Management Practices (71 FR 74580, Dec. 12, 2006; OCC Bulletin 2006-46). It sets no lending limit. Supervisory criteria that may identify an institution for further analysis: (1) construction, land development and other land loans at 100 percent or more of total risk-based capital; or (2) total CRE loans as defined in the guidance at 300 percent or more of total risk-based capital and the CRE portfolio up 50 percent or more over the prior 36 months. CRE for the guidance excludes loans secured by owner-occupied properties and focuses on loans repaid primarily from the real estate's cash flow.
- Institutions near or over the criteria are expected to have heightened risk management: board and management oversight, portfolio management, MIS, market analysis, portfolio stress testing, credit underwriting standards and credit risk review. A memo for a bank near the criteria shows the pro forma ratios after the loan.
- Status: OCC Bulletin 2006-46 remains listed on the OCC's concentrations page and is not in the OCC's 2025 rescission appendix (Bulletin 2025-46).
- 2015 Interagency Statement on Prudent Risk Management for CRE Lending (OCC 2015-51) and OCC Bulletin 2024-29 on refinance risk remain listed on the OCC CRE page.
- 2023 Policy Statement on Prudent Commercial Real Estate Loan Accommodations and Workouts (88 FR 43115, July 6, 2023; OCC Bulletin 2023-23). Supersedes the 2009 workout statement. For renewals, modifications and workouts: analyze global debt service coverage with realistic projections, obtain current collateral valuations, and note that modified loans to borrowers able to repay on reasonable terms are not adversely classified solely because collateral value fell below the loan balance. Workout documents go to the legal hand-offs.

## 4. Regulation O: insider lending

Applies to member banks (12 CFR 215), national banks and federal savings associations (12 CFR 31.2) and FDIC-supervised institutions (12 CFR 337.3, with stated exceptions to 215.5). Not to nonbank lenders.

- Insider: an executive officer, director or principal shareholder (more than 10 percent of a class of voting securities) of the bank or its affiliates, and any related interest: a company controlled by that person (215.2(h), (m), (n)). Control: 25 percent or more of a class of voting securities, control of the election of a majority of directors, or a controlling influence; presumed at more than 10 percent if the person is an executive officer or director of the company or no one holds more (215.2(c)).
- Screen the borrower, every owner of 10 percent or more, every guarantor and every entity under common control against the insider list. A screen not run is an unverified external lookup, not a negative result.
- Terms: substantially the same terms (including rate and collateral) and underwriting not less stringent than comparable transactions with non-insiders, and no more than normal risk of repayment (215.4(a)(1)). The exception is only a benefit or compensation program widely available to employees that does not prefer insiders (215.4(a)(2)).
- Prior approval: an extension that, aggregated with all other credit to the insider and related interests, exceeds the higher of $25,000 or 5 percent of unimpaired capital and unimpaired surplus needs advance approval by a majority of the entire board, with the interested party abstaining (215.4(b)(1)); in any event, approval is required above $500,000 aggregate (215.4(b)(2)). Discussion or influence by the interested party counts as participation (215.4(b)(4)). A line approved within 14 months covers later draws (215.4(b)(3)).
- Limits: individual limit equals the 12 U.S.C. 84 single-borrower limit (15 percent unsecured plus 10 percent readily marketable collateral) (215.4(c), 215.2(i)); aggregate to all insiders not above unimpaired capital and surplus, with a board-resolution exception up to two times for banks with deposits under $100 million (215.4(d)).
- Executive officers of the bank: additional limits on purpose and amount (215.5); "other purpose" credit capped at the higher of 2.5 percent of capital or $25,000, never above $100,000.
- Pending change: the Federal Reserve proposed a Regulation O rewrite (91 FR 49526, Aug. 4, 2026; comment period extended to November 4, 2026) that would raise the prior-approval threshold from $500,000 to $2 million and index thresholds; the FDIC proposed parallel changes (Aug. 6, 2026). Not in effect on October 30, 2026; apply current text and re-check before relying on any change.

## 5. Regulation B: notices of action taken

12 CFR 1002.9 applies to all creditors, bank and nonbank.

- Business applicant with gross revenues of $1 million or less in its preceding fiscal year: notify within 30 days after receiving a completed application (approval, counteroffer or adverse action); 30 days after adverse action on an incomplete application unless a notice of incompleteness is sent; 30 days after adverse action on an existing account; 90 days after a counteroffer the applicant does not accept or use (1002.9(a)(1), (a)(3)(i)). The statement of action may be oral or written; the right-to-reasons disclosure may be given at application.
- Business applicant over $1 million (and trade credit, factoring-related credit): notify within a reasonable time, orally or in writing; provide written reasons and the ECOA notice if the applicant asks in writing within 60 days (1002.9(a)(3)(ii)).
- Content: action taken, creditor name and address, the ECOA notice in 1002.9(b)(1), the federal agency from Appendix A to Part 1002 (for a bank: the CFPB if total assets exceed $10 billion; otherwise the OCC, the Federal Reserve or the FDIC by charter), and either specific reasons or the right to them within 30 days if requested within 60 days.
- Reasons must be specific and give the principal reasons. "Internal standards or policies" or failing a scoring threshold are insufficient (1002.9(b)(2)). "Incomplete application" may be the reason only when the missing information prevents a decision (comment 9(a)(1)-3, -4).
- An application is complete once the creditor has all the information it normally considers (comment 9(a)(1)-1). A creditor that received 150 or fewer applications in the preceding calendar year may notify orally (1002.9(d)). Days are counted as stated; 1002.9 contains no weekend or business-day adjustment.

## 6. Regulation B: spouse and guarantor signatures

- A creditor may not require the signature of an applicant's spouse or other person, other than a joint applicant, if the applicant qualifies alone (1002.7(d)(1)). The exceptions are limited: signatures needed under state law to reach jointly owned property relied on for unsecured credit, community property cases, and instruments needed to make offered collateral available (1002.7(d)(2)-(4)).
- If the creditor's standards require an additional party, it may request a guarantor or cosigner; the spouse may serve, but the creditor may not require that the spouse be the additional party (1002.7(d)(5)).
- The same rules apply to guarantors: a creditor may require personal guarantees of partners, officers, directors or shareholders of a closely held corporation based on their relationship to the business, but may not automatically require their spouses to sign (comment 7(d)(6)-1, -2).
- Joint intent must be shown at application, not by signatures on a note or a joint financial statement (comment 7(d)(1)-3).

## 7. Section 1071 small business lending data

12 CFR 1002 Subpart B, as revised by the final rule at 91 FR 23530 (May 1, 2026; effective June 30, 2026).
- Covered financial institution: a financial institution other than a Farm Credit System lender that originated at least 1,000 covered credit transactions for small businesses in each of the two preceding calendar years (1002.105(b)).
- Small business: gross annual revenue of $1 million or less for the preceding fiscal year (1002.106(b)), indexed every 5 years after January 1, 2030. Merchant cash advances, agricultural lending and small-dollar loans are excluded from covered credit transactions.
- Compliance date: January 1, 2028 for an institution with at least 1,000 originations in each of 2026 and 2027 (or, at its option, 2025 and 2026); later entrants no earlier than January 1, 2029 (1002.114(b), (c)(3)). The preamble states a grace period running January 1 through December 31, 2028.
- Early collection: an institution that reasonably anticipates coverage may, but need not, collect minority-owned and women-owned status and principal owners' ethnicity, race and sex beginning 12 months before its compliance date, only in accordance with 1002.107(a)(18)-(19), 1002.108 and 1002.111(b)-(c) (1002.114(c)(1)). Other voluntary collection is governed by 1002.5(a)(4)(vii)-(x) and also requires the subpart's protections; outside those paragraphs 1002.5(b) bars inquiring about race, national origin or sex.
- Firewall (1002.108, retained unchanged by the 2026 rule): employees and officers involved in determining a covered application may not access the applicant's responses on minority-owned and women-owned status and principal owners' demographics, unless the institution determines that an employee should have access to perform assigned duties and gives each such applicant the 1002.108(d) notice when making the inquiries.
- The tiered compliance dates set by the 2023 rule and moved by the 2024 and 2025 extensions were replaced by the single January 1, 2028 date in 1002.114(b). The CFPB page still notes court stays of the 2023 rule for certain plaintiffs; how those stays interact with the revised rule is [VERIFY].

## 8. Leveraged lending guidance status

The OCC and FDIC withdrew from the 2013 Interagency Guidance on Leveraged Lending and the 2014 FAQs on December 5, 2025 (OCC news release 2025-119, OCC Bulletin 2025-44); general safe-and-sound lending principles apply. A 6.0x leverage figure is not a current OCC or FDIC supervisory threshold. The Federal Reserve's SR 13-3 page showed no rescission when read; its status for state member banks is [VERIFY]. The withdrawal was confirmed against the OCC release by the sibling skill building-lbo-models; the OCC rescission appendix read for this skill (Bulletin 2025-46) also points to Bulletin 2025-44. Leverage limits in a memo come from the institution's own policy.

## 11. Defaults and where a rule overrides them

| Default the skill uses | Rule that overrides it, and when |
|---|---|
| Value for LTV is the appraised value | For a purchase, value is the lesser of acquisition cost and appraised value (supervisory LTV appendix) |
| An appraisal is required for real estate collateral | Exemptions in 34.43(a) (for example CRE at $500,000 or less) replace it with an evaluation; a nonbank lender follows its own policy |
| Board prior approval for insiders above the higher of $25,000 or 5 percent of capital | Approval is also required above $500,000 aggregate, whatever 5 percent of capital is (215.4(b)(2)) |
| Notice of action within 30 days of a completed application | For businesses over $1 million in revenue, a reasonable time applies (1002.9(a)(3)(ii)); small-volume creditors may notify orally (1002.9(d)) |
| A creditor may not require a spouse's signature | State-law exceptions in 1002.7(d)(2)-(4) for jointly owned property, community property and offered collateral |
| Institution policy DSCR definition governs | Where the policy is silent, the CADS definition in `references/underwriting-rules.md` Section 2 applies and is disclosed as a policy election |
