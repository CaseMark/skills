## 1. Classify the consideration

Put every element of consideration in one row before valuing it: cash at closing, escrow or holdback, seller note, deferred fixed payment, earnout or other contingent payment, rollover equity, assumed liabilities, purchase price adjustments, and payments to seller-employees. For each row record the payer, payee, timing, contingency, and whether it is tied to continued employment.

## 2. Deferred and contingent consideration

- **Installment method is the default.** A disposition with at least one payment received after the close of the year of sale is an installment sale (IRC 453(b)(1)), reported on the installment method unless the seller elects out on or before the due date (with extensions) of the return for the year of sale (453(d)). Income each year is payments received times gross profit over total contract price (453(c)).
- **Not available or limited:** dealer dispositions and inventory (453(b)(2)); stock or securities traded on an established securities market (453(k)(2)); purchaser debt payable on demand or readily tradable is treated as payment (453(f)(4)); recapture income under 1245 or 1250 is recognized in the year of sale regardless (453(i)).
- **Contingent payment sales** (aggregate price not determinable by the end of the year of sale) are reported on the installment method unless the seller elects out (Treas. Reg. 15a.453-1(c)(1)):
  - Stated maximum selling price: assume every contingency resolves to the maximum at the earliest date; basis is recovered against that maximum (15a.453-1(c)(2)(i)). Reduce the maximum later if the contingency fails.
  - No maximum but a fixed period: basis recovered in equal annual increments over the period (15a.453-1(c)(3)).
  - Neither: basis recovered over 15 years, and the arrangement is scrutinized as possibly rent or royalty (15a.453-1(c)(4)).
  - Election out: the contingent obligation is included at fair market value, which may not be less than the fair market value of the property sold less the other consideration; "open transaction" treatment is limited to rare and extraordinary cases (15a.453-1(d)(2)(iii)).
- **Section 453A:** for installment obligations from a sale of property with a sales price over $150,000, interest is charged on the deferred tax if the face amount of such obligations arising in the year and outstanding at year end exceeds $5,000,000 (IRC 453A(b)(1), (b)(2), (c)); pledging an obligation as security for debt is treated as a payment (453A(d)). How the face amount of a contingent obligation is measured for the $5,000,000 test is [VERIFY].
- **Imputed interest:** part of each deferred payment, including contingent payments, may be interest under section 483 or 1274 when the contract lacks adequate stated interest (IRC 483(a)). Compute it, or state that it was excluded, before reporting gain.
- **Escrows:** whether the seller is treated as owning escrowed consideration is fact- and agreement-specific [VERIFY with the tax adviser].

## 3. Earnouts: purchase price or compensation

- Classification follows the substance of the arrangement, not its label. Indicators that point to compensation: payments forfeited if employment ends; required service period equal to or longer than the earnout period; payments to seller-employees out of proportion to their ownership; above-market or missing salary. GAAP applies the same indicators and treats a contingent payment automatically forfeited on termination of employment as compensation for post-combination services (ASC 805-10-55-25(a), as excerpted by PwC); tax characterization is facts and circumstances and is not bound by the GAAP answer [VERIFY case authority with the tax adviser].
- Consequences to compute both ways when the facts are mixed:
  - Purchase price: seller capital gain under the installment rules above; buyer adds the payment to stock basis (stock deal) or allocates it under 1060 (asset deal, supplemental Form 8594).
  - Compensation: ordinary income to the recipient when paid, wage withholding and payroll taxes, a deduction for the payer when paid, and possible parachute payment status (Section 6).
- Never report a forfeitable earnout as capital gain without stating the compensation risk and its dollar effect.

## 4. Section 1060 residual method and Form 8594

- Applies to an applicable asset acquisition: a transfer of a group of assets constituting a trade or business where the buyer's basis is determined wholly by the consideration (IRC 1060(c); Treas. Reg. 1.1060-1(b)). Buyer and seller each allocate under the residual method of 1.338-6 and 1.338-7 (1.1060-1(a)(1), (c)).
- Classes, in order (1.338-6(b)): I cash and general deposit accounts; II actively traded personal property, certificates of deposit, foreign currency; III mark-to-market assets and debt instruments including accounts receivable; IV inventory and property held for sale to customers; V all other assets (equipment, real property); VI section 197 intangibles other than goodwill and going concern value; VII goodwill and going concern value.
- Consideration, reduced by Class I, is allocated to Class II assets in proportion to fair market value, then III, IV, V and VI, each capped at fair market value; the residual goes to Class VII (1.338-6(b)(2)(i), (c)(1)). An asset in more than one class goes in the lowest-numbered class.
- A written agreement on the allocation or on asset values binds both parties (IRC 1060(a); 1.1060-1(c)(4)), and a party can escape it only under the Danielson standard; the IRS may still challenge it.
- Seller and buyer each file Form 8594 with the return for the year including the sale date, and a supplemental Form 8594 for the year an increase or decrease in consideration is taken into account (1.1060-1(e)(1)(ii); Form 8594 instructions). Form 8594 is not required for the transfer of a partnership interest itself, but is required where a purchase of partnership interests is treated as a purchase of the partnership's assets (Form 8594 instructions).
- Inconsistent filings by buyer and seller are a known examination trigger; resolve them before filing, by a binding schedule in the agreement.
- Show the consequence of the allocation to each side: buyer bonus depreciation on Class V qualified property and 15-year amortization of Classes VI and VII (structure file, Section 8); seller ordinary recapture under 1245 and 1250 on Class V and the character of the rest.

## 5. Tax attributes: section 382 and 382(h)

- After an ownership change (more than 50 percentage points by 5-percent shareholders within the testing period, IRC 382(g)), the annual limit is the value of the old loss corporation's stock immediately before the change times the long-term tax-exempt rate (382(b)(1), (e)(1)). Start from the price paid for the loss corporation's stock, never the sponsor's equity check or enterprise value.
- **Rate:** the long-term tax-exempt rate is the highest of the adjusted federal long-term rates in effect for any month in the 3-calendar-month period ending with the calendar month of the change date (382(f)(1)). Show all three months' published rates (monthly revenue ruling) and pick the highest; never use the change month's rate alone.
- **Value adjustments:** reduce value for a redemption or other corporate contraction in connection with the change (382(e)(2)); disregard a capital contribution received as part of a plan a principal purpose of which is to avoid or increase the limit, and treat any contribution in the 2 years ending on the change date as part of such a plan except as regulations provide (382(l)(1)); if at least one third of the old loss corporation's total assets are nonbusiness (investment) assets, reduce value by their fair market value less their share of debt, looking through 50-percent subsidiaries (382(l)(4)).
- The limit is zero if the new loss corporation does not continue the old loss corporation's business enterprise for two years (382(c)(1)), subject to built-in gain increases (382(c)(2)).
- **Built-in gains and losses:** if the old loss corporation has a net unrealized built-in gain (NUBIG), the limit increases by recognized built-in gains during the 5-year recognition period, up to the NUBIG; a net unrealized built-in loss (NUBIL) makes recognized built-in losses subject to the limit (382(h)(1), (7)). NUBIG or NUBIL is zero unless it exceeds the lesser of 15 percent of the fair market value of the assets or $10,000,000 (382(h)(3)(B)). A 338 election made in connection with the change can increase the limit for the year of the deemed sale gain (382(h)(1)(C)).
- **Notice 2003-65:** sets out the 1374 approach and the 338 approach for identifying recognized built-in items. Proposed regulations (REG-125710-18, 2019 and 2020) that would have made the 1374 approach mandatory were withdrawn on July 2, 2025 (90 Fed. Reg. 28946); the withdrawal states that taxpayers may continue to rely on Notice 2003-65 until temporary or final regulations apply. Any later proposed regulations are [VERIFY] at the date of use.
- NOL deduction limits (80 percent of taxable income for post-2017 losses) and the LBO cash-tax mechanics are in building-lbo-models; detailed attribute modeling goes to modeling-tax-attribute-preservation.

## 6. Section 280G parachute payments

- A parachute payment is a payment in the nature of compensation to a disqualified individual (officer, shareholder or highly compensated individual who performs services) that is contingent on a change in ownership or control, where the present value of such payments equals or exceeds 3 times the individual's base amount (IRC 280G(b)(2), (c)). The excess over 1 times the base amount is nondeductible (280G(a), (b)(1)) and the recipient owes a 20 percent excise tax (IRC 4999(a)).
- Agreements entered into or amended within one year before the change are presumed contingent on it unless rebutted by clear and convincing evidence (280G(b)(2)(C)).
- Exemptions (280G(b)(5)): a corporation that was a small business corporation under 1361(b) (disregarding the nonresident alien rule) immediately before the change; or a corporation with no readily tradable stock where the payment is approved by more than 75 percent of the voting power of all outstanding stock entitled to vote, immediately before the change, after adequate disclosure of all material facts to all shareholders entitled to vote.
- The vote must determine the individual's right to receive or keep the payment, so the individual must waive the payment if the vote fails; stock held by disqualified individuals who would receive the payments is not counted as outstanding; an entity shareholder whose assets are substantially (one third or more) target stock votes by a separate vote of its owners unless its stake is 1 percent or less (Treas. Reg. 1.280G-1, Q&A-7).
- Compute the 3x safe harbor amount for each disqualified individual; detailed 280G and 409A work goes to analyzing-compensation-tax.

## 7. Tax representations, tax indemnity and straddle periods

The agreement skills draft the clauses; this section supplies the analysis they need.
- **Whose taxes:** a member of a consolidated group is severally liable for the group's entire federal income tax for every consolidated return year it was a member (Treas. Reg. 1.1502-6(a)). A definition of indemnified taxes limited to "Taxes of the Company" for its own pre-closing periods misses that liability, similar state combined-return liability, transferee liability and taxes of another person by contract. Name each.
- **Consolidated target leaving the group:** its tax year ends at the end of the closing day and pre-closing federal income goes on the seller group's consolidated return (1.1502-76(b)(1)(ii)(A)); transactions on the closing day after the event that are properly allocable to that later portion move to the next day (1.1502-76(b)(1)(ii)(B), the next day rule); a ratable allocation election exists in limited cases (1.1502-76(b)(2)(ii)).
- **Straddle periods:** for any tax period that begins before and ends after the closing (most state and local returns of a separate filer, property taxes), the agreement must allocate. Common conventions: closing of the books for income, receipts, payroll and sales taxes; per diem (days in the pre-closing portion over days in the period) for property and other periodic taxes. Compute both conventions on the deal's numbers so the negotiators see the stakes. The convention is a contract term, not a tax rule; the choice is a **policy election** for the parties.
- **Unified loss rule:** when a consolidated seller sells target stock at a loss, Treas. Reg. 1.1502-36 applies immediately before the transfer: basis redetermination ((b)), then basis reduction ((c)), then reduction of the target's attributes such as NOLs and asset basis ((d)), unless the common parent elects under (d)(6) to reduce stock basis or reattribute attributes instead. The buyer inherits the reduced attributes, so the agreement should require the seller to report the computation and any election.
- **Other items to allocate:** transaction tax deductions (success-based fee safe harbor under Rev. Proc. 2011-29: 70 percent treated as not facilitative and 30 percent capitalized only if a statement attached to the original return for the year paid elects the safe harbor, identifies the transaction and states the amounts deducted and capitalized; the election is transaction-specific and irrevocable), the section 174A catch-up deduction for unamortized 2022-2024 domestic research costs, refunds and their pre-closing source, tax sharing agreements to terminate at closing, seller-side taxes from a 338(h)(10) or 336(e) election and any gross-up, and transfer taxes.
- **Survival:** indemnity survival is usually tied to the statute of limitations for assessment; state limitation periods vary [VERIFY by state].

## 9. Withholding on deal payments

- **FIRPTA:** a transferee must withhold 15 percent of the amount realized when a foreign person disposes of a U.S. real property interest, which includes stock of a U.S. real property holding corporation (IRC 1445(a)). No withholding is required if the transferor furnishes a nonforeign affidavit under penalty of perjury with its TIN (1445(b)(2)), or if a nonpublicly traded domestic corporation furnishes an affidavit that it is not and has not been a U.S. real property holding corporation during the relevant period (1445(b)(3)). A transferee that obtains a certification of non-foreign status keeps it for five years (Treas. Reg. 1.1445-2(b)).
- **Partnership interests:** a transferee withholds 10 percent of the amount realized when gain on a disposition of a partnership interest would be effectively connected income under 864(c)(8), unless a nonforeign affidavit is furnished (IRC 1446(f)(1), (2)).
- **Backup withholding:** a payor of a reportable payment, including payments reportable on broker returns under section 6045, withholds at the fourth lowest section 1(c) rate when the payee has not furnished a TIN in the required manner or the IRS has notified the payor that the TIN is incorrect (IRC 3406(a)(1), (b)(3)) [VERIFY the current percentage and whether the paying agent files a 6045 return on these proceeds].
- **Compensatory payments:** option cash-outs, transaction bonuses and compensatory earnouts are wages subject to income and payroll tax withholding; route them to analyzing-compensation-tax and say so in the hand-off.
- Record for each payee: U.S. or foreign status, form on file, withholding regime, amount withheld and who remits it. The agreement skill drafts the withholding clause and the closing deliverables (FIRPTA affidavit, W-9 or W-8).
