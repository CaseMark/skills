## 1. Consent thresholds for waivers and amendments

| Change | Usual vote (confirm in the amendments section) | Filed example |
|---|---|---|
| Waive a financial covenant breach; reset covenant levels; amend financial definitions | Required Lenders | Agreement W 9.08(b): Required Lenders; an amendment to financial definitions is not a reduction in the interest rate even if pricing falls as a result |
| Waive or amend a springing revolver-only covenant | Often the revolving class (Required Revolving Facility Lenders) alone; read the clause | Agreement W defines Required Revolving Facility Lenders |
| Reduce principal, rate or fees; extend final maturity, installment or interest dates | Each Lender directly adversely affected | Agreement W 9.08(b)(i), (iii) |
| Increase or extend a Lender's commitment | That Lender | Agreement W 9.08(b)(ii); waiving covenants or defaults is not an increase |
| Change pro rata sharing or the payment waterfall | Each Lender adversely affected | Agreement W 9.08(b)(iv) |
| Change voting percentages or the amendments section | Each Lender adversely affected | Agreement W 9.08(b)(v) |
| Release all or substantially all collateral or guarantees | Each Lender | Agreement W 9.08(b)(vi)(A) |

Counting rules to apply every time:
- Use the agreement's exposure measure on the date of determination (Agreement W: outstanding Term Loans plus Revolving Facility Commitments, or exposure if commitments have terminated).
- Disregard Defaulting Lenders in both numerator and denominator (Agreement W and Agreement V definitions of Required Lenders).
- Apply the threshold as written: "more than 50%" is not met at exactly 50 percent.
- Apply any minimum-number rule (Agreement W: at least two unaffiliated Lenders, with a Lender, its Affiliates and Approved Funds counted as one).
- Count affiliated lenders and debt buybacks under any voting cap or exclusion in the assignment section.
- A waiver is effective only in the specific instance and for the stated purpose (Agreement W 9.08(a)); funding a loan is not a waiver.
- Record the date the threshold was first met; that date drives the status label and the accounting analysis.

Waiver and amendment fees, rate step-ups or added collateral modify the debt; the accounting (modification or extinguishment under ASC 470-50, troubled debt restructuring under ASC 470-60) goes to the accountant. Deloitte's Roadmap warns that a waiver fee cannot be assumed to pass the 10 percent cash flow test without the calculation.

## 2. Liability management transactions and pro rata sharing

- Pro rata sharing is usually a "sacred right": changing it needs each affected Lender (Agreement W 9.08(b)(iv)). Exceptions (Dutch auctions, open market purchases, purchases by assignment) are where non-pro-rata deals are built.
- In re Serta Simmons Bedding, L.L.C., Nos. 23-20181 et al. (5th Cir. Dec. 31, 2024, revised Feb. 14, 2025): applying New York law, the court held that an "open market purchase" under the 2016 agreement means a purchase on the secondary market for syndicated loans; the 2020 uptier, negotiated privately with a majority group, was not one. It reversed, remanded the excluded lenders' breach claims, and excised the plan indemnity for the uptier. The court said each contract is read on its own but such exceptions "will often not justify an uptier". Certiorari was denied November 10, 2025 (No. 24-1322).
- On remand the bankruptcy court (S.D. Tex., Adv. No. 23-09001, memorandum opinion entered July 7, 2026) held the participating lenders breached the sharing clause and awarded $261.13 million plus 9.00 percent prejudgment interest, allocated severally.
- Ocean Trails CLO VII v MLN Topco Ltd., 2024 NY Slip Op 06660 (1st Dept Dec. 31, 2024) (Mitel): a different clause let the borrower "purchase by way of assignment ... at any time"; the court held the uptier was a permitted purchase and dismissed the claims. The two results turn on different words.
- What this skill does: flag any proposed transaction that pays or prefers some lenders in a class without ratable treatment, quote the sharing clause and every exception, note whether the agreement has blocker provisions, state the consent class, and hand the interpretation to counsel. It does not opine on whether a transaction is permitted.

## 3. US GAAP classification after a covenant violation (ASC 470-10)

Codification text is quoted from Big 4 publications; see the source register.

- ASC 470-10-45-11: long-term obligations callable because of a violation at the balance sheet date, or that will become callable if a violation is not cured within a grace period, are current unless (a) the creditor has waived, or subsequently lost, the right to demand repayment for more than one year (or the operating cycle) from the balance sheet date, for example because the debtor cured after the balance sheet date and the debt is not callable when the financial statements are issued or available to be issued; or (b) for debt with a grace period, it is probable the violation will be cured within that period.
- ASC 470-10-45-1: when a lender waives its call right for more than one year but keeps future covenant tests, the debt is noncurrent unless both (a) a violation that gives a call right occurred at the balance sheet date, or would have occurred absent a loan modification, and (b) it is probable the borrower will not be able to comply at measurement dates within the next 12 months.
- ASC 470-10-55-4 to 55-6: noncurrent in cases (a) to (c) (covenants apply only after the balance sheet date, or the borrower complies at the balance sheet date, with probable future failure), with disclosure of the adverse consequences; current in cases (d) and (e) (a pre-balance-sheet modification that avoided a violation, or a waiver obtained after the balance sheet date and before issuance, with the same or a more restrictive covenant probably failing within three months), unless the refinancing criteria in 45-13 to 45-20 are met.
- Deloitte Roadmap (August 2026) sections 13.5.2.2 to 13.5.3.5: a credit-related violation after the balance sheet date but before issuance is treated like one at the balance sheet date; a waiver must be binding and not revocable at the lender's discretion; a lender's statement that it does not intend to call the debt is not a waiver; the likelihood of a future waiver is not considered; the grace-period exception requires cure to be probable, while 45-1 requires only that failure not be probable.
- Unamortized discount and issuance costs move with the carrying amount when the debt becomes current; the amortization period changes only if the facts (for example a stated intent to call) require it (Deloitte 13.5.1.1).
- A cure right exercised before issuance can remove the violation; an equity cure is a contract term, so confirm with the accountant that the deemed cure means the lender lost its call right before issuance.

## 4. IFRS classification (IAS 1) for comparison

IAS 1 as amended in 2020 and 2022 (Non-current Liabilities with Covenants; EU endorsement Regulation 2023/2822, for financial years starting on or after January 1, 2024):
- Paragraph 72B: covenants due on or before the reporting date affect the right to defer settlement even if compliance is assessed later; covenants due only after the reporting date do not.
- Paragraph 74: a breach on or before the reporting date that makes the liability payable on demand makes it current, even if the lender agrees after the reporting date and before authorization not to demand payment.
- Paragraph 75: noncurrent only if the lender agreed by the reporting date to a grace period ending at least 12 months after it.
- Paragraph 76ZA: disclose covenant information and the risk of repayment within 12 months.
So a post-period waiver that helps under ASC 470-10-45-11(a) does not help under IFRS. IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 for periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027; where these paragraphs sit after that date is [VERIFY].

## 5. Disclosure and standard-setting status as of October 30, 2026

- ASC 470-10-50-2: disclose the circumstances when debt in violation is classified long-term because cure within a grace period is probable.
- Regulation S-X Rule 4-08(c) (17 CFR 210.4-08(c)), for SEC registrants: state the facts and amounts of any default or covenant breach existing at the latest balance sheet date and not cured; if acceleration has been waived for a stated period beyond that date, state the amount of the obligation and the period of the waiver. AICPA Technical Q&A 3200.17 suggests similar disclosure for nonpublic entities.
- FASB project "Subjective Acceleration Clauses and Debt Default Disclosures": added May 27, 2026; tentative decisions to remove the probability assessment in 470-10-45-2 (a subjective acceleration clause would affect classification only when triggered by a lender demand), not to address grace periods, and to require event-of-default disclosures like S-X 4-08(c) for all entities, applied prospectively. No exposure draft had been issued as of October 30, 2026. It does not change 45-11 or 45-1 today.
- FASB project "Accounting for Changes in Debt": added August 26, 2026; tentative decisions include extinguishment accounting for modifications and exchanges that change cash flows and eliminating borrower TDR guidance. Initial deliberations continue; current ASC 470-50 and 470-60 still apply.
- FASB removed its 2017/2019 project to simplify debt classification from its agenda in April 2021, so that project produced no ASU. The August 2026 edition of Deloitte's Debt Roadmap lists no substantive change to its Chapter 13 classification guidance, and no ASU amending 470-10-45-11 or 45-1 was found.
