## 1. Examination: IDRs, concessions and agreement forms

- Answer each IDR item by item: produce what is responsive, state objections to scope in the cover letter, and log every document produced (Bates range, date, IDR item). Keep a privilege log for anything withheld (Section 8).
- A fact is conceded only on a documented decision: the client's written authorization, the evidence considered, and the effect on tax, penalties and other years. A concession made orally in a closing conference is still a concession; confirm or retract it in writing the same week.
- Signing an agreement form (Form 4549 or Form 870) waives the restrictions on assessment under 6213(d) for the agreed amounts. Inference from 6213(a) and (d), confirm before advising: once those amounts are assessed there is no deficiency left to petition, so the remaining route for them is pay, claim and sue. Partial agreement is possible; list exactly which adjustments are agreed.
- A closing agreement under 7121 is final and conclusive except for fraud, malfeasance or misrepresentation of a material fact. Do not treat Form 870 or 870-AD as a closing agreement; IRM 25.6.22.7.1 notes Form 870 does not prevent a later determination of additional tax.
- Before any concession, check what else it moves: penalties computed on the conceded amount, the same item in open later years, state conformity reporting, and the ASC 740 reserve (hand off; Related Skills).

## 2. Protests and the Independent Office of Appeals

- Appeals resolves controversies without litigation on a basis fair and impartial to both sides (7803(e)(3)); the process is generally available to all taxpayers (7803(e)(4)). A taxpayer with a notice of deficiency whose request for Appeals referral is denied must get a written explanation and protest procedure (7803(e)(5)).
- Formal written protest (Pub. 5, Rev. 4-2021) is required when any period's proposed change in tax and penalties exceeds $25,000, and for all partnership, S corporation, employee plan and exempt organization cases. Contents: a statement that the taxpayer wants to appeal; name, address, daytime phone; each disputed issue, period and proposed change with the reasons for disagreement; facts; law; the penalties of perjury declaration (the representative's version depends on personal knowledge). A small case request (at most $25,000 for each period) can be a brief statement or Form 12203.
- Facts in a protest are signed under penalties of perjury. Every fact carries a source locator; a fact the client cannot document is stated as the client's position, not as fact.
- New information first raised at Appeals may be sent back to the examiner (IRM 8.21.3.2.1; Pub. 5). Put the evidence in the record at exam where possible.
- Appeals weighs the hazards of litigation. Present each issue with the authority on both sides and a reasoned settlement range; never present a probability that the record does not support.
- Docketed cases: after a Tax Court petition, Appeals generally gets the first opportunity to settle (Pub. 1035 says a petitioner will generally have an opportunity for pretrial settlement).

## 3. Ex parte communications (Rev. Proc. 2012-18)

- Ex parte communication: any communication, oral or written, between an Appeals employee and an employee of another IRS function without the taxpayer or representative being given an opportunity to participate (sec. 2.01(1)). Originating functions are Examination, Collection and the campus functions (sec. 2.01(2)). Effective for communications after May 15, 2012; IRM 8.1.10 says the revenue procedure controls over conflicting IRM text.
- Opportunity to participate: notice of the meeting or call and a reasonable chance to attend; for a written communication, a copy and a chance to respond (sec. 2.01(3)). A waiver can be per communication or blanket and is revocable.
- Not ex parte: database inquiries, communications among Appeals employees, communications with other government entities, and communications in which the taxpayer was offered participation (sec. 2.01(1)(a)). Appeals may get legal advice from Counsel subject to section 2.06 limits.
- Practice: ask Appeals at the opening conference whether the administrative file includes any examiner rebuttal to the protest, ask for a copy, and object in writing to any communication on the merits you were not offered. Section 2.03(10) addresses remedies; Appeals asks the taxpayer for input, and a second-level manager decides.

## 4. Accuracy-related penalties and reasonable cause

- IRC 6662(a): 20 percent of the portion of the underpayment attributable to negligence or disregard, substantial understatement, substantial valuation misstatement, and the other listed grounds (6662(b)). It does not apply to any portion on which the 6663 fraud penalty is imposed.
- Substantial understatement (6662(d)(1)): individuals, understatement exceeds the greater of 10 percent of the tax required to be shown or $5,000 (5 percent if a 199A deduction is claimed); C corporations other than S corporations and personal holding companies, the lesser of 10 percent of the tax required to be shown (or $10,000 if greater) or $10,000,000.
- Reduction of the understatement (6662(d)(2)(B)) for substantial authority, or for adequate disclosure plus reasonable basis; not available for tax shelter items (6662(d)(2)(C)).
- 40 percent: gross valuation misstatements (6662(h)) and nondisclosed noneconomic substance transactions (6662(i)).
- Reasonable cause and good faith (6664(c)(1)) is a facts-and-circumstances defense; the most important factor is the taxpayer's effort to assess the proper liability (Treas. Reg. 1.6664-4(b)(1)). It is unavailable for economic substance (6662(b)(6)) and 170(h)(7) disallowances (6664(c)(2)), and limited for charitable property valuation overstatements (6664(c)(3)).
- Reliance on an adviser (1.6664-4(c)(1)): the advice must rest on all pertinent facts and the law; reliance fails if the taxpayer did not disclose a fact it knew or should have known was relevant, or if the advice rests on unreasonable assumptions. Build the record: engagement letter, what the client gave the adviser and when, the advice itself, and the adviser's competence. A gap in what was disclosed is a weakness to state, not to paper over.

## 5. Written supervisory approval (IRC 6751(b))

- Statute: no penalty may be assessed unless the initial determination of the assessment is personally approved in writing by the immediate supervisor of the individual making it, or a designated higher official. Exceptions: 6651, 6654, 6655, 6662(b)(9) and (b)(10), and penalties automatically calculated through electronic means (6751(b)(2)).
- Regulation (Treas. Reg. 301.6751(b)-1, T.D. 10017, for penalties assessed on or after December 23, 2024): approval may be any writing showing assent. Penalties subject to pre-assessment Tax Court review (notice of deficiency, FPA): approval on or before the date the notice is mailed. Penalties not subject to that review: before assessment. Penalties first raised in the Tax Court: no later than when the Commissioner asks the court to determine them. An electronically generated penalty stops being "automatic" once the taxpayer responds and an employee considers the response.
- Case law on timing (checked 2026-10-30):

| Forum | Rule |
|---|---|
| 9th Cir. (Laidlaw's, 29 F.4th 1066 (2022)) | Before assessment, while the supervisor still has discretion to approve |
| 11th Cir. (Kroner, 48 F.4th 1272 (2022); Battat (2025), cert. denied Apr. 27, 2026) | Before assessment; the statute regulates assessment, not communications to the taxpayer |
| 5th Cir. (Swift, 144 F.4th 756 (2025), cert. denied Apr. 20, 2026) | Before assessment or, if earlier, before the supervisor loses discretion |
| 10th Cir. (Minemyer, unpublished (2023)); 2d Cir. (Chai, 851 F.3d 190 (2017)) | Listed by the government and the Tax Court with the circuits accepting pre-assessment timing; Chai ties approval to the notice of deficiency or the answer asserting the penalty [VERIFY the exact Chai rule before relying on it] |
| Tax Court, cases appealable elsewhere | Clay, 152 T.C. 223 (2019); Belair Woods, 154 T.C. 1 (2020); Frost, 154 T.C. 23 (2020): approval before the first formal communication of a definite decision to assert the penalty. Computer Sciences Corp., 165 T.C. No. 8 (2025) (4th Cir. venue) found approval timely even under the reading most favorable to the taxpayer and rejected an APA "reasoned decision" challenge |

- Workflow: get the approval form (Civil Penalty Approval Form or equivalent) and the first communication proposing the penalty; identify the individual who first proposed it and that person's immediate supervisor; record the dates of proposal, approval, notice mailing and assessment; apply the rule of the circuit where the case is appealable, and the regulation where its date applies. A late or missing approval is a complete defense to that penalty; an approval that is timely under the governing rule ends the argument.

## 6. First Time Abate and Automatic Exemption from Penalty

- First Time Abate (IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1, rev. 03-29-2023) covers only failure to file (6651(a)(1), 6698(a)(1), 6699(a)(1)), failure to pay (6651(a)(2), (a)(3)) and failure to deposit (6656). It requires the same return type filed for the three prior years with no unreversed penalties other than the estimated tax penalty, and no prior FTA or tolerance abatement in that window; business accounts have added failure-to-deposit tests.
- It does not reach the 6662 accuracy-related penalty or any other penalty. Requesting FTA for a 6662 penalty is an error; use reasonable cause, 6662(d)(2)(B) reduction, or 6751(b).
- Automatic Exemption from Penalty (IRS page, read 2026-10-30): starting summer 2026, for 2025 tax-year returns and 2026 quarterly returns onward (Forms 1040, 1065, 1120, 940, 941, 943, 944, 945, CT-1), the IRS will not assess failure-to-file, failure-to-pay or failure-to-deposit penalties for taxpayers with three years (12 quarters) of timely compliance. Earlier periods still use FTA by request.

## 7. BBA centralized partnership audits

- Scope: adjustments to partnership-related items, and related penalties, are determined, assessed and collected at the partnership level (6221(a)) for partnership years beginning after 2017, unless a valid election out was made on a timely return (100 or fewer required K-1s and only eligible partners: individuals, C corporations, foreign entities treated as C corporations, S corporations, estates of deceased partners) (6221(b)).
- Authority: the partnership representative has sole authority to act for the partnership; if none is designated the IRS may select one (6223(a)). Partners are bound by the partnership's actions and by final decisions (6223(b)). Confirm the designation (and, for an entity representative, the designated individual) before anything is signed or elected.
- Imputed underpayment (6225(b)): net the adjustments within each 702(a) category, disregard reallocation decreases, and apply the highest rate in effect for the reviewed year under section 1 or 11 (37 percent individual for years after 2017 under 1(j); 21 percent corporate under 11(b)).
- Modifications (6225(c)), each requiring partner-level substantiation and IRS approval (6225(c)(8)): amended returns or the alternative payment procedure by reviewed-year partners; tax-exempt partners (portion allocable to a partner that would not owe tax because of its 168(h)(2) status; not to the extent the income would be taxable to it, such as unrelated business taxable income); rate modification for C corporation partners and for individuals' capital gains and qualified dividends (no lower than the highest rate for that partner type and income); certain publicly traded partnership passive losses; others by guidance. Deadline and its statute-versus-regulation counting conflict: `references/clocks-and-deadlines.md` Section 8.
- Push-out (6226): within 45 days after the FPA the partnership may elect to have reviewed-year partners take the adjustments into account; partners then pay with the correction amounts in the year the statement is furnished. Compare the push-out cost (partner rates, interest computed at a higher rate under 6226(c) [VERIFY the rate before quoting it], partner-level attributes) with paying the modified imputed underpayment.
- Sequence: NOPPA, then modification submissions within the 270-day window, then FPA no earlier than 270 days after the NOPPA, then 45 days for push-out and 90 days for a petition. An imputed underpayment computed without considering modifications overstates the partnership's liability whenever tax-exempt, corporate or capital-gain partners hold a share.

## 8. Privilege: IRC 7525, Kovel and work product

- IRC 7525(a): communications between a taxpayer and a federally authorized tax practitioner get the same common-law protection as attorney communications, to the extent they would be privileged if made to an attorney, and only for tax advice within the practitioner's authority. It may be asserted only in noncriminal tax matters before the IRS and noncriminal tax proceedings in federal court brought by or against the United States. It does not apply to written communications in connection with promoting participation in a tax shelter (7525(b)).
- Not covered by 7525: criminal investigations or prosecutions; state tax proceedings (the statute names only the IRS and federal courts); return preparation. Communications about preparing a return are generally not privileged; dual-purpose communications are tested by their primary purpose in the Ninth Circuit (In re Grand Jury, 23 F.4th 1088 (9th Cir. 2021), cert. dismissed as improvidently granted, 598 U.S. 15 (2023)).
- Kovel (United States v. Kovel, 296 F.2d 918 (2d Cir. 1961)): an accountant's communications can fall within the attorney-client privilege when the accountant is retained by the lawyer to help the lawyer give legal advice. Structure: engagement by counsel, at counsel's direction, separate from any return-preparation or audit engagement, with the accountant's work product delivered to counsel. A Kovel letter does not convert return preparation or business advice into privileged work.
- Work product protects documents prepared in anticipation of litigation (Hickman v. Taylor, 329 U.S. 495 (1947)); circuits differ on the test (the Ninth Circuit describes a "because of" test). Tax accrual workpapers prepared for financial-statement purposes were held not protected (United States v. Textron, 577 F.3d 21 (1st Cir. 2009) (en banc)), and there is no accountant work-product immunity for audit tax accrual workpapers (United States v. Arthur Young & Co., 465 U.S. 805 (1984)).
- Practice: if criminal exposure is possible, communications should run through counsel from the start; 7525 will not protect them. Mark privileged material, keep it out of IDR productions, and log what is withheld.

## 10. Competent authority (MAP) and APA requests

This skill handles the procedure: when and how to request competent authority assistance (Rev. Proc. 2015-40) or an advance pricing agreement (Rev. Proc. 2015-41), how the request interacts with exam, Appeals, consents and refund claims, and the clocks in `references/clocks-and-deadlines.md` Section 9. The pricing substance (method, comparables, range, documentation) goes to managing-transfer-pricing-compliance.

- Jurisdiction: once the U.S. competent authority accepts a request, it takes exclusive jurisdiction within the IRS over the competent authority issues, and assessment and collection on them are suspended unless it instructs otherwise (2015-40, sec. 6.02). Issues it does not take stay with exam or Appeals.
- Exam agreements: a standard Form 870 does not by itself cause rejection. A closing agreement, Form 870-AD or similar agreement with exam does not cause rejection either, but the U.S. competent authority will then seek only a correlative adjustment abroad and will not change the agreed U.S. taxable income (2015-40, sec. 6.03). Before signing any exam agreement on a transfer pricing issue, decide whether MAP relief may be needed.
- Appeals: an issue under Appeals' jurisdiction gets competent authority assistance only if the request is filed within 60 days after the Appeals opening conference, the issue is severed from the protest, no Appeals ADR program was invoked, and no Form 870-AD or closing agreement covers it (sec. 6.04(3)). The simultaneous appeals procedure (SAP) lets Appeals assist the competent authority with the U.S. position (sec. 6.04(2)). After a litigated or settled final determination, MAP is available only to seek correlative relief abroad.
- Denial grounds include rejecting a request to extend the assessment period for covered years, failing to coordinate with exam, Appeals or litigation, and presenting new material information that could have been given to exam (sec. 7.02(2)); the decision is not administratively reviewable (sec. 7.03).
- Protective claim: a written claim meeting IRC 6402 (not necessarily on the prescribed form) to preserve the U.S. refund side; file it when an adjustment is likely, then give the annual notification until the MAP request is filed (sec. 11). The competent authority does not police its timeliness.
- APA: request through APMA, normally covering at least five prospective years; a year is prospective only if a complete request is filed by its applicable return date; rollback years may be added or required. Consents to extend the assessment period for each proposed APA year are required (2015-41, secs. 2.03(3), 3.03, 3.08). User fees for requests filed after January 1, 2024: $121,600 original, $65,900 renewal, $57,500 small case, $24,600 amendment (IRS user fee page).
