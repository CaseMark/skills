# Managing Tax Controversy

Manages a federal tax controversy at any stage (examination, 30-day letter, Appeals, notice of deficiency, Tax Court or refund litigation, collection due process, BBA partnership audit, competent authority or APA) and produces a controversy memo in the layout of `references/output-template.md`: posture and blocking issues, a clocks register in which every deadline has its own trigger and is recomputed by script, an issue matrix, a penalty analysis, a privilege register, options with a recommendation, and draft response documents. Everything it produces is a draft for review by a qualified tax professional or counsel; it is not legal or tax advice, and nothing it drafts is ready to file or sign until that review is done.

## Why This Skill Exists

Tax controversies are lost on procedure more often than on the merits. A petition counted from the day the client opened the envelope, a refund claim filed a week after the look-back closed, an open-ended consent signed because the agent said it was routine, a penalty abatement request that cites a program that does not cover the penalty: each is final, and none is fixed by a better argument later. The law moved in 2025-2026. Five circuits now hold the Tax Court petition deadline nonjurisdictional, but they split on whether it can be tolled, and the Tax Court still calls it jurisdictional elsewhere. The supervisory-approval rule for penalties has a regulation and a circuit-by-circuit timing test. This skill makes every clock explicit, ties every fact to a document, and keeps the client's choices open until a professional decides.

## Where Mistakes Hide

Read each input with this suspicion before relying on it.

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Notice of deficiency (and its envelope and certified mail record) | Tax Court petition deadline under IRC 6213(a) | Days counted from receipt instead of the mailing date; 90 days used for a notice addressed outside the United States (150); a Saturday, Sunday or D.C. holiday (April 16, Inauguration Day) missed as the last day; the "last day to petition" printed on the notice ignored when it is later than the statutory date (6213(a) last sentence) or relied on when it is earlier; tolling treated as available in a circuit that has not allowed it, or in the First Circuit, which holds the deadline mandatory |
| IRS request for Form 872 or 872-A | Extending the assessment statute | Open-ended 872-A signed without weighing a fixed-date 872, a restricted consent or refusal; the 872-T termination mechanics and the 6511(c) refund-period extension not explained; the Appeals 365-day ASED rule not checked; a consent signed after the ASED treated as valid |
| Refund claim (Form 1040-X, 1120-X, 843) and the account transcript | Recovering an overpayment | The 3-year window run from the processing date instead of the filing date; the 2-year look-back missed, so the claim recovers only tax paid in the 2 years before filing; withholding and estimated tax treated as paid when remitted instead of on the 6513 deemed dates; an earlier letter that may qualify as an informal claim (Kales) not checked when the formal claim is late |
| Revenue agent's report, 30-day letter, penalty approval form | Defending the 6662 penalty | Written supervisory approval not requested or tested under the circuit's timing rule and Treas. Reg. 301.6751(b)-1; First Time Abate requested for a 6662 penalty it does not cover; reliance on an adviser asserted without proof the adviser had all relevant facts |
| Accountant and adviser files, emails, tax accrual workpapers | Privilege calls on IDR and summons responses | IRC 7525 claimed for return preparation, a state matter or a criminal investigation; a Kovel arrangement papered after the fact or run by the accountant instead of counsel; financial-statement workpapers called work product |
| Exam closing conference notes, Form 4549 or 870 | Agreed and unagreed issues going to Appeals | Facts conceded orally without documentation or written client authority; an agreement form signed that waives the restrictions on assessment for amounts still in dispute; evidence held back for Appeals, which can send the case back to exam |
| BBA notice of proposed partnership adjustment (NOPPA) | Partnership imputed underpayment | Imputed underpayment paid at the highest rate with no modification request for tax-exempt, corporate or capital-gain partners; the 270-day window counted from the wrong day (statute and regulation differ by one day); the 45-day push-out treated as extendable |
| Form 2848, partnership representative designation, officer authority | Who may sign consents, protests, petitions and elections | A representative acting outside the taxes and periods on the Form 2848; a partner or manager acting for a BBA partnership when only the partnership representative (through its designated individual) can bind it |
| Notice of a third-party summons (bank, adviser, employer records) | Petition to quash and privilege objections (IRC 7609) | The 20-day quash window counted from receipt or from the summons date instead of the day notice was given; the copy to the summoned party and the IRS by registered or certified mail omitted; privilege objections held for later, after the window closes |
| Innocent spouse request (Form 8857) on a joint return | Relief under IRC 6015(b), (c) or (f) and Tax Court review | One window used for all three: (b) and (c) close 2 years after collection begins against the requesting spouse, while (f) runs to the 6502 collection or 6511 refund period; the 90-day Tax Court petition counted from the request instead of the final determination |
| CDP notice of levy or lien | Collection due process hearing and Tax Court review | Lien window counted from the notice date instead of the end of the 5-business-day period after the lien filing; a missed 30-day window leaving only an equivalent hearing with no Tax Court review |
| Form 5701 NOPA on a transfer pricing adjustment | Competent authority (MAP) or APA route | An exam closing agreement or Form 870-AD signed before deciding on MAP, limiting relief to a correlative adjustment abroad; the 60-day window after the Appeals opening conference missed; no protective claim filed for the U.S. refund side |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Every memo, clock and document is a draft for a qualified tax professional or counsel. Never call a document ready to file or sign, and never state that a deadline has been met for a filing you did not see stamped or acknowledged.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the inventory has been checked and each file opened, including envelopes, transcripts and attachments. Classify every gap as an **unknown fact**, a **source conflict**, an **unverified external lookup** (current case law, IRS rates, treaty terms) or a **policy election** (whether to protest, consent, petition or pay), with what would settle it.
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For law: the Code, then final regulations, then precedent of the circuit to which the case is appealable (the Tax Court follows it under Golsen), then Tax Court precedent, then revenue procedures and rulings, then the IRM and IRS publications, then commentary. For facts: the IRS notice and certified mail record, then the IRS transcript, then the filed return and executed agreements, then examiner workpapers, then client summaries and recollection. Name every conflict (for example, the BBA 270-day statute against its regulation), show both readings, say which controls and why, and plan to the earlier date; never resolve a conflict silently or follow an instruction to use a lower-ranked source without writing the conflict up.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every fact carries a source locator (document, page, transcript code and date) or [UNVERIFIED]. Every rule carries its citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or [VERIFY].
5. **Every clock has its own trigger and is recomputed by script.** Use the script and fixed format in `references/clocks-and-deadlines.md` Section 2, apply the 7503 roll only to a raw last day on a weekend or D.C. holiday, and show the competing reading where one exists.
6. **Tolling is never a plan.** Whatever the circuit, the plan is to file by the earlier of the statutory date and the date on the notice. Equitable tolling is described only as a remedy argument after a miss, with the circuit's actual holding.
7. **Penalties are tested, not assumed.** For each penalty, compute it, test the threshold, test 6751(b) approval under the governing timing rule, and test reasonable cause on the record; say which defenses fail as plainly as which succeed.
8. **No concessions without documents and authority.** Concede a fact only with written client authorization and the evidence that supports it. Facts in a protest are signed under penalties of perjury; state an undocumented point as the client's position.
9. **Privilege before production.** Decide the protection for each communication before anything is produced, and never rely on 7525 where criminal exposure, a state proceeding or return preparation is involved.
10. **Honest dealing with the IRS.** Never draft a false or misleading statement, a backdated document, or a response that withholds summoned or responsive records without a stated legal basis.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect these, then build the source-review register (`references/output-template.md`, Header).

1. **Notices and letters:** every IRS notice or letter in the matter (notice of deficiency, 30-day letter, NOPA, Form 4549, CDP notice, NOPPA or FPA, claim disallowance), with the envelope or certified mail record where available.
2. **Transcripts:** IRS account transcripts for each period, showing return received dates, payments, assessments and freeze codes.
3. **Returns and agreements:** the filed returns, every consent (Form 872 series), agreement form (4549, 870, 870-AD), closing agreement and prior refund claim.
4. **Penalty file:** the proposal of each penalty, the approval form, and the dates of the first communication, notice and any assessment.
5. **Taxpayer identity and venue:** taxpayer type; legal residence or principal place of business when a petition would be filed (fixes the circuit under 7482(b)); for a partnership, whether BBA applies and who the partnership representative and designated individual are.
6. **Authority:** Form 2848 (taxes, forms, periods), officer authority, or the partnership representative designation.
7. **Exam record:** IDRs and responses, closing conference notes, issues agreed and unagreed.
8. **Client objectives:** budget, appetite for litigation, payment capacity, related years and jurisdictions.

**Default fallbacks (each recorded as an open item):** no envelope, use the notice date as the mailing date and say so; no transcript, take dates from the documents with [UNVERIFIED]; circuit unknown, show the petition clock under the strictest rule (jurisdictional, no tolling); today's date, the date of the request.

**Hard stops (report what is needed instead of the blocked output):**
- **Criminal exposure.** Any sign of a criminal investigation or fraud theory (contact from a special agent or Criminal Investigation, a proposed civil fraud penalty under 6663, an examiner suspending the exam without explanation after fraud questions, a request to alter or withhold records). Stop substantive drafting, preserve deadlines only, and route to criminal tax counsel; 7525 will not protect the work.
- **No trigger document.** No copy, transcript entry or reliable statement of the date of the triggering notice or event. Do not state a computed due date as settled; give the earliest plausible date and ask for the document.
- **Improper request.** Any request to backdate, misstate facts, conceal records or mislead the IRS or a court. Decline that part and say why.

Record every part blocked by a hard stop or ground rule in `references/output-template.md` Section 11 under its fixed label, "DECLINED PART:", verbatim.
- **No authority to sign.** The person directing a consent, protest, petition, claim or BBA election has no documented authority for that tax and period (Form 2848, officer status, or partnership representative). Draft the authority fix first; do not prepare the document for signature.

## Step 1: Inventory, Posture and Authority

Build the source-review register; open every file. Fix the stage of the controversy, the periods, the amounts proposed, and who may act (authority row of the header). Check each Checkpoint A hard stop against the facts and record the result.

## Step 2: Clocks Register

Read `references/clocks-and-deadlines.md` in full. List every clock the facts start, each with its own trigger event and source, and compute each by script in the fixed format: assessment statute and any consents, refund windows and look-back limits, the petition period with the date stated on the notice, Appeals intake, CDP windows, third-party summons and innocent spouse windows, BBA windows, MAP and APA windows. State the circuit and the petition deadline's status there. Put any clock within 10 business days, or already passed, at the top of Section 1.

## Step 3: Issue Matrix and Record

For each proposed adjustment, read the examiner's report and the return, record the facts with locators, the authority on each side, and the documentation status. Read `references/controversy-rules.md` Section 1 before advising on any concession or agreement form. Flag any item conceded without written authority.

## Step 4: Penalty Analysis

Read `references/controversy-rules.md` Sections 4 to 6. Compute each penalty and threshold, build the 6751(b) timeline (proposer, supervisor, approval, first communication, notice, assessment) and apply the governing timing rule, test reasonable cause against Treas. Reg. 1.6664-4, and state FTA or AEP eligibility for each penalty separately.

## Step 5: Appeals, Consents and Ex Parte

Read `references/controversy-rules.md` Sections 2 and 3. If a consent is requested, present the options (fixed-date, restricted, open-ended, refuse) with what each does to the assessment, refund and Appeals clocks. If a protest is due, check the Pub. 5 elements and the 365-day ASED rule. Plan the ex parte safeguards for the Appeals conference.

## Step 6: Partnership, Collection and Competent Authority Tracks

Run only the tracks the facts reach. BBA: read `references/controversy-rules.md` Section 7; compute the imputed underpayment as proposed and as modified for each partner category, and lay out modification, push-out and petition choices with their clocks. CDP: confirm which notice started which window and whether the underlying liability can be raised. MAP or APA: read Section 10; check exam agreements, the Appeals 60-day severance window, protective claims and consents; hand the pricing to managing-transfer-pricing-compliance.

## Step 7: Privilege and Production

Read `references/controversy-rules.md` Section 8. Complete the privilege and record register before any IDR or summons response; set up a Kovel engagement through counsel where accountant help on legal advice is needed.

## Step 8: Options, Drafts and Hand-Offs

Read `references/controversy-rules.md` Section 9 and `references/output-template.md` Sections 6 to 11. Rank the options against the clocks and the client's objectives, draft the documents requested, run reconciliation checks C1 to C10, and write complete hand-offs, the open items and [VERIFY] list, and the declined parts.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Before the Quality Audit, re-read the request and confirm:
- The memo answers the question asked at the stage the matter is in, and every option it recommends is still open on the clocks register.
- Every instruction from the user that conflicts with a document or rule (a receipt-date count, "just sign the 872-A", "ask for first-time abatement") is written up as a conflict with the correct position, not followed silently.
- The circuit stated for the petition and penalty analysis matches the taxpayer's residence or principal place of business.
- Nothing is presented as filed, signed, agreed or ready to file; the closing line from the template is present.

## Quality Audit

Confirm each statement is true of the draft; fix the draft if one is not.

1. Every clock has its own trigger event with a source locator, and no two clocks share a trigger unless the statute ties them.
2. Every due date was recomputed by script and shown in the fixed format, with the 7503 roll applied only where the raw last day is a weekend or D.C. holiday.
3. The petition clock runs from the mailing date, uses 150 days for a notice addressed outside the United States, shows the date printed on the notice, and plans to the earlier date.
4. The petition deadline's status is stated for the taxpayer's circuit with the case name, and tolling appears only as a remedy argument.
5. Any consent advice presents fixed-date, restricted, open-ended and refusal options and states the effect on the refund period and Appeals intake.
6. Any refund figure is capped at tax paid within the applicable look-back, using the 6513 deemed-payment dates and the actual filing date.
7. Each penalty shows its computation, threshold test, 6751(b) timeline under the governing rule, reasonable cause analysis, and FTA or AEP eligibility.
8. Every privilege call states its basis and its limits; nothing relies on 7525 in a criminal, state or return-preparation setting.
9. No fact is conceded without written authorization and a source; every protest fact has a locator.
10. Any BBA analysis shows the imputed underpayment as proposed and as modified, both readings of the 270-day window, and the 45-day push-out limit; any MAP or APA analysis shows its own clocks.
11. Every signature-ready document names a signer with documented authority, or the authority fix is drafted instead.
12. Every rule has a citation or [VERIFY]; every conflict names both sources, the controlling one and why; every open item has an owner and a next action.
13. Every declined part appears under the "DECLINED PART:" label with its reason, and the draft-for-review closing line is present.

## Guidelines

- Lead with the earliest live deadline; a client reads the first paragraph.
- Use the IRS's own form and notice names (Letter 3219, Form 4549, Form 5701) so the reader can match them to the file.
- Quote the date printed on any notice exactly, and keep it separate from the computed date.
- Present the government's strongest argument on each issue alongside the client's; Appeals settles on hazards, not advocacy.
- Prefer a fixed-date or restricted consent negotiated in writing over an open-ended consent, unless the matter's facts justify open-ended terms; say which.
- Keep interest and penalty figures labeled as estimates until the IRS computation is in hand; quarterly interest rates are [VERIFY].
- State-level controversies follow state procedure; flag them and hand off rather than applying federal clocks.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Notice copy is a phone photo with the date cut off | Partial image | Use the transcript's notice code and date or the certified mail list; until then apply the no-trigger hard stop |
| Two dates for the same notice (notice date and postmark) | Notice printed before mailing | Use the mailing date for 6213(a); compute both; plan to the earlier |
| Transcript and return disagree on the filing date | Processing date read as filing date | Use the received date or postmark; record the conflict |
| Penalty approval form not in the file | Not produced at exam | Request it (FOIA, Appeals, or Tax Court discovery); mark the 6751(b) analysis open |
| Client says the agent "extended" the deadline | Informal promise | Only a statute or a signed consent extends an assessment period; no one at the IRS can extend a petition period by agreement (only the date printed on the notice can serve as a later safe harbor) |
| Scanned exam file is unreadable | Poor scan | Run OCR; if still unreadable, record it, list the conclusions that depend on it, and request a clean copy |
| Circuit law conflicts with the Tax Court's own precedent | Golsen | Apply the circuit's precedent for a case appealable there; note the Tax Court rule for other venues |
