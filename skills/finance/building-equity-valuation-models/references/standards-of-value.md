# Standards of Value, Professional Standards and Levels of Value

Read this in Step 1 before any number is built. The standard of value decides whose assumptions the model uses (any market participant, a hypothetical willing buyer, or a particular owner), whether synergies belong in the cash flows, and which premiums and discounts are allowed. ## 1. Standards of value

| Standard | Definition (source) | Typical purposes | Model consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair value (financial reporting) | "The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date" (ASC 820-10-20; IFRS 13 uses the same definition, IVS 102 A70) | ASC 805 purchase price allocation, ASC 350 impairment, fund NAV, ASC 718 awards | Market participant assumptions, not the owner's; exit price; unit of account from the governing Topic; fair value hierarchy level disclosed |
| Fair market value | Price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither under compulsion and both with reasonable knowledge of relevant facts (Treas. Reg. 20.2031-1(b), 25.2512-1; VS Section 100 glossary adds "hypothetical", "able" and arm's length) | Estate, gift and income tax; many buy-sell agreements; ESOPs | Hypothetical buyer; buyer-specific synergies excluded; discounts for lack of control and marketability considered for the interest actually held |
| Investment value | "The value to a particular investor based on individual investment requirements and expectations" (VS Section 100 glossary); IVS 102 A40 investment value/worth, an entity-specific basis | Acquisition screening, hold-or-sell decisions, internal capital allocation | Owner's own synergies, financing and tax position may be included; say so in the header |
| Fair value (statutory or judicial) | Defined by statute and case law of the jurisdiction; for example, Delaware appraisal values the shares "exclusive of any element of value arising from the accomplishment or expectation of the merger" (8 Del. C. section 262(h)); IVS 102 A80 | Appraisal and dissenters' rights, oppression and buyout statutes, divorce | Jurisdiction-specific; treatment of minority and marketability discounts and of synergies varies by state [VERIFY] for the forum; hand off to counsel |
| Market value (IVS) | Estimated amount for which an asset should exchange on the valuation date between a willing buyer and a willing seller in an arm's-length transaction, after proper marketing, knowledgeably, prudently and without compulsion (IVS 102 A10.01) | IVS-compliant engagements outside US GAAP and tax | Similar to fair market value; follow the IVS conceptual framework in A10.02 |

Rules:
- State the standard of value in the header in these words. "Intrinsic value", "price target" and "what it's worth" are not standards of value; map them to one (usually investment value) and say so.
- The same words carry different meanings across promulgators (IVS 102 paragraph 20.03): "fair value" under ASC 820 is not statutory fair value.
- The governing document (purchase agreement, buy-sell agreement, statute, accounting Topic, engagement letter) defines the standard; the user's label does not override it. A conflict is a source conflict.

## 2. Premise of value and level of value

- Premise of value: going concern unless facts support liquidation (orderly or forced). State it (VS Section 100 paragraph 25 and glossary; IVS 102 A90 to A120).
- Level of value describes the interest valued: controlling, marketable minority (publicly traded shares), or non-marketable minority (a private minority stake). Control premium, discount for lack of control (DLOC) and discount for lack of marketability (DLOM) move between levels; definitions are in the VS Section 100 glossary.
- Identify the subject interest exactly: class of stock, number of shares or percentage, voting rights, transfer restrictions, and whose shares (the legal entity, not a group label).

## 4. Fair value under ASC 820

- Exit price between market participants at the measurement date (ASC 820-10-20); the transaction price at entry may differ.
- Hierarchy (ASC 820-10-35-37): Level 1 quoted prices for identical items in active markets, unadjusted; Level 2 other observable inputs; Level 3 unobservable inputs. The measurement is categorized in its entirety at the lowest level of any significant input (ASC 820-10-35-37A). A private company DCF or comparables model is ordinarily Level 3.
- A Level 1 price is used without adjustment (ASC 820-10-35-36B): a listed position is quoted price times quantity, not a DCF, and no control premium or blockage factor is added to a Level 1 input.
- Premiums and discounts in Level 2 and 3 measurements must reflect a characteristic of the asset consistent with its unit of account, not the size of the holder's position (ASC 820-10-35-36B).
- Calibration (ASC 820-10-35-24C): if the transaction price was fair value at initial recognition and a technique using unobservable inputs will measure fair value later, calibrate the technique so it returns the transaction price at initial recognition, then update the calibrated inputs for changes in the subject and the market. Ask for the entry price and entry model for any fund investment marked at Level 3.
- Section excerpts are as quoted by PwC Viewpoint and Deloitte DART; the FASB Codification itself is behind a login.

## 5. Premiums and discounts by subject interest

| Subject interest | Base indication | Adjustment to consider | Do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 percent, controlling | DCF on market participant (or owner, for investment value) cash flows; trading comps plus a supported control premium; precedent transactions | DLOM only if the standard and facts support it | Add a control premium to a DCF whose cash flows already reflect control improvements |
| Marketable minority (listed shares) | Trading comps; DCF on status-quo cash flows; for ASC 820, the quoted price | None to a Level 1 price | Apply precedent transaction multiples or a control premium |
| Non-marketable minority (private stake) | Trading comps; DCF on status-quo cash flows | DLOM (and DLOC if the base indication reflects control), each with support | Apply precedent multiples without a DLOC; stack discounts that measure the same thing |

For a closely held interest valued at fair market value for estate or gift tax, model the entity value, then DLOC and DLOM as separate, supported steps (restricted stock or pre-IPO studies, option models, control and transfer terms in the governing agreements), and show the value before and after each. The transfer-tax treatment of that value (the 2031 and 2512 standard, Chapter 14 restrictions, adequate disclosure, penalties) belongs to managing-estate-and-gift-tax.
