## 1. Hierarchy of authority

State insurance law and regulation of the domiciliary state govern; the 2026 Annual Statement Instructions say insurance laws and regulations "shall at all times take precedence over the actuarial standards". Then the Annual Statement Instructions and SSAPs (statutory basis) or U.S. GAAP (ASC 944) for a GAAP basis; then ASOPs; then Academy practice notes, which are not binding. For data: the filed Annual Statement and Schedule P govern over a reserve study's data exhibits, which govern over management summaries and emails. A state prescribed or permitted practice can override an SSAP for that company; never assume one exists.

## 2. ASOP No. 43 (unpaid claim estimates)

Doc. No. 159, last revised May 2011, in force on 2026-10-30. The ASB approved a proposal to revise it in 2025 and a task force was being formed; no exposure draft had been issued.
- Scope: unpaid claims for events that have occurred by the accounting date, gross, net or of recoverables; excludes health benefit plans under ASOP Nos. 5 and 42 (but covers workers compensation medical); addresses the undiscounted value and points to ASOP No. 20 for discounting (section 1.2).
- 3.1 purpose or use; 3.2 constraints disclosed if they create significant risk; 3.3 scope: intended measure (an actuarial central estimate is an expected value over reasonably possible outcomes; "best estimate" is not enough identification), gross or net, collectibility, types of claim adjustment expense, claims covered.
- 3.6.1 multiple methods unless reliance on one is reasonable and disclosed; 3.6.2 assumptions with no known significant bias and not internally inconsistent, with sensitivity to reasonable alternatives; 3.6.4 interaction of recoverables; 3.6.5 gross versus net; 3.6.6 external conditions (economic, judicial, social); 3.6.7 changing conditions, including claims practice and reinsurance program changes; 3.6.8 uncertainty.
- 3.7.1 reasonableness tests; 3.7.3 presentation as point, range, point with margin or distribution.
- 4.1 disclosures: purpose, limitations, scope, accounting, valuation and review dates, significant risks, significant reliances, prescribed assumptions, deviations; 4.2(a) basis of any range; 4.2(b) changes from a prior estimate.

## 3. ASOP No. 36 (statements of actuarial opinion) and this skill's labels

Doc. No. 213, effective for statements issued on or after October 1, 2024. Applies to a written statement of actuarial opinion on P/C loss, LAE or other reserves, including one prepared under the NAIC instructions; a reviewing actuary follows it to the extent practicable.
- 3.3 stated basis: nominal or discounted (items, rate basis, rates), explicit risk margin, gross or net, provision for uncollectable recoverables, LAE types.
- 3.4.4 using another party's analysis; 3.5 discounted reserves follow ASOP No. 20; 3.6 consider collectability of material recoverables (no opinion on a counterparty's financial condition).
- 3.8 opinion types: reasonable (within the actuary's range of reasonable estimates); deficient or inadequate (below the minimum reasonable amount, which is disclosed); redundant or excessive (above the maximum); qualified; no opinion.
- 3.10 materiality standard; 3.11 risk of material adverse deviation, assessed on a net basis when both bases are in scope.
- These opinion types belong to the opining actuary. This skill reports only a carried-reserve position label (`references/output-template.md` Section 7) and never writes "reasonable provision", "deficient" or "redundant" as a conclusion.

## 4. ASOP No. 20 (discounting)

Revised as "Analysis of Property/Casualty Cash Flows, Including Discounting", Doc. No. 220, adopted December 2025, effective for work products issued on or after June 1, 2026. Governs discount rates, risk margins and disclosures when discounting is used; it does not make discounting permissible on a basis that forbids it.

## 5. SSAP No. 55 (unpaid losses and LAE)

- Components: reported losses; IBNR (which may include bulk provisions for deficiencies in known case reserves); LAE in two categories.
- DCC: defense, litigation and medical cost containment, internal or external, including surveillance, litigation management, experts, attorney fees under a duty to defend, and appraisers or investigators working in defense of a claim.
- AO: everything else in LAE, including adjusters and settling agents, attorney fees incurred in the determination of coverage (including litigation between the reporting entity and the policyholder), appraisers or investigators working as adjusters, and adjustment expenses from extra-contractual obligation and bad-faith lawsuits.
- Measurement: estimated ultimate cost of settling claims, including the effects of inflation and other societal and economic factors; not discounted unless a specific SSAP (SSAP No. 65) authorizes it.
- Management records its best estimate by line and in aggregate, with an analysis of variability; the midpoint of a range applies only in the rare case where no point is better than another (paragraphs 13 and 14 in the 2024 guide's numbering).
- Anticipated salvage and subrogation, if elected, is estimated consistently, net of associated expenses, and deducted from the liability; impaired recoverables may not reduce reserves (paragraph 15).
- Changes in estimate go through the statement of operations in the period they become known (paragraph 16, via SSAP No. 3).

## 6. SSAP No. 65 (discounting and P&C contracts)

- Paragraph 10: except fixed and reasonably determinable payments such as workers compensation tabular indemnity reserves and long-term disability claims, P&C loss reserves shall not be discounted; no LAE reserves shall be discounted.
- Exhibit A of SSAP No. 65 gives guidelines for states that prescribe or permit nontabular discounting; whether a state does so is state-specific.
- Paragraph 14: disclose whether any unpaid losses or LAE are discounted.
- High-deductible policies: reserves net of the deductible, with no credit for uncollectible amounts due from the insured (paragraph 35).
- Schedule P is reported undiscounted except a discount implicit in tabular reserves in Part 1; Part 2 is gross of all discounts (2026 ASI, Schedule P instruction 9). Exhibit B items 9.1 and 9.2 of the SAO disclose nontabular and tabular discount.

## 7. SSAP No. 62R (reinsurance)

Reinsurance recoverables on unpaid case and IBNR losses and LAE are netted against gross liabilities (paragraph 31); recoverables on paid losses are an asset without offset (paragraph 24). Uncollectible reinsurance is addressed through the Schedule F provision for reinsurance and write-offs, separate from the actuary's collectibility assessment. Retroactive reinsurance is excluded from Schedule P.

## 8. Schedule P structure

Seven parts plus interrogatories (2026 ASI): Part 1 detail of premiums, losses and loss expenses (including anticipated salvage and subrogation, column 23); Part 2 incurred net losses and DCC at each year-end; Part 3 cumulative paid net losses and DCC, with closed claim counts; Part 4 bulk and IBNR reserves on net losses and DCC; Part 5 claim counts (direct and assumed); Part 6 earned premium history; Part 7 loss-sensitive contracts. Ten years for all lines plus a prior row; amounts in thousands; incurred year by accident date, report date (claims-made), policy issue date or discovery date; pooled companies report their share. AO is not in Parts 2 to 4.

## 9. NAIC Annual Statement Instructions, Actuarial Opinion (2026)

- Appointed Actuary: a Qualified Actuary appointed by the Board of Directors by December 31 of the year opined on initial engagement; commissioner notified within five business days. Qualified Actuary: meets the U.S. Qualification Standards' specific standard for NAIC P/C opinions, holds an Accepted Actuarial Designation (FCAS with Exam 6 US; ACAS with Exams 6 US and 7; FSA with GI 101, 201, 301 and 302; listed substitutions), and belongs to an organization under the Academy code and the ABCD; or is approved by the Academy's Casualty Practice Council or the commissioner. Continuing education attested annually.
- Qualification documentation goes to the Board on initial appointment: biography, how the definition is met, relevant responsible experience; the Board documents its review; it is a workpaper available to regulators.
- Sections: IDENTIFICATION, SCOPE (Exhibit A amounts; data relied on, evaluated and reconciled to Schedule P Part 1), OPINION (one of five types, identified in Exhibit B item 4), RELEVANT COMMENTS (company-specific risk factors; risk of material adverse deviation with the materiality standard in dollars; Exhibit B items 8 to 13; reinsurance collectability, retroactive and financial reinsurance; unusual IRIS reserve ratios; changes in methods and assumptions).
- Exhibit A item 3 (direct and assumed unpaid losses) equals Schedule P Part 1 Summary columns 13 and 15, line 12, times 1,000; item 4 (direct and assumed unpaid LAE) equals columns 17, 19 and 21.
- Actuarial Report: narrative and technical components from basic data to conclusions; exhibit tying to the Annual Statement comparing carried with the actuary's point and range; reconciliation of the data used to Schedule P; change from the prior report; IRIS comments; disclosures when another's analysis covers a material portion. Retained seven years.
- AOS (confidential, to the domiciliary state): A range, B point, C carried, D differences, each net and gross; E an explicit description where one-year adverse development exceeded 5% of prior year-end policyholders' surplus (Schedule P Part 2 Summary) in three or more of the past five calendar years. A pooled company reports its own share, not the pool total; a 0% participant reports the lead company's figures. AOS figures reconcile to the Annual Statement, the SAO and the Actuarial Report, or the difference is explained.

## 10. Filing calendar

Each element has its own trigger. Dates are NAIC defaults; a state may set a different date or a weekend rule, so confirm with the domiciliary state's law ([VERIFY] per state). The ASI states no weekend rule.

| Element | Trigger | Window |
|---|---|---|
| Exemption letter of intent | Insurer intends to claim an SAO exemption for a year | By December 1 of that year |
| Appointment of the Appointed Actuary | Initial engagement for a year-end | By December 31 of the year opined |
| Notice of appointment to the commissioner | Date of initial appointment | Within 5 business days |
| SAO filed with the Annual Statement | Year-end | On or before March 1 of the following year |
| AOS to the domiciliary state | Year-end | By March 15 of the following year, or a later state date |
| AOS to a non-domiciliary state | That state's request | Within 15 days of request, not before March 15 |
| Actuarial Report available | Year-end; or a commissioner's request | By May 1 of the following year; or within 2 weeks of the request |
| Notice of replacement of the Appointed Actuary | Board action replacing the prior Appointed Actuary | Within 5 business days |
| Disagreements letter | That notification | Within 10 business days |
| Notice to the Board of an SAO or AOS error | Appointed Actuary's determination of error from factually incorrect data | Within 5 business days |
| Workpaper retention | Opinion date | 7 years |

## 11. ASC 944 short-duration disclosures (ASU 2015-09)

For annual periods: undiscounted claims development by accident year, net of reinsurance, for incurred and for paid claims and allocated claim adjustment expenses, for the years claims typically remain outstanding up to 10 (944-40-50-4B; earlier years are required supplementary information); reconciliation to the balance sheet liability with reinsurance recoverable on unpaid claims shown separately (50-4C); for each accident year, IBNR plus expected development on reported claims and cumulative claim frequency (50-4D); methodology descriptions (50-4F); average annual percentage payout by age (50-4G); significant changes in methodologies and assumptions with reasons and effects (50-4I); plus the rollforward (50-3). Effective for public business entities for annual periods beginning after December 15, 2015.

## 12. Out of scope

Life, annuity and long-duration accident and health reserves (contract reserves, Actuarial Guideline LI asset adequacy for long-term care), and health benefit plan claim liabilities under ASOP No. 5, are outside this skill. Exhibit B item 12 of the P/C SAO discloses A&H long-duration reserves; their adequacy is for a credentialed life or health actuary.
