## 2. Computing estate and gift tax

- Estate tax (IRC 2001(b)): tentative tax on (taxable estate + adjusted taxable gifts) minus gift tax payable on post-1976 gifts, computed at the rates in effect at death (IRC 2001(g)(1)); then minus the applicable credit amount (IRC 2010(c)(1)), which is the tentative tax on the applicable exclusion amount.
- Adjusted taxable gifts are post-1976 taxable gifts other than gifts includible in the gross estate (IRC 2001(b)).
- Rate table (IRC 2001(c)): the top bracket is $345,800 plus 40 percent of the excess over $1,000,000. When both the base and the exclusion exceed $1,000,000, the net tax is 40 percent of the excess of the base over the applicable exclusion amount.
- Applicable exclusion amount = BEA + DSUE (IRC 2010(c)(2)); the BEA used at death is the BEA for the year of death, subject to Section 3.
- Gift tax uses the same rate table and credit (IRC 2505) on cumulative taxable gifts.
- Filing threshold (IRC 6018(a)(1), (3)): a return is required when the gross estate of a citizen or resident exceeds the BEA for the year of death less adjusted taxable gifts. Electing portability makes the estate one required to file (Treas. Reg. 20.2010-2(a)(1)).

## 3. Basic exclusion after Pub. L. 119-21 and anti-clawback

- Pub. L. 119-21 sec. 70106 set the BEA at $15,000,000 for estates of decedents dying and gifts made after December 31, 2025, indexed for years after 2026, and struck former IRC 2010(c)(3)(C), the $10,000,000 base that was to expire after 2025. There is no scheduled reduction in the statute. A memo or model that assumes the BEA falls to about half in 2026, or that 2026 gifts face a "use it or lose it" deadline, is applying repealed law.
- Treas. Reg. 20.2010-1(c) (anti-clawback): if the credit attributable to the BEA used on post-1976 gifts exceeds the credit attributable to the BEA at death, the estate credit is based on the larger amount used on the gifts. It matters only if a future statute lowers the BEA. DSUE is deemed used before the donor's own BEA (20.2010-1(c)(1)(ii)(A)).
- Proposed exceptions (REG-118913-21, 87 Fed. Reg. 24918, Apr. 27, 2022) would deny the special rule for gifts includible in the gross estate (IRC 2035, 2036, 2037, 2038, 2042), enforceable promises unpaid at death, transfers under 25.2701-5(a)(4) or 25.2702-6(a)(1), and such interests released within 18 months of death, with a 5 percent de minimis exception. They are proposed only: paragraph (c)(3) of the current regulation is "[Reserved]", and the Unified Agenda (Fall 2024) lists the final rule as pending. Present them as a risk, not as law.

## 4. Portability and the DSUE amount

- DSUE (IRC 2010(c)(4); Treas. Reg. 20.2010-2(c)(1)): the lesser of (a) the BEA in effect in the year of the deceased spouse's death, or (b) the deceased spouse's applicable exclusion amount minus (taxable estate + adjusted taxable gifts).
- Election (IRC 2010(c)(5)(A)): made by the executor on a timely filed, complete and properly prepared Form 706 (due nine months after death, plus any extension); irrevocable. Filing a complete return elects portability unless the executor opts out (20.2010-2(a)(2), (3)). An estate under the filing threshold may report estimated values for certain property (20.2010-2(a)(7)(ii)).
- Who elects: the appointed executor, or if none, a non-appointed executor in possession of property (20.2010-2(a)(6)). The surviving spouse cannot elect unless he or she is that executor.
- Late election (Rev. Proc. 2022-32): available only if the decedent was a U.S. citizen or resident survived by a spouse, the estate was not required to file under IRC 6018(a) by value, and no return was timely filed; the executor files a complete Form 706 on or before the fifth anniversary of death headed "FILED PURSUANT TO REV. PROC. 2022-32 TO ELECT PORTABILITY UNDER § 2010(c)(5)(A)." An estate that was required to file cannot get Treas. Reg. 301.9100-3 relief (20.2010-2(a)(1)). After five years, only a letter ruling under 301.9100-3 is possible for a non-required estate.
- Last deceased spouse (Treas. Reg. 20.2010-3(a)): only the DSUE of the surviving spouse's last deceased spouse counts. Remarriage alone does not change it; the death of a later spouse does, even if that spouse's executor made no election (then the DSUE is zero). DSUE already applied to the survivor's lifetime gifts before a later spouse's death is kept (20.2010-3(b); 25.2505-2).
- The IRS may examine the deceased spouse's return to determine the DSUE after the assessment period closes (IRC 2010(c)(5)(B)).

## 5. Gifts: annual exclusion, Crummey rights, split gifts, filing

- Annual exclusion (IRC 2503(b)): per donee per year, only for present interests. A future interest (reversions, remainders and other interests that begin in use, possession or enjoyment at a future time) gets no exclusion (Treas. Reg. 25.2503-3(a)). Most transfers to trusts are future interests.
- Crummey withdrawal rights: a beneficiary's right under the trust instrument to demand a contribution makes that part a present interest, including for minor beneficiaries who could make the demand through a guardian (Crummey v. Commissioner, 397 F.2d 82 (9th Cir. 1968)). Withdrawal rights held by contingent beneficiaries are [VERIFY]. The right must exist in the instrument when the transfer is made; it cannot be added later for a past transfer. Whether and when written notice is legally required, and the length of the withdrawal window, are [VERIFY]; follow the instrument.
- Tuition paid directly to a qualifying school and medical care paid directly to the provider are not gifts (IRC 2503(e)).
- Split gifts (IRC 2513): both spouses must be U.S. citizens or residents at the time of the gift, and consent applies to all gifts to third parties by either spouse in the year. Consent cannot be signified after April 15 of the following year unless no return was filed by then, and never after a notice of deficiency (2513(b)(2)). The consenting spouse files a separate Form 709 when, after splitting, his or her half is not fully excluded, for example any half of a future-interest gift (Treas. Reg. 25.6019-2).
- Form 709 is required for any gift other than excluded annual exclusion and 2503(e) transfers and certain marital and charitable transfers (IRC 6019).

## 6. Adequate disclosure and the gift tax statute

- Assessment period: three years after the return is filed; an early return is treated as filed on the due date (IRC 6501(a), (b)(1)). A gift required to be shown and not shown may be assessed at any time unless adequately disclosed (IRC 6501(c)(9)).
- Adequate disclosure (Treas. Reg. 301.6501(c)-1(f)(2)): (i) description of the property and any consideration; (ii) identity of and relationship between transferor and each transferee; (iii) for a trust, its EIN and a brief description of terms or a copy; (iv) a detailed description of the valuation method, financial data, restrictions and each discount claimed; for an entity valued on net assets, the fair market value of 100 percent of the entity without discounts, the pro rata portion transferred, and the value reported; the same for each lower-tier entity where material; (v) any position contrary to regulations or revenue rulings.
- An appraisal satisfies item (iv) only if it meets 301.6501(c)-1(f)(3): an appraiser who holds out or regularly appraises, is qualified, and is not the donor, the donee, a member of either's family as defined in IRC 2032A(e)(2) (ancestors, spouse, lineal descendants of the individual, the spouse or a parent, and their spouses), or their employee; and contents (A) to (H), including the transfer date, appraisal date and purpose, and data sufficient to replicate the value.
- Not adequately disclosed: the gift tax may be assessed at any time (301.6501(c)-1(f)(1)). Adequately disclosed and the period expired: the value is finally determined for computing later gift and estate tax (IRC 2001(f)).
- Reporting a transfer as incomplete keeps the period open; reporting it as complete with adequate disclosure starts it even if it is later found incomplete (301.6501(c)-1(f)(5)).

## 7. GST exemption and allocation

- Automatic allocation to a lifetime direct skip, unless the transferor elects out (IRC 2632(b)).
- Automatic allocation to an indirect skip only if the trust is a "GST trust" (IRC 2632(c)). A trust is not a GST trust if, among the listed exceptions, the instrument requires more than 25 percent of corpus to be distributed to, or allows it to be withdrawn by, non-skip persons before age 46 (2632(c)(3)(B)(i)); several other exceptions follow in the statute. For a trust that is not a GST trust, no exemption is allocated unless the transferor allocates it or elects to treat the trust as a GST trust (2632(c)(5)(A)(ii)).
- Elect out of automatic allocation, or elect in, on a timely filed Form 709 (2632(b)(3), (c)(5)(B); Treas. Reg. 26.2632-1(b)(2)(iii), (b)(3)). With split gifts each spouse is a separate transferor and must elect separately.
- Timely allocation: on a Form 709 filed by the IRC 6075(b) date including extensions actually granted; the trust is valued at the gift tax value (IRC 2642(b)(1); Treas. Reg. 26.2632-1(b)(2)(ii), (b)(4); 26.2642-2(a)(1)). A late allocation is effective, and the trust valued, on the date the Form 709 is filed (2642(b)(3); 26.2642-2(a)(2)).
- Allocations are irrevocable (IRC 2631(b)). Relief for missed allocations and elections is by regulation under IRC 2642(g) (Treas. Reg. 301.9100-2 and -3 procedures) [VERIFY the current procedure].
- An estate tax inclusion period (IRC 2642(f)) delays allocation for a trust that would be included in the transferor's or spouse's estate, such as a GRAT or QPRT during its term.

## 8. Gross estate inclusion: 2033, 2035, 2036, 2038, 2042

- IRC 2033: property the decedent owned at death.
- IRC 2036(a): property transferred (other than in a bona fide sale for adequate and full consideration) in which the decedent retained (1) possession, enjoyment or the right to income, or (2) the right, alone or with any person, to designate who enjoys the property or its income, for life or a period tied to death. Retained voting rights in a controlled corporation (20 percent of vote, with IRC 318 attribution) count as retained enjoyment (2036(b)). Discounts on a family entity interest do not help if the underlying assets are pulled back in under 2036.
- IRC 2038: a power at death to alter, amend, revoke or terminate, or one relinquished within three years of death.
- IRC 2042: life insurance receivable by the executor, or by others where the decedent held any incident of ownership.
- IRC 2035(a): a transfer of an interest or release of a power within three years of death is pulled back if it would have been included under 2036, 2037, 2038 or 2042 had it been kept. Gift tax paid on gifts within three years of death is added to the gross estate (2035(b)). The 35 percent test for 6166 must be met with and without 2035(a) (2035(c)(2)).

## 9. Valuation: fair market value of a transferred interest, 2032, 2703, Connelly

- Standard (Treas. Reg. 20.2031-1(b); 25.2512-1): the price between a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither under compulsion, both with reasonable knowledge of relevant facts; not a forced sale price. Gifts are valued at the date of the gift (IRC 2512(a)); estates at death unless 2032 is elected.
- Split of work: the fair market value of a closely held interest (the appraisal of the entity interest, the DCF or comparables, and discounts for lack of control or marketability) belongs to building-equity-valuation-models. This skill keeps the transfer-tax treatment of that value: the 2031 and 2512 standard, Chapter 14, adequate disclosure (including whether an appraisal meets Treas. Reg. 301.6501(c)-1(f)(3)), inclusion under 2035 to 2038, and valuation understatement penalties.
- Chapter 14 (IRC 2701-2704): 2701 values certain retained distribution and liquidation rights in a family corporation or partnership at zero or under special rules when a junior interest is transferred to family; 2702 values a retained interest in a trust for family at zero unless it is a qualified annuity or unitrust interest or a personal residence trust; 2703 is below; 2704(a) treats a lapse of a voting or liquidation right as a transfer when the family controls the entity before and after, and 2704(b) disregards an applicable restriction on liquidation when the family controls the entity. Flag each when the facts fit; the computation of a 2701 or 2702 value is [VERIFY] against the regulations.
- Valuation understatement penalty (IRC 6662(g), (h)): a value claimed on an estate or gift tax return that is 65 percent or less of the correct value is a substantial understatement (20 percent penalty); 40 percent or less is a gross misstatement (40 percent penalty); no penalty unless the underpayment attributable exceeds $5,000. Show the ratio of the reported value to the value the facts support; reasonable cause defenses go to managing-tax-controversy.
- Alternate valuation (IRC 2032): property valued six months after death, or at the date of an earlier distribution, sale or other disposition; only if the election decreases both the gross estate and the estate and GST tax (2032(c)); made on the return; irrevocable; not if the return is filed more than one year after the due date including extensions (2032(d)).
- Special use valuation (IRC 2032A) for qualified farm or business real property: reduction capped at $1,460,000 for 2026 deaths. Eligibility is not analyzed in this skill; route to legal-research.
- Buy-sell terms (IRC 2703): an option, agreement or restriction is disregarded unless it is a bona fide business arrangement, not a device to transfer to family for less than full consideration, and comparable to arm's length arrangements.
- Connelly v. United States, 602 U.S. 257 (2024): a corporation's obligation to redeem a decedent's shares at fair market value is not a liability that offsets life insurance proceeds the corporation receives to fund it. The proceeds increase the corporation's value; the redeemed shares are valued as a share of that larger value. In Connelly: $3.86 million plus $3 million of proceeds = $6.86 million; 77.18 percent = about $5.3 million, not about $3 million. The opinion notes that a cross-purchase arrangement would have kept the proceeds out of the corporation, with its own drawbacks and tax consequences. The Court quotes Treas. Reg. 20.2031-2(f)(2): value reflects "proceeds of life insurance policies payable to . . . the company."

## 10. Basis: 1014, 1015 and grantor trusts (Rev. Rul. 2023-2)

- Property acquired from a decedent takes a basis equal to its estate tax value (IRC 1014(a)); the categories in 1014(b) are exclusive, including property required to be included in the gross estate (1014(b)(9)).
- Rev. Rul. 2023-2: assets of an irrevocable grantor trust funded by a completed gift and not includible in the grantor's gross estate receive no basis adjustment under 1014 at the grantor's death. Their basis is carryover basis under IRC 1015.
- Basis consistency: basis may not exceed the finally determined estate tax value (IRC 1014(f)); the executor furnishes basis statements (Form 8971 and Schedule A) under IRC 6035 (timing in `deadlines-and-elections.md`).
- The trade-off to show in planning: an asset given away during life leaves the estate with its appreciation but keeps carryover basis; an asset held until death is taxed in the estate but gets the 1014 basis.

## 11. Payment relief: 6166 and 6161

- IRC 6166(a): if a closely held business interest of a citizen or resident decedent exceeds 35 percent of the adjusted gross estate (gross estate less deductions under 2053 and 2054), the executor may elect to pay the tax attributable to it in up to 10 annual installments, the first up to five years after the payment due date. Qualifying interests include a partnership or corporation with 20 percent or more included in the estate, or 45 or fewer partners or shareholders (6166(b)(1)). The election is made on a timely filed Form 706, including extensions (6166(d); Treas. Reg. 20.6166-1(b)).
- Interest: the 2-percent portion is based on $1,940,000 for 2026 deaths (IRC 6601(j); Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sec. 4.51).
- Acceleration (6166(g)(1)): dispositions or withdrawals totaling 50 percent or more of the interest end the deferral. A section 303 redemption is not counted only if estate tax at least equal to the redemption distribution is paid by the next installment date or within one year (6166(g)(1)(B)).
- IRC 6161(a)(1): the IRS may extend time to pay estate tax shown on the return for up to 12 months; 6161(a)(2): for reasonable cause, up to 10 years. A filing extension (Form 4768 under Treas. Reg. 20.6081-1) does not extend time to pay (20.6081-1(e); IRC 6151(a)).

## 12. Noncitizen spouses, state taxes and out-of-scope items

- No marital deduction for property passing to a noncitizen spouse except through a qualified domestic trust (IRC 2056(d)); no gift marital deduction to a noncitizen spouse, with an annual exclusion of $194,000 for 2026 (IRC 2523(i); Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sec. 4.42). QDOT terms and drafting go to legal-research.
- State estate and inheritance taxes differ by state in threshold, rate, portability, gift add-back and who pays. They are read from the domicile state's statute (and the situs state's for real property) for the year, or the state layer is marked "not modeled" with [VERIFY].
