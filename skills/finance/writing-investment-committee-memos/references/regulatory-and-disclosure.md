# Regulatory and Disclosure Rules

U.S. rules and guidance that bear on what an IC memo must disclose about conflicts, how it handles material nonpublic information, and what changes when memo content may reach investors. Status checked on October 30, 2026; citations and method are in `references/primary-sources.md`. This file supports drafting; it is not a legal opinion. Questions of application go to the firm's chief compliance officer or fund counsel.

## 1. Who the rules reach

- The Marketing Rule (17 CFR 275.206(4)-1) applies to advisers registered or required to be registered with the SEC under Advisers Act section 203. Its adopting release states it does not apply to exempt reporting advisers or state-registered advisers.
- Rule 206(4)-8 applies to any investment adviser to a pooled investment vehicle (an investment company, or a fund excluded by section 3(c)(1) or 3(c)(7) of the Investment Company Act), registered or not.
- Section 204A requires MNPI policies of every adviser subject to section 204 of the Advisers Act.
- Ask which adviser entity manages each investing fund and its registration status (Checkpoint A). A non-U.S. or unregistered adviser may face other regimes; mark them [VERIFY] for counsel.

## 2. Conflicts: the fiduciary disclosure standard

The SEC's 2019 interpretation of the investment adviser standard of conduct (Release IA-5248) states:

- Under the duty of loyalty an adviser must eliminate or make full and fair disclosure of all conflicts of interest that might incline it, consciously or unconsciously, to render advice that is not disinterested, so the client can give informed consent.
- Disclosure must be specific enough for the client to understand the conflict and decide whether to consent. Saying the adviser has "other clients" or "conflicts", without describing how they are managed, is inadequate.
- Saying the adviser "may" have a conflict is not adequate when the conflict actually exists.
- Disclosure and consent do not by themselves satisfy the duty to act in the client's best interest.

For the memo this means: name each actual conflict in the conflicts table with its facts and the consent route; never write "None" without the checklist; never use "may" for a conflict that exists on the deal.

## 3. Conflicts the examination staff has found under-disclosed

The June 23, 2020 risk alert, "Observations from Examinations of Investment Advisers Managing Private Funds", lists conflict areas the staff found inadequately disclosed. Each is a row in the memo's conflicts table when the facts touch it:

- Allocation of investments among flagship funds, co-investment vehicles, separately managed accounts and other clients, including preferential allocation to new or higher-fee clients.
- Multiple clients invested in the same portfolio company at different levels of the capital structure (for example, one fund holding debt or preferred and another holding equity).
- Fund restructurings and stapled secondaries.
- Adviser or personnel interests in recommended investments (pre-existing ownership, referral fees, options).
- Co-investment: following a disclosed allocation process, and agreements giving certain investors co-investment opportunities without disclosure to others.
- Service providers: portfolio companies contracting with entities controlled by the adviser, its affiliates or principals' family members; and representations that affiliate services are on terms no less favorable than third-party terms, without support.
- Fees and expenses, and MNPI policies (Section 8).

The Division of Examinations' fiscal year 2026 priorities include reviewing advisers to private funds that also advise separately managed accounts or new funds for favoritism in investment allocations and interfund transfers. Staff risk alerts and priorities are not rules; they show where examiners look.

## 4. ILPA Principles 3.0 and the ILPA templates

ILPA Principles 3.0 (2019) is industry guidance, not law; it binds a fund only where the LPA or a side letter adopts it. Cite it as best practice, and cite the LPA for what is required. Points that bear on the memo:

- **LPAC.** The LPAC's mandate generally includes conflicts such as cross-fund investments and GP investment in portfolio companies outside the fund's interest, and affiliated transactions. "No GP should clear its own conflicts under any circumstances", and it is best to consult the LPAC on all conflicts and non-arm's-length transactions. An IC approval is the GP's own approval; it does not clear a conflict the LPA sends to the LPAC.
- **Cross-fund investments** (a firm investing in the same company from different funds): limit overlaps; the LPA should set a threshold; carried interest and fee offsets should be consistent across both funds' positions; disclose overlapping investments and fees received from them (the "Affiliated Positions" section of the ILPA Reporting Template).
- **Fees.** Portfolio-company fees received by the GP or its affiliates should be offset against the management fee; fees generated by an affiliate of the GP (advisory firm, in-house consultancy) should be reviewed and approved by a majority of the LPAC; fees paid by portfolio companies to operating partners deemed affiliates should be fully offset.
- **Co-investment.** Disclose how transaction and other fees are allocated between the fund and co-investors and how offsets apply; broken-deal expenses should be shared pro rata with co-investment vehicles in most cases, and LPs told of any vehicle that is not.
- **Subscription lines.** Report IRR and MOIC with and without the facility.
- **Templates.** The updated ILPA Reporting Template (v. 2.0, released January 2025) and the ILPA Performance Template apply on a go-forward basis to funds commencing operations on or after January 1, 2026 (the Reporting Template also to funds still in their investment period in Q1 2026). Use their fee, expense and carried interest categories when the memo presents fees borne by the fund.

## 5. Status of the 2023 private fund adviser rules

The SEC adopted the private fund adviser rules on August 23, 2023 (Release IA-6383): quarterly statements, mandatory audits, adviser-led secondary fairness or valuation opinions, restricted activities and preferential treatment, plus amendments to Rules 204-2 and 206(4)-7. On June 5, 2024 the Fifth Circuit vacated the rules in their entirety (National Association of Private Fund Managers v. SEC, 103 F.4th 1097, No. 23-60471), holding the SEC exceeded its statutory authority. The SEC's technical amendments (89 Fed. Reg. 91252, Nov. 19, 2024) and its October 31, 2024 announcement confirm that the new rules and the amendments to Rules 204-2 and 206(4)-7 are no longer in effect. As of October 30, 2026 no replacement rule was located.

Consequences for the memo: do not cite the quarterly statement, restricted-activity or preferential-treatment rules as requirements. Fee, expense and conflict disclosure obligations come from the fiduciary duty, Section 206, Rule 206(4)-8, the LPA and side letters.

## 6. When memo content becomes an advertisement: the Marketing Rule

- An internal IC memo addressed to the deal team and committee is not a communication offering advisory services to clients or investors. The analysis changes when any part goes to prospective or current investors.
- **Advertisement** (Rule 206(4)-1(e)(1)(i)): a direct or indirect communication to more than one person, or to one or more persons if it includes hypothetical performance, that offers advisory services with regard to securities to prospective clients or investors in a private fund the adviser advises, or offers new advisory services to current clients or private fund investors. Excluded: extemporaneous live oral communications; required regulatory notices and filings; and hypothetical performance provided in response to an unsolicited request, or to a private fund investor in a one-on-one communication.
- **Hypothetical performance** (Rule 206(4)-1(e)(8)) includes "targeted or projected performance returns with respect to any portfolio or to the investment advisory services with regard to securities offered in the advertisement". A deal's projected IRR and MOIC offered to prospective co-investors fit this text. Projections of general market performance or economic conditions are not covered.
- **Conditions for hypothetical performance** (Rule 206(4)-1(d)(6)): policies and procedures reasonably designed to make it relevant to the likely financial situation and investment objectives of the intended audience; enough information to understand the criteria and assumptions; and enough information (for private fund investors, provided or offered promptly) to understand its risks and limitations. The proviso waives paragraphs (d)(2), (4) and (5) only; it does not waive (d)(1).
- **Gross and net** (Rule 206(4)-1(d)(1)): gross performance requires net performance with at least equal prominence, over the same period and methodology.
- **Extracted performance** (Rule 206(4)-1(d)(5)): one deal's track record from a fund is extracted performance; the staff's FAQ (updated March 19, 2025) describes when gross-only extracts are acceptable alongside the total portfolio's gross and net performance.
- **Case studies.** The adopting release treats portfolio-company case studies as specific investment advice subject to the fair-and-balanced requirement; showing only winners is not fair and balanced.
- **General prohibitions** (Rule 206(4)-1(a)): no untrue statement or omission of a material fact; no unsubstantiated material statement; fair and balanced treatment of material risks and limitations; no misleading inclusion or exclusion of performance.
- Whether a particular co-investment or LP communication is an advertisement is a facts-and-circumstances call for compliance. The memo flags it and hands it to managing-advertising-compliance; it does not release the external version.

## 7. Antifraud for pooled vehicles: Rule 206(4)-8

It is a fraudulent act for an adviser to a pooled investment vehicle to make an untrue statement of material fact, or omit a material fact necessary to make statements not misleading, to any investor or prospective investor in the vehicle. This applies whether or not a document is an advertisement. A memo excerpt given to investors that drops a known material risk or finding is the omission the rule describes; this is one reason Ground Rule 9 bars omissions.

## 8. Material nonpublic information: Section 204A

Every adviser subject to section 204 must establish, maintain and enforce written policies reasonably designed to prevent misuse of MNPI by the adviser and its associated persons. The 2020 risk alert found gaps around employees with periodic access to MNPI about public issuers, including through private investments. In the memo:

- List every public issuer the deal touches (a listed seller in a carve-out, a public target, a public borrower, listed peers whose data came from management, a public company in a cross-fund holding).
- For each, record what nonpublic information was received, under which NDA, and the wall-crossing and restricted-list status; route it to the firm's compliance officer.
- Keep MNPI in the internal memo only as needed for the decision; never in any external version.
- No finance skill in this library claims deal-team MNPI handling in its description. managing-trade-surveillance addresses wall-crossing records and the restricted list when reviewing trading, and managing-investment-adviser-compliance addresses the Rule 204A-1 code of ethics; neither covers screening an IC memo. The hand-off is to the firm's compliance function.

## 9. Checklist for any external version

1. Compliance review requested and recorded; version marked "Draft for compliance review".
2. MNPI removed, with compliance confirmation.
3. Projected returns treated as hypothetical performance: criteria, assumptions, risks and limitations included; net shown with gross at equal prominence.
4. Known material risks and diligence findings that appear in the internal memo and are material to the audience are not dropped.
5. Conflicts relevant to the recipients (co-investment allocation, fees, cross-fund positions) disclosed specifically.
6. Track record or case studies presented fair and balanced, with extracted-performance conditions met.
