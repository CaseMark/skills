## 1. Contract costs (ASC 340-40)

- **Costs to obtain** (340-40-25-1 to 25-3): capitalize incremental costs the entity expects to recover, such as a sales commission it would not have paid without the contract. Costs incurred regardless of winning the contract are expensed unless explicitly chargeable to the customer. Salaries, fixed bonuses not tied to a specific contract, and costs of failed bids are not incremental.
- **Practical expedient** (340-40-25-4): expense when incurred only if the amortization period of the asset that would otherwise be recognized is one year or less. The test is the amortization period, not the commission plan's payment period or the first invoice period. Where renewals are expected and no commensurate renewal commission is paid, the period can extend to the anticipated renewals, so a one-year contract can still fail the expedient.
- **Costs to fulfill** (340-40-25-5 to 25-8): capitalize only if they relate directly to a specific contract or anticipated contract, generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future obligations, and are expected to be recovered, and no other Topic applies (inventory, internal-use software, PP&E, software to be sold). General and administrative costs, wasted resources and costs of past performance are expensed.
- **Amortization** (340-40-35-1): on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services, which may include specific anticipated contracts. The period and pattern are a policy judgment documented with renewal data; a change is a change in estimate.
- **Impairment** (340-40-35-3 onward): impair when the carrying amount exceeds remaining consideration expected less costs not yet recognized as expense. US GAAP prohibits reversal.
- Record capitalized costs in a roll-forward: beginning balance, additions, amortization, impairment, ending balance.

## 2. Presentation and disclosure

- **Contract balances** (606-10-50-8 to 50-10): opening and closing receivables, contract assets and contract liabilities; revenue recognized from the opening contract liability; explanation of significant changes.
- **Remaining performance obligations (RPO)** (606-10-50-13): the aggregate transaction price allocated to unsatisfied or partly unsatisfied obligations at period end, and when it will be recognized, in time bands or qualitatively.
- Optional exemptions: contracts with an original expected duration of one year or less, and obligations recognized under the right-to-invoice expedient in 606-10-55-18 (606-10-50-14); certain variable consideration (sales-based or usage-based royalties, and variable amounts allocated wholly to an unsatisfied obligation or distinct period under 606-10-32-40) (606-10-50-14A). The 50-14(b) and 50-14A exemptions never apply to fixed consideration (606-10-50-14B). An entity using any exemption discloses which one and the nature and remaining duration of the excluded obligations (606-10-50-15).
- Original expected duration follows the enforceable term (606-10-25-3), not the invoicing cycle. A three-year non-cancellable subscription billed annually is not a one-year contract.
- Entities other than PBEs (and certain listed not-for-profit entities and SEC-filing employee benefit plans) may elect to omit the RPO disclosures in 606-10-50-13 to 50-15, may limit quantitative disaggregation, and may omit most contract balance disclosures except 606-10-50-8(a). Confirm PBE status before relying on any election.
- Disaggregation by categories that depict how economic factors affect revenue (606-10-50-5, 55-89 to 55-91); significant judgments on timing and on transaction price and allocation (606-10-50-17 onward); policy elections and practical expedients used.
- Pending: ASU 2025-11 makes RPO and contract balance disclosures expressly interim as well as annual, for interim periods within annual periods beginning after December 15, 2027 (PBEs) and December 15, 2028 (others).
- Interest from a significant financing component is presented apart from revenue (606-10-32-20).

## 3. IFRS 15 differences that change an answer

| Topic | US GAAP (ASC 606 and 340-40) | IFRS 15 |
|---|---|---|
| Collectibility threshold | Probable means likely to occur | Probable means more likely than not (paragraph 9(e)) |
| Licenses | Functional or symbolic IP decides right to use or access | Right to access if the three criteria in paragraph B58 are met (activities significantly affect the IP, customer exposed, no transfer) |
| License renewals | No revenue before the renewal period begins (606-10-55-58C) | No explicit renewal rule; revenue can come earlier |
| Shipping and handling after control | Policy election to treat as fulfillment | No election; usually a separate obligation |
| Sales taxes | Policy election to exclude all from the transaction price | Principal or agent analysis for each tax |
| Noncash consideration | Measured at contract inception | No measurement date specified |
| Contract cost impairment | Reversal prohibited | Reversal required when conditions improve (paragraph 104) |
| RPO disclosure | One-year and right-to-invoice exemptions (606-10-50-14) plus variable consideration exemptions in 606-10-50-14A | Same one-year and right-to-invoice expedients (paragraph 121); no variable consideration exemption |
| Interim disclosure | Disaggregation, contract balances and RPO (pending ASU 2025-11 for the last two) | IAS 34 requires disaggregation |
| Non-PBE relief | Disclosure elections exist | Applies to all IFRS reporters |
| Share-based consideration to customers | Measured under ASC 718 (ASU 2019-08, ASU 2025-04) | Not specified; consider IFRS 2, IFRS 15, IAS 32 |

Converged in substance: the five steps, the constraint, the significant financing component and its one-year expedient (paragraph 63), principal versus agent indicators (paragraphs B34 to B38), bill-and-hold criteria (paragraph B81), and the one-year contract cost expedient (paragraph 94). Onerous contracts are tested under IAS 37 for IFRS reporters.

## 4. SEC staff guidance after ASC 606

- SAB 116 (August 18, 2017; 82 Fed. Reg. 41146) states that SAB Topic 13 no longer applies once a registrant adopts ASC 606, and conforms Topics 8 and 11.A.
- Release No. 33-10402 tells registrants that, after adopting ASC 606, they no longer look to AAER 108 (In the Matter of Stewart Parness) for bill-and-hold criteria; ASC 606 governs. A memo citing SAB 104, Topic 13 or AAER 108 for a current-period conclusion is relying on superseded guidance.
- Registrant disclosure content beyond ASC 606 (MD&A, critical accounting estimates) belongs to managing-sec-reporting.

## 5. Audit readiness and side agreements

- PCAOB AS 2110.68: the auditor presumes a fraud risk involving improper revenue recognition. Expect testing of cut-off, quarter-end deals, manual entries and unusual terms.
- PCAOB AS 2401.54 lists responses to revenue fraud risk, including confirming relevant contract terms and the absence of side agreements with customers (acceptance, delivery and payment terms, return rights, guaranteed resale amounts, cancellation or refund provisions) and inquiring of sales and marketing personnel about unusual terms near period end. The note to AS 2401.66 points to confirming terms of significant unusual transactions, including oral or undisclosed side agreements and bill-and-hold sales (AS 2310.30).
- An audit-ready file holds: the complete contract set including every amendment and side agreement; the five-step memo per material contract or contract type; the SSP study; constraint estimates with their data; principal or agent and bill-and-hold evidence; the policy elections list; the contract cost roll-forward; and disclosure tie-outs. Every side agreement found goes to the auditor; the file does not choose which terms the auditor sees.
- Management representation letters, auditor sign-off and control conclusions are made by management and the auditor, not by this skill. Control design and testing go to managing-sox-compliance; PBC list management to managing-audit-preparation.

## 7. Signals that a request aims at a target rather than at faithful depiction

The legitimate aim is revenue that depicts the transfer of goods or services in the amount the entity expects to be entitled to. Treat these as requests to structure to a target and apply the no-structuring ground rule and hard stop:
- choosing the period, gross or net presentation, SSP, constraint level, or performance obligation split because of the revenue, EBITDA, bonus, covenant, guidance or valuation result it produces;
- dating, re-signing or re-papering a contract, amendment or acceptance so that it appears to exist before period end when it did not;
- omitting a side agreement, email or oral promise from the analysis or from the file the auditor receives;
- drafting documentation after the fact to support a conclusion already booked, without the evidence that existed at the time;
- asking how to word a contract so revenue can be recognized earlier while the commercial deal stays the same.
Ordinary contract design for business reasons, explained neutrally (for example, explaining how a distinct, separately priced service would be accounted for), is not structuring.
