# ASC 606 Five-Step Rules and Special Arrangements

Rules for each step of the model and for the arrangements where errors cluster. ## 1. Framework, reporting entity and counterparty

- Name the reporting entity (legal entity, consolidated group, or segment) and its framework (US GAAP or IFRS). A subsidiary that reports under US GAAP but feeds an IFRS parent needs both answers where they differ.
- Determine public business entity (PBE) status before drafting disclosures; it decides which disclosure elections exist.
- Confirm the counterparty is a customer: a party that contracts to obtain goods or services that are an output of the entity's ordinary activities. Collaborators sharing risks (ASC 808), lessees (ASC 842) and insurance contracts (ASC 944) are outside ASC 606 for those elements.
- Identify every party on each side: the customer's affiliates, distributors and resellers, and the entity's sales agents and affiliates. Contracts with related parties of the customer are candidates for combination (606-10-25-9).

## 2. Step 1: contract existence, combination and side agreements

- Five criteria (606-10-25-1): approval and commitment; identifiable rights; identifiable payment terms; commercial substance; collection of substantially all of the consideration for the goods or services that will be transferred is probable. Probable means likely to occur under US GAAP (ASC 450 meaning).
- Collectibility looks at the consideration for goods or services the entity will transfer, after credit-risk mitigation such as advance payment or the right to stop transferring; repossession rights do not count (606-10-55-3A to 55-3C).
- No contract exists where each party can terminate a wholly unperformed contract without compensating the other (606-10-25-4). Consideration received before the criteria are met is a liability until an event in 606-10-25-7 occurs.
- Combine contracts entered into at or near the same time with the same customer or its related parties if negotiated as a package, if the price of one depends on the other, or if the goods or services form a single performance obligation (606-10-25-9).
- A contract can be written, oral or implied by customary business practice. A side letter, email or oral promise that the parties agreed and that is enforceable is part of the contract. Test enforceability: who made it, whether that person could bind the entity under its delegation of authority or apparent authority, and whether the customer accepted it. A promise not enforceable but creating a valid customer expectation of a price concession is still variable consideration (606-10-32-7).
- An unapproved side promise found later is written up as a source conflict and a control finding; it is never dropped from the analysis.

## 3. Contract modifications

A modification is an approved change in scope or price, including an unpriced change order the parties approved (606-10-25-10, 25-11). Decide in this order:

| Test | Condition | Accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Separate contract (606-10-25-12) | Adds distinct goods or services and the price rises by their standalone selling price, adjusted only for this contract's circumstances | New contract; the existing one is unchanged |
| Remaining goods or services distinct from those already transferred (606-10-25-13(a)) | Includes the remaining distinct days or units of a series accounted for as one performance obligation | Prospective: termination of the old contract and a new one; allocate unrecognized consideration plus the new consideration to the remaining items |
| Remaining goods or services not distinct; one partly satisfied obligation (606-10-25-13(b)) | Typical of a construction or customization obligation | Cumulative catch-up adjustment to revenue at the modification date |
| Mix of both (606-10-25-13(c)) | Some remaining items distinct, some not | Apply each treatment to its part, consistent with the objective |

Errors: calling a discounted add-on a separate contract; applying a catch-up to a series because it is "one performance obligation"; treating a claim or unpriced change order as nothing until the price is agreed instead of estimating it as variable consideration.

## 4. Step 2: performance obligations, series and material rights

- List explicit and implied promises (606-10-25-16, 25-18). Set-up activities that transfer nothing are not promises (606-10-25-17). Promises immaterial in the context of the contract need not be assessed, but a material right can never be dropped on that basis (606-10-25-16A, 25-16B).
- Distinct means capable of being distinct and separately identifiable (606-10-25-19 to 25-21). Not separately identifiable where the entity provides a significant integration service, one item significantly modifies or customizes another, or the items are highly interdependent.
- Series (606-10-25-14(b), 25-15): distinct goods or services that are substantially the same, each satisfied over time, with the same measure of progress, are one performance obligation. Daily access to software or a stand-ready service is the usual case. The series affects modification accounting and variable consideration allocation (606-10-32-39 to 32-41).
- Customer options that give a material right are performance obligations (606-10-55-41 to 55-45).
- Shipping and handling after control passes may be treated as fulfillment by US GAAP policy election, applied consistently and disclosed (606-10-25-18B).
- Bundling errors: splitting implementation that significantly customizes software from the software; bundling a hardware unit with optional installation that third parties also perform.

## 5. Step 3: transaction price

- **Variable consideration** (606-10-32-5 to 32-9): discounts, rebates, refunds, credits, price concessions, incentives, bonuses, penalties and rights of return. Estimate with expected value or most likely amount, whichever better predicts, applied consistently through the contract.
- **Constraint** (606-10-32-11, 32-12): include variable consideration only to the extent it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue will not occur. Weigh likelihood and magnitude, and name each factor present: susceptibility to outside factors, long resolution period, limited or non-predictive experience, a practice of broad concessions or changing terms, and a wide range of outcomes. Show the estimate before and after the constraint and update both every period (606-10-32-14).
- **Right of return** (606-10-55-22 to 55-29): revenue only for products not expected to be returned, a refund liability, and a separately presented asset for the right to recover products at former carrying amount less recovery costs. With no return history, the constraint may leave little or no revenue until the return period ends.
- **Significant financing component** (606-10-32-15 to 32-20): assess when payment timing gives either party a significant financing benefit, comparing the promised amount with the cash selling price and the time between transfer and payment. None exists for customer-discretion advance payments, substantially variable consideration outside both parties' control, or differences arising for reasons other than financing (606-10-32-17). Practical expedient: no adjustment if, at inception, the gap between transfer and payment is one year or less (606-10-32-18). Use the rate of a separate financing transaction at inception, never updated, and present interest separately from revenue (606-10-32-19, 32-20).
- **Noncash consideration** at fair value at contract inception (606-10-32-21 to 32-24). From the first annual period beginning after December 15, 2026, share-based noncash consideration from a customer stays under ASC 606 until the right to it is unconditional (ASU 2025-07).
- **Consideration payable to a customer** reduces revenue unless it pays for a distinct good or service at fair value (606-10-32-25, 32-26).

## 6. Step 4: allocation and standalone selling price

- Allocate on relative standalone selling price (SSP) at contract inception (606-10-32-28 to 32-31). A contract or list price may be SSP but is not presumed to be (606-10-32-32).
- Hierarchy in practice: observable standalone sales in similar circumstances to similar customers first; otherwise estimate with the adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or the residual approach. Residual is allowed only when the price is highly variable or not yet established (606-10-32-33, 32-34).
- SSP methods are applied consistently in similar circumstances and maximize observable inputs. Keep an SSP study with the data range used (for example, sales within a band around the median), its date and its owner. A change in SSP method is a change in estimate with disclosure; SSP chosen deal by deal to move revenue between periods is an error.
- Discounts are allocated proportionately unless all three criteria in 606-10-32-37 give observable evidence it belongs to specific obligations. Variable amounts go wholly to one obligation or distinct period only if both 606-10-32-40 criteria are met.

## 7. Step 5: over time or point in time

- Over time if any criterion in 606-10-25-27 is met: simultaneous receipt and consumption; the customer controls the asset as it is created; or no alternative use plus an enforceable right to payment for performance to date, assessed at inception (606-10-25-28, 25-29).
- Otherwise point in time, using the control indicators in 606-10-25-30: present right to payment, legal title, physical possession, risks and rewards, and acceptance. No single indicator decides.
- Measure progress with one method per obligation, remeasured each period (606-10-25-31 to 25-37). The right-to-invoice expedient applies only where the invoice amount corresponds directly to value transferred (606-10-55-18).

## 8. Principal versus agent

- Identify each specified good or service, then ask whether the entity controls it before transfer (606-10-55-36, 55-36A). Momentary legal title is not control (606-10-55-37).
- A principal controls an asset it transfers, a right to direct another party's service, or inputs it integrates into a combined output (606-10-55-37A). It reports gross; an agent reports its fee or commission net (606-10-55-37B, 55-38).
- Indicators of control (606-10-55-39): primary responsibility for fulfillment and acceptability; inventory risk before transfer or after transfer through a return right; discretion in establishing price. Price discretion alone is weak evidence, since agents can have it. The relevance of each depends on the facts (606-10-55-39A).
- The conclusion is made per specified good or service; an entity can be principal for one and agent for another in the same contract.
- Revenue size, growth optics, gross margin presentation or peer practice are not indicators. A memo citing them is written up as an error.

## 9. Bill-and-hold, consignment and customer acceptance

- **Bill-and-hold** (606-10-55-81 to 55-84): besides the general control indicators, all four criteria in 606-10-55-83 must be met: (a) the reason is substantive, for example requested by the customer; (b) the product is identified separately as the customer's; (c) the product is ready for physical transfer; (d) the entity cannot use the product or direct it to another customer. Then assess custodial services as a separate obligation (606-10-55-84). Evidence for (b) and (d) is segregation or tagging, system locks against reallocation, and no history of substituting held goods.
- **Consignment** (606-10-55-79, 55-80): no revenue on delivery to a dealer or distributor that has not obtained control. Indicators: the entity controls the product until a specified event such as resale or until a period expires; the entity can require return or transfer to another dealer; the dealer has no unconditional obligation to pay, apart from any deposit.
- **Customer acceptance** (606-10-55-85 to 55-88): a formality only if conformity with agreed specifications can be determined objectively; trial or evaluation goods transfer on acceptance or lapse of the trial period.
- **Repurchase agreements** (606-10-55-66 to 55-78): a forward or call option usually means the customer lacks control.

## 10. Licenses of intellectual property

- First decide whether the license is distinct (606-10-55-55, 55-56); a license that is not distinct is combined with other promises, and the nature of the license still informs whether the combined obligation is satisfied over time or at a point in time and how progress is measured (606-10-55-57).
- **Functional IP** (significant standalone functionality, such as software or a film) is a right to use, recognized at a point in time, unless the entity's activities that transfer nothing will substantively change the functionality and the customer must use the updated IP (606-10-55-59(a), 55-62).
- **Symbolic IP** (brands, logos, franchise rights) is a right to access, recognized over time (606-10-55-59(b), 55-60).
- Revenue for a right to use is not recognized before the customer can use and benefit from the license; for a renewal, not before the renewal period begins (606-10-55-58C).
- Sales-based or usage-based royalties for a license are recognized at the later of the sale or usage and the satisfaction of the obligation; this exception replaces the constraint for those royalties (606-10-55-65).
- Attributes of a license (time, geography, use) do not create additional obligations; promises of additional rights do (606-10-55-64).

## 11. Contracts acquired in a business combination

ASU 2021-08 requires an acquirer to measure acquired contract assets and contract liabilities under ASC 606 as if it had originated the contracts (805-20-30-27, 30-28), with optional practical expedients for prior modifications and for SSP at the acquisition date (805-20-30-29). Effective for PBEs for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2022, and for all other entities a year later. Acquired deferred revenue is not remeasured at fair value. Purchase price allocation itself goes to managing-business-combinations.
