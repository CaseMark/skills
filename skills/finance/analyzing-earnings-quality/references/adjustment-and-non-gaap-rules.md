# Adjustment and Non-GAAP Rules

## 1. Adjustment classification labels

Every adjustment between reported and adjusted earnings gets exactly one label. Use the label text verbatim.

| Label | Test | Treatment in adjusted earnings |
|---|---|---|
| Supported | Documented; amount ties to the ledger or an invoice, agreement or order; not part of normal operations; no similar item in the prior two years and not reasonably likely to recur within two years | Kept (an add-back or a deduction) |
| Recurring | A similar item occurred in either of the prior two years or is reasonably likely within the next two, at any interval; or it is a normal, recurring cash operating expense | Removed; shown in a memo column with its history |
| Pro forma only | Run-rate savings, synergies, full-period effect of a contract, price change, hire or closure after or late in the period, or any figure not yet in the ledger | Removed from historical adjusted earnings; shown in a separate pro forma column with any dis-synergies and the realization evidence |
| Tailored | Changes GAAP recognition or measurement (revenue as billed instead of as earned, gross instead of net, cash instead of accrual, rebates or returns ignored) | Reversed |
| Unsupported | No documentation, or the amount does not tie | Removed pending support; listed as an open item with what would cure it |

Windows used in these tests, each with its own trigger:

| Window | Trigger event | Length | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Look-back for a similar charge or gain | End of the fiscal period being adjusted, counting back | Two years | Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B) |
| Look-forward recurrence | End of the fiscal period being adjusted, counting forward, judged on plans known at the analysis date | Two years | Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B) |
| Contract cost expensing expedient | Date the cost is incurred; test the amortization period the asset would have | One year or less | ASC 340-40-25-4 |
| ASU 2025-06 mandatory adoption | First day of the first annual period beginning after Dec. 15, 2027; early adoption allowed at the start of an annual period | Not applicable | ASU 2025-06 |
| Article 11 synergies | Beginning of the fiscal year presented (as if the synergy existed then) | The pro forma period | S-X 11-02(a)(7) |

Item 10(e) does not state the anchor date for its two-year windows; using the end of the period being adjusted is a policy election to record. The two-year test borrows Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B)'s wording. For a private company it is an analytical convention, not a legal rule; say so when applying it. Downward adjustments (one-time gains, reserve releases, related-party margin above market) use the same labels.

## 2. Classification tests by adjustment type

| Adjustment | Usual label | What would change it |
|---|---|---|
| Restructuring, severance, "integration" costs | Recurring if charges appear in two or more of the last three years | A single program with a documented end date and no prior-year charges: Supported |
| Litigation settlement or legal costs | Supported if the matter is concluded and the company has no similar matters | Ongoing or repeated litigation: Recurring |
| Sale-process costs (seller advisory, legal, data room) | Supported | Recurring if the company runs repeated financing or sale processes |
| Owner or sponsor compensation and fees | Supported only to the excess over market compensation for the role, with a market comparison | Unsupported without the comparison |
| Run-rate cost savings, synergies, new-contract full-year effect | Pro forma only | Never moved into historical adjusted earnings |
| Bonus overruns, pricing rebates, customer credits, normal hiring, pre-opening costs for a business that opens sites every year | Recurring (rebates and credits reduce revenue under GAAP: Tailored if added back to revenue) | Not changed by labeling them one-time |
| Stock-based compensation | State the policy; common in non-GAAP measures but a real cost of operating | Present both with and without |
| One-time gain inside operating income | Supported deduction | Recurring if the company sells assets or settles claims routinely |
| Reserve release, change in estimate | Supported deduction, unless evidence shows the estimate was overstated in prior periods (then restate those periods in the analysis) | |
| Capitalization-rate increase (software, contract costs) | Not an adjustment; a sensitivity at the prior-period rate | A documented policy change or new standard: explain and show both |

## 3. Who the non-GAAP rules bind (scope)

- Regulation G applies to a "registrant": a company with a class of securities registered under Exchange Act Section 12 or required to report under Section 15(d), excluding registered investment companies (17 CFR 244.101(c)). It binds public disclosures by the registrant or a person acting on its behalf.
- Item 10(e) applies to non-GAAP measures in filings with the Commission (17 CFR 229.10(e)). For earnings releases furnished under Form 8-K Item 2.02, Instruction 2 applies Item 10(e)(1)(i) (prominence, reconciliation, usefulness statements), as quoted in the ADT order; the 10(e)(1)(ii) prohibitions apply when the measure is in a filing such as a Form 10-Q or 10-K.
- A private company's QoE report, CIM or lender presentation is not a Regulation G disclosure. "Regulation G compliant" in a private QoE is a description, not an assurance. The rules apply if a registrant later puts the figure in its own public disclosure or filing.
- Business combinations: Regulation G and Item 10(e) do not apply to non-GAAP measures in communications subject to Securities Act Rule 425, Exchange Act Rule 14a-12 or 14d-2(b)(2), or Item 1015 of Regulation M-A (244.100(d); 229.10(e)(6)). The exemption does not extend to the registration statement, proxy statement or tender offer statement (CFI 101.04). Forecasts given to a financial advisor and disclosed to meet Item 1015 or state law are outside the definition (CFI 101.01); the same applies to forecasts disclosed to the board (101.02) or exchanged between parties and disclosed for antifraud reasons (101.03).
- Foreign private issuers listed outside the United States have a conditional exemption (244.100(c)).

## 4. Regulation G (17 CFR 244.100-102)

- 100(a): present the most directly comparable GAAP measure and a reconciliation (quantitative for historical measures; for forward-looking measures, quantitative to the extent available without unreasonable efforts).
- 100(b): no non-GAAP measure that, with its accompanying information, contains an untrue statement of a material fact or omits a material fact necessary to make it not misleading.
- 101(a): a non-GAAP measure excludes amounts included in, or includes amounts excluded from, the most directly comparable GAAP measure; operating and statistical measures and ratios built only from GAAP figures are not non-GAAP measures.
- 102: compliance does not affect Rule 10b-5 liability.

## 5. Regulation S-K Item 10(e)

Requirements in filings (10(e)(1)(i)): (A) the most directly comparable GAAP measure with equal or greater prominence; (B) a quantitative reconciliation; (C) why management believes the measure is useful; (D) to the extent material, other purposes for which management uses it.

Prohibitions in filings (10(e)(1)(ii)): (A) excluding charges or liabilities that required or will require cash settlement from non-GAAP liquidity measures, other than EBIT and EBITDA; (B) adjusting a performance measure to eliminate or smooth items identified as non-recurring, infrequent or unusual when the item is reasonably likely to recur within two years or a similar item occurred within the prior two years; (C) non-GAAP measures on the face of the GAAP statements or in the notes; (D) on the face of Article 11 pro forma information; (E) titles the same as or confusingly similar to GAAP titles.

## 6. SEC staff CFIs used most

The Division of Corporation Finance renamed its Compliance and Disclosure Interpretations "Corporation Finance Interpretations" (CFIs). The non-GAAP page reads "Last Update: December 13, 2022".

| CFI | Holding (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 100.01 | A performance measure that excludes normal, recurring, cash operating expenses necessary to operate the business could be misleading; an expense that occurs repeatedly or occasionally, including at irregular intervals, is recurring |
| 100.02 | Adjusting a charge in one period but not similar charges in prior periods can mislead unless disclosed and explained; recasting may be needed |
| 100.03 | Adjusting only for non-recurring charges while leaving out non-recurring gains in the same period can mislead |
| 100.04 | Adjustments that change GAAP recognition and measurement are individually tailored and may mislead (accelerating ratably recognized revenue, gross versus net, cash basis) |
| 100.05 | Failing to label a measure as non-GAAP, or labels like "net revenue", "gross profit", or "pro forma" not calculated under Article 11, violate Rule 100(b) |
| 100.06 | Even extensive disclosure of each adjustment may not cure a misleading measure |
| 102.03 | The non-recurring prohibition turns on the description; a company may still adjust for a recurring item if it does not call it non-recurring, subject to 100.01 |
| 102.07 | Free cash flow (CFO less capex) is permitted with a clear definition; it must not imply residual discretionary cash and must not be per share |
| 102.09 | A covenant "Adjusted EBITDA" that would otherwise violate 10(e) may be required in MD&A when the covenant is material; disclose terms, the required level and effects of noncompliance |
| 102.10(a)-(c) | Prominence examples: non-GAAP before GAAP or in a headline without GAAP; bold or larger type; "record" or "exceptional" without equal GAAP characterization; reconciliation starting from the non-GAAP measure; a non-GAAP income statement |
| 102.11 | Tax effects shown as a separate adjustment, not net of tax |
| 103.01 | "Earnings" in EBIT and EBITDA means net income; other calculations must be titled differently (for example "Adjusted EBITDA") and lose the EBIT/EBITDA liquidity exception |
| 103.02 | EBIT or EBITDA as a performance measure reconciles to net income, not operating income, and is not presented per share |

## 7. Disclosure review labels

For a registrant's non-GAAP measure, label each finding with one of: **Noncompliant** (fails an express requirement or prohibition of Regulation G or Item 10(e) that applies to the document, as the staff applies it in the CFIs), **Staff-view risk** (a practice the CFIs say could be misleading under Rule 100(b), depending on facts), or **Acceptable with disclosure**. Name the document (furnished release, 10-Q, 10-K, proxy) because the applicable rules differ.

## 8. Pro forma and synergy adjustments (Article 11 by analogy)

For registrants, Regulation S-X 11-02(a)(7) lets management present synergies and dis-synergies only as "Management's Adjustments" in the explanatory notes, with a reasonable basis for each, limited to effects on the historical statements as if they existed from the start of the year, any expense reduction capped at the related expense historically incurred, all related dis-synergies presented, and the basis and material limitations disclosed. A measure labeled "pro forma" that is not prepared under Article 11 can mislead (CFI 100.05). For private targets, apply the same discipline: separate column, dis-synergies, cap at historical cost, realization evidence (signed contracts, executed headcount actions, vendor price letters).

## 9. QoE engagements: no dedicated standard

No AICPA or other U.S. professional standard specific to quality-of-earnings engagements was located on the check date. CPA firms usually perform them as consulting services under the AICPA Statement on Standards for Consulting Services (CS section 100), whose scope "is determined solely by agreement with the client" (CS 100.05, as quoted by The CPA Journal), or as agreed-upon procedures under AT-C section 215 when structured that way. A QoE report is therefore not an audit, review or examination; read its engagement letter for scope, procedures, the data relied on, who prepared it (buy-side or sell-side) and who may rely on it.

## 10. SEC enforcement examples (verified on sec.gov)

| Matter | Date | What the SEC found | Penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADT Inc. (Rel. 34-84956) | Dec. 26, 2018 | Earnings releases gave non-GAAP measures more prominence than GAAP measures (Item 10(e)(1)(i)(A) through Form 8-K Item 2.02) | $100,000 |
| Under Armour (press release 2021-78) | May 3, 2021 | Pulled forward $408 million of orders over six quarters without disclosing reliance on pull-forwards | $9 million |
| Kraft Heinz (2021-174) | Sept. 3, 2021 | Unearned supplier discounts and false contracts reduced cost of goods sold; inflated adjusted EBITDA; $208 million of cost savings restated | $62 million |
| Rollins (2022-64) | Apr. 18, 2022 | Unsupported reserve reductions to round EPS up to consensus (EPS Initiative) | $8 million |
| DXC Technology (2023-49) | Mar. 14, 2023 | Ordinary expenses misclassified as transaction, separation and integration costs and excluded from non-GAAP net income | $8 million |
| Newell Brands (2023-210) | Sept. 29, 2023 | Pulled sales forward and used non-GAAP-inconsistent accounting to report core sales growth; Regulation G Rule 100(b) | $12.5 million |
| Archer-Daniels-Midland (AP File 3-22588) | Jan. 27, 2026 | Retroactive intersegment rebates and price changes shifted operating profit into the Nutrition segment; "approximating market" disclosure false | $40 million |
| Diebold (2010-93) | June 2, 2010 | Bill-and-hold misuse, reserve manipulation, capitalized expenses to meet forecasts | $25 million |

Cite these as illustrations of the pattern, not as the standard for a different company's facts. Each company settled without admitting or denying the findings or charges.
