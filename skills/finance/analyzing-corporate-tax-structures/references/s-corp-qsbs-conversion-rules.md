# S Corporation, QSBS and Conversion Rules

Eligibility and status rules that decide whether a structure works at all: S corporation eligibility and the one-class-of-stock rule, termination and relief, built-in gains, qualified small business stock (QSBS) under IRC 1202 as amended by Pub. L. 119-21, and entity conversions. Each rule carries its citation and the date or tax year it applies to; checked on October 30, 2026 (see `primary-sources.md`). Status questions are answered before any savings are computed.

## 1. S corporation eligibility

A small business corporation is a domestic corporation, not an ineligible corporation, that does not (IRC 1361(b)(1)):

- have more than 100 shareholders (family members and their estates count as one, IRC 1361(c)(1));
- have a shareholder other than an individual, an estate, a trust described in IRC 1361(c)(2) (including grantor trusts, testamentary trusts for two years, voting trusts and electing small business trusts) or an exempt organization described in IRC 1361(c)(6);
- have a nonresident alien shareholder; or
- have more than one class of stock.

A partnership, an LLC taxed as a partnership, a corporation (other than as parent of a QSub) and a nonresident alien are not permitted shareholders. Ineligible corporations: banks using the reserve method, insurance companies taxed under subchapter L, DISCs (IRC 1361(b)(2)). Read the stock ledger and every transfer, not the shareholder list in a prior return.

## 2. One class of stock

- All outstanding shares must confer identical rights to distribution and liquidation proceeds; differences in voting rights are disregarded (IRC 1361(c)(4); Treas. Reg. 1.1361-1(l)(1)).
- The test is applied to the **governing provisions**: charter, articles, bylaws, applicable state law and binding agreements relating to distribution and liquidation proceeds. Commercial agreements (lease, employment, loan) are not governing provisions unless a principal purpose is to circumvent the rule (Treas. Reg. 1.1361-1(l)(2)(i)). A shareholders' agreement or side letter giving one holder a liquidation preference or a priority return is a governing provision that creates a second class, whoever holds the shares.
- **Disproportionate distributions:** if the governing provisions give identical rights, distributions that differ in timing or amount do not create a second class; they are given appropriate tax effect (for example IRC 7872 or recharacterization) (Treas. Reg. 1.1361-1(l)(2)(i) and (l)(2)(vi) Example 2). The IRS will not treat them as violating the rule in that case (Rev. Proc. 2022-19, sec. 3.02). Correct them with catch-up distributions and document that no binding agreement caused them.
- **State tax payments for some shareholders** (withholding, composite or PTE payments) are disregarded only if, counting the constructive distributions, rights stay identical (Treas. Reg. 1.1361-1(l)(2)(ii)).
- **Buy-sell and redemption agreements** are disregarded unless a principal purpose is circumvention and the price is significantly above or below fair market value; book value or a price between book and fair market value is not (Treas. Reg. 1.1361-1(l)(2)(iii)).
- **Straight debt** is not a second class: a written unconditional promise to pay a sum certain, interest not contingent on profits or discretion, not convertible, held by an individual (not a nonresident alien), an estate, a permitted trust or a person actively and regularly engaged in lending (IRC 1361(c)(5)).

## 3. Termination, relief and re-election

- An S election terminates whenever the corporation ceases to be a small business corporation, effective on and after the date of cessation (IRC 1362(d)(2)). Issuing shares to a partnership, or adopting a governing provision with non-identical rights, is such a cessation.
- Excess passive investment income for three consecutive years with accumulated C corporation E&P also terminates the election (IRC 1362(d)(3)).
- Inadvertent termination relief requires an IRS determination that the cause was inadvertent and timely corrective steps (IRC 1362(f)). A deliberate transaction that breaks eligibility is not inadvertent in ordinary usage; present relief as uncertain and for the tax adviser.
- Rev. Proc. 2022-19 sec. 3.06 gives retroactive corrective relief for non-identical governing provisions only if no disproportionate distribution has been made, Forms 1120-S were timely filed, and the statements are completed before the IRS discovers the provision.
- Re-election is barred for five taxable years absent consent (IRC 1362(g)).

## 4. Built-in gains and passive investment income

- A former C corporation (or an S corporation that takes C corporation assets with carryover basis) pays corporate-level tax at the highest IRC 11(b) rate on net recognized built-in gain during the recognition period (IRC 1374(a), (b)(1), (d)(8)).
- The recognition period is the five years beginning on the first day of the first taxable year for which the corporation is an S corporation (IRC 1374(d)(7)(A)), not the date Form 2553 was filed and not the date of a later stock sale.
- Net recognized built-in gain is limited to taxable income computed as if the corporation were a C corporation, with excess carried to the next year (IRC 1374(d)(2)); total recognition cannot exceed net unrealized built-in gain at conversion (IRC 1374(c)(2)). C-year NOLs offset it (IRC 1374(b)(2)). The tax passes through to shareholders as a loss (IRC 1366(f)(2)).
- Excess net passive income of an S corporation with accumulated E&P is taxed at 21 percent when passive investment income exceeds 25 percent of gross receipts (IRC 1375(a)).

## 5. QSBS: which regime applies

The regime turns on the date the taxpayer acquired the stock (Pub. L. 119-21, sec. 70431, enacted July 4, 2025, the "applicable date"; IRC 1202(a)(6)).

| Element | Acquired on or before July 4, 2025 | Acquired after July 4, 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Holding period | More than 5 years | At least 3 years |
| Exclusion | 50 percent, 75 percent (acquired after Feb. 17, 2009 through Sept. 27, 2010), 100 percent (acquired after Sept. 27, 2010) | 50 percent at 3 years, 75 percent at 4 years, 100 percent at 5 years or more (IRC 1202(a)(5)) |
| Per-issuer dollar limit | $10,000,000 less prior excluded gain from that issuer (IRC 1202(b)(4)(A)) | $15,000,000 less prior excluded gain and current-year gain on older stock of that issuer, indexed for taxable years beginning after 2026, and zero once used up (IRC 1202(b)(4)(B), (5)) |
| Alternative limit | 10 times aggregate adjusted basis of the issuer's QSBS disposed of in the year (IRC 1202(b)(1)(B)) | Same |
| Gross assets test | $50,000,000 for stock issued on or before July 4, 2025 | $75,000,000 for stock issued after July 4, 2025 (Pub. L. 119-21, sec. 70431(c)), indexed for taxable years beginning after 2026 (IRC 1202(d)(1)) |

- The new holding-period tiers apply by **acquisition date**; the $75,000,000 test applies by **issue date**. For original-issue stock these coincide; check both.
- Gain not excluded because of the percentage is "section 1202 gain" taxed as 28-percent rate gain (IRC 1(h)(4), (7)); its application to the new 50 and 75 percent tiers follows the statutory definition but no IRS guidance was located [VERIFY].
- The 7 percent AMT preference applies only to stock acquired on or before Sept. 27, 2010 (IRC 57(a)(7)).

## 6. QSBS: qualification tests

- Stock of a domestic C corporation, originally issued after Aug. 10, 1993, acquired at original issue for money, property (not stock) or services (IRC 1202(c)(1)). Stock issued while an S election was in effect is not QSBS, and revoking the election later does not change that.
- **Gross assets:** cash plus aggregate adjusted bases of other property, both at all times before the issuance and immediately after it, counting amounts received in the issuance; **contributed property is counted at fair market value** when contributed (IRC 1202(d)(1), (2)). Parent-subsidiary groups at more than 50 percent are aggregated (IRC 1202(d)(3)).
- **Active business:** at least 80 percent by value of assets used in qualified trades or businesses during substantially all of the holding period (IRC 1202(c)(2)(A), (e)(1)). Excluded: services in health, law, engineering, architecture, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services, brokerage, or any business whose principal asset is the reputation or skill of employees; banking, insurance, financing, leasing, investing; farming; production or extraction for which percentage depletion is allowable; hotels, motels, restaurants (IRC 1202(e)(3)). A pivot into an excluded field can disqualify stock already issued.
- **Stock exchanged for property:** treated as acquired on the date of the exchange, with basis not less than the property's fair market value (IRC 1202(i)(1)), so only post-exchange appreciation can qualify.
- **Transfers:** gifts, transfers at death and qualifying partnership distributions carry over the transferor's acquisition and holding period (IRC 1202(h)).
- **Rollover:** gain on QSBS held more than 6 months can be deferred by buying replacement QSBS within 60 days (IRC 1045(a)).
- Redemptions around issuance (IRC 1202(c)(3)) and reporting requirements (IRC 1202(d)(1)(C)) are checked against the cap table and the redemption history; not summarized here.

## 7. Conversions and elections that move between regimes

| Move | Mechanics | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| LLC taxed as a partnership to C corporation (formless conversion or check-the-box election) | Treated as the partnership contributing its assets for stock and distributing the stock to partners (Rev. Rul. 2004-59; Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3(g)(1)(i)) | QSBS holding period starts at conversion (IRC 1202(i)(1)(A)); pre-conversion appreciation cannot be excluded; gross assets after the issuance use the fair market value of contributed property, so a business worth more than the applicable limit fails at once; the corporation takes the assets' built-in gain into corporate solution. Nonrecognition and liabilities in excess of basis are for the tax adviser. |
| C corporation to S corporation | Election under IRC 1362 | Built-in gains tax for five years from the first S year; LIFO recapture and C-year E&P (IRC 1375 and 1362(d)(3)) |
| S corporation to C corporation (revocation or termination) | IRC 1362(d) | Existing stock never becomes QSBS; 21 percent plus the dividend layer; five-year re-election bar |
| Corporation to LLC or partnership | Liquidation of the corporation | Corporate-level gain on appreciated assets (IRC 336) and shareholder-level gain; route the transaction to analyzing-m-and-a-tax-implications |

## 8. Status checklist

1. Shareholder list from the stock ledger and transfer records, each holder's type and residence.
2. Every governing provision read for distribution and liquidation rights; side letters and investor rights agreements included.
3. Distribution history compared with ownership percentages by year; catch-ups documented.
4. S election effective date and first S year; recognition-period end date; NUBIG computation.
5. For each QSBS block: issue date, acquisition date, consideration, gross assets before and immediately after issuance, active-business evidence, redemptions, prior excluded gain by issuer.
6. Each conversion's effective date, method and the fair market value at that date.
