## 1. GloBE scope and top-up mechanics

- Scope: MNE groups with consolidated financial statement revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the four fiscal years immediately preceding the tested year (Model Rules Article 1.1.1, per the OECD fact sheets).
- Minimum rate 15%, computed by jurisdiction: ETR = adjusted covered taxes / net GloBE income (Article 5.1).
- Top-up tax percentage = 15% minus jurisdictional ETR (Article 5.2.1). Excess profit = net GloBE income minus the substance-based income exclusion (payroll and tangible asset carve-out, Article 5.3). Jurisdictional top-up tax = top-up percentage times excess profit, plus additional current top-up tax, minus any qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (Article 5.2.3).
- Charging rules in order: a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT) in the low-taxed jurisdiction itself; then the income inclusion rule (IIR) at the parent; then the undertaxed profits rule (UTPR) on other group members.
- De minimis exclusion (Article 5.5): average GloBE revenue under EUR 10 million and average GloBE income under EUR 1 million in a jurisdiction, on a three-year basis.
- Safe harbours agreed January 5, 2026: Simplified ETR Safe Harbour (available from 2027, or 2026 in some circumstances); Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour extended to fiscal years beginning on or before December 31, 2027 but not including a year ending after June 30, 2029, with a 17% transition rate for 2026 and 2027; Substance-based Tax Incentive Safe Harbour; Side-by-Side and UPE Safe Harbours. Whether a given jurisdiction's domestic law has adopted each one is [VERIFY] per jurisdiction; the European Commission notice C/2026/253 (January 12, 2026) confirms all EU Member States consented to them under Article 32 of Directive (EU) 2022/2523.

## 2. The side-by-side arrangement: what it does and does not do

- History: G7 statement of June 28, 2025 (side-by-side system to fully exclude U.S.-parented groups from the IIR and UTPR, conditioned on removal of proposed section 899, which Pub. L. 119-21 did not enact); Inclusive Framework agreement of January 5, 2026 (147 members) on the Side-by-Side Package; Treasury statement of January 5, 2026.
- The United States is listed on the OECD Central Record as having a Qualified SbS Regime (Internal Revenue Code of 1986), with the SbS Safe Harbour applicable for fiscal years commencing on or after January 1, 2026 (Qualified SbS Regimes table as at January 5, 2026; the QDMTT and IIR tables are as at May 1, 2026).
- Effect: an MNE group whose ultimate parent entity is in a Qualified SbS jurisdiction may **elect** the SbS Safe Harbour, made in Section 1 of the GloBE Information Return. Where it applies, IIR and UTPR top-up tax is deemed zero for all constituent entities, domestic and foreign, including IIRs of intermediate parents.
- Limits: the election is not automatic; it does not reach fiscal years commencing before January 1, 2026; a jurisdiction that adopts it after that date is expected to do so retroactively, but one that cannot on constitutional grounds implements it from the earliest practical date; and **every group, including one that elects it, remains subject to QDMTTs in every QDMTT jurisdiction**, computed without push-down of CFC or branch taxes (Side-by-Side Package, chapter 1 paragraphs 15, 19 and 23, and chapter 5 paragraph 28 on the effective date).
- The UPE Safe Harbour covers only domestic profits of groups headquartered in a jurisdiction with a qualified domestic regime; it switches off the UTPR for profits in the UPE jurisdiction.
- An Inclusive Framework stocktake, to be concluded by 2029, may lead to changes. Re-check the Central Record and local adoption for each fiscal year.

## 3. Pillar Two analysis steps for a U.S.-parented group

1. Confirm scope (EUR 750 million test) and the fiscal years at issue.
2. Identify every jurisdiction with a QDMTT and its status on the Central Record; read the local law or mark it [VERIFY].
3. For each low-taxed jurisdiction: GloBE income, adjusted covered taxes, ETR, SBIE, top-up percentage and excess profit; test each available safe harbour (TCSH, simplified ETR, de minimis) under local adoption.
4. Compute QDMTT exposure; it survives the SbS election.
5. For fiscal years commencing on or after January 1, 2026: record whether the SbS election is made and where Section 1 of the GIR is filed; IIR and UTPR exposure is zero only with the election in a jurisdiction that recognizes it.
6. For fiscal years commencing before January 1, 2026: compute IIR and UTPR exposure under each jurisdiction's law; the SbS arrangement does not apply.
7. Transfer pricing adjustments: GloBE Model Rules Article 3.2.3 requires transactions between constituent entities in different jurisdictions to be recorded at the same arm's-length amount in both entities' accounts (as cited by the sibling skill managing-transfer-pricing-compliance), so a year-end true-up or audit adjustment changes GloBE income and the ETR in two jurisdictions; recompute both and note any QDMTT effect. The adjustment amount itself comes from managing-transfer-pricing-compliance.
8. U.S. interplay: NCTI on the same income, the creditability of a QDMTT under U.S. rules ([VERIFY] with the tax adviser), and ASC 740 accounting for NCTI and Pillar Two top-up taxes (route to managing-tax-provision-preparation).

## 4. Treaty analysis steps

1. Identify the payee that is the beneficial owner and its residence under the treaty's residence article; a certificate of residence proves residence only.
2. Read the specific treaty, protocols and Treasury technical explanation. Mark rates and articles [VERIFY] until read.
3. Test the LOB article item by item (Section 5). Record which test is met and the evidence, or that none is.
4. Apply the benefit article (dividends, interest, royalties, business profits) and its own conditions (ownership threshold, holding period, special tax regime or expatriated entity rules).
5. If no treaty benefit is available, apply the statutory rate (30% under 881 and 1442 for a foreign corporation) and any statutory exemption (portfolio interest, 871(i) deposits).
6. Name the withholding agent and its liability; route documentation and reporting to managing-international-withholding-tax.

## 5. Limitation on benefits under the 2016 U.S. Model (Article 22)

A resident gets treaty benefits only if it is a qualified person when the benefit would be accorded, or meets an item-by-item or discretionary test:

- Qualified persons (para. 2): individuals; governments; publicly traded companies (principal class regularly traded, and primarily traded in the residence state or primary place of management and control there); subsidiaries at least 50% owned by five or fewer publicly traded companies, with a base erosion test for non-dividend benefits; pension funds meeting residence-of-beneficiaries tests; and other persons meeting the ownership and base erosion test (at least 50% of vote and value owned by qualified residents on at least half the days of a 12-month period, and less than 50% of gross income paid in deductible payments to non-qualified persons or connected persons in special tax regimes).
- Active trade or business (para. 3): benefits for income emanating from, or incidental to, an active trade or business in the residence state. Operating as a holding company, group supervision or administration, group financing (including cash pooling) and making or managing investments do not count, except for banks, insurers and registered securities dealers in the ordinary course. Where income comes from a connected person, the residence-state business must be substantial relative to the U.S. activity.
- Derivative benefits (para. 4): at least 95% of vote and value owned by seven or fewer equivalent beneficiaries, plus a base erosion test. An equivalent beneficiary resident in a third state must be entitled to all the benefits of a comprehensive income tax treaty between that state and the United States under substantially similar LOB tests, and to a rate no higher than the one sought; a resident of a state with no U.S. treaty does not qualify under that branch.
- Headquarters company (para. 5): dividends and interest from group members only, with tests including a group active in at least four countries each generating at least 10% of group gross income.
- Competent authority (para. 6): discretionary grant on request; never assume it.

Many U.S. treaties in force follow earlier models with different LOB tests, and some older treaties have no LOB article. Read the treaty actually in force.

## 6. Treaty rates in the 2016 U.S. Model

- Dividends (Article 10): 5% where the beneficial owner is a company that has owned directly at least 10% of the payor's vote and value for the 12-month period ending on the date entitlement is determined; 15% otherwise; 0% for qualifying pension funds; special rules for RICs, REITs and expatriated entities.
- Interest (Article 11) and royalties (Article 12): generally exempt at source, subject to special tax regime, notional deduction and contingent interest exceptions.
- Actual treaties differ; every treaty rate in a deliverable carries the treaty name, article and protocol, or [VERIFY].

## 7. State conformity

States decide separately whether and how NCTI, subpart F income, the section 78 gross-up, the section 250 deduction and section 245A apply, often through a fixed or rolling IRC conformity date, a dividends-received deduction or an apportionment factor adjustment. This skill does not determine state treatment. List each filing state as an open item and route it to analyzing-state-income-tax with the federal amounts and years.

## 8. Pillar Two and treaty traps checklist

- SbS election treated as removing QDMTT exposure, or treated as automatic.
- Fiscal years beginning before January 1, 2026 analyzed as if the SbS Safe Harbour applied.
- Local adoption of a safe harbour assumed rather than checked.
- Covered taxes taken from U.S. NCTI calculations instead of the GloBE base.
- Residence certificate or Form W-8BEN-E treated as proof of LOB qualification.
- Holding or financing company treated as an active trade or business.
- Owners in a non-treaty state counted as equivalent beneficiaries.
- Portfolio interest claimed by a 10-percent shareholder.
