## 1. Penalty thresholds

| Penalty | Trigger | Rate | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transactional, substantial | Price claimed is 200 percent or more, or 50 percent or less, of the correct section 482 price | 20 percent of the underpayment attributable | IRC 6662(e)(1)(B)(i) |
| Transactional, gross | 400 percent or more, or 25 percent or less | 40 percent | IRC 6662(h)(2)(A)(ii) |
| Net adjustment, substantial | Net section 482 transfer price adjustment exceeds the lesser of $5,000,000 or 10 percent of gross receipts | 20 percent | IRC 6662(e)(1)(B)(ii) |
| Net adjustment, gross | Exceeds the lesser of $20,000,000 or 20 percent of gross receipts | 40 percent | IRC 6662(h)(2)(A)(iii) |

- No substantial valuation penalty unless the underpayment attributable exceeds $5,000 ($10,000 for a corporation other than an S corporation or a personal holding company), IRC 6662(e)(2).
- Net adjustment = net increase in taxable income from section 482 price adjustments, before carryovers (IRC 6662(e)(3)(A)). Amounts meeting the documentation tests in 6662(e)(3)(B) are excluded when testing the threshold; foreign-to-foreign transactions that do not affect U.S.-source or effectively connected income are excluded.
- IRC 6662(e)(3)(D): no reasonable cause defense under IRC 6664(c) for the net adjustment penalty unless the documentation requirements are met. For the transactional penalty, meeting 1.6662-6(d) establishes reasonable cause (1.6662-6(b)(3)).
- Arithmetic to show: adjustment, gross receipts, each threshold as "lesser of", which tier is met, the underpayment attributable (adjustment times the rate the user supplies, after any offsets the user supplies), and the penalty at 20 or 40 percent. Interest and state effects are outside this computation.

## 2. Documentation that excludes an adjustment (1.6662-6(d))

- Specified method requirement, (d)(2)(ii): the taxpayer selected and applied a specified method reasonably, having made a reasonable effort to evaluate the other specified methods under the best method rule. Factors include experience, a reasonably thorough data search, use of the most current reliable data available before year-end, following the regulations, reasonable reliance on a qualified professional (employee or not) who received all relevant information, avoiding an arbitrary extreme point in the range, reliance on a prior APA or IRS-approved method with unchanged facts, and the size of the adjustment relative to the transaction.
- Services cost method, (d)(2)(ii)(B): reasonable only if costs were allocated under 1.482-9(k) and the taxpayer reasonably concluded the 1.482-9(b)(2) conditions were met.
- Unspecified method, (d)(3): reasonable conclusion that no specified method was likely to clearly reflect income, plus the same documentation.
- The IRS FAQ on documentation best practices states that 6662(e) documentation does not protect automatically; adequacy and reasonableness are assessed, and inaccurate inputs, an inadequate search, failure to follow the best method rule, or manipulated comparable sets are signs of inadequacy.

## 3. Contemporaneous: the filing-date test

- 1.6662-6(d)(2)(iii)(A) and IRC 6662(e)(3)(B)(i)(II): documentation must be in existence when the return is filed. The only exceptions are principal documents (9) (data obtained after year-end and before filing) and (10) (the index).
- A study finished after the filing date cannot satisfy the requirement for that year, however good it is. It may still be produced as substantive support for the pricing, labeled with its true completion date.
- The district director may excuse a minor or inadvertent failure to provide required documents only if the taxpayer made a good faith effort and promptly remedies the failure. This does not convert a post-filing study into a contemporaneous one.
- Record, for each taxable year: return filing date (with extension), date each principal document was finalized, and the evidence of that date (file metadata, engagement letter, transmittal). Dates are facts; never estimate them.

## 4. The ten principal documents (1.6662-6(d)(2)(iii)(B))

1. Business overview, including economic and legal factors affecting pricing.
2. Organizational structure with an organization chart covering all related parties whose transactions are potentially relevant, including foreign affiliates affecting U.S. pricing.
3. Any documentation explicitly required by the section 482 regulations (for example the services cost method statement, CSA documentation).
4. The method selected and why, including whether its regulatory conditions were met.
5. Alternative methods considered and why not selected.
6. The controlled transactions (including terms of sale) and internal data; for profit splits, the income, cost and asset schedule; for cost-based methods, how costs were determined, allocated and apportioned.
7. The comparables used, how comparability was evaluated, and adjustments made.
8. The economic analysis and projections relied on.
9. Relevant data obtained after year-end and before filing.
10. A general index of principal and background documents and a description of the recordkeeping used to catalog and access them.

Background documents (1.6662-6(d)(2)(iii)(C)) need not be produced with the principal documents; if later requested, they are due within 30 days unless extended. CSA documentation under 1.482-7(k)(2) satisfies the list except items 2 and 10 for CSTs and PCTs (1.6662-6(d)(2)(iii)(D)).

## 5. Production within 30 days

- Principal documents must be provided within 30 days of an IRS request in connection with the examination of the year (1.6662-6(d)(2)(iii)(A); IRC 6662(e)(3)(B)(i)(III)). Compute the date from the request date the user supplies and state it; flag if the request date is unknown.
- Production is a deadline, not a drafting window: anything created after the return was filed is labeled with its creation date.

## 6. Country-by-country reporting (Form 8975)

- 1.6038-4(a): every ultimate parent entity of a U.S. MNE group files Form 8975, Country-by-Country Report. A U.S. MNE group's ultimate parent is a U.S. business entity that consolidates at least one foreign entity under U.S. GAAP and is not itself consolidated by another entity (1.6038-4(b)(1)). No filing is required for a reporting period if the group's annual revenue for the immediately preceding reporting period was less than $850,000,000 (1.6038-4(h)). The test uses the preceding period, not the current one.
- Filed with the ultimate parent's income tax return, by its due date including extensions (1.6038-4(f)).
- The filer is the ultimate parent. A U.S. subsidiary of a foreign-parented group is not an ultimate parent entity under 1.6038-4(b)(1); whether a foreign jurisdiction requires it to file or notify locally is [VERIFY].
- The ultimate parent need not create records reconciling Form 8975 to its tax return or financial statements (1.6038-4(g)), but inconsistencies between the CbC report, master file and local files are what tax authorities risk-assess (OECD paras. 5.24 and 5.25). Run check R9.

## 7. Master file and local file (OECD Chapter V)

- Master file (para. 5.18 onward): group overview, business, intangibles, intercompany financial activities, financial and tax positions, overall transfer pricing policies.
- Local file: local entity, controlled transactions, comparability analysis, method, financial information reconciled to the statutory accounts.
- The U.S. has no separate master file or local file filing requirement; U.S. penalty protection rests on section 4 of this file. Foreign content, thresholds and deadlines [VERIFY].

## 8. Related reporting and collateral effects

- IRC 6038A(d): $25,000 for each taxable year for a reporting corporation's failure to furnish Form 5472 information or maintain records, plus $25,000 for each 30-day period a failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notice.
- IRC 1059A: basis or inventory cost of property imported from a related person may not exceed the costs taken into account in its customs value. An upward true-up on imported goods therefore needs a matching customs value treatment, or the extra cost is not recoverable for tax.
- Customs: CBP's Reconciliation program lets an importer flag entries and finalize value later; CBP's page states a 21-month window from the earliest entry summary date for value issues [VERIFY current rules]. Prior disclosure, VAT or GST and withholding on true-ups route to managing-indirect-tax-analysis.
- Financial statements: a true-up or exposure reserve affects the income tax provision; route to managing-income-tax-provisions.
