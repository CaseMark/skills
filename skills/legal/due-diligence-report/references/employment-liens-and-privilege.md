## 1. Timed elements and their triggers

Each timed element runs from its own trigger. Never use one anchor for all of them.

| Element | Trigger event | Period | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| WARN notice for a group of terminations | That group's first individual termination date | At least 60 calendar days before it; each later group gets its own full 60 days | 29 U.S.C. 2102(a); 20 C.F.R. 639.5(a)(1) |
| WARN single-event test | Any employment loss at the site | Any 30-day period (look 30 days ahead and behind) | 29 U.S.C. 2101(a)(2)-(3); 20 C.F.R. 639.5(a)(1)(i) |
| WARN aggregation | Any employment loss at the site | Any 90-day period (look 90 days ahead and behind) | 29 U.S.C. 2102(d); 20 C.F.R. 639.5(a)(1)(ii) |
| WARN headcount snapshot | Date the first notice is required | Measured on that date | 20 C.F.R. 639.5(a)(2) |
| WARN part-time status | Date notice is required | Average hours over the shorter of actual employment or the most recent 90 days; months employed in the preceding 12 | 29 U.S.C. 2101(a)(8); 20 C.F.R. 639.3(h) |
| Seller or buyer notice duty | Effective date (time) of the sale | Seller up to and including it; buyer after it | 29 U.S.C. 2101(b)(1); 20 C.F.R. 639.4(c) |
| UCC financing statement lapse | Date of filing of that financing statement | Five years, unless continued | UCC 9-515(a), (c) |
| UCC continuation window | Expiration date of that financing statement | Only within the six months before it | UCC 9-515(d) |
| Patent assignment recording protection | Date of that assignment | Three months, or before the later purchase | 35 U.S.C. 261 |
| HSR waiting period | Receipt of both parties' completed notifications | 30 days (15 for a cash tender offer), subject to extension | 15 U.S.C. 18a(b)(1) |

## 2. WARN Act screen for planned reductions

Run this whenever the buyer plans reductions, the seller has made them in the last 90 days, or a site will close.

1. **Employer.** 100 or more employees excluding part-time employees, or 100 or more employees working at least 4,000 hours per week in the aggregate excluding overtime (29 U.S.C. 2101(a)(1)). After a sale, seller employees other than part-time employees become employees of the buyer (2101(b)(1)).
2. **Part-time employees.** An average of fewer than 20 hours per week, or fewer than 6 of the 12 months preceding the date notice is required (2101(a)(8)). Exclude them from both the numerator and the 33 percent denominator.
3. **Employment loss.** Termination other than discharge for cause, voluntary departure or retirement; a layoff exceeding 6 months; or a reduction in hours of more than 50 percent in each month of any 6-month period (2101(a)(6)). Transfers offered under 2101(b)(2) may remove a loss.
4. **Plant closing.** Shutdown of a single site, or of facilities or operating units within it, causing employment loss for 50 or more full-time employees in any 30-day period (2101(a)(2)).
5. **Mass layoff.** Not a plant closing, and employment loss in any 30-day period for at least 33 percent of the full-time employees at the site and at least 50 of them, or at least 500 (2101(a)(3)).
6. **Aggregation.** Groups at one site, each below the minimum, that together exceed it within any 90-day period are a plant closing or mass layoff unless the employer shows separate and distinct actions and causes, not an attempt to evade (2102(d)). Show the arithmetic for every 30-day and 90-day window.
7. **Who gives notice.** The seller for events up to and including the effective date of the sale; the buyer after. A seller aware of the buyer's definite plans may give notice as the buyer's agent if empowered, but the duty stays with the buyer (20 C.F.R. 639.4(c)(1)). In a stock purchase or reverse triangular merger the target stays the legal employer after closing, and the buyer parent may also be treated as the employer under the independence factors of 20 C.F.R. 639.3(a)(2). When a post-closing event needs notice before closing, the purchase agreement should allocate who sends it.
8. **Recipients.** Each affected employee's representative, or each affected employee if none; the state dislocated worker unit; and the chief elected official of the local government unit (2102(a)).
9. **Exposure.** Back pay and benefits for each day of violation up to 60 days, capped at one-half the days employed, less offsets; a civil penalty up to $500 per day for the local government notice, avoided if employees are paid within three weeks (2104(a)). Whether "day of violation" means calendar or work days is unsettled `[VERIFY]`.
10. **State law.** State plant-closing statutes can apply lower thresholds or longer notice `[VERIFY]` for the state of each site.

## 3. Worker classification and benefits

- List every individual paid as a contractor, consultant or through an agency who works full-time, long-term or in the target's core business. These are the misclassification candidates and also the IP assignment gaps of `references/contract-and-ip-review.md`, section 7.
- California applies an ABC test (Cal. Lab. Code 2775(b)(1)): control, work outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business, and an independently established trade. Statutory exceptions apply `[VERIFY]`. The federal FLSA standard and its current enforcement position are `[VERIFY]`.
- Report exposure categories (overtime, benefits eligibility, tax withholding, penalties) with the facts that drive them; do not compute penalties without the governing statute.
- Benefit plans: obtain plan documents, Form 5500s and determination letters; flag multiemployer plan participation and controlled-group exposure for benefits counsel. Tax consequences go to analyzing-m-and-a-tax-implications.

## 4. Lien, UCC and judgment searches against the debt schedule

1. **Where to search.** Perfection by filing is governed by the law of the debtor's location (UCC 9-301(1)). A registered organization organized under state law is located in its state of organization (9-307(e); Delaware enactment confirmed), whatever its headquarters. Search the central filing office of that state under the exact name on the public organic record (9-503(a)(1)). Search the headquarters state and counties for fixture filings, real property records, judgment liens and tax liens.
2. **Name.** A filing under a wrong name is seriously misleading unless a search under the correct name with the filing office's standard search logic would find it (9-506(b)-(c)). Search the exact name and former names.
3. **Lapse.** A financing statement lapses five years after filing unless continued within the six months before lapse (9-515(a), (c), (d)). Lapse ends perfection; it does not prove the debt was repaid or the security interest released. Report lapsed filings as unperfected, and request payoff evidence or a release.
4. **Reconcile** every filing to the debt schedule. Filings with no matching schedule entry (merchant cash advance purchasers, equipment lessors and financiers, factoring, vendor liens) are findings. Whether an arrangement is a lease or a security interest, or a sale of receivables or a loan, is a judgment call `[VERIFY]`.
5. **Closing deliverables.** Payoff letters, UCC-3 terminations, IP security interest releases and lien releases for each item to be removed; hand the list to disclosure-schedules and merger-agreement.

## 5. Litigation and regulatory exposure

- Compare the seller's litigation list with court docket searches in each jurisdiction where the target operates, demand letters, regulatory correspondence, board minutes, insurance claim notices and auditor legal letters.
- Threatened claims in correspondence and pending regulatory inquiries are findings even if no suit is filed.
- Consent orders, settlement agreements and corporate integrity obligations with continuing terms bind the target after closing; record their duration and reporting duties.
- Exposure is stated as the source states it (demand amount, reserve, insurer position). Never invent a probability or a dollar range.

## 6. Privilege, common interest and clean teams

- **Privilege in a merger.** Under Delaware law, absent an express contractual carve-out, privilege over all pre-merger communications, including those about negotiating the merger, passes to the surviving corporation (Great Hill, 80 A.3d 155 (Del. Ch. 2013), applying 8 Del. C. 259). Flag whether the agreement has a carve-out; the treatment in an asset deal or under other law is `[VERIFY]`.
- **Sharing privileged material in diligence.** In New York, the common interest exception requires pending or reasonably anticipated litigation to which the communication relates (Ambac, 27 N.Y.3d 616 (2016)). A transaction-only common interest may not protect privileged material shared with the other side. Other states' rules `[VERIFY]`. Recommend that privileged legal memos be summarized rather than produced, and record any that were produced.
- **Competitively sensitive information.** Current and future prices, customer-specific terms, costs and strategic plans exchanged between competitors before closing can create antitrust risk; the FTC staff recommends tailoring, clean teams excluding personnel responsible for pricing or strategy, and stopping problematic exchanges (FTC Bureau of Competition guidance, March 20, 2018). Information sharing that gives the buyer beneficial ownership or control before the HSR waiting period ends may be gun-jumping (15 U.S.C. 18a; Sherman Act section 1, 15 U.S.C. 1). Route any such issue to hsr-filing.
- **Report versions.** Where findings rely on clean-team material, prepare a clean-team version and a general version that summarizes without customer-level or price-level detail.
