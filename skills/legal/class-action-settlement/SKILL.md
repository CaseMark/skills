# Class Action Settlement Agreement and Approval Review

Drafts a federal class action settlement agreement from a term sheet, or reviews an existing agreement and its approval papers, so that the court can find the proposal fair, reasonable and adequate under Rule 23(e) and the class receives what the papers say it receives. The deliverable is an approval-readiness determination, ranked blocking issues, scope, value and clocks reconciliation tables, draft or redlined agreement articles, approval-paper inserts with a Rule 23(e)(3) agreements statement, a CAFA notice package, and hand-offs. Everything it produces is a draft for review and signature by a licensed attorney.

## Why This Skill Exists

A class settlement binds people who never saw the negotiation, so the court acts as their fiduciary and objectors test every term. The 2018 amendments to Rule 23(e) moved the hard look to the preliminary approval stage: the parties must show the court will likely approve the deal and certify the class, with the claims process, expected claims rate, fee terms and side agreements already on the table. Settlements fail on mechanical points: a class or release wider than the case, a value stated at the gross fund when a claims-made reversionary structure will pay a fraction of it, a fee measured against the wrong number, an incentive award the circuit forbids, an objection deadline set before the fee motion, or CAFA notice counted from the wrong day. This skill finds those before the papers are signed or filed.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Operative complaint, certification order, term sheet class definition | Who is in the settlement class and bound by the release | Settlement class broader than the litigated class (nationwide instead of one state, unpleaded products, longer class period) with no explanation; no named representative bought the added products; "certified for settlement" treated as relaxing Rule 23(a) and (b) |
| Released Claims and Released Parties definitions; list of other pending actions | Scope of what absent members give up, and who gets the benefit | Release of claims "relating in any way" to the defendant's products or practices beyond the identical factual predicate; privacy or TCPA claims swept into a pricing case; "all other persons" released; a parallel action's claims released without disclosure; a (b)(2) no-opt-out class releasing damages |
| Notice plan, administrator bid, claims-rate estimate, the motion's value statement | Showing adequacy of relief under 23(e)(2)(C) | Gross or face value presented as the class benefit; claims rate unsourced or omitted; notice and administration counted as benefit; no low and high case |
| Fee, expense and service-award provisions | Rule 23(h) award and the 23(e)(2)(C)(iii) factor | Fee measured only against the gross fund; clear-sailing clause and kicker (fee reductions revert to defendant) not flagged; fee motion due after objections close; service awards drafted in the Eleventh Circuit, where Johnson v. NPAS bars them |
| Voucher, credit, access-code or discount relief | Valuing in-kind relief and computing fees | Store-only credits not analyzed as coupons; fee computed on face value instead of redeemed value under 1712(a); no 1712(e) hearing and written finding |
| Residual and unclaimed-fund clause | What happens to money no one claims | Reversion to defendant with no justification; cy pres before redistribution is infeasible; recipient tied to a party or counsel; unused fee or award money returning to defendant |
| Allocation plan and representative roster | Equitable treatment under 23(e)(2)(D) | Groups with strong and weak claims paid equally under one representative and one counsel; no subclass or separate-counsel analysis |
| Proposed preliminary approval order and schedule | Every dated or counted deadline | All deadlines keyed to the preliminary approval order; opt-out and objection windows shrinking when notice is late; objection deadline before the fee motion; hearing date computed onto a weekend or holiday |
| CAFA notice package and proof of service | 1715 notice to federal and state officials | 10 days counted from preliminary approval instead of filing; final approval hearing inside the 90 days after the later service; wrong state official; notice skipped in a removed case |
| Side letters, supplemental opt-out termination agreement, objector deals | Rule 23(e)(3) statement and 23(e)(5)(B) approval | Blow provision or fee-sharing agreement left out of the statement; payment to an objector or objector's counsel to drop an objection or appeal without court approval |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is signed, filed or served by this skill. Every output is a draft for attorney review and is never described as ready to file.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (for example, the number of class members with email addresses), *source conflict* (the term sheet and the draft agreement state different class periods), *unverified external lookup* (a circuit rule or local rule not confirmed), or *policy election* (a choice for counsel, such as claims-made versus automatic payment).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For facts: court orders (certification, preliminary approval, scheduling) govern over the agreement's own formulas; the executed agreement governs over a signed term sheet, which governs over a mediator's proposal, emails and summaries; the administrator's data governs over counsel's estimates. For law: the Rule 23 text and CAFA statutes, then the Supreme Court, then the controlling circuit, then local rules, standing orders and the judge's procedures, then other circuits and commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation tables and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every figure, date, class count and identifier carries a source locator (document and page or section) or is marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or is marked `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a case, rule, local rule or pin cite.
5. **Forum and circuit first.** Identify the court, the circuit and whether the class is certified before analyzing terms. Rule 23 and 28 U.S.C. 1711-1715 apply only in federal court; a state-court settlement follows the state class rule `[VERIFY]`.
6. **Scope matches the case.** The settlement class, class period, released claims and released parties are compared element by element with the complaint and any certification order. Every expansion is stated with its justification or narrowed. The release reaches only claims arising from the identical factual predicate and adequately represented; released parties are named.
7. **Value as the court will measure it.** Reconcile gross fund to net fund to expected class payout with a sourced claims rate, showing low, expected and high cases and where the residue goes. Never state a gross fund, face value or "available" amount as the benefit to the class when a claims process, cap, coupon or reversion means less will be paid. Report the fee against the gross fund and against fee plus expected class payout.
8. **Fees and awards disclosed and tested.** Flag clear-sailing and kicker clauses and draft fee reductions to stay with the class. Apply 28 U.S.C. 1712 to coupon relief. Draft service awards only where the controlling circuit permits them; in the Eleventh Circuit, Johnson v. NPAS bars awards that pay a representative for time or for bringing suit.
9. **Every clock has its own trigger.** Each dated or counted deadline gets its own trigger event, rule and evidence in the clocks table. CAFA notice runs from filing of the proposed settlement, not from preliminary approval; no final approval order before 90 days after the later service on officials; opt-out and objection windows run from the Notice Date; the fee motion is filed and posted before the objection deadline. A default in an agreement never overrides a statute, rule or court order on the same point.
10. **Candor with the court; no concealed side deals.** Every agreement made in connection with the proposal is identified in the Rule 23(e)(3) statement, and any payment or other consideration to an objector or objector's counsel for withdrawing an objection or forgoing an appeal goes to the court for approval after a hearing under Rule 23(e)(5)(B). Decline to draft a side agreement meant to be kept from the court or a statement that omits a known agreement; offer the compliant statement and approval motion instead.
11. **Residual funds and allocation.** The agreement says where unclaimed money goes: redistribution first, cy pres only for an infeasible residue with recipient relationships disclosed, reversion only with a stated justification. Groups with materially different claims get an allocation that reflects the difference, and the memo addresses subclasses or separate counsel.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date, the court, judge, case number and circuit; whether the case was filed in or removed to federal court.
2. Operative complaint and any certification order (class definition, claims, Rule 23(b) category).
3. The negotiated terms: executed term sheet, MOU or draft agreement (fund or relief, class, release, fees, awards, residue).
4. Which side the user represents, and who signs (each settling defendant, class representatives, class counsel).
5. Class size, contact data available (mail, email), and the administrator's bid with notice methods and claims-rate experience in named comparable settlements.
6. Fee intentions: percentage or amount, lodestar to date, expenses, any clear-sailing or reversion terms; proposed service awards.
7. Any coupon, voucher, credit or injunctive relief and data on redemption or value.
8. Other pending or anticipated actions involving the same defendant or conduct.
9. Every side letter, supplemental agreement (opt-out threshold), fee-sharing agreement and objector communication.
10. Filing date or planned filing date of the proposed settlement; any scheduling order; the judge's procedures and local rules.

**Defaults if not supplied** (a statute, rule, local rule or court order on the same point overrides the default): non-reversionary fund; automatic payment where class data allow, otherwise a simple claim form; pro rata redistribution of the residue, then cy pres only for an infeasible residue; fee motion due at least 14 days before the objection deadline; opt-out and objection deadlines at least 35 days after the Notice Date (the N.D. Cal. floor; another district's rule controls where it exists); CAFA notice served by defendants within 10 days after the proposed settlement is filed; no service award in the Eleventh Circuit.

**Hard stops (label the determination "Declined: hard stop"; produce the blocking-issues list and the request or compliant alternative, not the requested document):**
- No negotiated terms: no executed term sheet, MOU or draft agreement stating the relief and the class. Produce a term-sheet questionnaire instead; never invent a fund amount, class definition or fee.
- The court is unknown, or it cannot be determined whether the case is in federal or state court.
- A request to conceal from the court an agreement made in connection with the proposal, to file an agreements statement that omits a known agreement, or to provide payment or other consideration to an objector or objector's counsel for withdrawing an objection or forgoing an appeal without court approval after a hearing.

**Not hard stops:** an unsourced claims rate (High item; run a stated range); a reversion, clear-sailing or kicker clause (Critical item; draft the alternative); a service award in the Eleventh Circuit (Critical item; draft without it); a schedule or CAFA error (Critical item; draft the corrected schedule).

## Step 1: Fix Forum, Posture and Governing Standard

Read `references/approval-standards-and-terms.md`, section 1. Record the court, circuit, certification status and Rule 23(b) category, and whether the task is preliminary approval, final approval or drafting from a term sheet. For an uncertified class, plan the likely-certification showing under 23(e)(1)(B)(ii).

## Step 2: Compare Class and Release Scope

Read `references/approval-standards-and-terms.md`, sections 2 and 3. Fill table 3A of `references/output-template.md`: geography, products, period, exclusions, each released-claim category with its factual predicate, each released party, and each other pending action whose claims would be released. Narrow or justify every expansion. Confirm a named representative can represent each part of the settlement class.

## Step 3: Reconcile Settlement Value

Read `references/approval-standards-and-terms.md`, section 4. Fill table 3B: gross fund through net fund, expected claims and payout at low, expected and high claims rates from named comparables, residue and its destination, and the fee ratios. Correct any value statement that treats gross or face value as the class benefit.

## Step 4: Test Fees, Service Awards and Coupons

Read `references/approval-standards-and-terms.md`, sections 5 and 6. Identify clear-sailing and kicker terms, fee timing, and any deferral tied to the claims rate. Apply the controlling circuit's service-award rule. For in-kind relief, analyze whether it is a coupon and, if so, compute the fee basis from expected redemption and plan the 1712(e) hearing and written finding.

## Step 5: Residual Funds and Allocation

Read `references/approval-standards-and-terms.md`, sections 7 and 8. Draft the residue waterfall. Test the allocation for groups whose claims differ in value or kind, and record subclasses or separate counsel as a judgment call with a recommendation.

## Step 6: Build the Clocks Table and CAFA Package

Read `references/clocks-and-cafa-notice.md`. Give each deadline its own trigger, rule and evidence; compute in the fixed format with Rule 6(a) adjustments; mark court-set dates as such. Compute the CAFA due date from the filing date and the earliest final approval order date from the later service. Build the CAFA package table in Part 6 of `references/output-template.md`.

## Step 7: Agreements Statement and Objector Terms

Read `references/approval-standards-and-terms.md`, section 9. List every agreement made in connection with the proposal for the Rule 23(e)(3) statement. Route any proposed objector payment to a motion for approval after a hearing. Apply the third hard stop if the request is to conceal.

## Step 8: Draft or Redline the Agreement and Approval Inserts

Use the article skeleton in `references/output-template.md`, Part 4, for a new agreement; for a review, redline only the affected sections and give the reason for each change. Draft the approval-paper inserts in Part 5.

## Step 9: Assemble the Deliverable and Hand-Offs

Lay out the header and Parts 1 to 7 per `references/output-template.md`. Rank blocking issues, fill the open-items table with gap classes, complete each hand-off with the facts the receiving skill needs, and list every `[VERIFY]` item drawing on `references/primary-sources.md`.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Does every term match the executed term sheet, and is every departure from it flagged for client and opposing-counsel sign-off?
2. Is the settlement class and release scope acceptable to the client once the narrowing recommendations are read?
3. Is the claims-rate basis one the administrator will put in a declaration?
4. Are the fee, award and residual terms ones class counsel will defend under the controlling circuit's law?
5. Does the clocks table fit the judge's calendar and procedures, and have defendants confirmed the CAFA service date?

If the user does not answer, recommend confirming scope, value and the schedule first; those are the most common grounds for denial of preliminary approval and for objector appeals.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. The court, circuit, certification status and Rule 23(b) category are stated, and the analysis uses that circuit's law.
2. Table 3A compares the settlement class and release with the complaint and any certification order, and every expansion is justified or narrowed.
3. Each released-claim category is tied to the identical factual predicate; released parties are named; other pending actions are listed.
4. Table 3B reconciles gross to net to expected payout with a sourced claims rate and low, expected and high cases; no gross or face value is called the class benefit.
5. The fee is reported against the gross fund and against fee plus expected class payout; clear-sailing and kicker terms are flagged and reductions stay with the class.
6. Service awards follow the controlling circuit; none is drafted as compensation for time or service in the Eleventh Circuit.
7. Coupon relief is analyzed under 1712, and any coupon-attributable fee uses redeemed value.
8. The residual-fund clause states the waterfall; any reversion or cy pres term is justified and disclosed.
9. Every dated or counted deadline has its own trigger, rule and evidence; CAFA runs from filing; the hearing is not inside the 1715(d) period; the fee motion precedes the objection deadline.
10. Every agreement made in connection with the proposal is identified, and no objector payment is drafted without a court-approval motion.
11. Every figure has a locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`, every legal statement a citation or `[VERIFY]`, and each blocking issue ends with a recommendation and an owner.
12. The readiness label is one of the four exact labels, and the output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Write the agreement for the judge and the objector who will read it later; plain terms with defined deadlines are easier to approve than broad catch-alls.
- Disclose problem terms with their justification rather than hoping they go unnoticed; courts and objectors find clear-sailing, kicker and reversion clauses.
- When a user's requested term conflicts with a rule, statute or controlling case, draft the compliant term and explain the change; do not silently follow or silently change the request.
- Keep the notice and claim process as simple as the data allow; a claims process that is unduly demanding counts against adequacy under the 2018 Committee Note.
- Securities (PSLRA), ERISA, wage-and-hour (FLSA collective, PAGA) and state-court settlements have extra requirements not covered here; flag them `[VERIFY]` and hand off.
- Personal data in class lists and CAFA state rosters is handled under the protective order; send officials only what 1715(b) requires.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Term sheet and draft agreement state different class periods or fund amounts | Drafting drift after mediation | Use the signed term sheet; record both in table 3D; flag for both sides' sign-off |
| Administrator gives a claims rate with no comparables | Bid prepared for price, not for the court | Request the named comparable settlements; run 2x and 0.5x sensitivity; mark the rate `[UNVERIFIED]` |
| Class data incomplete (no emails, stale addresses) | Defendant's records | State the reach estimate and the supplemental notice method; widen the claims period |
| Scanned or image-only agreement draft | Signed PDF only | Try OCR; list sections that remain unreadable; request the native file |
| Preliminary approval order already entered with conflicting dates | Order drafted from an earlier version | The order controls; recompute the clocks table; propose an amended order if a date breaks a rule |
| Circuit position on service awards not located | Circuit has not ruled | Record the search and date; mark `[VERIFY]`; draft the award as optional and severable |
| Objector's counsel proposes a private payment | Professional objector practice | Explain Rule 23(e)(5)(B); offer a motion for approval after a hearing; never draft a private deal |
| Case pending in state court | Not a "class action" under 1711(2) | Apply the state rule `[VERIFY]`; do not cite 1715 as mandatory |
