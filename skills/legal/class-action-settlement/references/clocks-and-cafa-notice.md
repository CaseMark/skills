# Clocks, Scheduling and CAFA Notice

How to build the settlement schedule so that every deadline has its own trigger, and how to serve CAFA notice under 28 U.S.C. 1715. ## 1. Computation rules

- **Rule 6(a)** applies to periods in the Federal Rules, in local rules and court orders, and in any statute that does not specify a method of computing time, which includes 28 U.S.C. 1715. Exclude the trigger day, count every day, include the last day; if the last day is a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the period runs to the next day that is not one (6(a)(1)). The "next day" is counted forward for periods measured after an event and backward for periods measured before an event (6(a)(5)). Legal holidays are listed in 6(a)(6), including Washington's Birthday, Veterans Day and any day the state where the court sits declares a holiday (for periods measured after an event).
- **Court-set dates are not computed.** The final approval hearing is a date the court sets on a court day. A schedule may propose "no earlier than" a date, but the order states the actual date.
- **The order controls.** Once the court enters a preliminary approval order, its dates govern over the agreement's formulas. If they differ, the reconciliation table records both and the order wins.

## 3. Ordering rules that courts enforce

1. **Fee motion before the objection deadline.** Rule 23(h) requires class members to have the fee motion and its support before they must object. Setting the objection deadline before the fee motion is filed misapplies Rule 23(h) (In re Mercury Interactive Corp. Sec. Litig., 618 F.3d 988 (9th Cir. 2010); Redman v. RadioShack Corp., 768 F.3d 622 (7th Cir. 2014); Johnson v. NPAS Solutions, LLC, 975 F.3d 1244 (11th Cir. 2020), which found the error harmless on its facts). Draft the fee motion due a stated number of days before the objection deadline and posted on the settlement website the same day.
2. **Opt-out and objection windows run from notice, not from the order.** A deadline counted from the preliminary approval order shrinks when notice is late. Count from the Notice Date and add a floor (for example, "the later of X days after the Notice Date or [date]").
3. **CAFA runs from filing, not from preliminary approval.** The 10-day clock in 1715(b) starts when the proposed settlement is filed. An agreement that says "within 10 days after preliminary approval" is late whenever preliminary approval comes more than 10 days after filing.
4. **No final approval order inside the 90 days.** The hearing may be set earlier only if the order issues on or after the 1715(d) date; the safer course is a hearing date on or after it. If a state official is served late, the 90 days run from that later service.
5. **Re-notice when terms change.** A material change after notice (a broader release, a larger fee request, a new subclass) may require new notice and a new objection window, and a new CAFA notice if the proposed settlement filed with the court changes `[VERIFY: controlling circuit]`.
6. **Second opt-out.** If a (b)(3) class was certified earlier and members already had an exclusion opportunity, the court may refuse approval unless the settlement affords a new one (Rule 23(e)(4)).

## 4. CAFA notice under 28 U.S.C. 1715

**Applies to:** a "class action" as defined in 1711(2): filed in a federal district court under Rule 23, or removed to federal court from a state class procedure. It does not apply to a settlement approved in state court.

**Who serves:** each defendant participating in the proposed settlement (1715(b)). Plaintiffs do not serve it, but class counsel should confirm service because 1715(d) controls the approval date.

**On whom:**
- The appropriate Federal official: the Attorney General of the United States, or, for a federal or state depository institution, holding company, foreign bank or their non-depository subsidiaries, the primary federal regulator if the matters alleged are subject to its supervision (1715(a)(1), (c)(1)).
- The appropriate State official of each State in which a class member resides: the primary regulator, supervisor or licensing authority of the defendant if the matters alleged are subject to its regulation; otherwise the State attorney general (1715(a)(2)). For a state depository institution, the State bank supervisor of the State of incorporation or charter plus the federal official (1715(c)(2)).

**Contents (1715(b)(1)-(8)):**
1. The complaint, materials filed with it and amended complaints (or notice of how to access them electronically).
2. Notice of any scheduled judicial hearing.
3. Any proposed or final notification to class members of the right to request exclusion (or a statement that none exists) and of the proposed settlement.
4. Any proposed or final class action settlement.
5. Any settlement or other agreement contemporaneously made between class counsel and defense counsel.
6. Any final judgment or notice of dismissal.
7. If feasible, the names of class members residing in each State and their estimated proportionate share of the claims; if not feasible, a reasonable estimate of the number in each State and their proportionate share.
8. Any written judicial opinion relating to items 3 to 6.

Supplement the notice when later items (final notice forms, hearing dates, opinions) come into existence `[VERIFY: practice; not stated in 1715]`.

**Consequence of non-compliance (1715(e)).** A class member may refuse to be bound by the settlement if it shows notice was not provided, unless notice was directed to the appropriate federal official and to either the State attorney general or the primary regulator or licensing authority. Section 1715 does not expand officials' authority (1715(f)).

**Proof.** File a declaration of CAFA compliance listing each official served, the date and method, and the contents, before the final approval hearing.

## 5. Fixed computation format

```text
Deadline: [name]
Trigger: [event] on [date, weekday]   Evidence: [document, locator]
Rule: [period and source]
Count: [trigger date] + [n] days = [date, weekday]
Adjustment: [none / Rule 6(a)(1)(C) rollover to date, weekday / backward under 6(a)(5)]
Result: [date, weekday]; status [computed / court-set / estimated pending trigger]
```

If the trigger has not happened, compute from a stated planning date, label the result "estimated," and add an open item to recompute from the actual trigger.

## 6. Common scheduling errors

| Error | Fix |
|---|---|
| All deadlines keyed to "entry of the Preliminary Approval Order" | Key opt-out, objection and claims deadlines to the Notice Date; key CAFA to filing |
| Objection deadline before the fee motion | Move the fee motion at least 14 days before the objection deadline `[VERIFY: judge's preference]` |
| Final approval hearing inside 90 days of CAFA service | Serve CAFA notice within 10 days of filing, or set the hearing after the 1715(d) date |
| Hearing date computed by formula lands on a weekend or holiday | Ask the court for a date; state "no earlier than" in the proposed order |
| CAFA notice omitted because the case started in state court and was removed | Removed cases are "class actions" under 1711(2); serve notice |
| Late notice not reflected in the deadlines | Extend opt-out and objection deadlines by order; recompute the clocks table |
