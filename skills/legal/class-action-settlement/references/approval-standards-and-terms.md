# Approval Standards and Settlement Terms

Rules for testing a class settlement agreement and its approval papers against Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(e) and (h), CAFA (28 U.S.C. 1711-1715) and the controlling case law. Every statement here was checked on 2026-10-30 against the source recorded in `references/primary-sources.md`. Anything marked `[VERIFY]` was not confirmed in a primary source. Rule 23 and circuit law on fees and incentive awards are still moving, so re-check the current rule text and the controlling circuit before filing.

## 1. Forum, posture and the governing standard

| Question | Why it matters | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Federal or state court? | Rule 23 and CAFA chapter 114 apply only in federal court; 28 U.S.C. 1711(2) defines "class action" as one filed in or removed to a federal district court | State court: apply the state class rule and its notice statute `[VERIFY: state rule]`; do not cite 1715 as mandatory |
| Class already certified, or certified only for settlement? | Rule 23(e) now covers "a class proposed to be certified for purposes of settlement" | Uncertified: the preliminary approval showing must cover likely certification, 23(e)(1)(B)(ii) |
| Which Rule 23(b) category? | (b)(3) classes get Rule 23(c)(2)(B) notice and an opt-out right; (b)(1) and (b)(2) classes do not | Individualized monetary claims belong in (b)(3), not (b)(2) (Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes, 564 U.S. 338 (2011)) |
| Which circuit? | Incentive awards, fee scrutiny and coupon rules differ by circuit | Sections 5 and 6 |

**Preliminary approval (Rule 23(e)(1)(B)).** The court must direct notice to everyone who would be bound if the parties show the court "will likely be able to" (i) approve the proposal under 23(e)(2) and (ii) certify the class for purposes of judgment. The 2018 Committee Note says the parties should ordinarily give the court, at this stage, all materials they intend to submit for final approval, including the claims process, "the anticipated rate of claims," the handling of unclaimed funds, the likely range of litigated outcomes, other pending litigation whose claims would be released, the proposed fee and any 23(e)(3) agreement.

**Final approval (Rule 23(e)(2)).** Only after a hearing and a finding that the proposal is fair, reasonable and adequate, considering whether (A) the representatives and class counsel adequately represented the class; (B) it was negotiated at arm's length; (C) the relief is adequate, considering (i) costs, risks and delay of trial and appeal, (ii) the effectiveness of the distribution method, including claims processing, (iii) the terms of any fee award, including timing of payment, and (iv) any 23(e)(3) agreement; and (D) it treats class members equitably relative to each other. Circuit factor lists still apply as supplements. The Second Circuit holds there is no presumption of fairness from arm's-length negotiation; all four factors are weighed holistically, including fees and incentive awards (Moses v. New York Times Co., 79 F.4th 235 (2d Cir. 2023)).

**Settlement-only certification.** All Rule 23(a) and (b) requirements apply except trial manageability; class definition protections "demand undiluted, even heightened, attention in the settlement context" (Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, 521 U.S. 591 (1997)).

## 2. Settlement class definition

Compare the settlement class with the class pleaded in the operative complaint and, if certified, the class in the certification order, element by element:

| Element | Check |
|---|---|
| Geography | State class expanded to nationwide; state-law variation goes to predominance |
| Products, accounts or conduct | Products or practices not pleaded, or not bought by any named representative |
| Class period | Start date earlier than pleaded; end date running to preliminary approval or later |
| Exclusions | Defendant's officers, judges, opt-outs from earlier actions, persons who already released |
| Subclasses | Groups whose claims differ in value or kind (section 8) |

Every difference is stated in the preliminary approval motion with the reason it is appropriate. The Northern District of California's Procedural Guidance for Class Action Settlements (modified Sept. 5, 2024) requires exactly this explanation for differences in the class and the released claims; other districts may have their own standing order `[VERIFY: local rules and the judge's procedures]`. An expansion that no named representative can represent is a Critical issue: adequacy and typicality must be shown for the class actually being bound.

## 3. Release scope and released parties

- **Identical factual predicate.** A class release may cover claims not pleaded, and even claims that could not have been pleaded, only if the released conduct arises out of the identical factual predicate as the settled conduct, and only if those claims were adequately represented (Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Visa U.S.A. Inc., 396 F.3d 96 (2d Cir. 2005)). A release reaching a different factual predicate is unenforceable as to those claims and may violate due process (Hesse v. Sprint Corp., 598 F.3d 581 (9th Cir. 2010)).
- **Drafting test.** For each category in the Released Claims definition, name the pleaded facts it rests on. Strike "relating in any way to the Released Parties' products, business or practices" and any claim type (privacy, TCPA, employment, personal injury) with no shared factual predicate.
- **Released parties.** Limit to the settling defendants, related entities that are named, and their insurers and agents for the released claims. Strike "all other persons." A non-party released for conduct outside the predicate, or one that contributes nothing, needs a stated reason.
- **Other pending actions.** List every pending or anticipated action whose claims would be released (2018 Committee Note to 23(e)(1)). Notify counsel in those actions; the N.D. Cal. guidance asks defendants to serve the preliminary approval motion on them within one day of filing.
- **Partial settlements: bar orders and judgment reduction.** When fewer than all defendants settle, a bar order cutting off non-settling defendants' contribution and indemnity claims against the settling defendant must be paired with a judgment credit that protects them; the Second Circuit upheld a bar order where the proportionate-fault credit adequately compensated non-settling defendants, and remanded on whether the bar should be mutual (Gerber v. MTC Electronic Technologies Co., 329 F.3d 297 (2d Cir. 2003)). In private securities actions the PSLRA requires a bar order on entry of the settlement, barring contribution claims by and against the settling covered person, and reduces any later verdict or judgment by the greater of the settling person's percentage of responsibility or the amount paid (15 U.S.C. 78u-4(f)(7)). Outside the PSLRA, the credit method (pro tanto, proportionate or capped) depends on circuit law and any governing contribution statute `[VERIFY: controlling circuit and state contribution law]`. Give non-settling defendants notice and an opportunity to be heard on the bar order.
- **(b)(2) classes.** A mandatory class without opt-out should not release individual damages claims (Dukes).
- **Unknown claims waivers.** Cal. Civ. Code 1542 (as amended effective Jan. 1, 2019) limits a general release to known or suspected claims; a 1542 waiver widens the release and is tested by the same predicate rule.
- **Matsushita.** A state-court class judgment may release exclusively federal claims and receive full faith and credit where the state's law gives the release that effect (Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367 (1996)), so a parallel federal action is not protected by subject matter alone.

## 4. Settlement value, claims rate and the fee denominator

Always reconcile, in this order, with a source for each figure: gross fund; minus notice and administration; minus requested fees and expenses; minus service awards; equals net fund; expected class payout = expected valid claims x payment per claim (or the net fund if distribution is automatic or pro rata); residue and where it goes.

- **Claims rate.** Use a sourced estimate: the administrator's experience in named comparable settlements (N.D. Cal. guidance item 6), with the examples identified. Show a low, expected and high case. An unsourced rate is `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- **What the class receives.** Notice and administration costs are costs, not class benefits (Redman v. RadioShack Corp., 768 F.3d 622 (7th Cir. 2014); Pearson v. NBTY, Inc., 772 F.3d 778 (7th Cir. 2014)). Never state the gross fund as the benefit to the class when a claims process, cap or reversion means payout will be lower.
- **Fee ratios to report.** Fee as a share of the gross fund; fee as a share of (fee + expected class payout), the Pearson ratio; and fee compared with expected payout. The 2018 Committee Note: "the relief actually delivered to the class can be a significant factor in determining the appropriate fee award," and one approach is to defer some or all of the fee until the actual claims rate is known.
- **Unclaimed money in a common fund.** Where each class member has an undisputed, mathematically ascertainable share of a lump-sum judgment, fees may be assessed against the whole fund, including unclaimed amounts (Boeing Co. v. Van Gemert, 444 U.S. 472 (1980)). That rule does not convert a claims-made, reversionary deal into one that delivers the gross amount; report both measures.

## 5. Fees, clear sailing, kickers and service awards

- **Rule 23(h).** Fees by motion under Rule 54(d)(2) at a time the court sets; notice of class counsel's motion directed to class members; any class member may object; the court finds facts and states conclusions under Rule 52(a). Rule 54(d)(2)(B)'s 14-days-after-judgment default yields to the court's order.
- **Warning signs.** Disproportionate distribution to counsel; a clear-sailing agreement (defendant will not oppose fees up to an amount); a kicker or reverter (any fee reduction returns to defendant, not the class). The Ninth Circuit requires this heightened scrutiny for post-certification settlements too (Briseño v. Henderson, 998 F.3d 1014 (9th Cir. 2021)); the Seventh Circuit treats clear-sailing and kicker clauses as signs of a selfish deal (Pearson; Redman). Draft any fee reduction to revert to the class fund.
- **Service (incentive) awards, by circuit.**

| Circuit | Position | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Eleventh | Awards that compensate a representative for time or reward bringing suit are prohibited; reimbursement of reasonable litigation costs was allowed in Greenough itself | Johnson v. NPAS Solutions, LLC, 975 F.3d 1244 (11th Cir. 2020); rehearing en banc denied, 43 F.4th 1138 (11th Cir. 2022) |
| First | Not prohibited if within Rule 23(e) | Murray v. Grocery Delivery E-Services USA Inc., 55 F.4th 340 (1st Cir. 2022) |
| Second | Not per se unlawful | Melito v. Experian Mktg. Sols., Inc., 923 F.3d 85 (2d Cir. 2019); Hyland v. Navient Corp., 48 F.4th 110 (2d Cir. 2022); Moses (2023) |
| Seventh | Permitted; prospect of an award supports the representative's stake | Scott v. Dart, 99 F.4th 1076 (7th Cir. 2024) |
| Ninth | Permitted | In re Apple Inc. Device Performance Litig., 50 F.4th 769 (9th Cir. 2022) |
| Federal | Permitted (reported) | Nat'l Veterans Legal Servs. Program v. United States (Fed. Cir. Mar. 20, 2026) `[VERIFY: reporter citation]` |
| Others | Not located in this review | `[VERIFY: controlling circuit]` |

A petition asking the Supreme Court to hold service awards unlawful, Isaacson v. Moses, No. 25-1411, was distributed for the November 6, 2026 conference `[VERIFY: disposition]`. In every circuit, the award must not be conditioned on supporting the settlement, its size must not undermine adequacy, and it must be supported by evidence of the representative's work and risk.

## 6. Coupon settlements (28 U.S.C. 1712)

- 1712 does not define "coupon." Store credit, vouchers, access codes or discounts usable only with the defendant raise the question. The Second Circuit held that one-month subscription access codes were coupons (Moses). Analyze the instrument's features: cash option, transferability, minimum purchase, expiry, whether it buys only the defendant's goods.
- **Fees.** The part of a fee attributable to coupons is based on the value to class members of coupons actually redeemed (1712(a)); otherwise lodestar, with a multiplier permitted (1712(b)); mixed relief splits the two (1712(c)); fees attributable to coupons cannot be computed on face value (In re HP Inkjet Printer Litig., 716 F.3d 1173 (9th Cir. 2013)).
- **Approval.** Only after a hearing and a written finding that the settlement is fair, reasonable and adequate (1712(e)); the court may require distribution of unclaimed coupon value to charitable or governmental organizations, which does not count toward fees.
- **Drafting.** Value coupons at an expected redemption rate from sourced data; defer the coupon-based fee until redemption is reported; offer a cash alternative where possible.

## 7. Residual funds, reversion and cy pres

- The agreement must say what happens to unclaimed funds (2018 Committee Note). Order of preference: further distribution to claimants when economically feasible; cy pres only for a residue that cannot feasibly be distributed (Klier v. Elf Atochem N. Am., Inc., 658 F.3d 468 (5th Cir. 2011)); reversion to defendant only with a stated justification.
- Cy pres is permissible but inferior to direct distribution; a court may consider the level of direct benefit to the class in setting fees (In re Baby Prods. Antitrust Litig., 708 F.3d 163 (3d Cir. 2013)). The Supreme Court granted review of a cy pres-only settlement but decided it on standing (Frank v. Gaos, 139 S. Ct. 1041 (2019) (per curiam)); the merits question remains open.
- Disclose any relationship between the parties or counsel and a proposed cy pres recipient, and the recipient's nexus to the claims (N.D. Cal. guidance item 8).
- Unused money set aside for fees, service awards or administration goes back to class distribution, not to the defendant.

## 8. Allocation and equitable treatment

Rule 23(e)(2)(D) asks whether the plan "takes appropriate account of differences among their claims" and whether the release affects members differently. Where groups with materially different claims share one representative and one counsel, consider subclasses with separate counsel: Amchem (present-injury and exposure-only claimants), and Murray (approval vacated because, without separate counsel for distinct groups, the court could not tell whether the allocation was equitable). Rule 23(c)(5) permits subclasses.

## 9. Agreements statement and objector payments

- **Rule 23(e)(3).** The parties seeking approval must file a statement identifying any agreement made in connection with the proposal: side letters, supplemental agreements setting an opt-out termination threshold ("blow provision"), fee-sharing and referral agreements, agreements with objectors. Identify each one; whether its terms are filed under seal or reviewed in camera is for the court `[VERIFY: judge's practice]`.
- **Rule 23(e)(5)(B).** Unless approved by the court after a hearing, no payment or other consideration may be provided in connection with forgoing or withdrawing an objection, or forgoing, dismissing or abandoning an appeal from approval. The rule does not distinguish by who pays. An objector may withdraw without approval if nothing is given (2018 Committee Note).
- **Rule 23(e)(5)(C).** If approval is not obtained before an appeal is docketed, the Rule 62.1 indicative-ruling procedure applies while the appeal is pending.
- **Objection content (23(e)(5)(A)).** The notice and agreement should require an objection to say whether it applies to the objector, a subset or the whole class, and state its grounds with specificity; do not add burdens that chill objections.
- **Appeals.** A non-named class member who timely objects may appeal without intervening (Devlin v. Scardelletti, 536 U.S. 1 (2002)).

## 10. Other CAFA protections

- **Net loss (28 U.S.C. 1713).** A settlement under which a class member must pay class counsel sums producing a net loss needs a written finding that nonmonetary benefits substantially outweigh the loss.
- **Geography (28 U.S.C. 1714).** No larger payments to some members solely because they are closer to the court.
- **Notice to officials (28 U.S.C. 1715).** See `references/clocks-and-cafa-notice.md`.
