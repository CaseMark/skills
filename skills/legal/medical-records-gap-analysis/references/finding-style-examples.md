# Finding Style Examples

Use these examples when drafting or revising the attorney-facing report. Preserve the source-supported facts and citations; do not copy details from an example into another matter.

## Missing referral workup

**Prefer:**

> The treating provider referred the patient to neurology and ordered an electroencephalogram (EEG). No neurology consultation, EEG result, or referral-closure record appears in the reviewed production. The missing records prevent determining whether the recommended neurologic workup occurred. [citations]

**Avoid:** A long list of referral-routing records, authorization mechanisms, possible custodians, and request methods in the substantive finding. Put those details in the retrieval appendix when they are useful.

## Planned therapy course

**Prefer:**

> Physical therapy was ordered twice weekly for six weeks, or 12 planned visits. The production contains the evaluation and three daily notes through [date], but no later attendance record, progress report, or discharge summary. The missing material prevents determining how many visits occurred or why the course ended. [citations]

State the date of loss to therapy start, planned visit count, produced visit count, and exact unsupported interval when the sources permit. Do not say all planned visits are missing when some are documented.

## Billing that changes the analysis

**Prefer:**

> An itemized bill lists treatment on [date], but no corresponding clinical note was produced. This may be a missing-record issue rather than a break in treatment. [citations]

**Omit or move to the appendix:** Generic statements that billing is incomplete when no billed service date, unknown provider, or clinical mismatch changes the gap analysis.

## Plain-language limitation

**Prefer:**

> Because the physical-therapy and neurology records are incomplete, the report cannot determine whether this interval was a true break in care.

**Avoid:** Methodological jargon or a multi-clause disclaimer that requires the reader to infer the practical limitation.
