# Jurisdictional Pre-Suit Notice Requirements

Read this file when Step 1 of `../SKILL.md` identifies a statutory pre-suit regime, or whenever the letter's recipient is in a jurisdiction listed below. Statutory pre-suit notice failures can **waive claims entirely** — this is the single highest-stakes failure mode for a demand letter.

Every citation here is a drafting reference, not a legal conclusion. Every pinpoint cite must be verified via `legal-research` before the letter is sent. Marked `[VERIFY]` throughout as a reminder. ## Massachusetts — Ch. 93A § 9 (consumer unfair / deceptive practices) `[VERIFY]`

- **Statute.** Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 93A, § 9(3).
- **Trigger.** A person injured by a § 2 unfair or deceptive act or practice who sues under § 9 (in practice, consumers). § 9(1) excludes anyone entitled to sue under § 11, the business-to-business section.
- **Not required.** No § 9(3) demand is required (a) for a claim asserted by counterclaim or cross-claim, (b) where the prospective respondent does not maintain a place of business or keep assets in Massachusetts, or (c) for a § 11 claim by a person engaged in trade or commerce. § 11 has no pre-suit demand; the respondent may instead tender a written offer of settlement for single damages with its answer. Do not label a § 11 letter a statutory prerequisite.
- **Notice period.** Written demand mailed or delivered at least **30 days** before filing suit.
- **Required contents.**
  - Identification of the claimant.
  - A reasonable description of the unfair or deceptive act or practice.
  - A statement of the injuries suffered.
- **Service method.** Mail to the recipient. No statutory specification of certified mail, but certified with return receipt is universal practice to prove delivery.
- **Response mechanism (leverage hook).** The recipient has 30 days from mailing or delivery of the demand to make a written tender of settlement. If the claimant rejects it and the court later finds the tender reasonable in relation to the injury actually suffered, recovery is limited to the relief tendered (§ 9(3)), and fees incurred after rejection of a reasonable written offer made within the 30 days are denied (§ 9(4)). Separately, a refusal to grant relief upon demand made in bad faith, with knowledge or reason to know of the violation, is a multiplier trigger under § 9(3). This cuts both ways: a weak demand invites a token tender that caps recovery.
- **Consequence of defect.** Where § 9(3) applies, a compliant demand is a prerequisite to suit; a missing or defective demand is a recurring ground for dismissal of the § 9 claim `[VERIFY]` current case law.
- **Exemplars.** `assets/examples/ma-93a-official-template.md`, `assets/examples/ma-93a-auto-body-repair.md`.
- **Verification.** `legal-research`: current case law on "reasonable offer," "knowing or willful," and the § 9(3) forfeiture mechanism. `citation-bluebook` for any case cites.

## Texas — DTPA § 17.505 (consumer unfair practices) `[VERIFY]`

- **Statute.** Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.505.
- **Trigger.** Any consumer claiming violations of the Deceptive Trade Practices Act under § 17.46 et seq.
- **Notice period.** Written notice at least **60 days** before filing a suit for damages under § 17.50(b)(1). Not required where 60 days' notice is impracticable because suit must be filed to prevent limitations from running, or where the claim is a counterclaim (§ 17.505(b)).
- **Required contents.** In reasonable detail, the consumer's specific complaint, and the amounts of economic damages, mental-anguish damages, and expenses including attorneys' fees reasonably incurred in asserting the claim (§ 17.505(a)). State each amount separately.
- **Service method.** Written notice; the statute names no method. Certified mail is standard.
- **Inspection.** During the 60 days the recipient may make a written request to inspect the goods.
- **Response mechanism.** The recipient may tender a settlement offer within 60 days after receiving the notice (§ 17.5052(a)), stating separately an amount for damages and an amount for attorneys' fees incurred to date. If the consumer rejects it and the court finds the damages offered the same as, substantially the same as, or more than the damages found, damages are capped at the lesser of the two, and fee recovery is limited as § 17.5052(h) provides.
- **Consequence of defect.** Abatement, not dismissal: the defendant may file a plea in abatement within 30 days after its original answer; the suit is abated until the 60th day after a compliant notice is served (§ 17.505(c)-(e)).
- **Exemplar.** No clean exemplar in corpus (MANIFEST coverage gap). Draft from `TEMPLATE.md` statutory-notice branch + this row.
- **Verification.** `legal-research` for "substantially the same" case law and current treble-damages "knowing / intentional" distinction.

## Texas — Stowers doctrine (common-law bad-faith policy-limits demand) `[VERIFY]`

- **Routing.** Any offer to a liability insurer to settle within limits, with or without a deadline, and any pre-answer offer prepared by or with an attorney to settle a Georgia motor-vehicle personal-injury, bodily-injury or death claim, whoever receives it (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-67.1(b)), is drafted with `bad-faith-demand`, which tests the governing statute, the deadline trigger and the lien matrix. This row is background only; this skill's `insurer-claim` branch presents a claim and makes no offer.
- **Doctrine.** Common-law, from *G.A. Stowers Furniture Co. v. Am. Indem. Co.*, 15 S.W.2d 544 (Tex. Comm'n App. 1929, holding approved). Not a statute. The duty is activated only when (1) the claim is within the scope of coverage, (2) the demand is within policy limits, and (3) its terms are such that an ordinarily prudent insurer would accept it, considering the insured's likely exposure to an excess judgment. *Am. Physicians Ins. Exch. v. Garcia*, 876 S.W.2d 842, 849 (Tex. 1994).
- **Trigger.** An insured is exposed to liability exceeding policy limits; the claimant has given the insurer the opportunity to settle within limits.
- **Notice period.** None statutorily required, but the demand must give the insurer a **reasonable time** to investigate, evaluate, and accept. Practice: 30 days is standard; 10–14 days is the floor and invites bad-faith arguments of its own.
- **Required contents.**
  - A clear offer to settle within policy limits.
  - Release of the insured from all liability for the incident.
  - Sufficient factual and medical documentation to allow the insurer a reasonable evaluation.
  - Specific deadline.
- **Service method.** To the insurer's claims representative (and, best practice, to the insured directly or through their counsel).
- **Consequence to the insurer of refusal.** If the insurer rejects a proper Stowers demand, and a judgment later exceeds limits, the insurer can be liable to the insured for the full excess. This is the extracontractual-damages leverage that makes these demands powerful.
- **Exemplars.** `assets/examples/pi-ga-witherite-liability-demand.md` and `assets/examples/pi-ga-auto-allen-v-liberty-mutual-demand.md` (Georgia policy-limits offers): read them here only for the liability narrative and specials presentation; their offer terms and deadlines are `bad-faith-demand` territory.
- **Verification.** `legal-research` on current Texas Stowers evolution (cases refine what counts as a "proper" demand). `citation-bluebook` for good-law status.

## California — CLRA § 1782(a) (consumers' Legal Remedies Act) `[VERIFY]`

- **Statute.** Cal. Civ. Code § 1782(a).
- **Trigger.** Before filing an action for damages under the CLRA (§ 1780(a)(1)–(2)).
- **Notice period.** **30 days** written notice before filing the damages claim. Injunctive relief may be sought without notice; damages may be added by amendment after the 30 days.
- **Required contents.**
  - Notification of the particular alleged violations.
  - Demand that the recipient correct, repair, replace, or otherwise rectify.
- **Service method.** Certified or registered mail, return receipt requested, to the place where the transaction occurred or to the person's principal place of business within California (§ 1782(a)).
- **Response mechanism.** No action for damages may be maintained if an appropriate correction, repair, replacement, or other remedy is given, or agreed to be given within a reasonable time, within 30 days after receipt of the notice (§ 1782(b)). The recipient's attempts to comply are treated as an offer to compromise, inadmissible under Evidence Code § 1152 (§ 1782(e)).
- **Consequence of defect.** Dismissal of the damages claim (injunctive claim can still proceed in some cases). Chronic failure point in consumer class actions.
- **Exemplar.** `assets/examples/clra-ca-fca-jeep-wrangler-lieff-cabraser-demand.md`.
- **Verification.** `legal-research` for current case law on what constitutes an "appropriate" cure.

## California / Texas / Florida — Construction defect pre-suit regimes `[VERIFY]`

- **California — SB 800 / Right to Repair Act.** Cal. Civ. Code §§ 895–945.5. Before filing, written notice to the builder by certified mail, overnight mail, or personal delivery, describing the claim in reasonable detail (§ 910(a)); builder has right to inspect and elect repair; strict timelines; failure bars homeowner's claim for construction defects covered by the Act `[VERIFY]` consequence.
- **Texas — RCLA.** Tex. Prop. Code Ch. 27. Written notice by certified mail, return receipt requested, to the contractor's last known address before the 60th day preceding suit, specifying the defects in reasonable detail and supplying discoverable evidence of the defect (§ 27.004(a)); contractor may inspect during the 35 days after it receives the notice and may make a written settlement offer within 60 days after receipt (§ 27.004(b)).
- **Florida — Ch. 558.** Fla. Stat. § 558.004. Notice of claim served at least 60 days before filing, or at least 120 days for an association representing more than 20 parcels; the notice must refer to Chapter 558. Inspection within 30 days after service (50 for such an association); written response within 45 days after service (75 for such an association).

## Florida — Medical malpractice Ch. 766 `[VERIFY]`

- **Statute.** Fla. Stat. Ch. 766 (Presuit Investigation).
- **Trigger.** Any action for medical negligence against a health-care provider.
- **Notice period.** No suit may be filed for **90 days** after the notice is delivered to any prospective defendant (§ 766.106(3)(a)). Statute of limitations tolled during this period `[VERIFY]` tolling mechanics in § 766.106(4).
- **Required contents.**
  - Notice of Intent to Initiate Litigation, served after the claimant's presuit investigation under § 766.203(2).
  - Verified written medical-expert opinion corroborating reasonable grounds for the claim (§ 766.203).
  - If available: a list of known health care providers seen for the injuries after the alleged negligence and those who treated the claimant in the prior 2 years, copies of the records the expert relied on, and the executed authorization form in § 766.1065 (§ 766.106(2)(c)).
- **Service method.** At least one verifiable means: USPS certified mail, return receipt requested; USPS mail with tracking; an interstate commercial carrier; or a person authorized to serve process (§ 766.106(2)(a)).
- **Response mechanism.** Defendant/insurer may investigate, reject the claim, offer admission of liability with arbitration on damages, or offer settlement.
- **Consequence of defect.** Dismissal; claim may be barred if statute of limitations has run.
- **Exemplar.** No clean exemplar in corpus (MANIFEST coverage gap).
- **Verification.** `legal-research` for current Ch. 766 requirements and the specific form of expert affidavit required.

## Federal — FDCPA (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act) `[VERIFY]`

- **Statute.** 15 U.S.C. §§ 1692 et seq. Governs **third-party debt collectors**, not creditors collecting their own debts in their own name (narrow exceptions).
- **Trigger test.**
  - Sender: "debt collector" as defined in § 1692a(6). Attorneys regularly collecting consumer debts qualify.
  - Subject: "debt" as defined in § 1692a(5) — a consumer obligation for personal, family, or household purposes.
- **Initial-communication disclosure (§ 1692g validation notice).** Within 5 days of the initial communication with the consumer (or in the initial communication if written), the debt collector must provide:
  1. The amount of the debt.
  2. The name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed.
  3. A statement that unless the consumer, within 30 days after receipt, disputes the validity of the debt, the debt will be assumed valid.
  4. A statement that if the consumer notifies the debt collector in writing within 30 days that the debt is disputed, the debt collector will obtain verification and mail a copy to the consumer.
  5. A statement that, upon the consumer's written request within 30 days, the debt collector will provide the name and address of the original creditor if different from the current creditor.
- **Regulation F (12 C.F.R. Part 1006).** Since November 30, 2021 the CFPB's rule fixes the validation content: validation information under § 1006.34(c) (including the itemization date, the itemization since that date, and the date the collector will treat as the end of the validation period). The validation period ends 30 days after the consumer receives, or is assumed to receive, the information; the collector may assume receipt on any date at least five days after providing it, excluding legal public holidays, Saturdays and Sundays. Model Form B-1 is a safe harbor for content and format.
- **Overshadowing.** Collection activity and communications during the 30-day period may not overshadow or be inconsistent with the disclosure of the consumer's dispute rights (§ 1692g(b)). A pay-or-sue deadline that falls inside the validation period is the classic overshadowing pattern.
- **Mini-Miranda disclosure (§ 1692e(11); 12 C.F.R. § 1006.18(e)).** See `ETHICS-AND-PRIVILEGE.md` for the required text and the initial-vs-subsequent distinction.
- **Service method.** Any method, but certified mail is standard; restrictions on communication with represented consumers (§ 1692c) apply.
- **Consequence of defect.** Actual damages; additional damages up to $1,000 in an individual action (in a class action, up to the lesser of $500,000 or 1 percent of the collector's net worth for unnamed members); costs and attorney's fees (§ 1692k(a)).
- **Exemplar.** No clean exemplar in corpus (MANIFEST coverage gap).
- **Verification.** `legal-research`; FDCPA case law evolves. **Also check any state-law analog** — many states (CA Rosenthal Act, NY GBL § 600, etc.) have parallel regimes with their own disclosures.

## Federal — Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (MMWA) `[VERIFY]`

- **Statute.** 15 U.S.C. § 2301 et seq. Applies to consumer-product written warranties.
- **Pre-suit notice.** Under § 2310(e), no action under § 2310(d) for failure to comply with a written or implied warranty or service contract may be brought unless the warrantor is "afforded a reasonable opportunity to cure such failure to comply." A demand letter is the standard mechanism. In a class action the opportunity is afforded by the named plaintiffs, who must then notify the defendant that they act for the class.
- **Federal-court thresholds.** § 2310(d)(3): no claim is cognizable in federal court if any individual claim is under $25, if the amount in controversy is under $50,000 computed on all claims in the suit, or, in a class action, if there are fewer than 100 named plaintiffs. These go to federal jurisdiction, not to the demand itself.
- **Service method.** No statutory method; certified mail is standard.
- **Consequence of defect.** Dismissal of the warranty claim; state-law warranty claims may survive.
- **Exemplar.** `assets/examples/multistate-mmwa-fca-slack-davis-sanger-demand.md`.
- **Verification.** `legal-research` for what counts as a "reasonable opportunity to cure" in the governing circuit.

## Defamation — state retraction statutes `[VERIFY]`

Many states require a pre-suit retraction demand as a prerequisite to claiming presumed or punitive damages in libel actions against media defendants. The specific mechanics are state-specific.

- **Common features.** Written demand; identification of the allegedly defamatory statement; opportunity for the defendant to publish a correction or retraction within a specified period. The consequence of a missing demand differs by state: some statutes limit damages, others bar the action.
- **California, Cal. Civ. Code § 48a.** Libel in a daily or weekly news publication, or slander by radio broadcast. Demand served on the publisher or broadcaster within 20 days after knowledge of the publication; without a demand and failure to correct, the plaintiff recovers special damages only.
- **Texas, Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 73.055.** A timely and sufficient request for correction, clarification, or retraction (or the defendant's own correction) is a condition of maintaining any defamation action; a request is timely within the limitations period, but if not made within 90 days after knowledge of the publication, exemplary damages are unavailable. § 73.055(d) lists the required contents.
- **Georgia, O.C.G.A. § 51-5-11.** Written retraction request at least 7 days before filing; if none was made, or the defendant retracted as the statute provides, no punitive damages.
- **Florida, Fla. Stat. §§ 770.01-.02.** Written notice at least 5 days before suing a newspaper, periodical, broadcast or other medium is a condition precedent (§ 770.01); a timely full and fair correction limits a good-faith publisher to actual damages (§ 770.02).
- **Exemplar.** `assets/examples/defamation-retraction-minc.md`.
- **Verification.** `legal-research` for the specific state's statute AND current case-law interpretation. Retraction statutes are constitutional minefields — some have been partially invalidated.

## Other jurisdictions and claims not listed here

This table is not exhaustive. When the letter's jurisdiction or claim type does not appear above, **invoke `legal-research`** before drafting, with a query like:

> "Pre-suit notice requirements for a {{CLAIM TYPE}} under {{STATE}} law. Identify any statutory or common-law notice requirements, required contents, notice period, service method, and consequence of defect. Provide citations for verification."

Claims / regimes known to have pre-suit requirements but not detailed here (check if applicable):

- **Tort claims against government entities** — federal FTCA (28 U.S.C. § 2675), state tort-claims acts (every state has one; 60–180 day windows; hyper-strict content requirements; late notice = absolute bar).
- **Employment discrimination (federal).** EEOC charge (not a demand letter, but a pre-suit prerequisite) under Title VII, ADA, ADEA.
- **FLSA pre-suit** — not required, but often used.
- **Lemon-law claims** — every state has one; each has different pre-suit notice mechanics.
- **Mechanics liens and construction lien perfection** — pre-lien notice, not demand-letter mechanics, but overlap.
- **Securities-fraud class actions** — PSLRA and state blue-sky equivalents.
- **Landlord-tenant** — state-specific cure-or-quit notice requirements.
- **HOA / condominium disputes** — state-specific pre-suit mediation requirements.

**Rule.** Never assume a jurisdiction does not have a pre-suit regime. The cost of checking is one `legal-research` call. The cost of missing one is an entire claim.
