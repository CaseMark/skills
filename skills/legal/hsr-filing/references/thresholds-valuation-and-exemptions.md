# Thresholds, Valuation, Parties and Exemptions

## 2. The reportability test, in order

1. **Commerce.** Either person is engaged in commerce or an activity affecting commerce (7A(a)(1)).
2. **Size of transaction.** Value of voting securities, non-corporate interests and assets of the acquired person that the acquiring person will hold as a result of the acquisition (7A(a)(2); 801.13). Not reportable if $133.9 million or less (2026).
3. **Size of person.** Applies only if the value is in excess of $133.9 million but not in excess of $535.5 million (2026). Met when one person has annual net sales or total assets of $267.8 million or more and the other $26.8 million or more, with the statute's manufacturing and non-manufacturing variants (7A(a)(2)(B)(ii)): a non-manufacturing acquired person is tested on total assets only unless it has annual net sales of $267.8 million or more.
4. **Exemptions.** Statutory (7A(c)) and regulatory (Part 802), each with its conditions met on the facts (section 7 below).
5. **Notification threshold.** For voting securities, the highest threshold crossed (801.1(h)): greater than $133.9 million; $267.8 million; $1.339 billion; 25 percent if the value is greater than $2.678 billion; 50 percent if the value is greater than $133.9 million. The 50 percent threshold is reported for any acquisition of 50 percent or more, whatever the value (Form Instructions, Item 2(c)).

## 3. Valuing the acquisition (801.10, 801.13, 801.15)

**Assets (801.10(b)).** Fair market value, or the acquisition price if determined and greater. The PNO's valuation guidance states the acquisition price is the total consideration received by the seller and **includes accrued liabilities assumed by the acquiring person and any separate payment for a covenant not to compete**; anticipated future payments are included **at face value and are not discounted to present value**; a price is "determined" if agreed or reasonably estimable, including post-closing adjustments and contingent payments that can be reasonably estimated.

**Debt retired.** The PNO's 2021 position: retirement of the target's debt is part of the acquisition price where the selling shareholders benefit from it (Treatment of Debt as Consideration, Aug. 26, 2021). Record who benefits and the source.

**Voting securities (801.10(a)).** Listed: the greater of market price and acquisition price (market price alone if the acquisition price is not determined). Unlisted: the acquisition price, or fair market value if it is not determined. Market price (801.10(c)(1)): the lowest closing quotation within the 45 calendar days before consummation, not earlier than the day before signing (non-801.30 deals), or before the 803.5(a) notice or consummation (801.30 deals).

**Fair market value (801.10(c)(3)).** Determined in good faith by the board of the acquiring person's ultimate parent entity (or its delegate) as of a day within 60 calendar days before filing, or before closing if no filing is made. A stale determination cannot be relied on.

**Non-corporate interests conferring control (801.10(d)).** Acquisition price of the interests acquired plus fair market value of interests already held in the same entity.

**Aggregation (801.13).**
- All voting securities of the issuer the acquirer will hold after closing are "held as a result of" the acquisition; previously held shares are valued at market price (listed) or fair market value (unlisted) and added (801.13(a)).
- Assets: assets from the same acquired person under a letter of intent or agreement within the previous 180 days are added if that earlier acquisition was not itself subject to the Act (801.13(b)(2)).
- Previously held voting securities are not added to a later asset-only acquisition if the earlier acquisition was filed or exempt under 802.21 (801.13(a)(3)).
- A non-controlling acquisition of non-corporate interests is not aggregated with other assets or securities from the same acquired person (801.13(c)(2)).
- Holdings whose acquisition was exempt under the rules listed in 801.15 are not held as a result of the acquisition (for example 802.1, 802.2, 802.5, 802.30, 802.31; and 802.4, 802.9 and 802.64 only within their limits).

## 4. Identifying the persons: ultimate parent entity and control

- **Person** (801.1(a)(1)): an ultimate parent entity (UPE) and every entity it controls directly or indirectly. **UPE** (801.1(a)(3)): an entity not controlled by any other entity. A natural person can be a UPE. Two 50 percent holders are both UPEs of the held entity.
- **Control** (801.1(b)): holding 50 percent or more of an issuer's outstanding voting securities; or, for an unincorporated entity, the right to 50 percent or more of its profits or of its assets on dissolution; or the contractual power presently to designate 50 percent or more of a corporation's directors (or of trustees of certain trusts). Board-designation rights do not confer control of a partnership or LLC (801.1(b), example 2).
- **Acquiring person** (801.2(a)): the person that will hold the assets or securities, directly or through any entity. The signing vehicle, a newly formed merger subsidiary, and a fund's investment manager are not the filing person; the filing person is the UPE (Form Instructions, Definitions; 803.2(a)).
- **Acquired person** (801.2(b)): the person within which the target entity is included, not necessarily the seller of the shares (801.2(b), example 1).
- **Investment funds.** Each fund is usually its own UPE because no partner holds rights to 50 percent of its profits or assets; the general partner and manager are associates, not the UPE (801.1(d)(2)). Test every limited partner's percentage: an anchor investor with 50 percent or more of profits or dissolution assets controls the fund.
- **Natural persons.** Holdings of spouses and minor children are aggregated (801.1(c)(2)). A revocable trust's holdings belong to the settlor (801.1(c)(4)).

## 5. Non-corporate interests (LLCs, partnerships)

- An acquisition of non-corporate interests is reportable only if it confers control of the unincorporated entity (801.2(f)(1)); the acquirer is then deemed to acquire all of the entity's assets, and the acquired person is the entity's pre-acquisition UPE. PNO informal interpretation 2212002 (Dec. 9, 2022): if control is not acquired there is no filing, "regardless of the size of the transaction," read with 801.90.
- Control is measured only by the right to 50 percent or more of profits or of assets on dissolution (801.1(b)(1)(ii)). Read the waterfall and the dissolution clause of the operating or partnership agreement; a manager-appointment right does not settle it.
- Formation of an LLC or partnership: each contributor that acquires control of the new entity tests the transaction (801.50). Contribution of assets to an existing unincorporated entity is an acquisition by that entity's UPE (801.2(f)(2)).
- A later step that crosses 50 percent (a call option, a redemption of another member) is a new acquisition that may be reportable.

## 6. Size of person (801.11)

- Annual net sales from the last regularly prepared annual income statement; total assets from the last regularly prepared balance sheet; statements dated not more than 15 months before filing or closing; recompute to include any controlled entity not consolidated (801.11(b)-(c)).
- An acquiring person with no regularly prepared balance sheet (a new vehicle not controlled by anyone) counts assets held at closing less cash used as consideration and for expenses (801.11(e)). A new vehicle controlled by a fund or company is measured at its UPE.
- A natural person's assets include only investment assets, voting securities and other income-producing property (801.11(d)).

## 7. Exemptions and the conditions each one requires

| Exemption | Conditions to confirm on the facts | Common misuse |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary course goods (7A(c)(1); 802.1) | New goods; current supplies; used durable goods meeting 802.1(d) | Applied to all or substantially all assets of an operating unit, which is never ordinary course (802.1(a)) |
| Real property (802.2) | New facility never producing income; used facility from a financing lessor to the sole lessee; unproductive real property (no more than $5 million revenue in 36 months; no facility operating in the prior 12 months); office or residential property | Applied to the whole deal: other assets transferred with the realty are tested separately |
| Entities holding exempt assets (802.4) | Acquired entity's non-exempt assets have fair market value of $133.9 million (2026) or less | Non-exempt assets valued at book, or entities from the same acquired person not combined (802.4(b)) |
| Investment only (7A(c)(9); 802.9; 801.1(i)(1)) | Ten percent or less held after the acquisition, and no intention of participating in the formulation, determination or direction of the issuer's basic business decisions | Used above 10 percent; used despite plans to nominate a director, hold a board seat or office, propose action requiring shareholder approval, solicit proxies, or by a competitor (Statement of Basis and Purpose examples, as quoted in the FTC's August 2015 guidance) |
| Foreign assets (802.50) | Assets outside the United States that generated sales in or into the U.S. of $133.9 million (2026) or less in the acquired person's most recent fiscal year; or both persons foreign and under the $294.5 million combined U.S. sales and assets tests and the deal not over $535.5 million | Sales "into" the U.S. omitted; prior foreign-asset acquisitions within 180 days not combined |
| Foreign issuer (802.51) | U.S. acquirer: issuer's U.S. assets and U.S. sales each $133.9 million (2026) or less. Foreign acquirer: exempt unless control is acquired and U.S. assets or sales exceed $133.9 million; 802.51(c) both-foreign test | Control test skipped for foreign buyers; U.S. assets of the issuer's controlled subsidiaries left out |
| Already holding 50 percent (7A(c)(3)) | Acquirer held 50 percent or more of the issuer's voting securities before the acquisition | Applied to non-corporate interests or to a different issuer in the target group |
| No increase in percentage (7A(c)(10)) | Percentage held does not increase (for example pro rata stock dividend) | Applied to a pro rata rights offering the acquirer over-subscribes |
| Convertibles, options, warrants (802.31) | No present right to vote for directors | Conversion or exercise is itself a potential acquisition (801.30(a)(6)-(7)) |
| Prior filing within 5 years (802.21) | Waiting period observed for a lower threshold within 5 years, and the next threshold not crossed | Relied on after the 1-year window of 803.7(a) to cross the filed threshold |

## 8. Avoidance devices (801.90)

Any transaction or device used to avoid the Act is disregarded and the obligation is determined by the substance. Splitting one acquisition into several below-threshold steps, interposing a sham vehicle, or timing closings to fall under a threshold are examples in the rule's own illustrations. This skill does not design such structures (SKILL.md, Ground Rule 9).
