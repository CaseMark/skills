## 2. Filing mechanics: who files, affidavit, certification, fee

- **Filer.** The UPE, or an entity within the person authorized by it (803.2(a)(1)). In a merger or a purchase from the issuer, both persons file and the waiting period starts when the last filing is received. In 801.30 acquisitions (open-market purchases, tender offers, purchases from third parties, conversions, certain option exercises) the waiting period starts on the acquiring person's filing and the acquired person must file by 5 p.m. Eastern on the 15th calendar day (10th for a cash tender offer) after receipt, rolling to the next business day if that day is a weekend or federal holiday (801.30(b)).
- **Affidavit (803.5).** Non-801.30 deals: an executed contract, agreement in principle or letter of intent, and the filer's good-faith intention to complete the transaction. 801.30 deals: written notice to the issuer under 803.5(a)(1) and, for a tender offer, public announcement of the intention to make it.
- **Certification (803.6).** By an officer or director (corporation), general partner (partnership), an individual exercising similar functions, or the natural person; the certifier must have actual authority. The Instructions require notarization or a 28 U.S.C. 1746 declaration.
- **Fee.** Paid by the acquiring person to the FTC (803.9(a)), tier from `references/thresholds-valuation-and-exemptions.md`. Submission through Kiteworks; the PNO no longer accepts paper or DVD filings.
- **Incomplete responses.** Each item not fully answered needs a statement of reasons for noncompliance (803.3), or the filing is deficient and the waiting period does not start (803.10(c)(2)).

## 3. Item 4(c) and 4(d) documents (pre-February 10, 2025 form)

| Item | Scope (Instructions rev. 02/04/23) | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| 4(c) | Studies, surveys, analyses and reports prepared by or for any officer or director (or individuals with similar functions) for the purpose of evaluating or analyzing the acquisition with respect to market shares, competition, competitors, markets, potential for sales growth or expansion into product or geographic markets | None |
| 4(d)(i) | Confidential information memoranda prepared by or for officers or directors that specifically relate to the sale of the acquired entity or assets; if none, documents given to the buyer's officers or directors that served that function | Produced up to one year before filing |
| 4(d)(ii) | Studies by investment bankers, consultants or other third-party advisors for officers or directors, on the same subjects as 4(c), that specifically relate to the sale; includes materials prepared to seek the engagement | Up to one year before filing |
| 4(d)(iii) | Studies evaluating or analyzing synergies or efficiencies prepared by or for officers or directors for the purpose of evaluating the acquisition; financial models without stated assumptions excluded | None |

Collection rules:
- Search the files of every officer and director of the UPE and of the acquiring or acquired entity, and of the deal team members who prepared material for them. "Prepared for" includes drafts and decks sent to a director. Title, privilege status or "draft" label does not remove a document from scope.
- Withheld or redacted documents need a privilege log meeting 803.3(d).
- Index every responsive document: title, date, author, recipients (officer or director), item, produced or withheld.
- A filing that omits responsive documents does not comply with the rules: the agencies may treat it as deficient, so the waiting period has not started (803.10(c)(2)), and the filer is exposed to penalties under 18a(g)(1). A document discovered after filing goes to counsel to decide on supplementing or refiling.

If the client elects the 2025 form, use that form's own instructions; this file does not summarize them `[VERIFY]`.

## 4. Waiting period computation

Rules (15 U.S.C. 18a(b)(1), (k); 16 C.F.R. 803.10):

| Element | Trigger event | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Date of receipt | Electronic submission | The submission date if it is a business day and completed by 5 p.m. Eastern; otherwise the next business day. Receipt is not effected on a weekend, a legal public holiday, or a holiday's observed date. If filings are not all effected the same day, the latest date governs (803.10(c)(1)) |
| Start, non-801.30 deals | Receipt of the last required filing from all persons | 803.10(a)(3) |
| Start, 801.30 deals | Receipt of the acquiring person's filing | 801.30(b)(1); 803.10(a)(1) |
| Acquired person's 801.30 filing | Receipt of the acquiring person's filing | Due 5 p.m. Eastern on day 15 (day 10 for a cash tender offer) |
| Initial waiting period end | Start date (day 0) | 11:59 p.m. Eastern on the 30th calendar day; the 15th for a cash tender offer or an acquisition under 11 U.S.C. 363(b) (803.10(b)(1)) |
| Weekend or holiday | Calculated end date | If it falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal public holiday under 5 U.S.C. 6103(a), the period extends to 11:59 p.m. Eastern of the next regular business day (803.10(b)(3); 18a(k)) |
| After a second request | Receipt of all requested material from all recipients (substantial compliance certified) | 11:59 p.m. Eastern on the 30th day (10th for a cash tender offer or 363(b) acquisition), or the original end date if later (803.10(b)(2)) |

Holiday detail: the PNO states that when a holiday is observed on a day other than the actual date (July 4 on a Sunday, observed Monday July 5), the agency is closed on the observed day but waiting periods still expire that day; when the holiday falls on its actual date, waiting periods roll to the next business day. A cash tender offer is one in which cash is the only consideration (801.1(g)(2)); a cash-and-stock exchange offer gets 30 days.

Fixed format (use exactly):

```
Filing(s) submitted:      <date> (<weekday>) <time> ET by <person>; <date> ... by <person>
Date of receipt:          <date> (<weekday>)   [rule applied]
Waiting period length:    30 days | 15 days (cash tender offer or 363(b))   [reason]
Raw end date:             <date> (<weekday>)
Expiry:                   11:59 p.m. ET <date> (<weekday>)   [rolled because <reason> | not rolled]
Earliest lawful closing:  after expiry, or on notice of early termination
Notification expires:     1 year after expiry (803.7(a)) on <date>
```

## 5. Early termination, second requests, expiry and refiling

- **Early termination** is discretionary and granted only on a filer's written request or sua sponte (803.11(c)), generally after all required notifications are received and the agencies have decided not to issue a second request (803.11(a)). A second request denies pending ET requests (803.11(b)). Grants are published in the Federal Register (18a(b)(2)); grants for September 1-30, 2026 were published on October 22, 2026, so ET is being granted in 2026. Never schedule closing on ET.
- **Second request** may issue before the initial period ends (18a(e)); it extends the period as in section 4. Timing agreements with the agencies may delay closing further.
- **Expiry of the notification**: one year after the waiting period expires (803.7(a)); after that, crossing the filed threshold requires a new filing. A notification also expires 18 months after receipt if a second request remains outstanding (803.7(b)).
- **Withdraw and refile (803.12)**: one refiling without a new fee if withdrawn before expiry or a second request, with no material change, and refiled by the close of the second business day after withdrawal.

## 6. Pre-closing conduct (gun-jumping)

The Act bars acquiring beneficial ownership before the waiting period ends (18a(a)); exercising operational control of the target before expiry violates it, and agreements to coordinate competitive conduct can also violate Sherman Act section 1. Penalty up to $53,088 per day (2025 level, unchanged for 2026).

Enforcement examples:
- United States v. Computer Associates International, Inc. (D.D.C., settled 2002): merger agreement required buyer approval for customer discounts over 20 percent off list and changes to standard contract terms, and a buyer employee was placed at the target to review contracts; $638,000 penalty and an injunction.
- United States v. XCL Resources Holdings, LLC et al. (D.D.C., FTC announced Jan. 7, 2025): buyers stopped the target's planned drilling, coordinated customer contracts and coordinated prices during the waiting period; $5.6 million civil penalty for 94 days in violation.

| Interim covenant or integration step | Treatment before the waiting period expires |
|---|---|
| Ordinary-course covenant; buyer consent for extraordinary acts that protect the deal's value (material acquisitions or dispositions, new debt, charter changes, capital spending above a stated amount, settling material litigation) | Generally acceptable; draft with value-protecting purpose stated |
| Buyer approval of target prices, discounts, bids, customer or supplier contracts in the ordinary course | Not acceptable; remove |
| Buyer personnel placed at the target with approval authority; joint sales calls; telling customers about combined pricing; reallocating territories or customers; hiring or firing target staff at the buyer's direction | Not acceptable before expiry |
| Exchange of current or future prices, costs, bids, strategic plans with a competitor | Only through a clean team of non-operational personnel, aggregated or historical where possible, under a written protocol (FTC Competition Matters, Mar. 20, 2018) |
| Integration planning (not implementation) | Acceptable when it does not transfer control and uses clean-team limits for sensitive data |
| Pre-closing payment of purchase price, or the buyer bearing the target's profits and losses before closing | Treat as a possible transfer of beneficial ownership; escalate `[VERIFY]` |
