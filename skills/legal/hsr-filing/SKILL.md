# Hart-Scott-Rodino Premerger Notification

Determines whether a proposed acquisition must be reported under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act, and if so prepares the filing workplan, the Item 4(c)/4(d) document index, the waiting-period computation and a review of pre-closing conduct. The deliverable is a reportability determination with the valuation, party and exemption analysis behind it, ranked blocking issues, a reconciliation table, the filing workplan, the closing timeline, and hand-offs. Its aim is accurate, timely notification and lawful conduct until the waiting period ends. Everything it produces is a draft for review by antitrust counsel; it never files, certifies or pays anything.

## Why This Skill Exists

HSR errors are rarely about antitrust theory. They come from arithmetic and identity: a headline price used where assumed liabilities and deferred payments belong, earlier stock purchases left out of the total, a fund manager named as the filer, a board seat treated as LLC control, an exemption relied on without its conditions, a waiting period counted from the wrong day, or interim covenants that hand the buyer control before the waiting period ends. The thresholds change every February, and the 2025 form was vacated in 2026. Each mistake can mean a missed filing, a deficient one, or penalties of up to $53,088 per day. This skill puts those checks in front of counsel before the filing and before closing.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase agreement price, funds-flow memo, assumed-liabilities schedule | Size of transaction and fee tier | Headline cash used alone; assumed accrued liabilities, non-compete payments, debt payoff benefiting sellers, or deferred payments left out; deferred payments discounted to present value; board fair market value older than 60 days |
| Cap table, share register, broker statements, earlier purchase agreements | Aggregation of holdings | Only the new tranche valued; previously held voting securities omitted (801.13(a)); asset purchases from the same seller within 180 days not combined; fee tier computed on the increment |
| Org chart, fund and LP agreements, LLC operating agreement | Acquiring and acquired person, ultimate parent entity, control | Signing vehicle or fund manager named as the filer; an anchor LP holding 50 percent of fund profits missed; LLC control judged by manager seats instead of profits or dissolution assets; acquired person taken to be the seller rather than the target's UPE |
| Exemption memo or client assertion ("investment only", "ordinary course", "real estate", "foreign") | Exemption screen | Investment-only claimed with a board nomination plan or above 10 percent; ordinary course claimed for an operating unit; real property exemption applied to the plant equipment too; foreign issuer exemption without the U.S. sales and assets test |
| Prior-year memo, deal-team threshold chart | Thresholds and fee | Last year's figures; the figures in effect at signing used although closing falls after the next February adjustment |
| Interim operating covenants, integration plan, clean-team protocol | Pre-closing conduct | Buyer approval of ordinary-course prices, discounts or bids; buyer staff installed at the target; joint customer messaging or territory changes before expiry; competitively sensitive data shared outside a clean team |
| Filing confirmations and the calendar | Waiting period and closing date | Counted from submission after 5 p.m. ET; 15 days used for a cash-and-stock exchange offer; weekend or holiday roll ignored; closing planned on early termination; 801.30 start rule missed |
| Board decks, banker books, CIMs, synergy models, officer and director files | Item 4(c) and 4(d) | Search limited to final board books; drafts and decks prepared for a director skipped; one-year limit applied to 4(c) or 4(d)(iii), which have none; no privilege log |
| FTC forms page, eCFR text, prior filings | Form version and item scope | Treating the vacated 2025 form as mandatory, or the old form as unavailable; relying on eCFR text that still shows the vacated 2024 amendments |
| Joint venture term sheet, target's investment schedule | Formation filings (801.40, 801.50) and secondary acquisitions (801.4) | Corporate JV tested as an ordinary stock purchase: contributors not treated as acquiring persons, committed future contributions and guarantees left out of the JV's assets, 801.40(c) size-of-person variant skipped; the target's non-controlling stakes in other issuers not tested as separate secondary acquisitions |
| Engagement letter, transaction structure memo | Who must file, who pays, which acquisitions are in scope | Only the buyer filing in a merger; the target's own filing in an 801.30 deal missed; the fee assumed to be split by law; a later step-up to control left out of scope |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is filed, certified, paid or closed by this skill. Every output is a draft for antitrust counsel, and no filing is called complete or ready to submit.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (closing date, holdings), *source conflict* (two prices), *unverified external lookup* (a threshold or form status not re-checked today), or *policy election* (form version, fee allocation).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For facts: executed agreements and schedules, then audited financial statements and board resolutions, then registers and funds-flow memos, then emails and summaries. For law: the statute, then the rules, then the Form Instructions, then PNO guidance and informal interpretations (staff views, not binding), then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every figure, date and percentage carries a source locator (document and section or page) or is marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every legal statement cites `references/primary-sources.md` or is marked `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a rule cite, informal interpretation number or enforcement matter.
5. **Dated thresholds.** Every annually adjusted figure is stated with its notice and effective date (2026 figures: 91 Fed. Reg. 2133, effective February 17, 2026) and an instruction to re-check. The figures in effect at closing govern; if closing may fall after the next annual adjustment, test both years.
6. **Full value, full holdings.** Value is everything the acquiring person will hold as a result of the acquisition, including previously held securities and every component of consideration, under `references/thresholds-valuation-and-exemptions.md`. Never treat the headline price as the value.
7. **Persons, not signatories.** Reportability, size of person and filing run at the ultimate parent entity. Show the control chain and the control test applied at each link.
8. **Exemptions only with every condition.** An exemption is relied on only when each of its conditions is met by a cited fact. An assertion by the client is a fact to verify, not a condition met.
9. **No avoidance, no early control.** Do not design structures, splits or timing meant to avoid the Act (801.90). Do not draft covenants, side letters or integration steps that give the buyer operational control or coordinate competitive conduct before the waiting period ends. Decline that part, explain why, and draft the compliant alternative.
10. **Clocks from their own triggers.** Each timed element (receipt, start, expiry, acquired person's 801.30 filing, notification expiry, fair market value window) is computed from its own trigger in the fixed format, with weekday, time and ET.
11. **Form version stated.** Say which form is used and why, with the status date. Do not apply 2025-form requirements as mandatory or treat them as forbidden.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date, signing date (or status of letter of intent), expected closing date and outside date.
2. Transaction type: merger, stock purchase from issuer or from holders, asset purchase, non-corporate interest, formation of a joint venture, open-market purchase, tender or exchange offer (cash only or mixed).
3. Every component of consideration: cash, stock, assumed liabilities, debt repaid, deferred, escrowed or contingent payments, non-compete or consulting payments.
4. Acquiring and acquired persons: ownership chain to each UPE, fund documents, LLC or partnership agreements (profit and dissolution percentages).
5. Holdings the acquirer already has in the target or seller, and acquisitions from the same seller in the last 180 days.
6. Latest regularly prepared financial statements of each UPE (date, net sales, total assets) and U.S. sales and assets for any foreign target.
7. Any exemption the client or seller relies on, with the facts behind it.
8. Interim operating covenants, integration plans and information-sharing arrangements.
9. Officer and director custodians and the document repositories for Item 4(c)/4(d).

**Defaults if not supplied** (a rule overrides any default it addresses): pre-February 10, 2025 form; fee paid by the acquiring person (803.9(a)) unless the agreement allocates it; closing assumed after the full waiting period, never on early termination; filing submitted by 5 p.m. ET on a business day; a contingent payment that can be reasonably estimated is included at its estimated face amount, not discounted, and one that cannot be estimated sends the value to fair market value (PNO valuation guidance), with the open item noted.

**Hard stops (label "Declined: hard stop" for the affected request and produce the request list or compliant alternative):**
- The ownership chain is unknown, so neither UPE can be identified: no reportability conclusion; produce the request list.
- No price, term sheet or fair market value basis exists: no size-of-transaction conclusion; produce the valuation request.
- A request to structure, split or time a transaction to avoid filing, or to omit or alter responsive Item 4 documents.
- A request to draft covenants, side letters or integration steps that give the buyer operational control or coordinate prices, bids, customers or territories before the waiting period ends.
- A request to certify, submit or represent that a filing is complete or accepted.

**Not hard stops:** a missing board fair market value when the acquisition price is determined; an unsigned deal with a draft agreement (analyze reportability, note that the affidavit needs an executed agreement or letter of intent); an open form-version election.

## Step 1: Classify the Transaction and the Persons

Read `references/thresholds-valuation-and-exemptions.md`, sections 4 to 6. Build the party table to each UPE, applying the control test link by link. For LLCs and partnerships, read the profit and dissolution provisions; an acquisition of non-corporate interests that does not confer control is not reportable. For a new corporation or LLC, apply 801.40 or 801.50; list the target's non-controlling stakes in other issuers as possible 801.4 secondary acquisitions. Decide whether 801.30 applies.

## Step 2: Value the Acquisition

Read section 3 of the same file. Build the valuation worksheet component by component with source locators, add previously held securities and 180-day asset purchases, and record which components were excluded and why. If two sources disagree, both go in the reconciliation table.

## Step 3: Apply the Thresholds and Size of Person

Read section 1 (re-check the figures and record the date) and section 2. Run the test in the fixed format of `references/output-template.md`, Part 1. If closing may fall after the next annual adjustment, run it under both years or mark the later year `[VERIFY]`.

## Step 4: Screen the Exemptions

Read section 7. For every exemption raised or plausible, list each condition and the fact that meets or fails it. Check 801.15 before excluding exempt holdings from the value, and 801.90 before accepting a multi-step structure.

## Step 5: Determine and Assign the Fee

Label each acquisition with the Part 1 vocabulary. For a reportable acquisition, take the fee tier from the aggregate value held, name the payer, and identify the notification threshold for voting securities.

## Step 6: Plan the Filing and the Item 4 Documents

Read `references/waiting-period-and-conduct.md`, sections 1 to 3. State the form version and its status date. List filers, certifier and affidavit. Collect the Item 3(b) transaction documents (executed agreement or letter of intent, non-competes and other agreements between the parties). Build the custodian list and the Item 4(c)/4(d) index; log withheld documents under 803.3(d). Note the Item 8 prior acquisitions and parallel notices.

## Step 7: Compute the Waiting Period and Closing Timeline

Read sections 4 and 5 of the same file. Compute receipt, start, expiry and notification expiry in the fixed format, with each element's own trigger. Treat early termination as possible but never as the plan.

## Step 8: Review Pre-Closing Conduct

Read section 6. Mark each interim covenant and integration step acceptable or not, redraft what is not, and apply the gun-jumping hard stop to any request for early control.

## Step 9: Assemble the Deliverable and Hand-Offs

Lay out the header and Parts 1 to 6 per `references/output-template.md`. Complete each hand-off with facts. List every `[VERIFY]` item from `references/primary-sources.md` that the draft relies on.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Are all components of consideration and all prior holdings captured, as counsel and the deal team understand the deal?
2. Is each UPE and control conclusion consistent with the fund and entity documents?
3. Does any exemption rest on a fact the client has not documented?
4. Does the closing date fall within the threshold year tested, and before the notification expires?
5. Are the interim covenants and integration plan consistent with the waiting period?

If the user does not answer, recommend settling value and UPE identity first; they decide whether there is a filing at all.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. Each acquisition carries exactly one Part 1 label, and the fixed-format test supports it.
2. The value includes every consideration component and prior holding, each with a source, and excluded items state the rule.
3. Every threshold and fee figure states the notice, effective date and a re-check instruction; the closing-year question is addressed.
4. Size of person is applied only in the band where the statute requires it, using the UPEs' qualifying statements.
5. Each UPE is identified with the control test shown; LLC and partnership control uses profits or dissolution assets.
6. Each exemption relied on lists every condition with a cited fact; none rests on an assertion alone.
7. Each clock is computed from its own trigger in the fixed format with weekday, time and ET, including weekend and holiday rolls.
8. The plan does not depend on early termination.
9. The form version and its status date are stated; no vacated requirement is applied as mandatory.
10. The Item 4(c)/4(d) index covers officer and director custodians, drafts, and a privilege log.
11. No covenant or integration step gives the buyer operational control or coordinates competitive conduct before expiry; any such request carries the hard-stop label and a compliant alternative.
12. Every figure has a source locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`, every legal statement a citation or `[VERIFY]`, and the reconciliation table shows each conflict.
13. The output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Lead with the answer: the label, the value, the threshold and the fee, then the analysis.
- Keep reportability separate from substantive merger review. Market definition and competitive effects belong to counsel; this skill reports overlaps factually.
- HSR filings are confidential under 18a(h), but documents written now may become Item 4(c) documents; advise deal teams to describe competition accurately and without overstatement.
- When the user's figure or instruction conflicts with a rule, show the compliant result and explain the difference; do not silently follow or silently change it.
- U.S. federal notification only; foreign merger control and CFIUS go to open items.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Price differs between the agreement and the funds-flow memo | Adjustments, debt payoff or fees netted differently | Use the agreement; show both in the reconciliation table; include debt payoff that benefits sellers |
| Cap table unreadable or stale | Scan or pre-closing version | OCR; request the current register; mark holdings `[UNVERIFIED]` and show the value with and without them |
| Fund documents silent on profit percentages | Side letters or series interests | Request the waterfall and side letters; label the UPE question Undetermined: open items |
| Financial statements older than 15 months | New entity or late audit | Use 801.11(e) for an entity without a balance sheet; otherwise request current statements |
| Client insists an exemption applies | Practice habit or prior deal | Run the condition table; if a condition is unconfirmed, label Undetermined: open items |
| Closing date uncertain around February | Next annual adjustment | Test under current figures and flag the re-test at the new effective date |
| eCFR text conflicts with the FTC forms page | eCFR still shows vacated 2024 amendments | Follow the PNO's posted form and mark the rule text `[VERIFY]` |
| Filing submitted late in the day | After 5 p.m. ET | Receipt is the next business day; recompute every clock |
