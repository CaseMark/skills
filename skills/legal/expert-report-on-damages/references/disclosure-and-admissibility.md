## 1. Rule 26(a)(2)(B) report checklist

A retained or specially employed testifying expert must provide a written report "prepared and signed by the witness," unless stipulated or ordered otherwise. Map each element to a report section and a status.

| Element (Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(B)) | Report section | Common defect |
|---|---|---|
| (i) complete statement of all opinions and the basis and reasons for them | Opinions; methodology; calculations | Opinion stated as a conclusion without the steps; opinions added later at deposition |
| (ii) the facts or data considered in forming them | Materials considered (appendix) | Lists only documents "relied upon"; omits interviews, data rejected, counsel-supplied data |
| (iii) exhibits that will be used to summarize or support them | Schedules and exhibits | Trial demonstratives built later from figures not in the report |
| (iv) qualifications, including all publications authored in the previous 10 years | CV appendix | Publications list limited to peer-reviewed articles or to the last 5 years |
| (v) all other cases in which, during the previous 4 years, the witness testified as an expert at trial or by deposition | Testimony list | Depositions omitted; only trials listed; cases outside damages work omitted |
| (vi) statement of the compensation to be paid for the study and testimony | Compensation paragraph | Rate omitted; staff rates omitted; fee contingent on outcome |

Also confirm against the scheduling order and any stipulation, which may change the content or format. Count the 10-year and 4-year windows back from the date the report is signed and served, use the earlier start date if those differ, and give each entry its date `[VERIFY: local practice on the anchor date]`.

## 2. Facts or data considered, and counsel's assumptions

- "Facts or data" is interpreted broadly: any material considered by the expert, from whatever source, that contains factual ingredients; the obligation extends to facts or data considered, not only those relied upon (Advisory Committee Note to the 2010 amendment).
- Communications with counsel that identify facts or data counsel provided and the expert considered, or assumptions counsel provided and the expert relied on, are discoverable (Rule 26(b)(4)(C)(ii) and (iii)). State each counsel-provided assumption in the report as an assumption provided by counsel, with what changes if it is wrong.
- Materials the expert reviewed and rejected (an opposing market study, a superseded projection) are listed as considered, with a sentence on why they were not used.

## 3. Drafts and attorney-expert communications

| Item | Protected? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Drafts of the report or disclosure, in any form | Yes, work-product protection | Rule 26(b)(4)(B) |
| Communications between the party's attorney and a reporting expert | Yes, except the three topics below | Rule 26(b)(4)(C) |
| Communications about compensation for the study or testimony | No | Rule 26(b)(4)(C)(i) |
| Facts or data counsel provided that the expert considered | No | Rule 26(b)(4)(C)(ii) |
| Assumptions counsel provided that the expert relied on | No | Rule 26(b)(4)(C)(iii) |
| The expert's own testing, analyses and notes of testing | No | 2010 Note: development, foundation and basis remain discoverable |
| Communications between the expert and anyone other than the party's counsel (the client's CFO, managers, other consultants) | Not protected by Rule 26(b)(4)(C) | 2010 Note |
| Alternative analyses or approaches, considered or not | Counsel may question the expert about them | 2010 Note |

Practical rule for the draft: write nothing in a draft or cover note to counsel that would be harmful if the protection were lost, keep management interview notes as factual records, and list them as considered.

## 4. Timing, rebuttal, supplementation and sanctions

| Event | Trigger | Default period | Override |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expert disclosure and report | Trial date, or date set for the case to be ready for trial | At least 90 days before (Rule 26(a)(2)(D)(i)) | Scheduling order or stipulation controls and usually sets earlier, staggered dates |
| Rebuttal report (solely to contradict or rebut the same subject matter) | The other party's Rule 26(a)(2)(B) or (C) disclosure | Within 30 days after it (Rule 26(a)(2)(D)(ii)) | Scheduling order or stipulation |
| Deposition of a reporting expert | Service of the report | Only after the report is provided (Rule 26(b)(4)(A)) | Court order |
| Supplementation of the report and deposition testimony | Learning the disclosure is incomplete or incorrect in a material respect | Timely; and by the time Rule 26(a)(3) pretrial disclosures are due (Rule 26(e)(2)) | Court order |
| Pretrial disclosures | Trial date | At least 30 days before trial (Rule 26(a)(3)(B)) | Court order |

Failure to disclose or supplement: the party may not use the information or witness on a motion, at a hearing or at trial unless the failure was substantially justified or harmless, and other sanctions may follow (Rule 37(c)(1)). An opinion that appears first at deposition or trial is at risk.

Initial disclosures already required "a computation of each category of damages claimed" with supporting documents (Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iii)); reconcile the expert's categories and totals to that computation, or explain the change so counsel can supplement.

## 5. FRE 702 as amended December 1, 2023

Text (effective December 1, 2023, per the Supreme Court's order of April 24, 2023): a qualified expert may testify "if the proponent demonstrates to the court that it is more likely than not that" (a) the expert's specialized knowledge will help the trier of fact; (b) the testimony is based on sufficient facts or data; (c) the testimony is the product of reliable principles and methods; and (d) the expert's opinion reflects a reliable application of the principles and methods to the facts of the case.

What the 2023 Committee Note adds, and how the report responds:

| Note point | Report response |
|---|---|
| The proponent must show each requirement by a preponderance (Rule 104(a)); rulings treating sufficiency of basis and application of method as weight, not admissibility, were "an incorrect application" | Show the basis and the application, step by step, for each opinion; do not rely on cross-examination to cure gaps |
| Once the court finds the requirement met, remaining attacks go to weight; experts may reach different conclusions on contested facts | Identify contested facts and show the result under each version |
| 702(d): each opinion must stay within what a reliable application of the basis and method can support | Opinions no broader than the calculations; ranges where the data support only a range |
| The amendment does not bar testimony that comports with substantive law requiring a particular degree of certainty | Use a "reasonable degree of certainty" phrase only where the governing law requires it, and never as a substitute for showing the basis |

Gatekeeping applies to all expert testimony, not only scientific (Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, 526 U.S. 137 (1999), as described in the 2000 Committee Note). Daubert factors (testing, peer review, error rate, standards, general acceptance) are non-exclusive; the 2000 Note adds others relevant to damages work: whether the opinion grew out of research independent of the litigation, unjustified extrapolation from an accepted premise, accounting for obvious alternative explanations, and whether the expert is as careful as in regular professional work.

Damages-specific exclusion patterns: projections whose author and assumptions the expert cannot explain (ZF Meritor); assumptions without record foundation (Elcock); analytical gap between data and conclusion (Joiner); license evidence that does not support the rate drawn from it (EcoFactor, applying Rule 702(b)); rules of thumb untied to the facts (Uniloc).

## 6. FRE 703 and FRE 1006

- FRE 703: an expert may base an opinion on facts or data that experts in the field would reasonably rely on, even if inadmissible; inadmissible underlying facts may be disclosed to the jury only if their probative value in helping evaluate the opinion substantially outweighs their prejudicial effect. The 2023 Note to Rule 702 says sufficiency of basis is decided under Rule 702; Rule 703's reasonable-reliance inquiry is narrower.
- FRE 1006 (as amended effective December 1, 2024): a summary, chart or calculation may be admitted to prove the content of voluminous admissible writings, with the originals made available; a summary that functions only as an illustrative aid is governed by Rule 107. Damages schedules intended as evidence should trace to admissible underlying records.

## 7. State standards

State courts apply their own versions. Examples, each to be confirmed for the forum and date `[VERIFY]`: some states follow Daubert-style rules; others keep a general-acceptance (Frye) test for novel methods; California trial courts act as gatekeepers against speculative expert testimony under Evidence Code sections 801 and 802 (Sargon, 55 Cal. 4th 747 (2012)). State disclosure rules may not require a full written report, or may require more. Do not assume federal Rule 26(a)(2)(B) or the 2023 Rule 702 text applies in state court.

## 8. Professional standards and compensation

- AICPA Statement on Standards for Forensic Services No. 1 (effective for engagements accepted on or after January 1, 2020) applies to AICPA members in litigation engagements: professional competence, due care, planning and supervision, sufficient relevant data; integrity and objectivity, and a member "should not subordinate his or her opinion to that of any other party"; no expert opinions on a contingent fee unless the AICPA contingent-fee rule allows it; no opinion on the ultimate conclusion of fraud.
- Non-CPA experts may follow other bodies' standards (NACVA, ASA, economics practice); name the standards actually followed.
