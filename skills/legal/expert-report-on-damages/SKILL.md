# Expert Report on Economic Damages

Prepares, or reviews, a retained damages expert's written report for U.S. litigation, with a readiness memo that tests it against Rule 26(a)(2)(B), FRE 702 as amended December 1, 2023, and the economics of the measure counsel has identified. Its aim is an independent, reproducible quantification that the testifying expert can adopt and defend; it declines requests to reach a figure chosen in advance. The deliverable is a readiness determination, ranked blocking issues, a reconciliation table with tie-outs and an overlap matrix, the draft report, schedules, and hand-offs. Everything it produces is a draft for review by the testifying expert and counsel.

## Why This Skill Exists

Damages opinions are excluded or cut down far more often for how the number was built than for the expert's credentials. Since December 1, 2023, Rule 702 states that the proponent must show the court it is more likely than not that the opinion rests on sufficient facts or data and reliably applies its method; the Committee Note calls rulings that treated those questions as weight "an incorrect application." The usual failures are mechanical: a CFO's litigation projection adopted untested, gross margin used where incremental margin belongs, lost profits added to a business value that already counts them, interest charged on losses that have not happened yet, or a testimony list that leaves out depositions. Each can exclude the opinion under Rule 702 or Rule 37(c)(1), or hand the other side its cross-examination. This skill catches them before the report is signed.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Management projections, budgets, business plans | But-for revenue and profit | Projection prepared after the dispute began and adopted without asking who built it, on what assumptions, or how earlier budgets compared with actual results; CFO-to-expert emails treated as privileged although Rule 26(b)(4)(C) protects only communications with the party's attorney |
| Income statements, general ledger, tax returns, cost detail | Baseline, margins, avoided costs | Gross margin used as the incremental margin; commissions, freight and a lease shed after the event not deducted; one-time items and owner compensation not normalized; book and tax figures mixed |
| Pleadings, contract, counsel's statement of claims and plaintiff entity | Measure, scope, and whose losses count | Parent or consolidated-group losses measured for a subsidiary plaintiff; dismissed claims or periods outside the limitations period or contract term included; consequential-damages waiver or liability cap ignored; model not tied to the liability theory |
| Post-event actual results and mitigation evidence (replacement contracts, new customers) | Actual-world comparison and offsets | But-for and actual periods of different length or basis (fiscal year against calendar quarters, accrual against cash); replacement profit ignored, or deducted without asking whether the plaintiff had capacity for both (lost volume) |
| Several damages categories in one report | The total | Lost profits through trial added to a business value as of the breach date that already capitalizes those years; defendant's profits added to lost profits on the same sales; royalty and lost profits on the same units |
| Discount rate, valuation date, prejudgment interest instruction | Present value and bringing past losses forward | Future losses left undiscounted or discounted twice (haircut flows plus a risk premium); interest charged on losses after the valuation date; the expert choosing the statutory rate, accrual date or compounding, which the law and the court decide (N.Y. C.P.L.R. 5001 and 5004) |
| New-venture history, yardstick comparables, market-share studies | Proof with reasonable certainty | "Comparable" firms that differ in size, maturity or market; market share assumed rather than derived; state new-business rules ignored (Teletron, Kenford, Sargon) |
| Industry data, timeline of other events | Causation and isolating the harmful act | Before-and-after comparison that blames the defendant for a market downturn, pandemic or competitor entry; no figures for the combinations of acts a jury may find unlawful |
| Rule 26(a)(2)(B) disclosure, CV, testimony list, engagement letter | Report contents and admissibility | Only documents "relied on" listed instead of all facts or data considered; depositions left off the 4-year list; publications cut to 5 years; counsel-supplied assumptions presented as the expert's own; contingent compensation |
| Opinion language and the governing admissibility standard | Getting the opinion admitted | "Reasonable degree of economic certainty" used in place of showing basis and application; opinion broader than the calculation supports; state court treated as bound by FRE 702 or federal Rule 26 |
| Licenses, royalty studies, the accused product's revenue | Reasonable royalty | 25 percent rule of thumb; entire product revenue used as the base without proof the patented feature drives demand; per-unit rate read into licenses that do not show it (Uniloc, EcoFactor) |
| Earnings history, worklife and survival data | Personal economic loss | Return-to-work or retirement assumptions with no record basis; inflation and discount rate treated inconsistently; tax treatment assumed without the governing law |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only, for the testifying expert.** Nothing is signed, served or filed by this skill. The opinions become the expert's only after the expert performs or directs the analysis and adopts the report, which Rule 26(a)(2)(B) requires to be "prepared and signed by the witness." The draft is never called final or ready to serve.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document or data set missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (post-event revenue for 2026), *source conflict* (ledger revenue differs from the tax return), *unverified external lookup* (a state's lost-profits rule or interest statute not confirmed), or *policy election* (a choice for counsel or the expert, such as the valuation date or lost-volume treatment).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For facts, in rough order (FJC Reference Guide, 4th ed.): audited statements and filed returns; records kept in the ordinary course; management reports in the ordinary course; budgets and plans; and last, projections or damages studies prepared for the litigation and interview statements. For law: the current rule or statute text and the scheduling order, then controlling cases, then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every figure, date and assumption carries a source locator (document, Bates, tab and cell) or is marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or is marked `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a case, rule, rate or pin cite.
5. **Counsel instructs on law; the expert quantifies.** The measure, the plaintiff entity, causation standards, contract caps, entitlement to and rate of prejudgment interest, and whether mitigation was reasonable are legal or factfinder questions. Record each instruction as an assumption with its source; the report never states a legal conclusion as an expert opinion.
6. **Projections are tested, not adopted.** Before relying on any projection, record who prepared it, when, for what purpose, its assumptions, and how earlier budgets compared with actual results. A projection that fails is not the basis of an opinion; present a supported alternative and show the projection only as a labeled sensitivity.
7. **One entity, one period, one basis.** But-for and actual figures cover the same entity, months and accounting basis. Lost profit is lost revenue times incremental margin, less other avoided costs; actual mitigation is measured the same way.
8. **No double counting.** Build the overlap matrix before any total. Categories that measure the same dollars for the same period are presented as alternatives or netted, never added.
9. **Time value done once.** Future losses are discounted at a rate matched to their risk, with risk counted once. Past losses are brought forward only as the law or counsel's instruction provides. No interest runs on losses after the valuation date. Report damages before interest; any interest schedule is separate, labeled, and follows counsel's written instruction.
10. **Complete disclosure.** Every Rule 26(a)(2)(B) element is present: all opinions with basis and reasons, all facts or data considered (including interviews and material reviewed and not used), exhibits, qualifications with 10 years of publications, every trial or deposition testimony in the previous 4 years, and compensation. Facts, data and assumptions supplied by counsel are identified as such (Rule 26(b)(4)(C)(ii) and (iii)).
11. **Independence; never back-solve.** No input is chosen, changed or dropped to reach a figure set by counsel, the client or a mediator, and no required disclosure is omitted or recast. The expert does not subordinate the opinion to any other party (AICPA SSFS No. 1). Decline such a request, explain why, and offer the compliant alternative: a sensitivity table over supported ranges.
12. **Opinions no broader than the analysis.** Each opinion stays within what a reliable application of its basis and method supports (Rule 702(d)). Use a range where the data support only a range. Use a certainty phrase only where the governing law requires one, and never in place of showing the basis.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; forum (federal or state court, or arbitration), case number, and the scheduling order's expert, rebuttal and trial dates.
2. Assignment: affirmative report, rebuttal report, or review of an existing draft; the retaining party.
3. Testifying expert: name, CV, publications for the previous 10 years, trial and deposition testimony for the previous 4 years, compensation terms.
4. Counsel's written statement of the claims, the measure for each, the plaintiff entity, contract limits, the damages period, the valuation date, and any interest instruction.
5. The Rule 26(a)(1)(A)(iii) damages computation already served.
6. Financial data for the plaintiff entity: three to five years before the event and all periods after it (statements, general ledger, tax returns, sales and cost detail).
7. Every projection, budget and plan, with author, date and purpose, and earlier budgets with the matching actual results.
8. Mitigation evidence: replacement contracts, new customers, capacity and staffing records.
9. Industry data, comparables, licenses, and, for a rebuttal, the opposing report with its schedules and data.
10. Every assumption counsel wants the expert to make, in writing.

**Defaults if not supplied** (the forum's rules and the governing law override any default they address): federal court applying Fed. R. Civ. P. 26 and FRE 702 as amended in 2023, marked `[VERIFY]` until the forum is confirmed (a state court applies its own disclosure and admissibility rules); valuation date at the expected trial date with a breach-date computation alongside, flagged as a legal question for counsel (contract damages are typically valued as of the breach date); prejudgment interest excluded from the damages opinion (the court or clerk adds whatever interest the governing law provides); figures in nominal dollars before taxes, with the tax question flagged; formal report tone.

**Hard stops (label the determination "Declined: hard stop"; produce the blocking-issues list and the request or compliant alternative, not the requested draft):**
- A request to reach or support a figure chosen in advance, to change or drop an input to hit a number, to present counsel-supplied facts, data or assumptions as the expert's own, or to omit or shorten a required Rule 26(a)(2)(B) element: decline that part and offer the compliant version.
- No identified claim, measure and plaintiff entity (no pleading and no counsel statement): produce the intake questions instead.
- No financial or transactional data at all for the plaintiff (or, for a rebuttal, no copy of the opposing report): produce the data request list instead.

**Not hard stops:** a testifying expert not yet named, or a missing CV, testimony list or compensation statement (Critical open items that block signing and service, not drafting); an untested projection (draft on a supported alternative, Critical item); missing post-event actuals (range or stated limitation); an unconfirmed state admissibility standard (`[VERIFY]` and the default).

## Step 1: Frame the Assignment

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, section 1, and `references/disclosure-and-admissibility.md`, section 4. Record each claim, its measure, the plaintiff entity, the period and the valuation date, each with its instruction source. Compute the disclosure and rebuttal deadlines from their own triggers. Reconcile the categories to the initial-disclosure computation.

## Step 2: Inventory and Validate the Data

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, section 4 (data validity). Fill the source-review register. Tie historical revenue to audited or filed statements; test completeness and internal consistency; classify every gap by gap class.

## Step 3: Build the But-For and Actual Worlds

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, sections 2, 3, 5 and 6. Choose the method (before-and-after, yardstick, market model, difference-in-differences) and say why it fits. Fill the period alignment table. Isolate the harmful act from other causes, and give figures for each combination of acts the factfinder may reach. For a new venture, apply the state's certainty rule and consider the lost-asset alternative.

## Step 4: Test Projections and Assumptions

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, section 4, and `references/disclosure-and-admissibility.md`, sections 2 and 3. Run the projection tests and the budget-to-actual table. Label every assumption by source: counsel-provided, management-provided, or the expert's own. List interviews and management communications as facts or data considered.

## Step 5: Margin, Avoided Costs and Mitigation

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, sections 3 and 7. Build the cost classification table and the incremental margin. Deduct costs actually shed. Quantify mitigation on the same basis and months; where lost volume is arguable, show damages with and without the offset and flag the question for counsel.

## Step 6: Other Measures

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, sections 9 and 11 to 13, as the claims require: lost business value, reasonable royalty and patent lost profits, unjust enrichment, personal economic loss. Hand class-wide models to expert-report-class-damages and personal injury wage-loss reports to economic-loss-report.

## Step 7: Remove Overlap and Total

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, section 8. Fill the overlap matrix. Choose one consistent framework for each overlapping pair, show the overlap amount, and total only non-overlapping categories, each as of a stated date.

## Step 8: Time Value and Interest

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, sections 9 and 10. Discount future losses to the valuation date with the formula, timing convention and rate components shown. Bring past losses forward only as the law or counsel instructs. Report damages before interest; put any instructed interest in a separate schedule.

## Step 9: Sensitivity, Opinions and Admissibility

Read `references/damages-methods.md`, section 14, and `references/disclosure-and-admissibility.md`, sections 5 to 7. Vary only uncertain inputs over supported ranges. Write each opinion no broader than its calculation. Fill the FRE 702 checklist for each opinion, or the state standard if it differs.

## Step 10: Assemble the Report and Disclosures

Read `references/disclosure-and-admissibility.md`, sections 1 to 3 and 8, and lay out the header and Parts 1 to 6 per `references/output-template.md`. Complete the Rule 26(a)(2)(B) checklist, the materials-considered list, the reconciliation table and tie-outs, and the hand-offs. List every `[VERIFY]` item with what would settle it, drawing on `references/primary-sources.md`.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Does counsel confirm the measure, plaintiff entity, period, valuation date and every legal instruction recorded as an assumption?
2. Has the expert reviewed each projection test and accepted or rejected each projection?
3. Are the testimony list, publications list and compensation statement complete and current to the service date?
4. Does the total reconcile to the initial-disclosure computation, or does counsel need to supplement it?
5. Has counsel confirmed the forum's disclosure and admissibility rules and the scheduling order dates?

If the user does not answer, recommend closing the projection tests, the overlap matrix and the testimony list first; those are the most common grounds for exclusion or impeachment.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. Every claim has a measure, plaintiff entity, period and valuation date, each tied to an instruction or authority.
2. Every Rule 26(a)(2)(B) element (i) to (vi) appears in a named report section, or its gap is a Critical item.
3. The materials list covers all facts or data considered, including interviews and materials reviewed and not used, and every counsel-supplied fact, datum or assumption is identified as such.
4. Each projection relied on has passed the tests in Step 4; any that failed is used only as a labeled sensitivity.
5. But-for and actual figures match by entity, months and accounting basis, as shown in the period alignment table.
6. Lost profits use the incremental margin with avoided costs deducted, and mitigation is measured on the same basis (with and without the offset where lost volume is arguable).
7. The overlap matrix is complete, and no total adds two categories that measure the same dollars for the same period.
8. Future losses are discounted once at a stated rate; no interest runs on losses after the valuation date; damages are reported before interest.
9. No legal conclusion (entitlement to interest, reasonableness of mitigation, contract interpretation, liability) is stated as an expert opinion.
10. Every figure has a source locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`, and the tie-outs hold or each difference is explained.
11. Every legal statement has a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`; no invented authority.
12. No input was chosen or changed to reach a target figure, and every opinion is no broader than its calculation.
13. The output closes with the draft-for-expert-review line.

## Guidelines

- Write for a judge deciding a Rule 702 motion: every step from data to figure is visible and reproducible from the schedules.
- Prefer a supported range to a precise number the data cannot carry.
- Keep counsel's instructions, management's information and the expert's judgments visibly separate in the assumptions section.
- Model each category and period separately so an excluded category or a shortened period can be removed without rebuilding the report.
- When the user's requested approach conflicts with a rule or with the economics, prepare the compliant version and explain the change; do not silently follow the request or silently change it.
- In a rebuttal, address the opposing expert's methods, data and arithmetic, not the person.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Ledger revenue differs from the tax return or audited statements | Timing, consolidation or book-tax differences | Use the audited figure where one exists; show both in the reconciliation table; explain the difference |
| Spreadsheet or production is image-only or unreadable | Scanned printouts or native file not produced | Try OCR; list unreadable pages and the figures that depend on them; request native files |
| Post-event actual results are not yet produced | Discovery lag | Use the latest available period; state the limitation and the supplementation plan under Rule 26(e)(2) |
| Management cannot say who prepared the projection | Projection assembled by several people or for financing | Do not rely on it; use a historical or yardstick method; record the attempt |
| Opposing expert used a different valuation date | Different legal instruction | Show results under both dates; ask counsel which the law requires |
| Counsel's interest instruction conflicts with the governing statute | Contract rate against statutory rate | Exclude interest from the damages opinion; flag the conflict for counsel |
| Forum is a state court with different expert rules | Federal template applied by habit | Mark the disclosure and admissibility rows `[VERIFY]`; follow the state rule once confirmed |
| Request to make the damages reach a number | Settlement or mediation pressure | Decline under Ground Rule 11; offer a sensitivity table over supported ranges |
