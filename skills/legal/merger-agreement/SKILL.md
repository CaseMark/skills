# Merger Agreement (Drafting and Review)

Prepares a merger agreement draft, or a review and markup of a counterparty's draft, for the side the user represents, with Delaware law as the default for each constituent corporation. The deliverable is a determination, ranked blocking issues and judgment calls, a term-sheet reconciliation and defined-term map, a tied price waterfall, the draft or markup, a clocks table, and hand-offs to the related deal skills. Everything it produces is a draft for review by a qualified M&A attorney; nothing it produces is ready for signature.

## Why This Skill Exists

Merger agreements fail in the definitions and the cross-references, not in the headings. A carve-out moved inside a disproportionate-effect exception, a materiality scrape paired with the wrong bring-down, an item counted in both Indebtedness and working capital, or an interim covenant that hands the buyer control before antitrust clearance can each change who bears a nine-figure risk without anyone rewriting the price. Delaware amended its corporation law in 2024 and 2025 in ways that change merger drafting (board approval in substantially final form, lost-premium remedies, stockholder representatives, disclosure schedules, controlling stockholder safe harbors), and the courts have read MAE and ordinary course clauses literally. This skill makes those risks visible before signing.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Definitions article: Material Adverse Effect, Knowledge, Indebtedness, Transaction Expenses, Fundamental Representations | Every walk right, price deduction and qualifier that uses the term | "Prospects" in the MAE base; a disproportionate-effect exception attached to the announcement or pendency carve-out; a due-inquiry Knowledge standard; Indebtedness that repeats a working capital liability; paid Transaction Expenses deducted again |
| Representations and the closing conditions (bring-down) | Whether a breach gives damages, a walk right, or both | Representation, covenant and condition run together; "in all material respects" bring-down plus a materiality scrape gives the buyer a lower walk threshold than an MAE standard; capitalization not in the fundamental list |
| Interim operating covenants and access provisions | Protecting value between signing and closing | "Consistent with past practice" read as an absolute standard (AB Stable); no emergency exception; buyer consent over pricing, bids or hiring, or competitor sales data shared outside a clean team, before the HSR waiting period expires |
| Price mechanics: working capital peg, closing statement, Accounting Principles, QoE report | Closing Merger Consideration and the post-closing true-up | Peg built on a different basis from the closing statement; an item in both Indebtedness and current liabilities; seasonal peg; true-up silently capped by the indemnity cap |
| Indemnification article, survival clause, R&W insurance binder | Post-closing recovery in a private deal | Anti-sandbagging clause plus a buyer Knowledge definition that includes the data room; known diligence issue excluded by the policy and left to general representations; survival clause silent on the 8106 limitations period; fraud carve-out drafted too wide or too narrow |
| Stockholder approval, appraisal and letter of transmittal (party and authority) | Who must approve, who is bound, who can demand appraisal | "Majority of votes cast" used where 251(c) needs a majority of outstanding stock; release first imposed in the letter of transmittal; uncapped, indefinite indemnity of non-signing holders (Cigna); 262 notices anchored to the wrong trigger; controlling stockholder deal without the 144 approvals the parties mean to rely on |
| Support, voting and rollover agreements signed with the target's holders (public or widely held Delaware target) | Locking up votes or tenders for the deal | Under DGCL 203 the buyer becomes the "owner" of shares it has a right to vote under an agreement, so support agreements covering 15% or more can make it an "interested stockholder"; the target board's 203 approval must come before the agreements are signed, not at the merger vote; the 203(b)(4) exemption for stock that is neither listed nor held by more than 2,000 holders is not checked |
| Termination article, fees and remedies | Closing certainty and the price of walking | Outside date shorter than a second-request timetable; reverse termination fee as sole remedy even for willful breach, blocking specific performance; lost-premium damages not drafted under 261(a)(1); fiduciary out missing in a locked-up deal (Omnicare) |
| Disclosure schedules and diligence findings | Exceptions to representations; special indemnities | Schedules drafted against superseded representation numbers; general "deemed disclosed" language; diligence finding with no representation, condition or special indemnity |
| Term sheet, LOI and tax structure memo | Economics and structure the draft must carry | Draft departs from the signed LOI without a flag; intended tax treatment written as a representation or condition without tax counsel's instruction |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is signed, delivered to the counterparty or filed by this skill. Every output is a draft for attorney review and is never called final or ready to sign.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item in one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (the cap table count of holders), *source conflict* (LOI price differs from the draft), *unverified external lookup* (a statute, an exchange rule, an HSR threshold not confirmed), or *policy election* (a choice for the client or counsel, such as basket type or bring-down standard).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** Facts: signed LOI or term sheet and the client's written instructions, then the charter, bylaws and stock ledger, then the QoE report and diligence memos, then data room summaries, management presentations and deal-team email. Law: the current statute text, then Delaware Supreme Court decisions, then Court of Chancery decisions, then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every figure, share count, date and defined-term choice carries a source locator (document and section or page) or `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a case, statute, pin cite or "market" statistic.
5. **Current statute text.** Re-read the DGCL sections relied on (251, 261, 262, 268, 228, 144, 147, 122(18)) on the drafting date; if that cannot be done, say so at the top and mark those rows `[VERIFY]`.
6. **Know the side.** Positions are drafted for the represented party. Say which side every recommendation favors, and never describe a term as "market" without a dated source the user supplies.
7. **Definitions are operative.** No definition is accepted or drafted until its uses are mapped (`references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, section 1).
8. **Representations, covenants and conditions stay separate.** Each representation gets its date, its qualifiers and its bring-down standard; each materiality scrape states its purpose.
9. **No gun-jumping.** Interim covenants protect value; they never give the buyer control of ordinary-course operations or competitively sensitive information before required clearance, and closing never precedes expiry of a required waiting period. Requests to the contrary are declined and routed to hsr-filing.
10. **The price ties.** Every balance sheet item appears in exactly one line of the waterfall, and the peg and the closing statement use one basis.
11. **Bind only those the statute lets you bind.** Releases and indemnity from non-signing holders must rest on consideration and be capped and time-limited (`references/delaware-mechanics.md`, section 5).
12. **Tax structure is stated, not chosen.** Record the intended treatment only as tax counsel instructs; route the analysis, the tax representations, the tax indemnity and the tax treatment of deferred or rollover consideration to analyzing-m-and-a-tax-implications.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; the side represented; new draft or review of a counterparty draft (obtain the draft).
2. Parties, the state of incorporation of each constituent, and the structure (forward, reverse triangular, 251(h) two-step).
3. Public or private target: exchange listing, number of record holders, controlling stockholder or other interested parties.
4. Signed LOI or term sheet: price, form of consideration, escrow and holdback, earnout, exclusivity.
5. Charter, bylaws and cap table: classes, vote requirements, written consent permitted or not, preferred terms, options, RSUs, warrants, convertibles.
6. Financing: commitment letters, conditions, sponsor equity and guarantee.
7. Regulatory: whether an HSR or other filing is required (from hsr-filing), foreign investment or sector approvals, and the realistic timetable.
8. Diligence findings and their status (from due-diligence-report); disclosure schedule status (from disclosure-schedules).
9. Recovery model: public no-survival, private indemnity, R&W insurance (binder), or a mix.
10. QoE report and the proposed Accounting Principles and working capital target.
11. Tax intent and any instruction from tax counsel.
12. Timeline: signing target, outside date, stockholder meeting or consent plan.

**Defaults if not supplied** (each is a policy election stated in the deliverable): Delaware law and the Court of Chancery for the agreement; reverse triangular merger through a newly formed Delaware merger subsidiary (a merger subsidiary that is an LLC or formed elsewhere moves its statute rows to `[VERIFY]`); MAE with carve-outs for general economic, industry, law and GAAP changes, pandemics, war, and announcement, with a disproportionate-effect exception on the economic, industry, law and GAAP, pandemic and war carve-outs only; fundamental representations true in all respects (capitalization de minimis), other representations to an MAE standard (public) or in all material respects (private), with a scrape for the bring-down stated; stockholder approval by a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote, which the charter can raise and a controlling stockholder transaction under 144(b) or (c) may require supplementing; private survival of 12 to 18 months for general representations as a negotiating position, not a rule, noting that a period beyond three years needs the 8106(c) contract period; specific performance available to both sides.

**Hard stops** (label the determination "Declined: hard stop" and produce what the stop calls for):
- The side represented is unknown: produce a neutral issues list only.
- The target's state of incorporation is unknown (or, in a direct two-party merger, the buyer's): produce an issues list and the intake questions; no approval, appraisal or filing mechanics. A merger subsidiary that is not yet formed is not a stop; it takes the Delaware default below.
- Neither a term sheet nor a client instruction fixes the price and form of consideration: produce term sheet questions, not a draft agreement.
- A request for covenants or side arrangements that give the buyer control of the target's ordinary-course operations or competitively sensitive information before a required waiting period expires, or for closing before it expires: decline those provisions, draft the compliant alternative, and hand off to hsr-filing.

**Not hard stops:** an incomplete cap table, missing schedules, an unread RWI binder, or an unconfirmed HSR determination. Draft with brackets and list each as an open item with its gap class.

## Step 1: Classify the Deal and the Deliverable

Read `references/delaware-mechanics.md`, sections 1 to 3. Record side, structure, public or private, approval path and the deliverable mode. For a non-Delaware constituent, mark its statute rows `[VERIFY]` (section 9).

## Step 2: Complete the Source Review and Reconcile the Term Sheet

Build the source-review register in `references/output-template.md`. Open every file; reconcile each economic and risk term in the draft against the LOI and other sources in Part 3; record every departure as a conflict with the controlling source.

## Step 3: Map the Definitions

Read `references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, section 1. Build the defined-term map. For MAE: list the base, each carve-out, which carve-outs carry the disproportionate-effect exception, and every section using the term. For a counterparty draft, quote the text before proposing changes.

## Step 4: Representations, Bring-Down and Scrapes

Read `references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, sections 2 and 3. Assign each representation its date, qualifiers and bring-down standard; list each scrape and its purpose; confirm the fundamental list. Route schedule-dependent representations to disclosure-schedules and fact-dependent ones to due-diligence-report.

## Step 5: Interim Covenants, Efforts and Regulatory

Read `references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, section 4. Draft or review the ordinary course standard, the negative covenants and their thresholds, the consent standard, the emergency exception, access and clean-team terms, and the regulatory efforts ceiling. If a filing may be required, send the interim covenant and access text to hsr-filing before treating it as final.

## Step 6: Price Mechanics

Read `references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, section 5. Build Part 4 of the output: the waterfall with sources and the one-line-per-item check. Recompute every figure; never copy a total from the counterparty draft or the QoE summary page.

## Step 7: Indemnification, Survival and R&W Insurance (Private Targets)

Read `references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, section 6, and `references/delaware-mechanics.md`, sections 5 and 8. Set basket, cap, survival, sandbagging position, exclusive remedy and fraud carve-out; read the RWI binder's retention and exclusions; give every known diligence finding a recovery path (special indemnity, escrow or price). For a public target, confirm representations end at closing and say so.

## Step 8: Approval, Appraisal and Stockholder Mechanics

Read `references/delaware-mechanics.md`, sections 3, 4 and 6. Before any support or voting agreement is signed for a listed or widely held Delaware target, confirm the board's DGCL 203 approval. Draft the approval covenant for the chosen path, the vote standard in the statute's words, the appraisal provisions, and the clocks table with a separate trigger for each element. For a controlling stockholder or going-private deal, state the 144 approvals relied on.

## Step 9: Termination, Fees and Remedies

Read `references/deal-terms-and-traps.md`, section 7, and `references/delaware-mechanics.md`, section 7. Check every termination right against the conditions and the outside date; read fee, exclusive-remedy and specific performance clauses together; draft any lost-premium remedy under 261(a)(1).

## Step 10: Assemble the Deliverable and Hand-Offs

Lay out the header and Parts 1 to 7 per `references/output-template.md`. Rank blocking issues by the severity rules there. Complete every hand-off with the facts the receiving skill needs. List every `[VERIFY]` item with what would settle it.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user, or list as open items:

1. Does every economic term match the signed LOI or a documented client change?
2. Is each bring-down standard, scrape and MAE carve-out the client's decision, given the walk-right consequences explained in the memo?
3. Does the outside date exceed the regulatory timetable hsr-filing gave?
4. Does the price waterfall tie, and has the client's finance team checked the peg basis?
5. Has counsel re-read the DGCL sections relied on, and the governing statute of any non-Delaware constituent?

If the user does not answer, recommend settling the MAE and bring-down package, the price waterfall and the outside date first; those are the provisions most often litigated when a deal breaks.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. The side, structure, state of incorporation of each constituent and approval path are stated in the header.
2. Every file in the register has a status; nothing is called missing without an inventory check and a retrieval attempt.
3. Every economic term traces to the LOI or a documented instruction, and every conflict appears in the reconciliation table with the value used.
4. Every high-risk defined term is mapped to every section that uses it, and MAE carve-outs and disproportionate-effect exceptions are listed one by one.
5. Each representation has a stated bring-down standard; each scrape states its purpose; representations, covenants and conditions are not conflated.
6. No interim covenant gives control of ordinary-course operations or competitively sensitive information before a required clearance, and no closing precedes a required waiting period.
7. The price waterfall is recomputed from sourced figures, and each balance sheet item appears in exactly one line on one basis.
8. Every known diligence finding has a recovery path, and RWI exclusions were read against it.
9. No release or indemnity binds non-signing holders without consideration, a cap and a time limit.
10. Vote standards, notices and appraisal steps match the statute's words, and each clock has its own trigger event.
11. Termination rights, fees, exclusive-remedy and specific performance clauses were read together, and the outside date fits the regulatory timetable.
12. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`, and every figure a locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`.
13. Blocking issues are ranked; each ends in a recommendation, the alternative and an owner; the output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Mark up the counterparty's text rather than replacing it wholesale; quote before changing, so the reviewing attorney sees what moved.
- Keep the memo and the agreement separate: analysis in the memo, operative language in the draft.
- Prefer defined terms used consistently over new synonyms; a new defined term needs a new row in the map.
- Do not draft a representation both parties know is false; use a schedule exception or special indemnity.
- Explain each walk-right change in money and probability terms the client can decide on, not adjectives.
- Where the user's instruction conflicts with a statute or a hard stop, draft the compliant version and explain the change; do not silently follow or silently change the instruction.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Counterparty draft is a scanned PDF or a redline with lost formatting | Image-only file or flattened comparison | Try OCR; list sections that stay unreadable and the issues that depend on them; ask for the Word file |
| LOI price differs from the draft | Later negotiation or drafting error | Record both in the reconciliation table; treat the LOI as controlling until the client confirms a change |
| Cap table totals differ from the charter's authorized or the stock ledger | Unrecorded issuances or stale model | Use the stock ledger; flag the gap as a source conflict; bracket per-share figures |
| QoE peg cannot be traced to line items | Summary page only | Request the peg schedule; mark the peg `[UNVERIFIED]`; keep the waterfall check open |
| RWI binder not yet available | Underwriting in progress | Draft seller indemnity as bracketed alternatives; list each known issue as needing a recovery path |
| HSR status unknown | Filing analysis not done | Treat a filing as possible; keep interim covenants within Ground Rule 9; hand off to hsr-filing |
| Target incorporated outside Delaware | Different statute | Mark approval, appraisal and filing rows `[VERIFY]`; read that state's statute before drafting mechanics |
| Two definitions of the same term in the draft and an ancillary | Copied precedent | Name the conflict; propose one definition and an order-of-precedence clause |

## Related Skills

- hsr-filing: whether a filing is required, the waiting period, and review of interim covenants, access and integration planning for gun-jumping.
- disclosure-schedules: drafting the schedules against the representations and the cross-application standard.
- due-diligence-report: confirming facts a representation, condition or special indemnity depends on.
- analyzing-m-and-a-tax-implications: structure, intended tax treatment, tax representations, tax indemnity and the tax treatment of deferred or rollover consideration; this skill drafts the clauses only as that analysis and tax counsel direct.
- stock-purchase-agreement: the deal is a stock purchase, not a merger.
- asset-purchase-agreement: the deal is an asset acquisition.
- letter-of-intent: the economics are not yet agreed and a term sheet is needed first.
- escrow-agreement: escrow and holdback mechanics once amounts are set.
- Support, voting and rollover agreements with the target's stockholders in an M&A deal stay in this skill (including the DGCL 203 approval); voting-agreement covers closely held and venture voting agreements, not deal support agreements, and legal-research takes any open 203 question.
- Sandbagging split: the pro- or anti-sandbagging clause and the Knowledge definitions are drafted here; schedule supplements between signing and closing and the buyer's knowledge at closing go to disclosure-schedules.
- proxy-statement: stockholder meeting disclosure for a public target.
- ma-closing-checklist: closing deliverables and signature logistics.
