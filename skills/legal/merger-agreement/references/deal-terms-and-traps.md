# Deal Terms and Where They Fail

Drafting and review rules for the risk-allocation provisions of a merger agreement. Case holdings are stated as read in the opinions listed in the primary sources file. Practice statements ("usual", "common") are not law; never call a term "market" without a dated source the user supplies.

## 1. Definitions that move operative terms

Build a cross-reference map: for each defined term, every section that uses it and what the term does there. A definition reviewed alone is not reviewed.

| Term | Check | Failure that changes the deal |
|---|---|---|
| Material Adverse Effect | Each part of the base (business, results of operations, financial condition; "prospects"; ability to consummate); "has had or would reasonably be expected to have"; each carve-out; which carve-outs carry a disproportionate-effect exception; every place the term is used (definition, bring-down, stand-alone MAE condition, MAE-qualified representations, termination) | "Prospects" widens the walk right to forecasts; a disproportionate-effect exception attached to the announcement, pendency or compliance-with-agreement carve-outs reopens risks the target cannot measure against peers; a carve-out list without "pandemic" or "change in law" leaves those risks with the target |
| Knowledge | Named individuals; actual knowledge or after "due inquiry" or "reasonable inquiry"; of direct reports | Due-inquiry standard silently converts knowledge-qualified representations into near-flat ones; a buyer-side knowledge definition that includes the whole data room feeds an anti-sandbagging clause (section 6) |
| Indebtedness | Each item listed; whether it also sits in Closing Working Capital or Transaction Expenses | An item counted in Indebtedness and as a current liability in working capital is deducted twice |
| Transaction Expenses | Unpaid at closing only; who bears change-of-control payments, the employer share of payroll taxes, RWI premium, filing fees | Expenses already paid from cash before closing deducted again; a filing fee the term sheet allocates to the buyer swept into seller expenses |
| Closing Working Capital and Accounting Principles | Same line items, same policies and same cut-off as the peg; specific policies listed before "GAAP consistently applied" | Peg built on one basis, closing statement on another (section 5) |
| Fundamental Representations | Which representations; their bring-down and survival | A capitalization representation omitted from the list takes the general cap and survival |
| Willful Breach or Fraud | Knowledge plus intent, or deliberate act that breaches; whether limited to the speaker's own representations | Hexion read "knowing and intentional breach" to mean a deliberate act that itself breaches, even if breach was not its conscious object; a fraud definition drawn too wide or narrow moves the cap carve-out |

MAE law: the party seeking to excuse performance bears the burden absent clear contrary language, and the effect must be durationally significant, measured in years rather than months, from the view of a reasonable acquiror (Hexion, 965 A.2d 715, relying on IBP, 789 A.2d 14). Akorn (Del. Ch. Oct. 1, 2018, aff'd, 198 A.3d 724 (Del. 2018)) found a general MAE, a bring-down failure measured against an MAE standard, and a material breach of the ordinary course covenant, each supporting termination. No numeric MAE threshold is law; a percentage in a draft is a negotiated term.

## 2. Representations, covenants and conditions

- A representation states a fact as of a date; a covenant promises conduct; a condition decides whether a party must close. The same fact can appear in all three with different consequences: a breached representation may give damages (if it survives) and may defeat the bring-down condition; a breached covenant gives damages and may defeat the covenant-compliance condition; a failed condition gives a walk right, not damages.
- Every representation needs its dates (signing, closing, or a stated date), its qualifiers (knowledge, materiality, MAE, "except as set forth in the Disclosure Schedules"), and its bring-down standard.
- Public target deals usually end liability for representations at closing; the bring-down and MAE condition are the buyer's main protection. Private deals add indemnity or RWI (section 6).
- Specific indemnities cover known issues; do not draft a representation to cover a fact both sides know is false.

## 3. Bring-down standards and materiality scrapes

| Representation group | Typical bring-down standard (policy election) | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental (organization, authority, capitalization, brokers) | True in all respects; capitalization often "other than de minimis inaccuracies" | Define de minimis by a number of shares or a dollar amount |
| Other representations, public target | True except where failures would not, individually or in the aggregate, reasonably be expected to have an MAE | Akorn applied this standard |
| Other representations, private target | True in all material respects, or the MAE standard | "In all material respects" is a lower bar for a walk right than the MAE standard |
| Covenant compliance | Performed or complied with in all material respects | Akorn read "in all material respects" in the covenant-compliance condition as different from and less onerous than common-law material breach, adopting a test close to whether the breach would have significantly altered the total mix of information |

Materiality scrape: reading representations as if materiality and MAE qualifiers were deleted, for the bring-down, for calculating losses, or for both. Record for each scrape which purpose it serves. A scrape exists to avoid double materiality (a material inaccuracy in a representation already qualified by materiality). Paired with an "in all material respects" bring-down, it leaves a single, low threshold for the walk right, which favors the buyer; paired with an MAE bring-down, it is the usual public-deal combination. Exclude the "no MAE since the balance sheet date" representation and defined terms such as "Material Contract" from any scrape.

## 4. Interim operating covenants, efforts and gun-jumping

- Ordinary course covenant: "ordinary course" alone, "consistent with past practice", "in all material respects", and an efforts qualifier ("commercially reasonable efforts to") each change the standard. AB Stable VIII LLC v. MAPS Hotels & Resorts One LLC, 268 A.3d 198 (Del. 2021): a covenant to operate "only in the ordinary course of business, consistent with past practice in all material respects" was judged against the seller's own past operations, not industry peers; the seller's drastic COVID-era changes without first seeking the buyer's consent breached it and excused the buyer from closing, although the MAE definition carved out calamities. The seller could have sought consent and challenged an unreasonable refusal.
- Draft an emergency exception (actions reasonably required by law or to respond to an emergency, with notice) and a consent standard ("not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed"), or state that neither applies, as a policy election.
- Negative covenants (no dividends, no new debt above a threshold, no charter amendments, no equity issuances, no material contract entry or amendment above a threshold) protect value. Covenants that let the buyer direct pricing, customer bids, hiring of ordinary staff, or day-to-day operations before clearance, or that give the buyer's commercial staff competitively sensitive information outside a clean-team protocol, risk a premature transfer of beneficial ownership. 15 U.S.C. 18a(a) bars acquiring voting securities or assets before the waiting period expires where a filing is required; 18a(g)(1) sets a civil penalty per day of violation (adjusted for inflation; current amount `[VERIFY]` with hsr-filing). Route every interim covenant to hsr-filing review when a filing is required.
- Efforts: best efforts, reasonable best efforts, commercially reasonable efforts. Akorn noted the practitioner hierarchy but courts look at what the party actually did; define the regulatory efforts obligation by its concrete limits (divestitures, litigation, a "burdensome condition" ceiling) rather than by the adjective.
- Outside date: longer than the realistic regulatory timetable, with any automatic extension for a pending regulatory condition stated with its own trigger.

## 5. Purchase price mechanics and working capital

Waterfall (private deal, cash-free debt-free):

```
Base Purchase Price
- Closing Indebtedness
+ Closing Cash (if cash is added and excluded from working capital)
+/- (Closing Working Capital - Working Capital Target), subject to any collar
- unpaid Transaction Expenses
= Closing Merger Consideration (estimated at closing, trued up after)
```

Rules: each balance sheet item appears in exactly one line; the peg and the closing statement use the same line items, policies and cut-off; a seasonal business needs a peg that matches the closing month or a normalized basis stated in the agreement; state the estimate, review period, objection period and an independent accountant acting as expert (not arbitrator) on disputed items only, unless the parties elect otherwise; state whether the true-up is paid from an adjustment escrow, the indemnity escrow, or directly, and whether it counts against the indemnity cap. Show the arithmetic with every figure sourced. 251(b) requires formulas that depend on outside facts to state clearly how those facts operate.

## 6. Indemnification, survival, sandbagging and R&W insurance

| Term | Choices | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Basket | Deductible (losses above the threshold) or tipping (first dollar once exceeded); per-claim de minimis | Mixing a tipping basket with a de minimis that counts toward it |
| Cap | Percentage of price for general representations; higher or full price for fundamental; fraud uncapped | Specific indemnities silently subject to the general cap and basket |
| Survival | General, fundamental, tax (often statute of limitations plus a period) | Delaware three-year default and the 20-year contract period (10 Del. C. 8106; Delaware mechanics file, section 8) |
| Sandbagging | Pro-sandbagging clause, anti-sandbagging clause, or silence | Arwood v. AW Site Services (Del. Ch. Mar. 9, 2022): Delaware is, or should be, pro-sandbagging when the contract is silent; whether the Delaware Supreme Court agrees is `[VERIFY]`; an express anti-sandbagging clause controls whatever the default |
| Exclusive remedy and fraud | Indemnity as sole remedy, carve-out for fraud | ABRY Partners (891 A.2d 1032): sophisticated parties may limit liability for unintentional misstatements, but public policy does not permit insulating a seller from liability for its own intentional false representations within the contract |
| Non-signing holders | Escrow, holdback, joinders, capped pro rata exposure | Cigna v. Audax (Delaware mechanics file, section 5) |

R&W insurance: the policy, not the agreement, decides coverage. Read the binder: retention and its drop-down, exclusions (known breaches, identified diligence issues, specific matters, forward-looking statements, purchase price adjustments), the policy's own materiality scrape and knowledge provisions, and subrogation against sellers only for fraud. A known diligence finding is usually excluded; cover it with a special indemnity, escrow or price change. The agreement's seller indemnity (if any) should match the retention structure.

## 7. Termination, fees and remedies

- Termination rights: mutual consent; outside date (with a bar on a party whose breach caused the failure to close); final non-appealable injunction; failed stockholder vote; uncured breach that would cause a condition to fail (with a cure period ending no later than the outside date); fiduciary out for a superior proposal with notice and matching rights (public target); change of recommendation.
- Deal protections must leave the board able to act on its fiduciary duties. Omnicare (818 A.2d 914): voting agreements locking up a majority plus a force-the-vote provision and no fiduciary out were preclusive and coercive and unenforceable.
- Fees: target termination fee triggers (change of recommendation; tail provision after a failed vote or breach followed by an alternative deal within a stated period); reverse termination fee triggers (financing failure, regulatory failure). State whether a fee is the sole and exclusive remedy and whether that exclusivity survives willful breach.
- Specific performance: an express entitlement, waiver of bond, and the relationship to any reverse termination fee. A financing-conditioned buyer's draft may allow specific performance of the equity commitment only if debt is funded; read every remedy clause together.
- Lost-premium damages: draft under 261(a)(1) with an enforcement and retention clause (Delaware mechanics file, section 7).
- Survival of provisions after termination (confidentiality, fees, remedies for willful breach) must be listed expressly.
