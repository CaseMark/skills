## 1. Choosing the statute and the structure

- Section 251 governs a merger of two or more Delaware corporations. Section 251(b) requires the agreement to state the terms and conditions, the mode of carrying them into effect, any charter amendments of the survivor (or that its charter continues), and the manner of converting or cancelling shares and the consideration. Terms may depend on facts ascertainable outside the agreement if the agreement states clearly and expressly how those facts operate (251(b), last paragraph). Use this for escrow releases, earnouts and adjustment mechanics; state the formula, not just the outcome.
- Structures: forward merger (target merges into buyer or buyer's subsidiary), reverse triangular merger (buyer's subsidiary merges into target; target survives), two-step tender offer then merger under 251(h). Record the reason for the choice (contract assignment, consents, tax intent) as a policy election; the tax consequence goes to analyzing-m-and-a-tax-implications.
- A merger with an LLC or other entity uses other sections (for example 263, 264). This file covers 251 only; mark other paths `[VERIFY]`.

## 2. Board approval

- 251(b): each board adopts a resolution approving the agreement and declaring its advisability.
- Section 147 (added by 84 Del. Laws c. 309, effective August 1, 2024): where the DGCL requires board approval of an agreement, the board may approve it in final form or in substantially final form, and may later ratify a document that is required to be filed (or referenced in a filed certificate) before the filing becomes effective.
- Section 268(a) (same Act): in an all-cash or non-survivor-stock deal, the agreement the board approves need not include the survivor's charter, and later changes to that charter are not amendments to the merger agreement.
- Drafting consequence: the board resolution should identify the version approved, and the deliverable should record any change after approval and whether it requires re-approval under 251(d) (after stockholder adoption, no amendment may change the consideration, the survivor's charter, or terms in a way that adversely affects any class or series).

## 3. Stockholder approval paths

| Path | Rule (section) | What the agreement must provide |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting | 251(c): notice to every holder, voting or nonvoting, at least 20 days before the meeting, with a copy or brief summary of the agreement; adoption by a majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote. The charter can require more. | Covenant to call the meeting, recommendation and change-of-recommendation mechanics, vote standard stated as the statute and charter require |
| Written consent | 228(a): consent of holders with not less than the votes needed at a meeting with all shares present; 228(e): prompt notice to non-consenting holders. The charter can prohibit action by consent. | Delivery deadline for the consent (often hours after signing), the 228(e) notice, the 262(d)(2) appraisal notice |
| Tender offer then merger | 251(h): target has a class listed on a national securities exchange or held of record by more than 2,000 holders immediately before signing; agreement expressly permits or requires a 251(h) merger and requires it as soon as practicable after the offer; offer for all shares that would vote, which may carry a minimum condition; after consummation the buyer holds at least the percentage needed to adopt; non-tendered shares receive the same consideration. | Express 251(h) election; offer conditions; merger as soon as practicable; same consideration |
| No survivor vote | 251(f): no vote of the survivor's stockholders if the charter is not amended, each share stays identical, and new common issued is not more than 20% of pre-merger outstanding common. | Exchange listing rules may still require a buyer vote for a stock issuance `[VERIFY]` |

"Majority of the outstanding stock entitled to vote" (251(c)) is not "majority of the votes cast" (the 144(b)(2) cleansing vote, section 6). Never substitute one for the other.

## 4. Appraisal and the clocks table

Section 262(b) gives appraisal rights in a 251 merger, subject to the market-out exception in 262(b)(1) (stock listed on a national securities exchange or held of record by more than 2,000 holders at the record date, or immediately before signing for a 251(h) deal). The market-out does not apply, and appraisal returns, if holders must accept anything other than survivor stock, listed or widely held stock of another corporation, cash in lieu of fractional shares, or a combination of them (262(b)(2)). A cash merger of a listed target therefore carries appraisal rights.

For listed shares, 262(g) requires dismissal unless the shares seeking appraisal exceed 1% of the class, or their merger consideration exceeds $1 million, or the merger was approved under 253 or 267. Fair value excludes value arising from the merger; interest runs from the effective date at 5% over the Federal Reserve discount rate, compounded quarterly, unless the court orders otherwise, and the survivor may prepay to stop interest on the prepaid amount (262(h)).

Each clock has its own trigger. Do not anchor them all to the effective date.

| Element | Period | Trigger event | Section |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notice of the stockholder meeting | At least 20 days before | Date of the meeting | 251(c) |
| Appraisal-availability notice (meeting path) | Not less than 20 days before | Date of the meeting | 262(d)(1) |
| Written appraisal demand (meeting path) | Before the vote | Taking of the vote | 262(d)(1) |
| Notice of effective date to demanding holders (meeting path) | Within 10 days after | Effective date | 262(d)(1) |
| Prompt notice of action by consent | Promptly | Taking of the action by less than unanimous consent | 228(e) |
| Appraisal notice (consent, 251(h), 253, 267 paths) | Before the effective date, or within 10 days after | Effective date | 262(d)(2) |
| Appraisal demand (consent path) | Within 20 days after | Date of giving the 262(d)(2) notice | 262(d)(2) |
| Appraisal demand (251(h) path) | Later of consummation of the offer and 20 days after the notice | Consummation of the offer; date of giving notice | 262(d)(2) |
| Withdrawal of demand as of right | Within 60 days after | Effective date | 262(e) |
| Petition for appraisal | Within 120 days after | Effective date | 262(e) |

Appraisal closing conditions (for example, holders of not more than a stated percentage have demanded appraisal) are a policy election; no statute sets the percentage.

## 5. Binding non-signing stockholders; the stockholder representative

- Cigna Health & Life Ins. Co. v. Audax Health Solutions, Inc., 107 A.3d 1082 (Del. Ch. 2014): a release first required in the letter of transmittal, after the merger fixed the holder's right to consideration, was unenforceable for lack of consideration; a post-closing indemnification obligation on non-signing stockholders that was neither capped nor limited in time, covering all of the merger consideration, made the value of the consideration unknowable and violated 251. Obligations limited in both amount and time were not invalidated on that motion.
- Drafting consequences: put any release or indemnity in the merger agreement or in a support or joinder agreement signed before closing for its own consideration; cap per-holder exposure (escrow, holdback, pro rata share of a cap); state survival periods; do not make payment conditional on a new release in the letter of transmittal.
- 261(a)(2) (2024): the agreement may appoint a stockholder representative, effective at or after stockholder adoption, with sole and exclusive authority to enforce stockholders' rights under the agreement, irrevocable and binding on all holders from adoption.

## 6. Controlling stockholder and interested-party deals; stockholder agreements

- Section 144, as amended by 85 Del. Laws c. 6 (SB 21, approved March 25, 2025; applies to acts and transactions before or after enactment except actions or demands pending or completed on or before February 17, 2025): a controlling stockholder transaction other than a going-private transaction is protected from equitable relief and damages on fiduciary claims if approved by a committee of at least two directors determined to be disinterested, with negotiating and rejection authority (144(b)(1)), or if conditioned at submission on, and approved by, a majority of the votes cast by disinterested stockholders (144(b)(2)), or if fair (144(b)(3)). A going-private controlling stockholder transaction needs both the committee and the disinterested-stockholder vote, or fairness (144(c)).
- Drafting consequence: if the buyer is a controller or the deal is going-private, the agreement's conditions must state the 144 approvals the parties intend to rely on and when the condition attaches ("by its terms, as in effect at the time it is submitted to stockholders"). Whether to seek the protection is a policy election for counsel; the deal structure is not chosen by this skill.
- Section 122(18) (2024): a corporation may contract with current or prospective stockholders, notwithstanding 141(a), including to restrict its own actions or require a person's consent, unless the provision is contrary to the charter or would be contrary to Delaware law if included in the charter. It responded to West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. Moelis & Co., 311 A.3d 809 (Del. Ch. 2024); the Delaware Supreme Court reversed that decision on January 20, 2026 (No. 340, 2024), holding that provisions at odds with 141(a) are voidable, not void, and that the challenge was barred by laches. Support, voting and rollover agreements with stockholders should state the power they rely on.

## 7. Remedies for failure to close; disclosure schedules

- 261(a)(1) (2024): a 251 merger agreement may subject a party that fails to perform or to consummate to penalties or consequences set out in the agreement, including an amount representing or based on the stockholders' lost premium, and the corporation may enforce and retain that payment. It responded to Crispo v. Musk (Del. Ch. Oct. 31, 2023), which questioned whether a target can recover its stockholders' lost premium absent third-party beneficiary status. A lost-premium remedy should cite 261(a)(1) and state who enforces it and who keeps the payment.
- 268(b) (2024): unless the agreement expressly provides otherwise, disclosure letters and schedules that qualify representations, covenants or conditions are not part of the agreement for DGCL purposes but have the effects the agreement gives them. The agreement must still say how schedules qualify each representation (section reference, "reasonably apparent" cross-application, or none). Schedule drafting goes to disclosure-schedules.
- 84 Del. Laws c. 309, section 6: these 2024 changes took effect August 1, 2024 and apply to agreements whenever made, except civil actions completed or pending on or before that date.

## 8. Survival, limitations and the 20-year contract period

- 10 Del. C. 8106(a): three years from accrual for an action based on a promise.
- 8106(c): an action on a written contract involving at least $100,000 may be brought within a period the contract specifies, if brought within 20 years from accrual.
- A survival clause therefore does two jobs: it says how long a representation survives and, under Delaware law, it can operate as the contractual limitations period. State whether a claim must be noticed or filed by the survival date; say expressly whether the parties intend a period longer than three years. Whether a survival clause that says only that representations "survive" shortens or extends the limitations period is a question of construction `[VERIFY]`.

## 9. Non-Delaware constituents and limits of this file

For a constituent incorporated elsewhere, the merger statute, vote, notice, appraisal or dissenters' rights and filing rules of that state govern its side of the merger; record the statute read and the date, or mark every such rule `[VERIFY]`. This file does not cover securities law disclosure (proxy statement, tender offer documents), exchange rules, or LLC and partnership mergers.
