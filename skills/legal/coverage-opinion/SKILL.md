# Insurance Coverage Opinion

Prepares a coverage opinion on whether a liability insurer owes a defense and indemnity for a claim or suit, addressed to the client (the insurer or the insured), with the policy and forms reconciliation, the analysis in a fixed order, the clocks that govern reservation and disclaimer, a recommended position and next steps, and hand-offs. It reviews existing reservation and denial letters for timing and content. Every output is a draft for review and signature by a licensed attorney.

## Why This Skill Exists

Coverage opinions go wrong in predictable places. The analyst reads a specimen form instead of the policy issued, applies an exclusion before showing the insuring agreement is met, decides the duty to defend on facts the governing state does not let the insurer use, counts a disclaimer clock from the wrong day, or treats an other-insurance clause as a reason to refuse a defense the insured is owed. Each error has a cost: a denial that turns into estoppel, a disclaimer that is untimely as a matter of law, or an insured left without a defense. The state rules differ on exactly these points, so the opinion has to name the governing state's rule and its source, or say plainly that the rule is unconfirmed and show both readings.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Declarations, forms schedule, coverage form and endorsements for each period and layer | The contract actually issued | Specimen or wrong edition read (CG 00 01 12 07 in the file, 04 13 scheduled); scheduled endorsement missing from the file; an endorsement from one renewal applied to another; exclusion text taken from the insurer's denial letter instead of the policy |
| Complaint or petition, and extrinsic facts (adjuster statements, recorded interviews, investigation reports) | Deciding the duty to defend under the governing materials rule | Insured's statement used to deny a defense in an eight-corners state though it overlaps the merits and contradicts the pleading (Texas, Monroe); known facts showing potential coverage ignored where they count (California, Gray and Montrose); labels of counts read instead of facts alleged |
| Insuring agreement and definitions ("suit", "damages", "occurrence", insured status) | Establishing potential coverage before exclusions | Exclusion analyzed first; burden at each stage not stated; exception to an exclusion skipped (reasonable-force exception to the expected-or-intended exclusion) |
| Policy periods, retroactive date, claim and report dates, dates of injury | Trigger | Date of the act, of injury, of discovery and of the claim treated as one date; continuous damage confined to one period; known-injury clause not checked at renewal; claims-made reporting window missed |
| Notice letters, tender, claim notes | Late-notice and other condition defenses | Denial on late notice alone where the governing statute requires prejudice (N.Y. Ins. Law 3420(a)(5)); prejudice burden placed on the wrong party; delay treated as prejudice without evidence |
| Reservation-of-rights and disclaimer letters, with dates of the facts that supported them | Preserving defenses | Clock counted from the tender instead of when the insurer had the facts (Jetco); reservation treated as a disclaimer; Florida 30-day reservation and 60-day follow-up missed; flat refusal with no reservation or declaratory action (Illinois estoppel, Ehlco) |
| Choice-of-law facts (where issued, insured's base, location of risk, loss, forum) | Selecting the state whose rules apply | The rule of the forum or of the client's preference assumed without analysis; a state rule named that was never verified |
| Other policies (successive periods, excess and umbrella, additional insured endorsements) and their other-insurance clauses | Sharing and allocation | Other-insurance clause used to refuse a defense owed to the insured; successive-period CGL called "other insurance" without reading 4.b; allocation method assumed rather than read from policy language |
| Engagement letter, retention by insurer, requester's role | Who the client is and what the drafter may do | Defense counsel asked to write a coverage opinion for the insurer against the insured, or to pass on privileged facts bearing on coverage; conflict from a reservation not flagged (Cal. Civ. Code 2860) |
| Prayer for relief | Whether "damages" are sought | Injunctive relief, fines, restitution and punitive damages treated as covered damages without analysis; punitive-damages insurability assumed |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is sent, filed or communicated to the other side by this skill. Every output is a draft for attorney review and is never called final.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (the date the insured learned of the occurrence), *source conflict* (scheduled edition differs from the form in the file), *unverified external lookup* (a state rule not in `references/primary-sources.md`), or *policy election* (a choice for the client or counsel, such as filing a declaratory action).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For the contract: the issued policy (certified copy or complete issued forms matching the schedule), then the underwriting file and binder, then specimen forms, then broker summaries, certificates and letters quoting the policy. For the claim: the operative complaint as filed, then demand letters, then claim notes and interviews. For law: the governing state's statute and highest court, then its intermediate courts, then federal predictions, then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every policy quotation carries form number, edition, section and page; every fact carries a source locator (document and page or paragraph) or `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`, and a state's rule is never attributed to that state by name unless it is in the register. Re-read the current text of each statute relied on before the opinion goes out. Never invent a case, statute, form provision or pin cite.
5. **Know the client first.** Identify who the client is (insurer, named insured, additional insured, excess carrier) and the requester's role before any analysis. Never draft coverage advice for the insurer against an insured the drafter represents in the underlying suit, and never use information from the insured's defense to build a coverage defense.
6. **Governing law before any rule.** Decide choice of law, then apply that state's rule on which materials decide the duty to defend. Use extrinsic facts only as that rule allows; where the rule is unconfirmed, analyze the complaint-only and extrinsic-facts readings and show where they differ.
7. **Insuring agreement before exclusions.** Analyze insured status, "suit", "damages", injury, "occurrence", territory, trigger and known injury first; then exclusions and their exceptions; then conditions; then other insurance and limits. State who bears the burden at each stage under the governing law.
8. **Read the policy actually issued.** Reconcile the forms schedule for every period and layer against the forms in the file, edition by edition. A provision not read in the issued policy is never applied as if it were.
9. **Defense and indemnity are separate.** "Duty to defend: not owed" requires that no claim in the suit is potentially covered under the governing materials rule and that every scheduled form has been read. Indemnity stays "Duty to indemnify: not yet determinable" while the facts that decide it are unresolved.
10. **Clocks from their own triggers.** Compute every reservation, disclaimer, notice and statutory deadline from the trigger the rule uses, with the date, weekday and evidence, and state the consequence of missing it. Defaults never override a statute (Florida 627.426, New York 3420(d)(2)).
11. **Other insurance governs sharing, not the insured's defense.** Never recommend refusing a defense owed under a triggered primary policy because another carrier may share; recommend tenders and a contribution position instead.
12. **Objective analysis, not a predetermined result.** The opinion states the facts and authorities on both sides, including those adverse to the client's preferred position. Decline a request to omit known material facts or controlling authority, or to reach a stated conclusion regardless of the analysis; produce the complete opinion instead.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; the client and the requester's role; any retention by the insurer to defend the insured in the same matter.
2. Every policy that may respond: carrier, policy number, layer, period, limits; declarations, forms schedule and every scheduled form and endorsement.
3. The operative complaint or petition (latest amendment), and any demand or arbitration demand.
4. Tender and notice: when the insured learned of the occurrence, when it gave notice and how, and to whom.
5. Every reservation, disclaimer, denial and acknowledgment letter, with dates and recipients, and the date the insurer had the facts behind each ground.
6. Extrinsic facts in the claim file (statements, reports), labeled by source.
7. Choice-of-law contacts: where issued and delivered, insured's principal place of business, location of risk, location of loss, forum.
8. Other insurance: successive carriers, excess and umbrella, additional insured endorsements, tenders made and responses.
9. Any settlement, judgment or arbitration award already reached.
10. The question asked (defense only, defense and indemnity, review of a letter, tender response) and the deadline.

**Defaults if not supplied** (a governing statute or conflict rule overrides any default it addresses): governing law is the state where the policy was issued to the named insured, marked `[VERIFY]` and tested against the forum's rule; duty-to-defend materials rule unconfirmed, so both readings are shown; indemnity "not yet determinable"; reservation sent as soon as the ground and facts are confirmed, unless Florida 627.426 or New York 3420(d)(2) sets the clock; formal tone.

**Hard stops (label the opinion status "Declined: hard stop"; produce the request, routing or alternative, not the requested opinion):**
- No policy wording at all (only a certificate, binder, quote, broker summary or a letter quoting the policy): produce the policy request and a list of issues to test once it arrives.
- No claim document (no complaint, demand, or written claim) and no description of the claim from the client.
- The requester is defense counsel retained to defend the insured and is asked to advise the insurer on coverage against the insured, or one firm would represent both insurer and insured on coverage: decline, explain the conflict, and route the insurer to separate coverage counsel.
- A request to omit known material facts or controlling authority, or to deliver a fixed conclusion regardless of the analysis: decline that version and offer the complete opinion.

**Not hard stops:** a scheduled endorsement or edition missing or in conflict (Critical item; status "Not ready: blocking issues"); governing state contested (analyze each candidate); a clock already missed (state the consequence and the remaining options).

## Step 1: Client, Role and Scope

Record the client, the requester's role and the conflict check in the header of `references/output-template.md`. Read `references/notice-reservation-and-allocation.md`, section 5. If a reservation creates a conflict that defense counsel could influence (California Civ. Code 2860(b)), flag independent counsel rights now.

## Step 2: Reconcile the Policy as Issued

Read `references/defense-and-indemnity-rules.md`, section 2. Build the forms reconciliation table in Part 2 for every period and layer. Any scheduled form not in the file, or any edition mismatch on a provision relied on, is a Critical open item, and no conclusion is drawn on that provision.

## Step 3: Choice of Law and the Materials Rule

Read `references/defense-and-indemnity-rules.md`, sections 3 and 4, and check `references/primary-sources.md` for the state. Record the contacts, the conflict rule and the result. Sort the claim file into the complaint and policy, extrinsic facts the governing rule allows, and extrinsic facts it excludes, with the reason for each.

## Step 4: Insuring Agreement and Trigger

Read `references/defense-and-indemnity-rules.md`, sections 5 and 6. Work each element of the insuring agreement for each policy period, quote the issued text, and state the burden. Record the act, injury, discovery, claim and report dates separately; apply the governing trigger, or each candidate trigger where the rule is unconfirmed; check known injury at every renewal.

## Step 5: Exclusions and Exceptions

Read `references/defense-and-indemnity-rules.md`, sections 7 and 8. Quote each exclusion and every exception from the issued form, apply the governing construction standard and burden, and list exclusions considered and rejected. Where an exclusion turns on a fact the underlying suit will decide, treat it as a reservation ground. For a mixed action, state whether the governing law requires defending the whole suit and any reimbursement right.

## Step 6: Conditions and Clocks

Read `references/notice-reservation-and-allocation.md`, sections 1 and 2. Fill the clock table in the fixed format of `references/output-template.md`, one row per element, each from its own trigger. For late notice, state whether prejudice is required, who bears the burden, and the evidence of prejudice, if any.

## Step 7: Reservation, Disclaimer and Waiver

Read `references/notice-reservation-and-allocation.md`, sections 3 and 4. Review each letter sent for timing, recipients and specificity; state the waiver or estoppel risk; recommend the reservation, disclaimer, declaratory action or withdrawal, with the letter outline in Part 6.

## Step 8: Other Insurance and Allocation

Read `references/notice-reservation-and-allocation.md`, section 6. Build the policy matrix, quote each other-insurance clause, and keep the duty owed to the insured separate from sharing among insurers. Recommend tenders to every potentially triggered carrier.

## Step 9: Positions, Indemnity and Assembly

Assign one duty-to-defend label and one duty-to-indemnify label per policy and period from `references/output-template.md`, Part 1, and the opinion status. List the facts that will decide indemnity, the blocking issues ranked by severity, the reconciliation table, next steps, hand-offs and every `[VERIFY]` item with what would settle it.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Is the client and its role correct, and has the conflict check cleared?
2. Has counsel accepted the choice-of-law analysis and the materials rule applied?
3. Has every scheduled form been read in the issued edition, or is the gap listed as Critical?
4. Are the clocks and their trigger dates confirmed from documents?
5. Is the recommended position one the client can act on now (defend under reservation, disclaim, seek a declaration, tender)?

If the user does not answer, recommend confirming the issued forms and the clock trigger dates first; those are the most common reasons a coverage position fails later.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. The client and the requester's role are stated, and no advice runs against a party the drafter represents.
2. The governing state is chosen with its contacts and conflict rule, or the contest is stated and each candidate analyzed.
3. The duty-to-defend materials rule is named with its source, and every extrinsic fact used or excluded has a reason.
4. Every policy quotation is from the issued form with number, edition, section and page; every scheduled form is reconciled or listed as Critical.
5. The insuring agreement is analyzed before any exclusion, with the burden stated at each stage.
6. Every exclusion relied on is quoted with its exceptions, and exclusions considered and rejected are listed.
7. Trigger dates (act, injury, discovery, claim, report) are separate, and known injury is checked for each renewal.
8. Each clock has its own trigger, date with weekday, evidence, period, and status, and statutory clocks override defaults.
9. Late notice is not treated as a defense without the governing prejudice rule and burden, or both readings where unconfirmed.
10. Other-insurance and allocation analysis does not reduce the defense owed to the insured, and tenders are recommended.
11. Defense and indemnity labels are used exactly as in `references/output-template.md`, one per policy and period.
12. Every legal statement has a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`; no state is named for an unverified rule.
13. The output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Write for the client, but write as if the other side and a court will read it later. Adverse facts and authority go in the opinion, not in a side note.
- Be definite where the law and documents allow, and state the confidence and the open items where they do not. "Defend under reservation while investigating" is a position, not a hedge, when potential coverage exists and a ground remains unresolved.
- Keep defense-side information and coverage-side analysis apart. Do not ask defense counsel for facts bearing on a reserved coverage issue.
- A reservation letter is specific: grounds, provisions quoted, facts. A general reservation of all rights does not preserve an unnamed ground.
- When the user's instruction conflicts with a statute or the governing rule (for example, deny on late notice without prejudice evidence), draft the compliant recommendation and explain the change.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Forms in the file do not match the schedule | Specimen packet, wrong renewal, or broker copy | List each mismatch; request a certified copy; analyze on the base form only for potential coverage and mark every affected conclusion |
| Policy pages scanned and unreadable | Image-only PDF or poor scan | Try OCR; list unreadable pages and the provisions that depend on them; request a clean copy |
| Complaint is vague on dates or facts | Notice pleading | Apply the governing rule to the gap (Texas permits limited extrinsic evidence under Monroe); otherwise resolve doubt as the governing rule requires and list the fact |
| Two dates for when the insured learned of the occurrence | Incident report versus interview | Use the documented date; record both; show the clock under each |
| Insurer's claim notes and letters disagree on when it had the facts to disclaim | Rolling investigation | Use the earliest date supported by documents for the clock; record the later date as the insurer's justification to be proven |
| Governing state unclear | Multistate risk or forum different from issuance | Flag as Critical; analyze each candidate; show where the position changes |
| Other carriers unknown | No policy search done | Recommend the insured's broker and prior-carrier search; tender to each one found |
