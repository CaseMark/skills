## 1. Threshold screen: is Stark implicated at all

Answer every question in order, with a source locator for each answer. If any answer is "no", the label is "Stark: not implicated" for that relationship, and the analysis continues under the AKS and state law.

| # | Question | Rule | Common error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the referral source a "physician" or an immediate family member of one? | 411.351 adopts 42 U.S.C. 1395x(r): doctors of medicine or osteopathy, and, within limits, dentists, podiatrists, optometrists and chiropractors. "Immediate family member" is defined in 411.351 | Applying Stark to a nurse practitioner, physician assistant, marketer, or a hospital-to-hospital arrangement |
| 2 | Is there a "referral"? | 411.351: request, order, certification or plan of care for DHS; excludes DHS personally performed by the referring physician; excludes certain pathologist, radiologist and radiation oncologist requests on consultation | Treating a physician's own professional service as a referral, or treating hospital services the physician orders as personally performed |
| 3 | Is the service "designated health services" payable by Medicare? | 411.351: ten categories, including inpatient and outpatient hospital services; DHS means only DHS payable in whole or part by Medicare; services paid as part of a composite rate (for example ASC services) are not DHS unless the listed service is itself paid under a composite rate (home health and hospital services are DHS) | Starting the exception analysis before confirming DHS; treating commercial-only business as Stark business |
| 4 | Is the DHS "entity" the party in the arrangement? | 411.351: the entity performs the DHS or presents the claim, including the party to which payment is reassigned | Analyzing the wrong legal entity in a system |
| 5 | Is there a financial relationship? | 1395nn(a)(2); 411.354(a)-(c): ownership or investment interest, or a direct or indirect compensation arrangement; remuneration is anything of value, in cash or in kind, with the narrow carve-outs in 411.351 | Ignoring free space, staff, or below-market items as remuneration |

Medicaid: 42 U.S.C. 1396b(s) bars federal matching payments to a State for DHS furnished on a referral that Medicare would deny. Whether that supports False Claims Act liability on Medicaid claims is contested; mark any Medicaid-Stark conclusion `[VERIFY]`.

## 2. Direct, indirect and stand in the shoes

- **Direct** (411.354(c)(1)(i)): remuneration passes between the physician and the DHS entity with no intervening person.
- **Stand in the shoes, mandatory** (411.354(c)(1)(ii)): a physician who has an ownership or investment interest in his or her "physician organization" (a physician, a physician practice, or a group practice under 411.352) is deemed to stand in its shoes, so the organization's arrangement with the DHS entity is the physician's own direct arrangement. Not applied to a titular ownership interest with no right to profits, dividends or sale proceeds (411.354(c)(3)(ii)(C)).
- **Stand in the shoes, permissive** (411.354(c)(1)(iii)): non-owner physicians may be treated as standing in the shoes.
- **Effect on exceptions** (411.354(c)(3)(i)): signature requirements look to the physician organization and each physician deemed to stand in its shoes; all other requirements, including "referrals ... between the parties", look to the DHS entity and the physician organization with all its members, employees and independent contractor physicians.
- **Indirect** (411.354(c)(2)): an unbroken chain of financial relationships; the physician receives aggregate compensation from the link closest to him or her that varies with the volume or value of referrals or other business generated; and the per-unit compensation is not FMV, could rise or fall with referrals or other business, or is payment for a lease of space or equipment; and the DHS entity has actual knowledge or acts in reckless disregard or deliberate ignorance. Only 411.355 and the indirect compensation exception at 411.357(p) apply to an indirect arrangement (411.354(c)(4)(i)), with narrow MCO, IPA and value-based exceptions.
- **Order of work**: decide stand in the shoes first. If the physician is deemed to stand in the shoes, the arrangement is direct and the indirect definition and 411.357(p) are never reached.

## 3. The three separate compensation tests

CMS describes commercial reasonableness, the volume or value standard, and fair market value as "separate and distinct requirements, each of which must be satisfied" (85 FR 77530). Give each its own evidence and its own conclusion.

| Test | Definition | Evidence | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fair market value | 411.351: value in an arm's-length transaction consistent with general market value; for space and equipment, value for general commercial purposes, not adjusted for proximity or convenience to a referral source | Independent valuation with method, data, date and scope; time records showing services actually furnished | Valuation that assumes the services are needed; stale report; valuation of a stipend with no hours |
| Commercially reasonable | 411.351: the particular arrangement furthers a legitimate business purpose of the parties and is sensible, considering their size, type, scope and specialty; it may be commercially reasonable even if it does not produce profit. Most exceptions add "even if no referrals were made" | Documented need, no duplication of services already bought, organizational size and service line | Treating an FMV appraisal as proof of need; denying reasonableness only because one party loses money. CMS: multiple arrangements for the same services are questionable and may fail the "reasonable and necessary" element of 411.357(d)(1)(iii) (85 FR 77533, 77535); an arrangement whose purpose is to attract a physician's business is not commercially reasonable (85 FR 77534) |
| Volume or value; other business generated | 411.354(d)(5)-(6): compensation to a physician takes referrals into account only if the formula includes referrals as a variable that correlates positively; compensation from a physician, only if it correlates negatively. Unit-based compensation at FMV that does not vary with referrals is deemed not to (411.354(d)(2)-(3)) | The written formula; payment ledger | Productivity pay on DHS the physician orders (technical or facility component) rather than personally performs; percentage-of-revenue or per-click lease charges for lessor-referred patients |

Directed referral requirements must meet 411.354(d)(4), including patient choice, insurer determination and the physician's medical judgment.

## 4. Set in advance and modifications

- 411.354(d)(1)(i): the aggregate amount, a time-based or per-unit amount, or a specific objectively verifiable formula is set out in writing before the items, services, space or equipment are furnished.
- 411.354(d)(1)(ii): compensation may be modified mid-term only if (A) every requirement of an applicable exception is met on the effective date of the modified compensation; (B) the modified compensation is determined before the items or services are furnished; and (C) before they are furnished, the modified formula is set in writing in sufficient detail to be objectively verified. **The 90-day writing and signature rule in 411.354(e)(4) does not apply to (C).**
- A retroactive increase, an oral rate change, or a "true-up" to a new rate for services already furnished is not set in advance.
- The writing requirement may be met by a collection of contemporaneous documents (411.354(e)(2)), and electronic signatures valid under federal or state law count (411.354(e)(3)); neither makes a later document contemporaneous.

## 6. Group practice, in-office ancillary services and recruitment

Read in full on 2026-10-30: 411.352, 411.355(b), 411.357(e)(1)-(4).

| Rule | Elements to test | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| Group practice, 411.352 | Single legal entity operating primarily as a physician group practice ((a)); at least two physician members ((b)); each member furnishes substantially the full range of services through shared space, equipment and staff ((c)); at least 75 percent of members' patient care services furnished through and billed by the group ((d)), with HPSA, start-up and relocation relief; expense and income distribution methods set before the payments are received ((e)); unified business with centralized decision-making and consolidated billing and accounting ((f)); members personally conduct at least 75 percent of encounters ((h)); no member paid directly or indirectly on the volume or value of his or her referrals except under (i) ((g)) | Calling any practice a "group practice"; informal affiliations formed to share referral profits or practices under common control by a management company or hospital treated as one entity ((a)) |
| Profit shares, 411.352(i)(1) | Share of overall DHS profits of the whole group, or of a component of at least five physicians (all DHS of the group if fewer than five), divided reasonably and verifiably; deemed compliant if per capita, distributed by non-DHS revenue, or DHS revenue under 5 percent of group revenue and 5 percent or less of each physician's pay | Allocating imaging or lab profits to the physician who ordered them; carving a component smaller than five physicians |
| Productivity bonuses, 411.352(i)(2) | Based on services the physician personally performed or "incident to" them, not directly related to referrals; deemed compliant if based on total encounters or personally performed RVUs, on non-DHS services, or under the same 5 percent test; value-based enterprise profits under (i)(3); documentation available on request ((i)(4)) | Paying a bonus on the technical component of DHS the physician orders |
| In-office ancillary services, 411.355(b) | Furnished personally by the referring physician, a group member, or an individual the physician or group supervises ((b)(1)); in the same building under one of the three hours tests (35/30, 8/6 with usual patients, or 8/6 with the physician present) or a centralized building used by the group ((b)(2)); billed by the physician, the group, a wholly owned entity, or a qualifying billing agent ((b)(3)); DME limited to the listed items ((b)(4)); written notice of at least five other suppliers within 25 miles for MRI, CT and PET referrals ((b)(7)) | Skipping the location and billing tests; DME outside the list; missing imaging notice |
| Physician recruitment, 411.357(e) | Hospital pays to induce a physician to relocate the practice into the hospital's geographic area served to join its staff ((e)(1)); signed writing; not conditioned on referrals; amount not based on actual or anticipated referrals; physician free to hold privileges and refer elsewhere; relocation means a move of at least 25 miles into the area, or at least 75 percent of revenue from new patients ((e)(2)(iv)); relocation waived for residents and physicians in practice 1 year or less and certain federal employment ((e)(3)); when paid through a practice: practice signs if it keeps any amount, only actual recruiting costs retained, income-guarantee cost allocations limited to actual incremental costs, records kept 6 years, no unreasonable practice restrictions ((e)(4)) | Paying an established local physician who has not relocated; the practice keeping more than its incremental costs; noncompete terms that keep the recruit out of the area |

## 8. Sanctions and burden

- No Medicare payment for DHS furnished on a prohibited referral, and amounts collected must be refunded (1395nn(g)(1)-(2); 411.353(c)-(d)). These consequences do not depend on intent.
- Civil money penalties need knowledge: up to $15,000 per service for a claim the person knows or should know is prohibited (1395nn(g)(3)) and up to $100,000 per circumvention scheme with a principal purpose of assuring referrals (1395nn(g)(4)). These are statutory amounts; the 2025 inflation-adjusted maximums in 45 C.F.R. 102.3 are $31,670 and $211,146. Whether a 2026 adjustment has issued is `[VERIFY]`.
- On appeal of a payment denial, the entity bears the burden of persuasion that the service was not furnished on a prohibited referral (411.353(c)(2)). In FCA litigation the Third Circuit placed on the defendant the burden of proving an exception once a violation is shown (Kosenske, 554 F.3d 88); burden allocation in other circuits is `[VERIFY]`.
- Advice of counsel protects only a client that disclosed the facts fully and followed the advice; a client that shopped for favorable opinions while ignoring negative ones lost the defense (Drakeford v. Tuomey, 792 F.3d 364 (4th Cir. 2015)).
