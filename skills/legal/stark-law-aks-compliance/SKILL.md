# Stark Law and Anti-Kickback Compliance Analysis

Prepares counsel's compliance analysis of one or more financial relationships between a health care entity and physicians or other referral sources: a threshold screen, a Stark exception analysis, a separate Anti-Kickback Statute analysis, state-law and reliance checks, exposure, a remediation and disclosure recommendation, and hand-offs, in the fixed format of `references/output-template.md`. It reviews proposed arrangements before signing and existing arrangements after a problem is found. The aim is to document compliant arrangements accurately and to cure or disclose noncompliance through the mechanisms the law provides. Every output is a draft for review by a licensed health care regulatory attorney.

## Why This Skill Exists

Physician arrangement opinions fail in a few places. The drafter starts with an exception before asking whether the referral source is a physician and the service is Medicare-payable DHS, accepts a fair market value appraisal as proof that the arrangement is needed, treats a Stark exception as AKS clearance, or applies a 90-day grace period to a rate change it cannot cure. Stark payment denial needs no intent and reaches every DHS claim the physician referred while the arrangement was out of compliance; the AKS is a felony with a one-purpose intent test; either can become False Claims Act liability. The opinion therefore keeps the two statutes apart, ties each conclusion to a regulation or case actually read, and says plainly where a circuit or state rule is unconfirmed.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement letter, organization chart, licensure of each referral source | Who the client is, which entity furnishes DHS, whether the referral source is a "physician" (42 U.S.C. 1395x(r)) | Stark applied to a nurse practitioner, physician assistant or marketer, then the AKS skipped because "Stark does not apply"; the billing entity after reassignment misidentified; one firm advising both sides of the same arrangement |
| Service lines, referral and claims data, payer mix | Whether a referral for Medicare-payable DHS exists, and what federal program and commercial business is involved | Exception analysis begun before confirming DHS; personally performed services treated as referrals; hospital services the physician orders treated as personally performed; commercial-only business assumed unregulated despite state all-payor statutes and EKRA |
| FMV valuation report, statement of need, existing contracts for the same services | Fair market value and commercial reasonableness | Appraisal treated as proof of need; a second directorship duplicating services already bought; reasonableness denied only because a party loses money (411.351 says profit is not required); space value adjusted for proximity to referrals |
| Compensation formula, amendments, rate-change emails | Set in advance; volume or value | Retroactive or oral rate increase; the 90-day writing rule applied to a modification though 411.354(d)(1)(ii)(C) excludes it; productivity pay on ordered DHS; percentage or per-click rent for lessor-referred patients |
| Executed agreement, signature dates, expiration date, payment ledger | Writing, signature, term, holdover, reconciliation | 90-day cure counted from the effective date instead of the noncompliance date; cure claimed where FMV or set in advance also fails; holdover on changed terms; payments that differ from the contract; temporary-noncompliance relief used twice in 3 years |
| Group practice records (ownership, compensation plan, billing numbers, office hours), recruitment agreements | In-office ancillary services, group practice, physician recruitment | A practice called a "group practice" without testing 411.352 (single legal entity, 75 percent substantially-all and encounter tests, methods set in advance); DHS profits split by each physician's orders instead of overall profits or a personally performed productivity bonus (411.352(i)); in-office ancillary location and billing tests skipped; a recruitment payment to a physician who has not relocated, or one passed through a practice that keeps more than its incremental costs (411.357(e)) |
| Ownership charts, operating agreements, chain of contracts | Direct versus indirect; stand in the shoes | Physician owners of a contracting practice not treated as standing in its shoes, so a direct arrangement is analyzed as indirect and per-unit FMV is used to say no arrangement exists |
| Emails, minutes, pro formas tracking referrals; safe harbor elements | AKS intent and safe harbor fit | Stark exception met treated as AKS clearance; one-purpose test ignored; safe harbor elements that differ from Stark (aggregate rent set in advance, 1-year term, exact part-time schedules) skipped; no safe harbor treated as a violation; the FMV exception's own AKS condition (411.357(l)(5)) missed |
| OIG or CMS advisory opinions, prior counsel opinions | Reliance | Another party's OIG opinion relied on (42 C.F.R. 1008.53); an OIG opinion treated as deciding Stark or FMV; material factual differences ignored; opinions shopped (Tuomey) |
| Discovered noncompliance, claims history | Exposure, refund and disclosure path | Stark-only conduct sent to the OIG SDP or AKS conduct to the CMS SRDP; both protocols used; the 60-day clock counted from the wrong event |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is signed, sent to a government agency, or given to the other party by this skill. Every output is a draft for attorney review and is never called final.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (whether time records exist), *source conflict* (ledger rate differs from the contract rate), *unverified external lookup* (a state statute not in `references/primary-sources.md`), or *policy election* (whether to disclose, refund or restructure).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For facts: executed agreements and signed amendments, then payment ledgers and time records, then valuation reports, then term sheets, emails and summaries. For law: statute, then current regulation, then CMS and OIG preamble guidance, then advisory opinions (binding only as their own terms allow), then the governing circuit's decisions, then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every contract term carries document, section and page; every fact carries a source locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`; every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`. A circuit's or state's rule is named only if it is in the register. Re-read the current statute and eCFR text before the opinion goes out. Never invent a citation, an advisory opinion number or a pin cite.
5. **Threshold screen before exceptions.** Confirm a physician or immediate family member, a referral, Medicare-payable DHS, and a direct or indirect financial relationship before testing any Stark exception. If any is absent, label the relationship "Stark: not implicated" and continue with the AKS and state law.
6. **Two statutes, two analyses.** Stark payment denial and refund are strict liability; Stark CMPs need knowledge; the AKS needs knowing and willful conduct, judged under the governing circuit's intent standard, with no specific intent to violate the statute required (1320a-7b(h)). A Stark exception never establishes AKS compliance; a missed safe harbor is not itself a violation.
7. **Three separate tests.** Fair market value, commercial reasonableness, and volume or value each get their own evidence and conclusion (85 FR 77530). A valuation report proves only what its scope covers.
8. **Clocks from their own triggers.** Compute each grace period, cure, holdover and repayment deadline from the trigger its rule uses, with date and weekday (`references/stark-rules-and-clocks.md`, section 7). A set-in-advance modification cannot use the 90-day writing and signature rule.
9. **No backdating.** Never date a signature or document earlier than the day it is signed or created, or prepare a document meant to look as if it existed earlier. Cures run through the rules that allow them, prospective amendments, refunds and disclosure.
10. **Objective analysis.** State adverse facts and intent evidence in the opinion. Decline to omit known material facts or to deliver a fixed conclusion regardless of the analysis; a favorable opinion built on edited facts gives no protection (Drakeford v. Tuomey).
11. **Reliance only where the law allows it.** An OIG advisory opinion protects only its requestors; it does not decide Stark or fair market value.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; the client, its role, the requester, and the conflict check.
2. Each arrangement: parties and their legal entities, what is exchanged, compensation formula, term, effective and expiration dates, signature dates, amendments.
3. Payments actually made (ledger), and time records or deliverables for services.
4. Each referral source's licensure, and each physician's ownership in any contracting entity.
5. Services the physician orders from the entity, DHS categories, and payer mix (Medicare, Medicaid, other federal, commercial).
6. FMV reports with date, method and scope, and any statement of need or existing contracts for the same services.
7. Intent evidence: emails, minutes, pro formas, referral tracking.
8. Advisory opinions and prior legal opinions relied on, with their facts.
9. States where the parties operate and services are furnished; the federal circuit.
10. The question (structure a proposed arrangement, review an existing one, look-back, disclosure decision) and the deadline.

**Defaults if not supplied** (a statute or regulation overrides any default it addresses): payer mix includes Medicare, so DHS screening proceeds, marked `[UNVERIFIED]`; governing circuit is the one where the DHS entity sits; state law "State law: not researched [VERIFY]"; a writing is treated as missing until located; the 90-day writing and signature rule is never assumed available until 411.354(e)(4) is tested; formal tone.

**Hard stops (label the whole request, or the declined part, "Declined: hard stop"; produce the request, routing or compliant alternative in place of what is declined):**
- No compensation terms at all: no agreement, term sheet or description of the parties, what is paid, and for what. Produce the document and fact request.
- A request to backdate a signature or document, to create a document meant to appear contemporaneous, or to draft terms under which a referral source's pay rises with the volume or value of that source's referrals (for example a per-procedure or per-patient payment): decline that part, explain the lawful options, and complete the rest of the request.
- A request to omit known material facts or intent evidence from the opinion, or to state a conclusion regardless of the analysis: decline that version and produce the complete opinion, including the facts the requester wanted left out.

When only part of a request is declined, use the partial-decline labelling in `references/output-template.md`, Part 1: "Declined: hard stop" for the declined part, and the status of the remainder on a second line. Analyzing an existing arrangement with a referral-based term is not a hard stop; drafting or adopting that term is.
- One firm asked to advise both the entity and the physician on the same arrangement without a cleared conflict: route one side to separate counsel.

**Not hard stops:** a missing FMV report or time records (Critical item; status "Not ready: blocking issues"); an unknown state rule (`[VERIFY]` and both readings); a request to sign now with a stated retroactive effective date (not itself a backdate; explain why it does not satisfy set in advance); an arrangement already terminated (look-back analysis).

## Step 1: Client, Scope and Inventory

Record the client, role, conflict check and question in the header of `references/output-template.md`. Build the source-review register and the arrangement matrix in Part 2, one row per arrangement and per period in which terms changed. Compare compensation as written against compensation as paid now; a mismatch is a Critical item.

## Step 2: Threshold Screen

Read `references/stark-rules-and-clocks.md`, sections 1 and 2. Answer the five threshold questions with sources. Decide stand in the shoes before anything else: if physician owners of a contracting practice stand in its shoes, the arrangement is direct and the indirect definition is never reached. Assign "Stark: not implicated" where the screen fails, and carry the relationship to Step 5.

## Step 3: Stark Exception Elements

Read `references/stark-rules-and-clocks.md`, sections 3 to 6. Choose every exception that could apply, including in-office ancillary services, group practice and recruitment where the facts raise them, and test each element with a source. Give fair market value, commercial reasonableness, and volume or value separate conclusions. For the FMV compensation exception, carry the AKS conclusion from Step 5 back into element (l)(5).

## Step 4: Writing, Signature, Term and Clocks

Read `references/stark-rules-and-clocks.md`, sections 4 and 7. Test set in advance for the original terms and for every rate change, with the date each change was decided and first put in writing. Fill the clock table with one row per timed element and its own trigger. Whenever the label is "Stark: exception not met", address every cure in the clock table (the 90-day writing and signature rule, temporary noncompliance, payment reconciliation, holdover) and state for each whether it is available and why; say when the nonmonetary-return and value-based monitoring periods do not apply to the exception tested.

## Step 5: Anti-Kickback Analysis

Read `references/aks-state-and-disclosure.md`, sections 1 to 3. Identify the remuneration and the federal program business, test the nearest safe harbor element by element, list intent evidence for and against, and apply the governing circuit's intent standard from `references/primary-sources.md` or both readings. Assign the AKS label. Analyze every referral source, physician or not.

## Step 6: State Law and Other All-Payor Laws

Read `references/aks-state-and-disclosure.md`, section 4. Screen the states involved and EKRA where a laboratory, recovery home or treatment facility is involved. Name a state's rule only if it is in the register; otherwise mark `[VERIFY]` and hand off to legal-research.

## Step 7: Reliance

Read `references/aks-state-and-disclosure.md`, section 5. For each advisory opinion or prior opinion, record who requested it, what it decided, what it could not decide, and each material factual difference from the client's arrangement.

## Step 8: Exposure, Remediation and Disclosure Path

Read `references/aks-state-and-disclosure.md`, section 6. For noncompliant periods, list the affected DHS claims or the data needed, the 60-day clock and any suspension, and the disclosure path label with reasons. Recommend prospective fixes and the arrangement controls in Part 7 of the template; send FCA quantification to conducting-false-claims-analysis.

## Step 9: Assemble the Opinion

Assign the opinion status and one label of each kind per arrangement and period from `references/output-template.md`, Part 1. Add the blocking issues ranked by severity, the reconciliation table, hand-offs, and every `[VERIFY]` item with what would settle it.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Is the client and its role correct, and has the conflict check cleared?
2. Are the executed agreements, signature dates and payment ledgers confirmed for every period analyzed?
3. Has counsel accepted the stand-in-the-shoes and DHS conclusions?
4. Does the client accept the recommended remediation, and who decides the disclosure path?
5. Are the state-law and circuit items marked `[VERIFY]` assigned to someone?

If the user does not answer, recommend confirming payments against the contract and the dates of every rate change first; those facts decide most set-in-advance and disclosure questions.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. The client, the requester and the conflict check are stated.
2. The threshold screen is answered with sources before any exception, and every non-physician referral source is still analyzed under the AKS.
3. Stand in the shoes is decided before any indirect-arrangement analysis.
4. Every exception relied on has an element table with sources, and no element is assumed.
5. Fair market value, commercial reasonableness, and volume or value have separate conclusions and evidence.
6. Every rate change is tested for set in advance, no 90-day rule is applied to a modification, and every "exception not met" label addresses each cure in the clock table.
7. Each clock has its own trigger, date with weekday, period and status.
8. The AKS analysis is separate, with safe harbor elements, intent evidence, and the circuit's standard or both readings.
9. State and all-payor laws are screened; unverified rules are `[VERIFY]` and routed.
10. Advisory opinion reliance is limited to what the law allows, with factual differences listed.
11. The disclosure path matches the conduct (Stark only to the SRDP; AKS or both to the OIG SDP), and the 60-day rule is addressed.
12. Labels are used exactly as in `references/output-template.md`; no document is backdated or recommended to be.
13. The output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Write for the client, but as if a regulator, a relator and a court will read it. Adverse facts go in the opinion, not in a cover email.
- Be definite where the regulation and documents allow it. "Stark: exception not met" is a finding that drives remediation, not an accusation.
- Separate prospective advice (how to fix the arrangement) from look-back advice (what happened to past claims).
- When an instruction conflicts with a rule (sign a renewal now to cure past payments, rely on another party's opinion), draft the compliant recommendation and explain the change.
- If a subpoena, civil investigative demand or unsealed qui tam exists, note it, preserve documents, and hand the exposure work to conducting-false-claims-analysis and litigation counsel.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Signed agreement cannot be found | Contract database gaps, signatures on a separate page | Search email and e-signature logs; treat as unsigned until found; test 411.354(e)(2)-(4) |
| Ledger rate differs from the contract rate | Unrecorded amendment or payroll error | Record both; treat the ledger as what was paid; test set in advance and reconciliation under 411.353(h) |
| Scanned agreement unreadable | Image-only file | Try OCR; list unreadable pages and the elements that depend on them; request a clean copy |
| FMV report is stale or silent on scope | Older engagement or narrow scope | Mark High or Critical; do not extend the report to need or to a different structure |
| Ownership of a contracting practice unknown | No cap table | Request it; show the analysis with and without stand in the shoes |
| Payer mix unknown | No claims data | Assume Medicare for Stark screening, mark `[UNVERIFIED]`, request data |
| Governing circuit or state rule unclear | Multistate parties | Analyze each candidate; show where the conclusion changes |
