# Summary Judgment Brief (FRCP 56 and State Equivalents)

Prepares a memorandum supporting or opposing a motion for summary judgment or partial summary judgment, with the statement of undisputed material facts or the responsive statement, evidentiary objections and, where discovery is incomplete, a Rule 56(d) declaration. The deliverable is a readiness determination, a burden matrix, a deadline computation, ranked blocking issues, a fact-to-record reconciliation table, the draft papers and hand-offs. Its aim is a record-true motion or opposition the court can decide on the cited evidence; it declines to draft a declaration that contradicts the declarant's earlier sworn testimony without acknowledging and explaining the change. Everything it produces is a draft for review and signature by a licensed attorney.

## Why This Skill Exists

Summary judgment is decided on the cited record, not on the brief. Motions fail for mechanical reasons: a plaintiff moving on its own claim uses the "no evidence" route that belongs to a party without the trial burden; a fact cites a whole deposition instead of a page and line; a declaration repeats hearsay; an opposition answers a statement of facts with blanket denials and the facts are deemed admitted; a deadline is taken from Rule 56(b) when the scheduling order or state rule sets another. Oppositions fail when "discovery is not complete" appears only in the brief, or when a new declaration contradicts the client's deposition. This skill makes the burden, the record and the clock explicit before any argument is written.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Pleadings, jury instructions, substantive law of each claim and defense | Allocating the trial burden element by element and choosing the movant's initial showing | Plaintiff moving on its own claim, or defendant on its own affirmative defense, merely "points out" the other side's lack of evidence instead of establishing every element (Fontenot, Fitzpatrick); heightened clear-and-convincing standard ignored (Anderson); California defendant argues absence of evidence without presenting evidence (Aguilar) |
| Deposition transcripts, declarations, exhibits, discovery responses | Pinpoint support for each fact under Rule 56(c)(1) | Facts cited to the complaint, the brief or "Smith Dep." with no page and line; a declaration refers to documents never placed in the record; a statement paragraph cites a page that does not say what the paragraph says (Holtz) |
| Declarations, business records, expert reports, emails | Showing the material can be presented in admissible form | Corporate declarant repeats what others told her; no foundation witness for a document; hearsay with no exception; Rule 56(c)(2) objection omitted or filed as a separate motion; California objections not made at the hearing and waived |
| Statement of undisputed facts and the responsive statement (S.D.N.Y./E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 56.1; Cal. CCP 437c(b) and Rule 3.1350) | Fact-by-fact framing of what is and is not disputed | "Disputed" with no record cite, argument in place of evidence, or a blanket denial, so the fact is deemed admitted; California response that does not state "disputed" or "undisputed"; movant treats deemed admissions as an automatic win (Rule 56(e)(3), Vermont Teddy Bear) |
| Scheduling order, local rules, judge's individual practices, state procedure code | Filing, opposition, reply and hearing dates | Rule 56(b)'s 30-days-after-discovery default used when an order sets another date; California 81-day notice, mail extension and 30-days-before-trial limit missed; Texas response and hearing clocks taken from the pre-March 2026 rule; New York 120-day note-of-issue cutoff missed (Brill) |
| Discovery status: open requests, scheduled depositions, motions to compel | Whether the motion is premature; Rule 56(d) or CCP 437c(h) relief | Opposition says "discovery is incomplete" only in the brief, with no declaration of the facts sought, why they matter, the efforts made and why they failed (Hudson River Sloop, Paddington); early motion called "untimely" when Rule 56(b) allows it |
| Conflicting testimony: deposition versus later declaration, competing witnesses, video | Whether a dispute is genuine | Movant asks the court to disbelieve a witness or weigh testimony (Anderson, Reeves, Tolan); Scott v. | Notice of motion, operative pleading, party list, requested relief (scope) | Defining what the motion seeks, for and against whom | Notice does not identify each claim or defense or part (Rule 56(a)); relief sought for a party that did not move or against claims not pleaded; California summary adjudication of an issue that does not completely dispose of a cause of action, defense, duty or punitive damages claim without the (t) stipulation |
| Concessions "for purposes of this motion only" and requests for partial relief | Rule 56(g) orders and their effect at trial | Nonmovant concedes a fact without the limiting phrase and it is treated as established; movant never asks, in the alternative, for an order establishing undisputed facts or damages items |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is filed or served by this skill. Every output is a draft for attorney review and is never called ready to file.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a transcript, exhibit or order missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as an *unknown fact* (the date discovery closed), a *source conflict* (two balance figures), an *unverified external lookup* (a local rule not read in current text), or a *policy election* (whether to concede a fact, cross-move, or seek partial relief).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For facts: sworn testimony and authenticated documents in the record, then discovery responses, then unsworn correspondence; the transcript governs over a summary of it, the executed document over a description of it, the court's docket over a calendar entry. For law: the current rule or statute text and the scheduling order, then controlling appellate cases, then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and state which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every fact in the brief and statement carries a record pinpoint or is marked `[UNVERIFIED]` and kept out of the statement until supported. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or is marked `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a case, a pin cite, a deposition line or a local rule number.
5. **Burden first.** Build the burden matrix before drafting argument. A party moving on a claim or defense it must prove at trial establishes every element with evidence; only a movant without the trial burden may discharge its initial burden by pointing out an absence of evidence, and in California a moving defendant must still present evidence. Where the allocation on a defense is unconfirmed, analyze both and mark it `[VERIFY]`.
6. **The record, by pinpoint.** Cite page and line, paragraph, exhibit page or Bates number. Place every cited document in the record through a declaration that can authenticate it. A party's own pleading is not evidence for that party.
7. **Admissible form.** Each declaration is on personal knowledge and shows competence. Object to the other side's inadmissible material in the papers under Rule 56(c)(2) (federally no separate motion to strike is needed); in California, object so the objection is made at the hearing.
8. **No weighing.** The movant argues that the element fails even crediting the nonmovant's evidence; it never asks the court to disbelieve a witness. The nonmovant identifies the conflict and the record supporting each side. Scott v. Harris is invoked only where the record blatantly contradicts an account.
9. **Truthful declarations only.** Never draft a declaration the declarant cannot make on personal knowledge, or one that contradicts the declarant's prior sworn testimony without acknowledging that testimony and explaining the change, and never describe as undisputed a fact the record contradicts. Offer instead a declaration that confronts and explains the prior testimony with whatever corroborates it, or discovery to obtain the evidence. Rule 56(h) sanctions bad-faith declarations.
10. **The forum's rules control.** The scheduling order, the judge's individual practices and the local rule override Rule 56(b)'s default; state rules replace it entirely. Read each in current text on the drafting date and compute every deadline from its own trigger. If a rule cannot be read, mark it `[VERIFY]` and list it as a Critical open item.
11. **Answer every numbered fact.** An opposition responds to each paragraph of the movant's statement: admit what the record does not genuinely dispute, deny with a pinpoint to admissible evidence, and say when the cited material does not support the assertion. A movant never argues that unanswered facts alone win the motion.
12. **Scope matches the relief the rule allows.** Identify each claim, defense or part, and the parties for and against whom relief is sought. Request a Rule 56(g) order in the alternative where partial relief is realistic. A nonmovant conceding a fact says "for purposes of this motion only" and has client authority to concede it.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; the court (federal district and judge, or state court and county); case number.
2. The paper wanted: motion, opposition, reply or cross-motion; who moves; the claims, defenses or parts at issue, and the parties for and against whom relief is sought.
3. The operative pleadings and answer, including every affirmative defense.
4. The scheduling order and amendments, the judge's individual practices, and the local rules; the date discovery closes or closed; any note of issue or trial date.
5. If opposing: the motion, memorandum, statement of facts and every supporting declaration and exhibit, with the filing and service dates and method.
6. The record: transcripts with page and line numbers, declarations, exhibits with Bates numbers, interrogatory answers, admissions.
7. Discovery status: outstanding requests, scheduled depositions, pending motions to compel, and what each would show.
8. Prior sworn statements of any witness who will sign a declaration.
9. Governing substantive law for each claim and defense, and any controlling authority counsel already relies on.
10. Client authority to concede any fact or to limit the relief sought.

**Defaults if not supplied** (a scheduling order, local rule or state rule overrides any default it addresses): federal court under FRCP 56; motion deadline 30 days after the close of all discovery under Rule 56(b), which a scheduling order usually displaces and which is marked `[VERIFY]` until the order is read; opposition and reply deadlines `[VERIFY]` from the local rule; Bluebook citation; a statement of undisputed material facts in numbered paragraphs even where not required; no concession of any fact.

**Hard stops (label the determination "Declined: hard stop"; produce the blocking-issues list and the request or compliant alternative, not the requested paper):**
- The court cannot be identified, so neither the governing rule nor the clock can be determined.
- The claims or defenses at issue, or which party moves, cannot be determined.
- The facts the motion or opposition depends on cannot be tied to any deposition, declaration, document, admission or discovery response in hand or obtainable: produce a record-gap memo and a declaration or discovery plan instead.
- A request to draft a declaration the declarant cannot make on personal knowledge, one that contradicts the declarant's prior sworn testimony without acknowledging and explaining it, or a statement describing as undisputed a fact the record contradicts: decline that document, explain why, and offer the compliant alternative under Ground Rule 9.

**Not hard stops:** a filing or hearing date that is missed or infeasible (a Critical item that blocks filing, not drafting; set out leave, continuance or good-cause options); incomplete discovery (Step 4); a local rule or individual practice not yet read (Critical `[VERIFY]` item).

## Step 1: Fix the Forum and the Clock

Read `references/state-and-local-practice.md`, sections 1 and 2, and the forum section that applies. List each timed element (filing, notice, opposition, reply, hearing, trial cutoff) with its own trigger, rule and computed date with weekday. If a deadline has passed or cannot be met, say so at the top of Part 1 and set out the options (leave to file late, modification of the scheduling order for good cause, a continuance, a good-cause order) with what each requires.

## Step 2: Build the Burden Matrix

Read `references/rule-56-standards.md`, sections 1 and 2. One row per claim, defense and element: who bears the trial burden and on what authority, the evidentiary standard, the movant's route (negate, point out, or establish), and the record cites. For a California motion, apply Aguilar and CCP 437c(p); for Texas, decide traditional, no-evidence or combined and title the motion accordingly.

## Step 3: Build the Record Map

Read `references/rule-56-standards.md`, sections 3 and 4. For every fact the argument needs, record the pinpoint, the witness or foundation that makes it admissible, and any contrary evidence. Facts with no pinpoint go to the open-items list, not the statement. Draft the evidentiary objections to the other side's material in the same pass.

## Step 4: Check Discovery Status

Read `references/rule-56-standards.md`, section 6 (and CCP 437c(h) or Texas 166a(d)(3) in those courts). If opposing and evidence essential to the opposition is unavailable, prepare the declaration with the facts sought, how they would create a genuine dispute on a named element, the efforts made and why they failed, and the order requested; oppose on the merits as well. If moving early, say what discovery the opponent is likely to claim and whether the motion should wait.

## Step 5: Draft the Statement or the Responsive Statement

Use the format of the forum (`references/state-and-local-practice.md`, sections 3 to 6) and Part 5 of `references/output-template.md`. One fact per paragraph, each with a pinpoint. In a response, reproduce each movant paragraph where the rule requires and use the response vocabulary; never a blanket denial.

## Step 6: Draft the Argument

Read `references/rule-56-standards.md`, sections 7 and 8. Element by element: rule, record application with statement paragraph numbers, the other side's best evidence credited, and why the element fails or is genuinely disputed. Test every declaration against the declarant's prior sworn testimony; where a contradiction exists, apply Ground Rule 9 and analyze the sham-affidavit rule of the forum circuit.

## Step 7: Fix Scope and Relief

Read `references/rule-56-standards.md`, section 9, and for California `references/state-and-local-practice.md`, section 4 (summary adjudication scope). State exactly which claims, defenses or parts, for and against which parties; request a Rule 56(g) order or summary adjudication in the alternative where it fits; protect "for purposes of this motion only" concessions. Draft the proposed order where the forum requires one.

## Step 8: Assemble the Deliverable and Hand-Offs

Lay out the header and Parts 1 to 6 per `references/output-template.md`. Build the reconciliation table from Steps 3 and 5. Complete each hand-off with the facts the receiving skill needs. List every `[VERIFY]` item with what would settle it, drawing on `references/primary-sources.md`.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Does the burden matrix match counsel's view of who bears the burden on each element and defense?
2. Is every computed deadline consistent with the scheduling order and the judge's practices as counsel reads them?
3. Does the client authorize every concession, including those "for purposes of this motion only"?
4. Has each declarant reviewed and confirmed every sentence of the declaration, and compared it with prior testimony?
5. Is the relief requested what the client wants, including any alternative partial relief?

If the user does not answer, recommend confirming the deadlines and the burden allocation first; those two decide whether the brief is heard and which argument it can make.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. The forum, governing rule, local rule and scheduling order are named, with whether each was read in current text today.
2. Every deadline appears in the clocks table with its own trigger, rule, date and weekday, and any missed or infeasible deadline is a Critical item with options.
3. The burden matrix covers every claim, defense and element at issue, and the movant's route matches who bears the trial burden.
4. Every fact in the brief appears in the statement with a pinpoint; no fact cites a pleading, the brief or a whole transcript.
5. Every cited document is placed in the record through a declarant who can authenticate it, and every declaration is on personal knowledge.
6. Admissibility objections to the other side's material appear in the papers in the form the forum requires.
7. A response addresses every numbered paragraph with the response vocabulary and a pinpoint for each denial; no blanket denial.
8. The brief never asks the court to weigh evidence or judge credibility, and Scott v. Harris is used only for a blatant record contradiction.
9. No declaration contradicts the declarant's prior sworn testimony without acknowledging and explaining it.
10. Where discovery is incomplete and the client opposes, a Rule 56(d) or equivalent declaration is drafted and the opposition also meets the merits.
11. The relief requested identifies each claim, defense or part and each party, fits what the rule allows, and includes alternative partial relief where realistic.
12. Every legal statement has a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`; blocking issues are ranked with owners; the output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Lead with the strongest single dispositive ground; a motion with ten weak grounds invites denial of all of them.
- Keep the statement of facts free of argument and adjectives; the court compares it line by line with the record.
- For a movant, a fact that is genuinely disputed but immaterial should be shown to be immaterial, not argued away.
- For a nonmovant, one well-supported genuine dispute on one element defeats the motion on that claim; spend the pages there.
- Cross-motions are separate motions; each side carries its own burden on its own motion, and Rule 56(f) lets the court rule for a nonmovant after notice.
- Raise purely legal grounds clearly as such; Dupree and Ortiz affect what must be renewed after trial.
- Respect page and word limits and any pre-motion letter requirement; a stricken brief is worse than a short one.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Transcript has no line numbers or is a rough draft | Certified copy not yet received | Cite page and quote; list certified transcript as a Critical item before filing |
| Scanned exhibits are unreadable | Image-only PDF | Try OCR; list pages that remain unreadable and the facts that depend on them; request clean copies |
| Two balance or damages figures differ | Transposition, different as-of dates, or interest included in one | Show both in the reconciliation table; use neither in the statement until the declarant reconciles them; consider liability-only relief with a 56(g) request |
| The scheduling order and local rule give different deadlines | Order modified the default | The order governs for the case; record both; confirm with counsel |
| Judge's individual practices cannot be found | Not posted or recently changed | Mark `[VERIFY]`; Critical item; check the court website and chambers' instructions |
| Client's recollection now differs from the deposition | Refreshed memory, misunderstanding, or pressure to create a dispute | Apply Ground Rule 9; collect documents that corroborate; consider Rule 30(e) timing with deposition-errata-sheet |
| Movant's statement cites deposition pages not in the record | Excerpts omitted from the appendix | Respond that the cited material is not in the record and does not support the assertion; do not supply it |
| Forum is a state not covered in the references | Not researched | Record the search and date; mark every procedural rule `[VERIFY]`; draft to the stricter reading |
