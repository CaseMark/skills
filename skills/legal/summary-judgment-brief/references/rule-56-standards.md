## 1. The standard

- Rule 56(a): the court shall grant summary judgment if the movant shows no genuine dispute as to any material fact and entitlement to judgment as a matter of law. A party may move on a claim, a defense, or part of either, and must identify each one.
- Material: only disputes over facts that might affect the outcome under the governing substantive law count. Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986).
- Genuine: the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party. Anderson, 477 U.S. at 248. A scintilla is not enough. Id. at 252.
- The inquiry applies the substantive evidentiary standard of proof that governs at trial (for example, clear and convincing evidence where the claim requires it). Anderson, 477 U.S. at 254-55.
- The nonmovant must do more than show "some metaphysical doubt as to the material facts." Matsushita Elec. Indus. Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp., 475 U.S. 574, 586 (1986). Where the claim is implausible in its factual context (there, an antitrust conspiracy that "makes no economic sense"), more persuasive evidence is needed. Id. at 587. Do not cite Matsushita's plausibility gloss outside that kind of inference problem without saying so.

## 2. Burden matrix

Build one row per claim, defense and element. The first column decides everything else.

| Who bears the burden at trial on this element | What the movant must do to discharge its initial burden | What the nonmovant must then do |
|---|---|---|
| The nonmovant (for example, plaintiff's element when defendant moves; defendant's affirmative defense when plaintiff moves) | Either produce evidence negating the element, or point out to the court the absence of evidence to support the nonmovant's case, after adequate time for discovery. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317, 322-25 (1986); Fitzpatrick v. City of Atlanta, 2 F.3d 1112, 1115-16 (11th Cir. 1993). The movant still must identify the record portions that show the absence. Celotex, 477 U.S. at 323. | Go beyond the pleadings and designate specific facts showing a genuine issue (Celotex, 477 U.S. at 324), including evidence the movant overlooked, or new evidence sufficient to withstand a directed verdict. Fitzpatrick, 2 F.3d at 1116-17. |
| The movant (plaintiff on its own claim; defendant on its own affirmative defense) | Establish every essential element with evidence that would entitle it to a directed verdict if uncontroverted; pointing to the other side's lack of evidence is not enough. Fontenot v. Upjohn Co., 780 F.2d 1190, 1194 (5th Cir. 1986) ("beyond peradventure"); Fitzpatrick, 2 F.3d at 1115-16. | Come forward with evidence sufficient to call into question the inference created by the movant's evidence. Fitzpatrick, 2 F.3d at 1116. |

Rules that follow from the matrix:

- If the movant fails its initial burden, the motion must be denied even if the nonmovant shows nothing. Fitzpatrick, 2 F.3d at 1116; see Adickes v. S.H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144, 159 (1970) (movant's initial burden), as explained in Celotex, 477 U.S. at 323-26.
- A plaintiff moving on its own claim and a defendant moving on its own affirmative defense (Rule 8(c)(1) lists release, payment, waiver, statute of limitations and others) are in the second row. Who bears the burden on a given defense is a question of the governing substantive law; if it is not confirmed, analyze both rows and mark the allocation `[VERIFY]`.
- Rule 56(c)(1)(B) is the textual home of the "pointing out" route: a party may show that "an adverse party cannot produce admissible evidence to support the fact." The 2010 Committee Note says this route belongs to "a party who does not have the trial burden of production."
- California does not allow the pure "pointing out" route; see `references/state-and-local-practice.md`.

## 3. Record support

- Rule 56(c)(1)(A): every assertion that a fact cannot be or is genuinely disputed must cite "particular parts of materials in the record": depositions, documents, ESI, affidavits or declarations, stipulations, admissions, interrogatory answers, or other materials.
- 2010 Committee Note: materials not yet in the record, including materials referred to in a declaration, must be placed in the record; a pinpoint cite to an appendix satisfies the requirement.
- Rule 56(c)(3): the court need consider only the cited materials. A fact with no pinpoint may simply be ignored.
- A statement of facts is not evidence. Where the record does not support an assertion in a Local Rule 56.1 statement, the assertion is disregarded and the record reviewed independently. Holtz v. Rockefeller & Co., 258 F.3d 62, 73-74 (2d Cir. 2001).
- Pinpoint format: deposition page:line; declaration paragraph; exhibit number with page or Bates number; interrogatory or request number with the response. "See Smith Dep." or "Ex. 4 passim" is not a pinpoint.
- A party's own pleading is not evidence of its own allegations; the nonmovant must go beyond the pleadings. Celotex, 477 U.S. at 324.

## 4. Admissible form

- Rule 56(c)(2): a party may object that cited material "cannot be presented in a form that would be admissible in evidence."
- 2010 Committee Note: the objection functions like a trial objection; the burden is on the proponent to show the material is admissible as presented or to explain the admissible form anticipated; there is no need for a separate motion to strike; failing to object at summary judgment does not forfeit a trial objection.
- Celotex, 477 U.S. at 324: the nonmovant need not produce evidence in a form admissible at trial; the question is whether the content could be presented in admissible form.
- Rule 56(c)(4): a declaration must be on personal knowledge, set out facts that would be admissible, and show competence. 28 U.S.C. 1746 permits an unsworn declaration under penalty of perjury in place of an affidavit.
- Typical defects: hearsay with no exception identified; a corporate declarant reciting what others told her; documents with no witness who can lay a foundation; expert opinions with no stated basis. Whether unauthenticated documents may be considered after 2010 varies by circuit `[VERIFY]`.
- Rule 56(h): a declaration submitted in bad faith or solely for delay can lead to expenses, fees, contempt or other sanctions.

## 5. Unanswered facts are not a default

- Rule 56(e)(2): if a party fails to properly address another party's assertion, the court may consider the fact undisputed for purposes of the motion. Many local rules "deem admitted" such facts.
- Rule 56(e)(3) and the 2010 Committee Note: summary judgment cannot be granted by default even if there is a complete failure to respond; the motion and supporting materials, including facts considered undisputed, must show the movant is entitled to it. Accord Vermont Teddy Bear Co. v. 1-800 Beargram Co., 373 F.3d 241, 242-44 (2d Cir. 2004).
- For the movant: never argue that silence wins. For the nonmovant: never rely on that rule; respond to every numbered fact.

## 6. Rule 56(d)

- Text: if a nonmovant shows by affidavit or declaration that, for specified reasons, it cannot present facts essential to justify its opposition, the court may defer or deny the motion, allow time for discovery, or issue another order. The 2010 Note says the party may also seek an order deferring the time to respond. (Former Rule 56(f) was carried forward without substantial change.)
- Second Circuit content requirements: the affidavit explains (1) what facts are sought and how they are to be obtained, (2) how they are reasonably expected to create a genuine issue of material fact, (3) what effort the affiant made to obtain them, and (4) why the effort was unsuccessful. Hudson River Sloop Clearwater, Inc. v. Dep't of Navy, 891 F.2d 414, 422 (2d Cir. 1989).
- A request in the memorandum of law is not a substitute; failing to file the affidavit is itself sufficient ground to reject the claim that discovery was inadequate. Paddington Partners v. Bouchard, 34 F.3d 1132, 1137 (2d Cir. 1994).
- Other circuits use similar elements (for example, Family Home & Fin. Ctr., Inc. v. Fed. Home Loan Mortg. Corp., 525 F.3d 822 (9th Cir. 2008)); confirm the forum circuit's formulation `[VERIFY]`.
- Celotex ties the "pointing out" route to "adequate time for discovery." 477 U.S. at 322. An early motion is not untimely under Rule 56(b); prematurity is raised through Rule 56(d), not by calling the motion late.
- Pair the declaration with an opposition on the merits using the evidence already in hand, so the court can deny on either ground.

## 7. Credibility, weighing and inferences

- Credibility determinations, weighing the evidence and drawing legitimate inferences are jury functions; the nonmovant's evidence is believed and justifiable inferences are drawn in its favor. Anderson, 477 U.S. at 255.
- The court reviews the whole record but must disregard evidence favorable to the movant that the jury is not required to believe, crediting only evidence supporting the movant that is uncontradicted and unimpeached and comes from disinterested witnesses. Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Prods., Inc., 530 U.S. 133, 150-51 (2000).
- A court that weighs evidence and draws inferences against the nonmovant's competent evidence errs. Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650, 656-60 (2014) (per curiam).
- Narrow exception: where one side's account is blatantly contradicted by the record (there, a videotape) so that no reasonable jury could believe it, the court need not adopt it. Scott v. Harris, 550 U.S. 372, 380 (2007). Do not invoke Scott for ordinary conflicts in testimony.
- Drafting rule for the movant: do not argue that the opponent's witness is not believable, that your witnesses outnumber theirs, or that their evidence is weaker. Argue that, even crediting it, the element fails as a matter of law.

## 8. Sham affidavits and declarations

- A party cannot create a genuine issue simply by contradicting its own prior sworn statement without explaining the contradiction or attempting to resolve the disparity. Cleveland v. Policy Mgmt. Sys. Corp., 526 U.S. 795, 806 (1999) (describing the lower courts' "virtual unanimity").
- Ninth Circuit limits: the court must make a factual determination that the contradiction is a sham, and the inconsistency must be clear and unambiguous; a declaration may elaborate, explain or clarify, and minor inconsistencies from honest discrepancy, mistake or newly discovered evidence do not justify striking it. Van Asdale v. Int'l Game Tech., 577 F.3d 989, 998-99 (9th Cir. 2009) (discussing Kennedy v. Allied Mut. Ins. Co., 952 F.2d 262 (9th Cir. 1991)).
- Other circuits' formulations `[VERIFY]` before relying on them.
- For the movant: identify the prior testimony by page:line, quote it, and show the later declaration flatly contradicts it with no explanation.
- For the nonmovant: a later declaration that changes testimony must acknowledge the earlier testimony, explain the change truthfully (refreshed recollection from a document, a misunderstood question, newly found evidence) and attach whatever corroborates it. A declaration that hides the contradiction is not drafted (SKILL.md Ground Rule 9).

## 9. Partial summary judgment, Rule 56(g) and Rule 56(f)

- Rule 56(a) allows a motion on part of a claim or defense; the notice must identify each one.
- Rule 56(g): if the court does not grant all the relief requested, it may enter an order stating any material fact, including an item of damages, that is not genuinely in dispute and treating it as established in the case.
- 2010 Committee Note: the court must take care that 56(g) does not interfere with a party's ability to accept a fact for purposes of the motion only. A nonmovant that concedes a fact for the motion should say "for purposes of this motion only" in its response.
- Rule 56(f): after notice and a reasonable time to respond, the court may grant summary judgment for a nonmovant, on grounds not raised, or on its own. Cross-motions should be briefed with that possibility in mind.

## 10. Timing

- Rule 56(b) default: any time until 30 days after the close of all discovery, unless a different time is set by local rule or the court orders otherwise. The default is overridden in most cases: Rule 16(b)(3)(A) requires the scheduling order to limit the time to file motions, and Rule 16(b)(4) allows modification only for good cause and with the judge's consent.
- Read, in this order: the scheduling order and any amendment, the judge's individual practices (pre-motion letters, page or word limits, courtesy copies), the local rules, then Rule 56(b).
- Response and reply deadlines are set by local rule or order, each from its own trigger; see the clocks table in `references/state-and-local-practice.md`.

## 11. Preservation after denial

- A party may not appeal an order denying summary judgment after a full trial on the merits on a sufficiency question; it must be renewed through Rule 50. Ortiz v. Jordan, 562 U.S. 180 (2011).
- A purely legal issue resolved at summary judgment need not be renewed in a Rule 50 motion to be preserved. Dupree v. Younger, 598 U.S. 729 (2023).
- Flag both in the hand-off when the brief raises legal and factual grounds together.
