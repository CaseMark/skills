## 1. Order of reading

1. The scheduling order and every amendment to it (Rule 16(b)(3)(A) requires a motion deadline; Rule 16(b)(4) allows changes only for good cause with the judge's consent).
2. The assigned judge's individual practices: pre-motion letters or conferences, page or word limits, whether a statement of facts is required or barred, courtesy copies, hearing practice.
3. The local rules in force on the filing date (check the court's rules page for the effective date).
4. The rule or statute: Rule 56, or the state equivalent.
Where two of these conflict, the more specific and later order usually governs; name the conflict in the reconciliation table and ask counsel, rather than choosing silently.

## 3. S.D.N.Y. and E.D.N.Y. Local Civil Rule 56.1 (Joint Local Rules effective January 2, 2026)

- (a) Movant files a separate, short and concise statement in numbered paragraphs of the material facts it contends are not in genuine dispute. Failure to file one may be grounds for denial. Not required in APA or FOIA cases.
- (b) The opposition includes a correspondingly numbered paragraph admitting or denying, and otherwise responding to, each numbered paragraph, plus, if necessary, additional paragraphs of material facts the opponent contends are genuinely disputed.
- (c) Each numbered paragraph is deemed admitted for purposes of the motion unless specifically denied and controverted by a correspondingly numbered paragraph.
- (d) Every statement, including each denial, must be followed by citation to evidence that would be admissible, as Rule 56(c) requires.
- (e) Where all parties have counsel, the movant provides an electronic word-processing copy, and the counterstatement reproduces each movant entry with the response directly beneath it.
- The 2024 Committee Note says each counterstatement paragraph must admit or deny the truth of the corresponding paragraph.
- A deemed admission does not win the motion by itself, and an assertion the record does not support is disregarded (Holtz; Vermont Teddy Bear; see `references/rule-56-standards.md`).
- A July 1, 2026 notice proposed amending 56.1 to bar incorporating the statement by reference into briefs; whether it has been adopted is `[VERIFY]`. Do not incorporate the statement by reference in the meantime; put the argument in the brief.

Response vocabulary for each paragraph: "Admitted."; "Admitted for purposes of this motion only."; "Denied." followed by the controverting evidence by pinpoint; "Admitted in part and denied in part" with the split stated; "Denied: the cited material does not support the assertion" quoting the cited page and line (Rule 56(c)(1)(B)); an admissibility objection under Rule 56(c)(2) stated in the same paragraph. Never "Disputed" alone, never argument without evidence, never a blanket denial.

## 4. California: CCP 437c (as amended by Stats. 2024, ch. 99, AB 2049, eff. Jan. 1, 2025) and Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1350

Papers and format:
- Separate statement in support: all material facts claimed undisputed, each followed by a reference to the supporting evidence; failure to comply may in the court's discretion be ground for denial. CCP 437c(b)(1).
- Separate statement in opposition: respond to each fact, saying whether the opposing party agrees or disagrees that it is undisputed, and set out any other disputed material facts with evidence; failure to comply may be ground, in the court's discretion, for granting the motion. CCP 437c(b)(3).
- Two-column format; the response must unequivocally state "disputed" or "undisputed"; a dispute states its nature and cites the evidence by exhibit, title, page and line. Rule 3.1350(f), (h).
- For summary adjudication, each cause of action, affirmative defense, damages claim or issue of duty must be stated specifically in the notice and repeated verbatim in the separate statement. Rule 3.1350(b), (d).
- The reply may not add new evidence, new material facts or a new separate statement. CCP 437c(b)(4).
- Evidentiary objections not made at the hearing are deemed waived. CCP 437c(b)(5), (d). Objections the court does not rule on are preserved for appeal. CCP 437c(q).

Burdens:
- A plaintiff meets its burden by proving each element; a defendant by showing one or more elements cannot be established or a complete defense; then the burden shifts and the opponent must set out specific facts, not pleadings. CCP 437c(p)(1), (2).
- Aguilar v. Atlantic Richfield Co., 25 Cal. 4th 826, 854-55 (2001): California follows the federal framework in most respects but still requires a moving defendant to present evidence, not simply point out that the plaintiff lacks evidence; factually devoid discovery responses are one way to do it.
- Credibility: summary judgment is not denied on credibility grounds, with two discretionary exceptions (sole witness to a fact; state of mind established only by the person's own affirmation). CCP 437c(e).
- Inferences contradicted by other inferences or evidence that raise a triable issue cannot support summary judgment. CCP 437c(c).

Scope:
- Summary adjudication is limited to a cause of action, an affirmative defense, a claim for punitive damages under Civil Code 3294, or an issue of duty, and is granted only if it completely disposes of one. CCP 437c(f)(1).
- A legal issue or non-punitive damages claim that does not completely dispose of one of those may be adjudicated only under CCP 437c(t): before filing, a joint stipulation of the parties whose claims or defenses are at issue plus a declaration from each that the motion will further judicial economy, and the court's permission to file.
- One summary judgment motion per adverse party without leave on good cause. CCP 437c(a)(4), (5).

Continuance: if opposition declarations show facts essential to justify opposition may exist but cannot for stated reasons be presented, the court shall deny the motion, continue it for discovery, or make another just order; the request may be made ex parte on or before the opposition due date. CCP 437c(h).

## 5. Texas: Rule 166a (Misc. Docket No. 26-9012, final approval Feb. 27, 2026; effective March 1, 2026)

- Applies only to a motion filed on or after March 1, 2026; an older motion follows the prior rule, whose hearing-based deadlines are `[VERIFY]` from the pre-2026 text. The 2026 comment says that, other than the deadline changes, the rewrite is not intended to change the law substantively.
- Title must be "Traditional Motion for Summary Judgment," "No-Evidence Motion for Summary Judgment," or "Combined Motion for Traditional and No-Evidence Summary Judgment" (a wrong title is not grounds for denial); any oral-hearing request goes in the title.
- A traditional motion states specific grounds; a no-evidence motion states the elements as to which there is no evidence and may be filed only after adequate time for discovery. The 1997 comment: no conclusory or general no-evidence challenges.
- Response (166a(d)): within 21 days after the motion is filed; the nonmovant must produce any evidence supporting it. A nonmovant needing more time files an affidavit or declaration specifying the reasons (166a(d)(3)). Reply (166a(e)): within 7 days after the response; no new or independent grounds.
- Hearing or submission (166a(g)): not within 35 days after filing; set within 60 days (or 90 days as stated). Written ruling within 90 days after the hearing or submission (166a(i)).
- Evidence (166a(j)): documents referred to in an affidavit or declaration must be attached and sworn or certified; defects in form are not grounds for reversal unless pointed out by objection and the party refused to amend (166a(j)(4)).

## 6. New York supreme court: CPLR 3212

- CPLR 3212(a): a party may move after issue is joined; the court may set a cutoff no earlier than 30 days after the note of issue; if it sets none, the motion must be made no later than 120 days after the note of issue, except with leave on good cause shown.
- Brill v. City of New York, 2 N.Y.3d 648, 652 (2004): good cause for a late motion requires a satisfactory explanation for the delay; merit alone does not excuse it.
- Whether a statement of material facts is required (Uniform Rule 202.8-g and the individual justice's part rules) is `[VERIFY]` on the drafting date.

## 7. N.D. Cal. Civil Local Rules (read on the court's site 2026-10-30)

- Civil L.R. 56-2(a): unless required by the assigned Judge, no separate or joint statement of undisputed facts shall be submitted. Put the facts, each with a pinpoint, in the brief; check the judge's standing order for an exception.
- Civil L.R. 56-3: statements in an order denying summary judgment are not issues deemed established for trial unless the Court so specifies.
- Civil L.R. 7-2(a): motions are noticed for hearing not less than 35 days after filing. Civil L.R. 7-3(a) and (c): opposition not more than 14 days after the motion was filed, reply not more than 7 days after the opposition was due; evidentiary objections go inside the brief; Rule 6(d) does not extend these filing-based deadlines. A briefing order overrides these defaults.
