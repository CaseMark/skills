# Summary of Data and Guidance for the Investigator

The closing section of the Investigator's Brochure, which turns the data into what an investigator needs to do. Source status is in `primary-sources.md`.

## Purpose

The section gives an overall discussion of the nonclinical and clinical data, summarizing information from different sources so the investigator has the most informative interpretation and an assessment of what it implies for clinical trials. Where relevant, discuss published reports on related products, which helps investigators anticipate adverse reactions.

Its aim is a clear understanding of the possible risks and adverse reactions, and of the specific tests, observations and precautions that may be needed, based on the available physical, chemical, pharmaceutical, pharmacological, toxicological and clinical information.

## Contents

1. **Overall data discussion.** Integrate nonclinical and clinical findings; say what the data do and do not predict.
2. **Identified and potential risks.** Each risk with its basis (observed in humans, anticipated from nonclinical findings, class effect), consistent with the reference safety list.
3. **Safety margins.** Table below.
4. **Tests, observations and precautions.** Linked to target organs and risks.
5. **Recognition and treatment of adverse reactions and overdose.** Based on previous human and nonclinical experience and the pharmacology of the product.
6. **Related products.** Published reports and class information relevant to anticipated problems.

## Safety Margins Table

Take values from the Module 2.4 exposure-multiple table when it exists, or from the toxicokinetic data in the study reports. Use the same basis on both sides.

| Species, sex | Study | Dose at NOAEL (mg/kg/day) | Exposure at NOAEL (AUC, Cmax; day, analyte, matrix) | Human exposure at first and maximum dose | Multiple at first / maximum | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Human exposure comes from the sponsor; otherwise `[SPONSOR INPUT REQUIRED]` and the multiples stay blank.
- For programs without a NOAEL (for example, advanced cancer under ICH S9), tabulate the dose measure the studies report (such as STD10 or HNSTD) and say what it is.
- Where a mg/kg comparison is the only one available, label it as the fallback.

## Monitoring and Precautions

For each target organ or risk: the test or observation, the reason (the finding it follows), and a pointer to the protocol section that sets the schedule. Do not invent alert limits or thresholds. Numeric thresholds for laboratory values, intervals for electrocardiograms or hold criteria are sponsor decisions: write `[SPONSOR INPUT REQUIRED]` and point to the protocol.

Examples of how a nonclinical finding becomes guidance, to be filled from the data:

- Hepatic findings in a toxicity study at exposures near the expected human range: liver chemistry monitoring and a precaution for baseline hepatic impairment.
- A cardiovascular finding in a safety pharmacology or telemetry study: electrocardiogram monitoring and a precaution for known prolongation risk.
- Renal findings: renal biomarker monitoring.
- Protein products: monitoring for immune-mediated reactions and for anti-product antibodies where nonclinical studies showed them.

Each example is a template, not a recommendation; include one only if the study data support it.

## Overdose and Adverse Reaction Management

State what is known: signs expected from nonclinical dose escalation or human experience, symptomatic and supportive measures, and any antidote or specific treatment. If nothing is known, say so rather than inventing a treatment.

## What Belongs in the Protocol

| Content | Where |
|---|---|
| Risks, tests, observations, precautions, recognition and treatment guidance | IB, this section |
| Stopping rules, dose-modification algorithms, dose-escalation criteria | Protocol; the IB points to the section |
| Eligibility and exclusion criteria | Protocol; the IB may state which findings motivate them |
| Alert limits and monitoring schedules | Protocol |

If the sponsor wants protocol-type content in the IB, include only text identical to the protocol and log it so the two cannot drift apart.
