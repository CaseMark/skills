# M&A Disclosure Schedules

Prepares seller disclosure schedules that qualify the representations and warranties in a definitive acquisition agreement, and reviews a counterparty's schedules or schedule supplements for the buyer. The schedules exist to allocate known risk accurately: each known exception is stated specifically enough that both sides know what the buyer is accepting and what remains indemnifiable. The deliverable is a readiness determination, ranked blocking issues, a rep-to-schedule map, the draft schedules or a review markup, a reconciliation table and hand-offs. Everything it produces is a draft for review by a licensed attorney.

## Why This Skill Exists

Disclosure schedules are where the reps actually get their meaning, and they are usually assembled last, by several people, from a data room and a prior draft. The common failures are mechanical: a schedule keyed to last week's section numbers, a contract listed without the change-of-control right that makes it an exception, a general reference to the data room that the buyer struck, a litigation entry that says the company "likely violated" a statute, or a supplement after signing that the seller assumes cures a breach. Courts read the agreement and the schedules, not the diligence calls: Delaware has held that what a buyer heard on a call or could have found in the data room does not qualify a rep the schedule did not cover. This skill catches those problems before the schedules are delivered.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |

| Newest agreement draft: rep article, section numbers, article and section lead-ins | Building the rep-to-schedule map and numbering every schedule | Schedules keyed to an earlier draft after a section was inserted, so litigation disclosures sit under the new environmental rep; a rep with no "except as set forth" lead-in under an article with no lead-in, so no schedule can qualify it; list reps treated as exception reps |
| Agreement definitions: Company, Subsidiaries, Material Contract, thresholds, lookback dates, Knowledge (party, authority and scope) | Deciding which entities, contracts and events are in the population to list | A contract signed by the seller's parent listed as a Company contract; monthly or quarterly fees compared with an annual threshold; items before the lookback date listed while items after it are missed; Knowledge persons other than those defined |
| Cross-reference clause ("reasonably apparent on its face") and the schedule's general terms | Deciding whether one disclosure qualifies other reps | A litigation entry relied on to qualify the accounting-controls rep when its words never mention accounting; preamble terms that try to change the agreement |
| Executed contracts, government notices, audit letters, demand letters | The substance of each exception | Contract named by title only, without the consent, termination or default that makes it an exception; one audit listed and two others omitted; amendments and dates missing |
| Data room index, diligence Q&A, management calls | Finding exceptions; never a substitute for disclosure | "All matters in the Data Room are deemed disclosed" or "see folder 7.3" in place of a description; reliance on what was said on a call |
| Litigation files, counsel memos, reserve analyses | Describing pending and threatened matters | "Likely violated", exposure estimates, intent to settle, or counsel's advice written into the schedule as an admission and a possible privilege waiver |
| Rep qualifiers: knowledge, materiality, MAE, materiality scrape | Deciding what must be scheduled | "To Seller's Knowledge" added in the schedule against a flat rep; immaterial items omitted although a scrape will apply for indemnity |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is delivered, signed or filed by this skill. Every output is a draft for attorney review, and no schedule is called final.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call a document missing until the matter inventory has been checked and the file opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (for example, whether a customer's notice was in writing), *source conflict* (two option counts), *unverified external lookup* (a docket status not confirmed), or *policy election* (a choice for counsel or client, such as scheduling a below-threshold item).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** The newest agreement draft governs numbering, defined terms and what can be qualified. Executed documents (contracts, pleadings, agency notices, the stock ledger) govern the facts in each disclosure, over the data room index, the diligence report, trackers, and client or banker summaries. For law: the agreement text, then the governing state's controlling cases, then commentary. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table with the value used; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every date, amount, party name and docket or contract identifier in a schedule carries a source locator or is marked `[UNVERIFIED]`. Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or is marked `[VERIFY]`. Never invent a case, holding or pin cite.
5. **The agreement controls what can be qualified.** A schedule qualifies only a rep that refers to it, directly or through an article-level lead-in. If a needed exception has nowhere to go, say so and route the agreement change; never draft a schedule that pretends to qualify an unqualified rep.
6. **Specific, factual disclosure.** Each item identifies the document or matter and states the fact that makes it an exception, with dates and amounts. A title alone, a data room reference, or a general incorporation clause is not disclosure.
7. **Express cross-references.** Every disclosure that must qualify more than one rep is cross-referenced in each schedule or repeated there. A "reasonably apparent" clause is a safety net, not the method.
8. **Qualifiers live in the agreement.** Never put in a schedule a knowledge or materiality qualifier the rep does not have, and never use a schedule to strip one the rep has. If the seller needs one, propose it in the agreement.
9. **Facts, not admissions.** No legal conclusions, liability characterizations, exposure estimates, settlement intentions or counsel's advice in a schedule; describe what was alleged, by whom, when, and the status.
10. **Updates are classified, never assumed.** For each supplement, answer separately whether it counts for the closing condition, whether it cures for indemnity, and whether it triggers a termination right or deadline, each from the agreement text and each with its own trigger date. Never call a supplement curative unless the agreement says so.
11. **Accurate allocation, not concealment.** The schedules exist so the buyer knows what it is accepting. Never draft a disclosure that misstates a known fact, or that describes a known matter so as to hide its substance while appearing to disclose it; offer an accurate disclosure instead.
12. **A flag is not a deliverable.** Every conflict and gap ends in a recommendation, the alternatives, an owner and a next action.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; the side represented (seller drafting, or buyer review); deal structure; governing law.
2. The newest agreement draft, with its number and date, and any earlier draft the existing schedules were built on.
3. The schedule's general terms (or the seller's preferred form) and the agreement's cross-reference clause.
4. Existing schedule drafts, the data room index, and the diligence request list and responses.
5. Source documents per rep: contracts, litigation and agency files, cap table and stock ledger, real property, IP, benefit plans, insurance, tax returns and audits, permits.
6. Qualifier provisions: Knowledge definition and persons, materiality scrape, sandbagging clause, supplement covenant, closing conditions, indemnity basket and cap.
7. For a public target: the SEC reports carve-out text and its date window.
8. For updates: the supplement or notice, its delivery date and method, and the planned closing date.

**Defaults if not supplied** (an agreement provision on the same point overrides each default): seller-side drafting; disclosures qualify the numbered section plus express cross-references only; standard general terms from `references/output-template.md` without data room incorporation; below-threshold items not scheduled unless the client elects to (policy election); supplements treated as not curing anything until the agreement text is read.

**Hard stops (label the determination "Declined: hard stop"; produce the request or alternative named, not the requested schedules):**
- No draft of the agreement's representations article is available: produce the intake request and a blank rep-to-schedule map, not numbered schedules.
- A request to draft a disclosure that misstates a known fact, or that names a known matter while hiding the fact that makes it an exception: decline that text and offer an accurate disclosure.

**Not hard stops:** missing source documents for a schedule (draft with `[UNVERIFIED]` placeholders and a Critical open item); a rep that cannot be qualified without an agreement change (Critical item and hand-off); an unknown governing law for sandbagging (`[VERIFY]` and analysis under both candidate laws).

## Step 1: Map the Agreement

Read `references/schedule-drafting-rules.md`, section 1. Build the rep-to-schedule map from the newest draft: section number, subject, rep type (list, exception, or both), lead-in at section and article level, qualifiers, entity scope, schedule number and the prior number where it moved. Compute every threshold from the definition, annualizing periodic fees and showing the arithmetic. Flag every rep the client needs to qualify that has no lead-in.

## Step 2: Fix the General Terms

Read `references/schedule-drafting-rules.md`, section 2. Draft or review the general terms against the agreement. Remove data room incorporation unless the agreement provides for it. Make the cross-reference statement consistent with the agreement's clause. Report any preamble term that would change the agreement as a conflict for merger-agreement.

## Step 3: Build the Population for Each Schedule

For each list rep and exception rep, assemble the items from source documents within the defined entities, thresholds and lookback. Compare against the data room index, the diligence report and the existing schedule draft; each item found in one and not another goes in the reconciliation table. Do not call the population complete while a folder or file is unreviewed.

## Step 4: Draft Each Disclosure

Read `references/schedule-drafting-rules.md`, sections 3, 5 and 6. Write each item to the description standard: document or matter, parties, date, amendments, the exception itself, status, amounts, defined terms. Keep qualifiers out of the schedule. Rewrite any admission, estimate or advice as facts. Note any third-party confidentiality restriction and personal data.

## Step 5: Cross-References and Consistency

Read `references/schedule-drafting-rules.md`, sections 4 and 8. For every disclosure, ask which other reps it bears on and add an express cross-reference in each. Reconcile capitalization, contracts, litigation and terminology across the schedules, the cap table, the data room index and the diligence report.

## Step 6: Public Company Items (If Applicable)

Read `references/schedule-drafting-rules.md`, section 7. Apply the SEC reports carve-out exactly as written (window, exclusions, reps it does not reach). Schedule specifically anything that must qualify a rep and is outside the carve-out. Prepare the list of omitted schedules for any filed exhibit and flag material information the filing must disclose.

## Step 7: Supplements, Buyer Knowledge and Closing

Read `references/updates-and-sandbagging.md`. For each supplement or notice, fill the three-question classification with the agreement section for each answer and its own trigger date. Identify the governing law's buyer-knowledge default and any sandbagging clause. On the buyer side, recommend a written reservation of rights, an amendment, a special indemnity or an escrow where the default makes closing a waiver, and show the indemnity arithmetic in the fixed format.

## Step 8: Assemble the Deliverable and Hand-Offs

Lay out the header and Parts 1 to 6 per `references/output-template.md`. Rank blocking issues, fill the reconciliation table and open items, and complete each hand-off with the facts the receiving skill needs. List every `[VERIFY]` item with what would settle it, drawing on `references/primary-sources.md`.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Is the agreement draft used for numbering the one that will be signed, and who will tell the schedule team if it changes again?
2. Does the client accept each policy election (below-threshold items, cross-reference approach, scheduling for a materiality scrape)?
3. Are the agreement changes identified in Part 2 (lead-ins, qualifiers, supplement rules) being raised in the agreement negotiation?
4. Has the litigation and employment counsel reviewed every litigation and employment disclosure for admissions and privilege?
5. For a supplement: has counsel decided whether to reserve rights, seek an amendment or close as is, before the closing date?

If the user does not answer, recommend confirming the agreement draft number and the lead-in fixes first; schedules keyed to the wrong draft or attached to unqualifiable reps protect no one.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. Every schedule number matches the newest agreement draft, and the map shows every renumbered schedule.
2. Every rep the client needs to qualify has a section or article lead-in, or the gap is a Critical item routed to merger-agreement.
3. Every threshold and lookback is applied as defined, with periodic amounts annualized and the arithmetic shown.
4. Every listed contract is one to which a defined Company entity is a party; other-entity contracts are flagged separately.
5. Every disclosure identifies the document or matter and states the fact that makes it an exception, with dates and amounts sourced.
6. No general data room incorporation and no disclosure by folder reference alone.
7. Every multi-rep disclosure carries express cross-references; no reliance on the "reasonably apparent" clause alone.
8. No schedule adds a knowledge or materiality qualifier the rep lacks or strips one the rep has.
9. No legal conclusion, exposure estimate, settlement intention or counsel's advice appears in any disclosure.
10. Every supplement is classified separately for closing condition, indemnity and termination, each with its agreement section and trigger date; none is called curative without agreement text.
11. Buyer-knowledge analysis names the governing law and any sandbagging clause, and recommends a reservation of rights where the default requires one.
12. Every figure and identifier has a source locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`; every legal statement has a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`.
13. The readiness label is one of the four in `references/output-template.md`, and the output closes with the draft-for-attorney-review line.

## Guidelines

- Over-disclosure by description is cheap; over-disclosure by general reference fails. When in doubt whether an item is an exception, describe it specifically and let the no-admission clause carry the risk.
- Keep the schedules factual and dull. The buyer, its lender, its insurer and possibly a court will read them.
- Draft the schedules against the agreement, not the other way round; when the agreement is wrong, fix the agreement.
- Freeze numbering only after the reps article is final, and re-run the map on every new draft.
- On the buyer side, read the schedules against the diligence findings before signing; a disclosure accepted at signing is an exception the buyer bought.
- Treat the disclosure schedules as potentially public for a public target and as producible in any later dispute.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule numbers do not match the agreement | Schedules built on an earlier draft | Rebuild the map from the newest draft; show old and new numbers; confirm the draft that will be signed |
| A needed exception has no rep lead-in | Rep drafted flat; article has no lead-in | Critical item; propose lead-in text to merger-agreement; do not draft a schedule that claims to qualify it |
| Contract is a scanned image or missing pages | Poor scan or partial upload | Try OCR; list the provisions that could not be read and the reps that depend on them; request a clean copy |
| Data room index and contract files disagree | Stale index or misfiled document | Use the executed document; record both in the reconciliation table |
| Cap table and stock ledger differ | Unrecorded transfer or option exercise | Use the ledger and board approvals; Critical item for the capitalization rep |
| Client insists on "all data room materials are disclosed" | Practice habit or buyer's prior acceptance | Check the agreement; if it does not provide for it, explain the risk (Dura Medic) and draft specific disclosures |
| Litigation counsel's memo is the only source for a matter | No pleading or demand on file | Describe only the facts the memo reports from documents; request the underlying documents; keep the memo's analysis out |
| Supplement arrives days before closing | Late-discovered matter | Classify it under Step 7 at once; give counsel the closing-condition, indemnity and termination answers and the reservation-of-rights recommendation |
| Governing law unclear | Choice-of-law clause missing or contested | Analyze buyer knowledge under each candidate law; mark `[VERIFY]` |
