## 1. What a schedule supplement can do

The agreement decides. Read the update or notice covenant, the closing conditions, the termination article and the indemnity article together, and classify each supplement on three separate questions. Never answer one from another.

| Question | Where the answer is | Common formulations and effect |
|---|---|---|
| Does the supplement count for the bring-down closing condition? | Closing conditions article | "Supplements shall be disregarded for purposes of Section 7.2(a)": buyer may refuse to close if the updated fact makes the rep untrue to the stated standard. Silence: the condition is tested against the reps as made, so the updated fact still counts against the seller |
| Does it cure the breach for indemnity? | Indemnity article and the update covenant | "No supplement shall cure any breach for purposes of Article VIII": indemnity preserved. "If Buyer closes, supplements are deemed accepted": indemnity lost for the supplemented matter. Silence: governing-law default applies (section 2) |
| Does it trigger a termination right or deadline? | Termination article and update covenant | Some agreements give the buyer a window (for example, five business days after a material supplement) to terminate, after which the supplement is deemed accepted; count that window from its own trigger (delivery of the supplement), not from signing or closing |

Each timed element runs from its own trigger. Write it as: element / trigger event / evidence of the trigger / period / resulting date and weekday. A supplement delivery date is not a closing date, and a notice of a claim is not the date the claim arose.

Never describe a supplement as curing a breach unless the agreement says so in terms. When the agreement is silent, say it is silent and present the governing-law default as the answer, with the risk of the contrary reading.

## 2. Buyer knowledge and sandbagging by governing law

A pro-sandbagging clause lets the buyer recover for a breach it knew about; an anti-sandbagging clause bars recovery for breaches the buyer knew about before closing (the knowledge standard and whose knowledge counts are drafting points). When the agreement is silent:

| Governing law | Default on silence | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| New York | Reliance on the truth of an express warranty is not required; the buyer purchased the promise (Ziff-Davis). But where the buyer closes with full knowledge of facts disclosed by the seller that would breach the warranty, the buyer has waived the breach unless it expressly preserves its rights (Galli). If the buyer learned of the falsity from a third party or common knowledge rather than from the seller, it may still recover (Rogath) | CBS Inc. v. Ziff-Davis, 75 N.Y.2d 496 (1990); Galli v. Metz, 973 F.2d 145, 151 (2d Cir. 1992); Rogath v. Siebenmann, 129 F.3d 261 (2d Cir. 1997) |
| Delaware | Chancery decisions treat Delaware as pro-sandbagging: breach of a contractual rep does not require justifiable reliance, and the seller's disclosure outside the schedule does not change the rep (Cobalt, Akorn, Dura Medic). The Supreme Court has reserved the question in dicta (Eagle Force n.185) | Dura Medic (Del. Ch. Feb. 20, 2025), quoting Cobalt (Del. Ch. 2007); Eagle Force, 187 A.3d 1209, 1236 n.185 (Del. 2018) |
| Any other state | Not researched | `[VERIFY]` for the governing state before advising |

Practical rules:

- A seller's supplement is the classic Galli fact pattern under New York law: the seller is the source of the buyer's knowledge. A buyer that wants to close and claim later must reserve rights in writing before closing, or get an amendment.
- Under Delaware law on silence, the buyer's risk is lower, but the Supreme Court has not settled the point; a reservation of rights costs little and is still recommended.
- Fraud claims and their limits (anti-reliance clauses, exclusive-remedy provisions) are separate from the sandbagging question; route to merger-agreement.

## 3. Reservation of rights before closing

Elements of a reservation letter or closing-certificate rider:

1. The supplement or notice identified by date and schedule number.
2. A statement that the buyer does not accept the supplement as curing any breach or as amending the Disclosure Schedule.
3. A statement that by closing the buyer does not waive any right under the agreement, including indemnification, for the matters disclosed.
4. Delivery before closing, by the notice method in the agreement, with evidence of delivery.

The seller may refuse to close on those terms; that negotiation is a judgment call for counsel. Alternatives: a special indemnity for the matter (first-dollar, outside the basket), a price adjustment, an escrow holdback, or an amendment stating that supplements do not cure.

## 4. Representations and warranties insurance

- Policies commonly exclude breaches of which the deal team had actual knowledge before signing or closing, and new matters arising between signing and closing (interim breaches). The policy text controls; read the definitions of knowledge, deal team members and interim breach.
- A supplement that discloses a new matter usually puts it outside coverage. Where the indemnity is limited to a retention or excludes covered losses, the buyer may be left with no recovery unless the agreement or a special indemnity covers it.
- Do not state what a policy covers without the policy; record it as an unknown fact.

## 6. Interim covenant exceptions

Exceptions to interim operating covenants are often moved into the disclosure letter. In Williams v. Energy Transfer (Del. Ch. 2021), the court found the agreement ambiguous and, weighing extrinsic evidence, read exceptions that had been moved from the covenants to the disclosure letter at the last minute as applying to the specific covenant under which they were listed; the cross-reference clause did not extend an equity-issuance exception to other covenants the issuance would otherwise breach. Draft each covenant exception under the covenant it excuses, cross-reference expressly where it must excuse more than one, and state the dollar or volume limit.
