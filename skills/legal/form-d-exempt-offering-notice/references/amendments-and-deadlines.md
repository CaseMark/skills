# Date of First Sale, Deadlines and Amendments

## Date of first sale

The Form D instructions define it: the date on which the first investor is irrevocably contractually committed to invest, which, depending on the terms and conditions of the contract, could be the date on which the issuer receives the investor's subscription agreement or check. Some SEC web pages shorten this to the first clause; the form instruction and the staff interpretation that repeats it are the controlling text. Quote the full sentence in the memo.

### How to decide

Do this per issuer and per offering. Read the earliest executed subscription for each vehicle and answer three questions.

1. When did the investor sign and deliver the subscription?
2. Could the investor revoke or withdraw after delivering it, and until when?
3. Does the contract make the investor's commitment depend on a later act of the issuer (acceptance, countersignature, a condition precedent) before the investor is bound?

Then apply:

- Irrevocable on delivery: the date the issuer received the signed subscription can be the date of first sale.
- Revocable until the issuer accepts: the date the issuer accepted. Use the acceptance evidence (countersignature date, acceptance notice) from the executed subscription documents, not the date in a closing memo.
- Conditions to the investor's obligation (for example a minimum raise or a conditions-precedent list): decide whether a condition outside the investor's control means the investor is bound before the condition is met. Name the clause and the reading.
- Silent or ambiguous: use the earlier defensible date for the deadline, record the other candidate date, and list it as a judgment call.
- Escrowed offerings with a minimum: a staff interpretation says the notice is due within 15 days after the first subscription is received into escrow `[VERIFY]`.

Do not use: the scheduled or actual closing date unless it is also the date of irrevocable commitment; the date capital is called, funded or wired; the date in an administrator ledger or closing memo unless it matches the executed document. For funds, capital is called after the commitment, so payment is never the test. A mandatory capital commitment call is made under the original offering and does not require a new Form D.

A weekend or holiday first-sale date is valid. Do not move it.

### Early filing

A notice may be filed at any time once the issuer has determined to make the offering, using First Sale Yet to Occur.

## Deadline computation

Rule 503(a)(1) requires a notice of sales on Form D for each new offering no later than 15 calendar days after the first sale of securities in the offering, unless the end of that period falls on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday, in which case the due date is the first business day following. By its terms that rule covers offerings under Rule 504 or Rule 506; the Form D instructions apply the same period to Section 4(a)(5) offerings. The form instructions also apply the weekend and holiday roll to amendment due dates.

Procedure, shown in full every time:

1. Date of first sale (day 0), with weekday.
2. Raw day 15: day 0 plus 15 calendar days, with weekday.
3. If raw day 15 is a Saturday, Sunday or federal holiday, move forward to the next day that is not one of those; otherwise it is the due date.
4. Report: "Date of first sale: <date> (<weekday>); raw day 15: <date> (<weekday>); due date: <date> (<weekday>)", and say whether it rolled and why.

Notes:

- The first-sale date's weekday does not matter; only the end of the period can roll.
- Rule 503 does not define "holiday". Treat federal legal holidays as holidays and state the assumption `[VERIFY]`. Check the calendar for the year, including holidays that are observed on an adjacent weekday.
- The electronic system accepts filings until 10 p.m. Eastern on business days, but a submission after 5:30 p.m. Eastern is deemed filed on the next business day, and Form D is not among the submission types dated same-day after 5:30 p.m. `[VERIFY]` Recommend filing before 5:30 p.m. Eastern on the due date.
- Compare the due date with today's date. If it has passed, follow the late-filing escalation in the workflow.

## When a separate notice is required

A new notice is required for each new offering. Treat each issuer that sold in the offering as covered by a notice, either its own or as a co-issuer on a shared filing. A vehicle formed later with its own subscription documents has its own offering and its own date of first sale unless it is a co-issuer in an offering already noticed. Whether a later closing, a successor fund, or an alternative investment vehicle is part of the original offering or a new offering is a judgment call `[VERIFY]`: state the facts and reasoning, and err toward the earlier deadline. For parallel funds and feeders that share one offering memorandum, weigh: whether one document offers all vehicles under a shared cap and common terms; whether the sponsor, general partner and plan of financing are the same; whether the vehicles sell at overlapping times; and the staff position that issuers selling in the same Regulation D offering may be identified on one Form D (a master fund and its feeders are the staff's example). `[VERIFY]` the integration analysis under the current Securities Act rules. Present separate notices, co-issuer filing, and adding the new vehicles to the existing offering by amendment as options, with a recommendation and the deadline consequence of each.

## Amendments: Rule 503(a)(3)

An amendment is required:

1. To correct a material mistake of fact or error in the previously filed notice, as soon as practicable after discovery.
2. To reflect a change in the information provided in the previously filed notice, as soon as practicable after the change, except as listed below.
3. Annually, on or before the first anniversary of the filing of the notice or of the most recent amendment, if the offering is continuing.

An issuer may file an amendment at any time. Every amendment must give current information in response to all items, whatever the reason for filing. The annual anniversary resets with each filed amendment, so report both the current annual date and the date it would reset to if the change-driven amendment is filed first. The change-driven amendment is due on its own timeline; do not hold it for the annual date.

An offering is continuing while the issuer is engaged in an ongoing effort to offer and sell, whether or not it has sold anything.

### Exceptions: no amendment is required for a change that occurs after the offering terminates, or a change that occurs solely in the following information

The rule text, to be applied item by item:

- (A) The address or relationship to the issuer of a related person identified in Item 3.
- (B) An issuer's revenues or aggregate net asset value.
- (C) The minimum investment amount, if the change is an increase, or if the change, together with all other changes in that amount since the previously filed notice, does not result in a decrease of more than 10%.
- (D) Any address or state(s) of solicitation shown in response to Item 12.
- (E) The total offering amount, if the change is a decrease, or if the change, together with all other changes in that amount since the previously filed notice, does not result in an increase of more than 10%.
- (F) The amount of securities sold in the offering or the amount remaining to be sold.
- (G) The number of non-accredited investors who have invested, as long as the change does not increase the number to more than 35.
- (H) The total number of investors who have invested.
- (I) The amount of sales commissions, finders' fees or use of proceeds for payments to executive officers, directors or promoters, if the change is a decrease, or if the change, together with all other changes in that amount since the previously filed notice, does not result in an increase of more than 10%.

### Applying the exceptions

- Start from the previously filed notice. The 10 percent tests are cumulative against that baseline: the sum of all changes since the last filed notice or amendment, measured against the figure on that filing. Compute the percentage and show it.
- "Solely" matters. If a change touches an excepted item and a non-excepted item, an amendment is required.
- A change in the total offering amount that is a decrease never requires an amendment; an increase requires one only if the cumulative increase exceeds 10 percent. A change in the minimum investment that is an increase never requires one; a decrease requires one only if the cumulative decrease exceeds 10 percent. Do not reverse these directions.
- Adding a new related person is not excepted; only an address or relationship change for a person already listed is. Adding a new sales compensation recipient is not excepted; only an address or states-of-solicitation change is.
- A change of the exemption relied on (for example, 506(c) to 506(b), or a change of 3(c) paragraph) and a change of entity type are changes in information and require an amendment.
- When the original notice said First Sale Yet to Occur, a later sale does not by itself require an amendment, but any amendment must state the actual date.
- Amendments under the change trigger or the error trigger are due "as soon as practicable". Give a target date and the reason; do not invent a fixed number of days.

For a multi-issuer filing, apply the tests to the information in the filing as a whole and to each issuer's own entries.

## Late filing

Filing Form D is not a condition of the Rule 504 or 506 exemption, and a late Form D does not by itself cause the offering to lose covered-security status. Rule 507 denies the exemption to an issuer, or its predecessors or affiliates, that has been enjoined by a court for failure to comply with Rule 503, subject to a good-cause waiver by the Commission. States may impose notice filing requirements and consequences for late notices. The SEC's published position is that an issuer that missed the 15-day deadline should make a good-faith effort to file as soon as practicable. Treat any late filing as a compliance failure to be escalated, not a footnote, and send the state-notice question to `blue-sky-filings`.
