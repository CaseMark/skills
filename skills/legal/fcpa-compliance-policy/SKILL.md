# FCPA and Anti-Corruption Compliance Policy

Prepares an anti-corruption policy, or a review of an existing one, that a company can implement: a scope map of which laws reach which entities, the policy text, a program gap analysis against the DOJ Evaluation of Corporate Compliance Programs and the UK adequate-procedures principles, blocking issues, a reconciliation table, the clocks that matter, and hand-offs. Its aim is a genuine program that prevents and detects bribery and records every payment accurately; it declines to draft a policy designed to look compliant while enabling improper payments. Every output is a draft for review by qualified counsel and is never adopted on the skill's say-so.

## Why This Skill Exists

Anti-corruption policies fail in predictable places. A U.S.-centric template permits facilitation payments for a group that also carries on business in the UK, where no such exception exists. A dollar threshold is treated as a safe harbor, so a $140 dinner during a tender is "allowed" while the purpose test is never asked. Physicians at public hospitals and engineers at a national oil company are left out of "foreign official". Intermediaries are tiered by spend, so a success-fee agent with no retainer is rated low risk. A bribe passes through the ledger as "reinspection fees" or "market research", which is an issuer's books-and-records violation even if the anti-bribery case is never brought. And since February 2025, some companies have read the enforcement pause as a change in the law; it is not, and the limitation periods outlast any review period. Each error costs the company its compliance credit under the DOJ Corporate Enforcement Policy and its defence under section 7 of the UK Bribery Act.

## Where Mistakes Hide

| Input / Document | What It Is Used For | Where the Error Hides |
|---|---|---|
| Entity chart, SEC filings, UK offices and sales, employee nationality and residence | Scope map: issuer, domestic concern, 78dd-3 person, UK relevant commercial organisation | Private company told the FCPA accounting provisions apply to it; issuer's foreign subsidiaries left out of books-and-records duties; UK business presence missed, so the UK Act's s.7 is never applied; 50-percent-or-less affiliates treated like controlled subsidiaries |
| Facilitation-payment instruction and customs, permit and inspection practices | The facilitation-payment election | FCPA exception carried into a group that carries on business in the UK (no exception, MoJ para. 45); payments that change an outcome (passing a failed inspection, reducing duties) called "facilitating" (Kay; Scoular); payments allowed but not recorded at their true nature |
| Enforcement-posture inputs: EO 14209, DOJ June 9, 2025 guidelines, March 2026 CEP, client's reading of them | Dated enforcement note and program priorities | Pause treated as repeal; controls cut on the strength of a policy memo that creates no rights; 5-year (18 U.S.C. 3282) and 6-year (3301) limitation periods and conspiracy reach ignored; cartel-linked border conduct, a stated DOJ priority, treated as low risk |
| Gifts, travel and hospitality thresholds and invitations | Approval triggers and the purpose test | Thresholds read as safe harbors; no business-purpose or pending-decision test; travel with side trips, spouses or cash per diems placed under the 78dd-1(c)(2) defense; recipient-employer and local caps ignored |
| Customer and counterparty lists (state-owned enterprises, public hospitals, universities, sovereign funds) | Who is a "foreign official" | SOE and public-hospital staff excluded; "any government stake" or "under 50 percent is safe" used instead of the ownership, control, status and function analysis (Resource Guide; Esquenazi factors); FEPA's wider definition (senior foreign political figures) missed |
| Third-party inventory, agent contracts and compensation terms | Risk tiering, diligence, contract clauses, payment controls | Tiering by annual spend, so success-fee agents rate low; official-recommended agent, offshore payment account and vague deliverables not escalated; the "knowing" standard (awareness of high probability) not explained; no refresh |
| General ledger extracts, expense reports, vendor master and invoices | Books-and-records and internal-controls design | Bribes coded as consulting, commissions, "logistics surcharges", or reinspection fees; no materiality threshold applied; payment-splitting under approval limits; personal messaging channels outside retention |
| Hiring, internship and referral requests | Employment of officials' relatives as a thing of value | Relative of an official on a pending decision hired or given an internship at the official's request; referral handled by HR alone; benefits to family members not treated as indirect benefits to the official (Resource Guide, family members) |
| Charitable donation and sponsorship requests | Donations and sponsorships linked to officials | Donation requested by an official with business pending, or to a charity an official heads or controls, treated as good citizenship; no diligence on the recipient or confirmation of use (Resource Guide, Charitable Contributions) |
| Local-law memos and country practice | Country annexes and conflicts | "Customary" practice used as the written-law defense; stricter local gift caps ignored; data-protection and labor-law conflicts resolved by dropping the control |
| Acquisition and joint-venture files | Successor liability and integration | Pre-closing conduct assumed to vanish at closing; post-closing continuation not stopped; JM 9-28.900 180-day and 1-year windows counted from signing instead of closing |
| The request itself: who asks, for whom, and why | Whether the skill drafts at all | A policy built to look compliant while enabling payments (hidden exceptions, two versions, coded descriptions); a request to backdate approvals or records for a past payment |

## Ground Rules

1. **Draft only.** Nothing is adopted, published or sent to a regulator by this skill. Every output is a draft for counsel's review and is never called final.
2. **Source-review gate.** Never call an input missing until the supplied files have been checked and opened, with OCR or extraction tried. Classify every open item as one of four gap classes: *unknown fact* (whether any entity carries on business in the UK), *source conflict* (the agent contract says fixed fee, the ledger shows a commission), *unverified external lookup* (a local gift cap not in `references/primary-sources.md`), or *policy election* (facilitation-payment election, threshold amounts).
3. **Hierarchy of authority.** For law: statute (FCPA, FEPA, UK Bribery Act, local written law), then binding court decisions, then agency guidance (Resource Guide, ECCP, MoJ s.9 guidance, SFO guidance), then enforcement policy statements and press releases, then commentary. Enforcement policy never displaces the statute. For company facts: executed contracts, filings and ledger entries govern over summaries, slide decks and the requester's description. Name every conflict in the reconciliation table and say which source controls; never resolve one silently.
4. **Citation discipline.** Every legal statement carries a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`; every company fact carries a source locator or `[UNVERIFIED]`. Re-read the current statute and the current DOJ policy pages before the policy is adopted. Never invent a case, statute, guidance paragraph or enforcement action.
5. **Enforcement posture is not the law.** State EO 14209, the June 2025 guidelines and the March 2026 CEP with the date checked, and say that the statute, its penalties and its limitation periods are unchanged. Never remove or weaken a control because of current enforcement priorities.
6. **Most restrictive applicable law governs the group rule.** Where the UK Bribery Act or local law reaches any part of the group, facilitation payments are prohibited for that part, and the recommended rule is group-wide prohibition with a duress and safety exception. Never draft the FCPA exception for persons the UK Act or local law reaches.
7. **Purpose before thresholds.** Every gift, meal, trip or other benefit to a foreign official meets the purpose test in `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 3, at any value. Thresholds decide who approves; they never make an expense permissible.
8. **Classify counterparties on the factors.** Apply the ownership, control, status and function analysis the Resource Guide tells companies to use (with the Esquenazi factors, binding only in the Eleventh Circuit) to state-owned and public entities and record the basis. Until classified, use the label "Treat as foreign official pending classification".
9. **Scope fidelity.** Apply 78m(b) to issuers, with 78m(b)(6) for affiliates at 50 percent or less, and say plainly when a private company is not subject to it. Apply UK s.7 only where an entity is a relevant commercial organisation, and say what fact would make it one.
10. **A genuine program, never a paper one.** The policy exists to prevent and detect bribery and to record every payment truthfully. Decline any request for a policy designed to look compliant while enabling or concealing improper payments, and any request to create or alter a record to cover a payment; offer the compliant policy instead.
11. **Transactions are worked examples, not clearances.** When the user describes a specific payment, agent or trip, analyze it under the draft policy, flag red flags, recommend holding any payment with an unresolved red flag, and route past conduct to counsel (legal-research). Never state that past conduct is beyond enforcement.

## Checkpoint A: Pre-Draft Intake (Mandatory)

Collect, from documents where possible:

1. Today's date; the requester and role; the deliverable (new policy, revision, review of an existing policy, gap memo).
2. Every group entity: where organized, principal place of business, whether any is an issuer (registered securities or 78o(d) reporting), ownership percentages for affiliates and joint ventures.
3. UK nexus: UK-incorporated entities, UK offices, UK customers or staff, UK nationals or residents among employees.
4. Countries of operation and government touchpoints: sales to government and state-owned customers, licenses, customs, tax, inspections.
5. Counterparty list for classification, with ownership and control facts where known.
6. Third-party inventory: role, country, compensation structure, who introduced them, payment accounts.
7. Existing policies, risk assessment, audit and hotline findings, prior incidents.
8. Gifts, travel and hospitality practice and any thresholds the client wants.
9. The client's facilitation-payment instruction.
10. Governance: compliance owner, reporting line to the board or audit committee, resources.
11. Pending or recent acquisitions and joint ventures, with signing and closing dates.
12. Local-law constraints known to the client.

**Defaults if not supplied** (a statute or local written law overrides any default it addresses): issuer status unknown, so the accounting-provisions section is drafted in both the issuer and private-company versions and the item is Critical; facilitation payments prohibited with a duress and safety exception (the UK Act and many local laws override any permission); state-owned and public-entity staff treated as foreign officials pending classification; approval triggers from `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 3, with any lower local or recipient-employer cap governing; third parties tiered by risk factors; formal policy tone.

**Hard stops (label the status "Declined: hard stop"; produce the declined output in `references/output-template.md`, not the requested text):**
- A request for a policy, provision, version or annex designed to look compliant while permitting or concealing payments the requester describes as improper or wants hidden from the board, auditors, regulators or employees (for example a "customary payments" exception approved without Compliance and withheld from the board version, or coded ledger descriptions for payments to officials).
- A request to create, backdate or alter a policy version, approval, contract, invoice description or accounting entry to cover a specific past or pending payment.
- A request for instructions on how to route, split, describe or time a specific payment to an official or intermediary so that it escapes review or accurate recording.

**Not hard stops:** a good-faith request to permit payments the requester believes are lawful facilitating payments, even one that matches a paper-program indicator such as business approval without Compliance, provided nothing is described as improper or hidden (draft the compliant election, say which indicator the request matched, and explain why); issuer status or UK nexus unknown (Critical item; draft both versions); a described past payment that may have been improper (analyze under the policy, mark Critical, hand off to legal-research for counsel); local law unknown (`[VERIFY]` and country annex placeholder).

## Step 1: Scope Map

Read `references/anti-corruption-law.md`, sections 1 and 9. Fill Part 2 of `references/output-template.md` for every entity and person class: status, statutes, whether 78m(b) applies, and the facts relied on. If any entity carries on business in the UK, record it now; it fixes the facilitation-payment election in Step 4.

## Step 2: Enforcement Posture and Clocks

Read `references/anti-corruption-law.md`, sections 7 and 10. Write the dated enforcement note for Part 2. If the user relies on the pause to cut controls or to treat past conduct as closed, say what the order and guidelines do and do not do, and compute each limitation date from its own trigger in the Part 6 clock table. Route the past-conduct question to legal-research.

## Step 3: Risk Assessment and Counterparty Classification

Read `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 1, and `references/anti-corruption-law.md`, section 3. Record government touchpoints by country and business line. Classify each state-owned or public counterparty with one label from Part 1 and the factors relied on.

## Step 4: Core Prohibitions and Facilitation-Payment Election

Read `references/anti-corruption-law.md`, sections 2, 4 and 8, and `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 2. Draft the prohibitions, definitions, the knowing standard, the duty to report demands (FEPA), and the election with its label. Test every payment practice the client describes against "routine governmental action"; any payment that changes an outcome is a bribe, not a facilitating payment.

## Step 5: Gifts, Travel, Hospitality, Donations and Hiring

Read `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 3. Put the purpose test first, then the approval triggers, then travel rules tied to 78dd-1(c)(2). Review any proposed trip or event the user describes against those rules and list what must change.

## Step 6: Third Parties

Read `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 4. Tier by risk factors, list red flags with stop-and-escalate steps, draft contract clauses and payment controls, and set the refresh cycle. Apply the tiers to any intermediary the user names.

## Step 7: Books, Records and Internal Controls

Read `references/anti-corruption-law.md`, section 5, and `references/program-design-rules.md`, section 5. Draft the issuer version, the private-company version, or both under the Checkpoint A default. Compare ledger descriptions supplied with contracts and invoices and record each mismatch in the reconciliation table.

## Step 8: M&A, Joint Ventures and Local Law

Read `references/program-design-rules.md`, sections 6 and 7. Draft acquisition diligence and integration steps with clocks from the closing date, joint-venture terms, and country annexes for local-law conflicts.

## Step 9: Program Elements and Assembly

Read `references/program-design-rules.md`, sections 8 and 9, and assemble the deliverable in the order of `references/output-template.md`. Map each program element to the three ECCP questions and the six MoJ principles in Part 4. Run every provision against the paper-program indicators; remove any that match. Assign the policy status, list blocking issues by severity, the reconciliation table, clocks, hand-offs and every `[VERIFY]` item.

## Checkpoint B: Post-Draft Alignment

Confirm with the user or state as open items:

1. Is the scope map right: issuer status, UK business presence, and every entity covered?
2. Does counsel accept the facilitation-payment election and its basis?
3. Are the counterparty classifications and approval triggers right for the business?
4. Are the third-party tiers workable for the commercial teams, with Compliance approval where required?
5. Has any described past payment been routed to counsel?

If the user does not answer, recommend confirming issuer status and UK nexus first; they decide the accounting-provisions section and the facilitation-payment election.

## Quality Audit

Before responding, confirm each statement. If one fails, fix the draft.

1. The scope map states each entity's status and the statutes applied, and no private company is told 78m(b) applies to it.
2. The facilitation-payment election uses an exact label, and no person or entity the UK Act or local law reaches is permitted to make facilitation payments.
3. No payment that changes an official outcome is described as facilitating.
4. The enforcement note is dated, says the statute and limitation periods are unchanged, and no control is weakened because of enforcement priorities.
5. Every limitation or policy clock has its own trigger, date with weekday, period and source.
6. The gifts, travel and hospitality section states the purpose test before any threshold and says thresholds are approval triggers.
7. Travel rules exclude side trips, family members and cash per diems, and tie to 78dd-1(c)(2).
8. State-owned and public-entity counterparties carry a classification label with the factors or the missing facts.
9. Third parties are tiered by risk factors, and success-fee and official-recommended intermediaries are High.
10. The books-and-records section prohibits generic or false descriptions and states there is no materiality threshold.
11. No provision matches a paper-program indicator, and no hard-stop request was drafted.
12. Every legal statement has a citation from `references/primary-sources.md` or `[VERIFY]`.
13. The output closes with the draft-for-counsel-review line.

## Guidelines

- Write the policy for the employee in the field: short rules, concrete examples, who to call. Put legal analysis in the gap memo, not the policy.
- Be firm where the law is firm (no bribes, accurate records, no facilitation payments where the UK Act applies) and give options where the company has a real choice (threshold amounts, tier triggers).
- Commercial bribery and bribe receipt belong in the policy even though the FCPA reaches only officials; the UK Act s.1 and s.2, local law and fraud statutes reach them.
- When the user's instruction conflicts with the statute (allow "expedite fees" for failed inspections), draft the compliant provision and explain the change in the gap memo.
- Never promise employees indemnity for individual criminal fines under the anti-bribery provisions.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer status unclear | Foreign parent with ADRs, debt-only reporting, or recent deregistration | Check SEC filings; draft both versions; mark Critical |
| UK nexus disputed | Sales agents in the UK but no office | Record the facts; apply MoJ para. 36; draft the group prohibition as the recommendation |
| Ledger extract unreadable or in a foreign language | Scanned export or local chart of accounts | Try OCR; request the account mapping; list affected payments as open items |
| Agent contract and ledger disagree on compensation | Side letter or amended terms | Record both in the reconciliation table; treat the agent as High tier until resolved |
| Counterparty ownership unknown | Opaque state holding structures | Label "Treat as foreign official pending classification"; list the facts needed |
| Local counsel memo relies on custom | No written-law citation | Do not apply the written-law defense; request the written provision |
| Existing policy has multiple versions in circulation | Regional adaptations | Reconcile against the board-approved version; list each divergence |
