# Program Design Rules

How each policy section is built and reviewed. Legal rules come from `references/anti-corruption-law.md`; this file turns them into policy text, controls and review tests. Dollar figures here are company elections, not legal limits.

## 1. Risk assessment and proportionality

The policy is built on the company's own risk assessment (ECCP, Risk Assessment; MoJ Principles 1 and 3). Record for each business line and country:

- government touchpoints: sales to government or state-owned customers, licenses and permits, customs and ports, tax audits, inspections, visas, concessions;
- counterparties classified under the instrumentality test, with the basis (section 3 of the law file);
- intermediaries used and how they are paid;
- gifts, travel, hospitality, donations and sponsorships spend, by recipient type;
- prior incidents, audit findings, hotline reports, and lessons from peers;
- emerging risks (new markets, acquisitions, new technology and messaging channels).

A public country-risk index may inform tiering, but it is a company input, not a legal standard. A low-risk country does not lower the diligence owed to a high-risk counterparty.

## 2. Facilitation-payment election

| Facts | Election to draft | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Any group entity incorporated in the UK or carrying on business in the UK, or any UK national or UK resident employee | **Facilitation payments: prohibited** for the whole group, with a duress and safety exception | No UK exception; s.7 reaches associated persons anywhere; a split rule is unworkable in shared functions (MoJ para. 45) |
| Local law of an operating country prohibits them | **Facilitation payments: prohibited** at least in that country | The FCPA exception is not a defense to local law |
| U.S.-only exposure, client insists on the FCPA exception | **Facilitation payments: permitted only within the FCPA exception** | Only if: payment is to secure a non-discretionary routine governmental action the company is entitled to; pre-approval by Compliance; recorded at actual amount with recipient, purpose and action; never for avoiding a duty, inspection result, license decision or penalty; reviewed for local-law legality first |
| Payment demanded under threat to health, safety or liberty | Make it, then report within 24 hours to Compliance, record accurately as a duress payment | Duress is not corrupt intent (Resource Guide; MoJ para. 48); economic pressure is not duress |

State the election with one of the labels in bold and the facts that support it. Never describe a payment that changes an outcome (passing a failed inspection, waiving a penalty, reducing a duty, skipping a required document) as facilitating.

## 3. Gifts, travel, hospitality, donations, hiring

**Purpose test first.** Every gift, meal, entertainment, travel or other benefit to a foreign official or to any person connected with a pending decision must meet all of these, regardless of value:

1. a legitimate business purpose stated in writing (product demonstration, contract performance, relationship courtesy);
2. not offered to influence a pending or anticipated decision; heightened scrutiny during tenders, licensing, audits and disputes;
3. permitted by local law and by the recipient's employer rules;
4. reasonable in value and frequency, given locally, with no cash or cash equivalents;
5. given openly, to the official's organization where possible, and recorded accurately.

**Thresholds are approval triggers.** A dollar threshold decides who must approve, not whether the expense is allowed. The policy says so in terms. Recipient-employer or local-law caps that are lower override the company threshold.

Default triggers if the client gives none (company elections; adjust to risk):

| Item | Without pre-approval | Pre-approval by Compliance |
|---|---|---|
| Promotional items of nominal value | Allowed if the purpose test is met | Not needed |
| Business meal with a foreign official | Up to the company's stated per-person amount, at most 2 per official per year | Above that amount, or more often |
| Gift to a foreign official | Not allowed | Always |
| Any travel or lodging for a foreign official | Not allowed | Always, in writing |
| Any benefit during an open tender or decision involving the recipient | Not allowed | Always, with Legal |

**Travel and hospitality (78dd-1(c)(2)).** To fit the affirmative defense the expense must be reasonable and bona fide and directly related to promoting, demonstrating or explaining products or to performing a contract. Policy rules: the host organization selects the attendees; the itinerary is business-driven; class of travel and hotels follow the company's own employee policy; pay vendors directly, never cash per diems; no side trips, leisure stops or extra nights; no spouses, family or guests at company expense; written invitation to the agency, not the individual.

**Charitable contributions and sponsorships.** Due diligence on the recipient and its ties to officials (an official as founder, director or patron is a red flag); no contribution requested by an official in connection with business; paid to the organization, never an individual; post-payment confirmation of use. A bona fide charity does not cure a donation made to influence an official (Resource Guide, Charitable Contributions).

**Political contributions.** No contributions to foreign political parties, officials or candidates on behalf of the company.

**Hiring and internships.** Candidates referred by, or related to, officials go through standard hiring with Compliance review; no hiring as a favor connected with pending business. A job or internship for an official's relative is a thing of value to the official when given to influence (Resource Guide, family members); record who referred the candidate and any pending decision the official touches.

## 4. Third-party intermediaries

**Tier by risk factors, not by spend.** Spend-based tiering misses intermediaries paid by success fee, whose spend at onboarding is zero.

| Tier | Triggers (any one is enough) | Minimum diligence | Approver |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | Interacts with officials or state-owned customers for the company; success fee or commission; recommended or requested by an official; official or relative among owners or staff; customs, licensing, tax or permit work; high-risk country | Questionnaire; beneficial-ownership verification; sanctions, adverse-media and litigation screening; reference checks; qualifications and capacity; compensation benchmark; interview; anti-corruption certification and training | Compliance, with Legal for any unresolved red flag |
| Elevated | Occasional official contact; distributor or reseller to public customers; joint venture partner | Questionnaire; ownership; screening; references; compensation review | Compliance |
| Standard | No government interaction; fixed fee; low-risk services | Registration check; screening; contract clauses | Business owner, Compliance notified |

**Red flags (stop and escalate before engagement or payment):** payment to an account in a third country or in another name; requests for cash or round sums; success fees out of line with services; vague or generic invoices ("services rendered", "market research" with no deliverable); official recommended the intermediary; refusal to certify or to disclose owners; shell or newly formed entity; lack of qualifications or staff; requests for unusual secrecy; late requests for extra fees near a decision.

**Contract clauses:** description of services and deliverables; compensation tied to documented work; anti-corruption representations and covenants; no subcontracting without consent; books-and-records and audit rights; annual certification; training; cooperation with investigations; payment only to the contracting party in its home country; termination and fee forfeiture for breach.

**Payment controls:** three-way match of contract, invoice and evidence of work before payment; no payment on red flags; periodic transaction testing; diligence refresh at least every two years for High and on any change of ownership or scope.

## 5. Books, records and internal controls

For issuers, the policy covers every transaction, not only those involving officials:

- record every payment at its true amount, payee, date and purpose, in reasonable detail;
- prohibited: off-books accounts, false or generic descriptions, splitting payments to stay under limits, coding a payment to an account that hides its nature, backdating, petty cash without receipts;
- general-ledger accounts for gifts, hospitality, donations and intermediary fees, with recipient-level detail;
- approvals by authority matrix; segregation of duties; analytics flagging round sums, payments to new vendors, offshore accounts, duplicate invoices, and expenses near tender dates;
- consolidation: subsidiaries' books feed the issuer's books, so the policy binds them; for affiliates at 50 percent or less, document good-faith efforts under 78m(b)(6);
- retention: business communications, including approved messaging apps, are preserved under the retention policy (CEP remediation element).

For a private company, keep these controls as company policy and say that the FCPA accounting provisions do not apply to it directly.

## 6. Mergers, acquisitions and joint ventures

- Pre-signing: risk-based anti-corruption diligence on the target (government customers, intermediaries, prior investigations, books and controls); price, representations, indemnity and closing conditions for findings.
- Successor liability: the acquirer generally assumes the target's liabilities; acquisition does not create FCPA liability retroactively for a target that was never subject to it, but continuing the conduct after closing creates the acquirer's own liability (Resource Guide).
- Post-closing: apply the policy, training, and third-party review to the target promptly; stop any improper payments immediately; audit high-risk accounts.
- Clocks from the closing date: under Justice Manual 9-28.900, a presumption of declination generally needs disclosure within 180 days and remediation within one year, plus disgorgement. The decision whether to disclose belongs to counsel; the policy only requires prompt escalation.
- Joint ventures: diligence on partners; anti-corruption clauses; audit rights; for minority interests, a record of good-faith influence on controls.

## 7. Local-law conflicts

| Conflict | Policy approach |
|---|---|
| Local law stricter than the policy | Apply local law; add a country annex |
| Data protection limits on hotline topics, anonymity or data transfer | Country annex with permitted channels and transfer mechanism; route to local counsel |
| Works council or employee representative consultation | Consult before rollout; record the date |
| Labor-law limits on discipline or investigation interviews | Country procedure; never drop the prohibition |
| Local "custom" or tolerance of payments | Not a defense; only written law counts |

## 8. Training, reporting, investigation, discipline, testing

- Training: risk-based by role (sales, procurement, logistics and customs, finance, country managers, intermediaries); at hire, annually and on role change; completion and comprehension tracked.
- Reporting: multiple channels, anonymous where lawful; non-retaliation; employees report demands and solicitations by officials (FEPA evidence) and any red flag.
- Investigation: intake, triage by Compliance, independence from the business, privilege under counsel, documented closure, root-cause analysis; escalation to the board or audit committee for significant matters.
- Discipline and incentives: consistent discipline regardless of seniority, including for failure of supervision; compliance in compensation decisions; clawback where available.
- Testing: periodic audits of high-risk accounts and intermediaries; data analytics; program review at least annually and after any incident; board reporting.
- Map each element to the three ECCP questions and to the six MoJ principles in the program gap analysis.

## 9. Paper-program indicators

These features make a policy look compliant while enabling improper payments. The skill does not draft them (Ground Rule 10 and the hard stops in SKILL.md):

- an exception for "customary", "local practice" or "facilitation" payments approved by the business without Compliance, or not disclosed to the board, auditors or employees;
- two versions of a policy, or an annex withheld from reviewers;
- coded or generic accounting descriptions for payments to officials or intermediaries;
- approval routing that lets the person who benefits approve the payment;
- instructions not to put requests or approvals in writing, or to use unapproved channels;
- thresholds or tiering engineered so that known high-risk payments fall below review;
- backdated approvals, policies or invoices.
