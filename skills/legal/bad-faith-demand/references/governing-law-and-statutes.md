# Governing Law, Demand Statutes and Deadline Rules

Rules for classifying the claim, choosing the governing standard, testing a demand against a time-limited demand statute, and computing the response deadline. Every statement here was checked on 2026-10-30 as recorded in `references/primary-sources.md`; anything marked `[VERIFY]` was not confirmed in official text. Re-read the current statute in full before any demand goes out: these statutes were rewritten in 2017, 2022 and 2024 and may change again.

## 1. Classify the claim first

| Question | Third-party failure-to-settle | First-party bad faith |
|---|---|---|
| Who is the claimant to the insurer? | A stranger to the policy suing the insured (tort claimant) | The insured or a covered person (UM/UIM, PIP, property, disability, health) |
| What does the demand protect? | The insured against an excess judgment | The insured's own right to benefits owed under the policy |
| Whose duty is breached? | Insurer's duty to the insured to accept a reasonable offer within limits | Insurer's duty to pay or handle the insured's own claim fairly |
| Excess-exposure framing | Central | Usually inapplicable: no third-party judgment threatens the insured |
| Typical pre-suit notice | Statutory time-limited demand rules (where a statute exists) | Statutory notice before a bad faith or unfair practices suit (Florida CRN, Washington IFCA, Texas Chapter 541) |

Hard rules:
- A UM/UIM claim is first-party even though the facts are a car crash with a third-party tortfeasor. Do not write "excess verdict" or Stowers language to the insured's own UM/UIM carrier.
- A claimant generally has no direct contractual relationship with the tortfeasor's insurer; the failure-to-settle claim belongs to the insured and is usually pursued after an excess judgment by the insured or by assignment `[VERIFY: assignment and direct-action rules by state]`.
- Name the classification in the memo and the reason. If the matter has both (claimant's third-party claim plus the client's own UIM claim), produce two separate instruments.

## 2. Common-law failure-to-settle standards (third-party)

| Jurisdiction | Authority | Rule as checked |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | G.A. Stowers Furniture Co. v. American Indemnity Co., 15 S.W.2d 544 (Tex. Comm'n App. 1929, holding approved) | An insurer that controls settlement is held to ordinary care in deciding whether to settle; refusing an offer an ordinarily prudent insurer would accept can make it liable for the excess judgment |
| Texas | American Physicians Ins. Exch. v. Garcia, 876 S.W.2d 842, 849 (Tex. 1994) | Stowers duty is triggered only when (1) the claim is within the scope of coverage, (2) the demand is within policy limits, and (3) the terms are such that an ordinarily prudent insurer would accept, considering the likelihood and degree of the insured's exposure to an excess judgment. The demand must propose to release the insured fully in exchange for a stated sum within limits |
| Texas | Texas Farmers Ins. Co. v. Soriano, 881 S.W.2d 312 (Tex. 1994) | With multiple claimants, an insurer may enter a reasonable settlement with one claimant even though it reduces the limits available to others |
| Georgia | Southern General Ins. Co. v. Holt, 262 Ga. 267, 416 S.E.2d 274 (1992) | An insurer that negligently, fraudulently or in bad faith fails to settle within limits may be liable for the excess; demands that set up this exposure are called "Holt demands" (holding as described in commentary) |
| Georgia | First Acceptance Ins. Co. of Ga. v. Hughes, 305 Ga. 489 (2019) | Duty to settle arises when the injured party presents a valid offer to settle within limits; correspondence that is not a clear time-limited demand does not trigger it |
| California | Comunale v. Traders & General Ins. Co., 50 Cal. 2d 654 (1958); Crisci v. Security Ins. Co., 66 Cal. 2d 425 (1967) | Implied covenant; liability for failure to accept a reasonable settlement within limits |
| Florida | Boston Old Colony Ins. Co. v. Gutierrez, 386 So. 2d 783 (Fla. 1980); Harvey v. GEICO Gen. Ins. Co., 259 So. 3d 1 (Fla. 2018) | Insurer's good-faith duties to the insured judged on the totality of the circumstances; negligence is relevant but is not the standard |

## 4. Time-limited demand statutes: content checklists

Use the checklist for the state whose law governs the claim. A demand that misses a listed term is drafted as non-compliant and flagged; it is never described as a valid statutory demand.

### Georgia: O.C.G.A. section 9-11-67.1 (as amended by Ga. L. 2024, SB 83)

Scope: offers to settle tort claims for personal injury, bodily injury or death arising from a motor vehicle collision; not product liability claims. The 2024 version applies to offers made on or after April 22, 2024 `[VERIFY: official sources also give a July 1, 2024 effective date]`. Window: from accrual until the named defendant answers, or all named defendants have answered or defaulted (subsection (b)) `[VERIFY: interaction with Ga. L. 2025, SB 68, which lets a defendant move to dismiss in lieu of answering]`.

- [ ] Offer to enter a bilateral contract (subsection (a)); written acceptance of the material terms in their entirety forms it.
- [ ] Specifically references O.C.G.A. section 9-11-67.1.
- [ ] Sent by certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, return receipt requested.
- [ ] Acceptance period of not less than 30 days from receipt.
- [ ] Amount of the monetary payment.
- [ ] Party or parties to be released.
- [ ] Type of release (full or limited) and, if limited, the claims and parties covered.
- [ ] Claims to be released.
- [ ] Payment date not less than 40 days from receipt of the offer.
- [ ] Sworn statement regarding insurance, a material term the offeror may waive.
- [ ] Medical or other records in the offeror's possession, incurred as a result of the claim, sufficient to let the recipient evaluate it.
- [ ] Address and a fax number or email address for written acceptance.

The statute treats the listed terms as the only material terms `[VERIFY: exact wording and lettering of subsection (b)(1)]`. Safe harbor (subsection (i)): no failure-to-settle action where the recipient timely accepts in writing, provides any required sworn statement, and pays the lesser of the demand or the available bodily injury limits.

### California: Code of Civil Procedure sections 999-999.5 (SB 1155, Stats. 2022, ch. 719)

Scope (section 999.5): claims covered by automobile, motor vehicle, homeowner or commercial premises liability policies; time-limited demands transmitted on or after January 1, 2023. Applies to pre-litigation demands `[VERIFY: section 999 definition, "prior to the filing of the complaint or demand for arbitration"]`.

- [ ] In writing, labeled as a time-limited demand or referencing section 999.1.
- [ ] Time to accept not fewer than 30 days from transmission if sent by email, fax or certified mail; not fewer than 33 days if by mail (section 999.1(a)).
- [ ] Clear and unequivocal offer to settle all claims within policy limits, including satisfaction of all liens (999.1(b)).
- [ ] Offer of a complete release from the claimant for the insurer's insureds from all present and future liability for the occurrence (999.1(c)).
- [ ] Date and location of loss (999.1(d)); claim number if known (999.1(e)).
- [ ] Description of all known injuries (999.1(f)).
- [ ] Reasonable proof, which may include medical records or bills, sufficient to support the claim (999.1(g)).
- [ ] Delivered to the insurer's address designated for time-limited demands as posted by the Department of Insurance, or to the assigned adjuster if known (999.2).

Consequence (999.4): a demand that does not substantially comply is not a reasonable offer within limits in a later suit for extracontractual damages. Section 999.4 does not apply to a claimant not represented by counsel (999.4(b)).

### Missouri: RSMo section 537.058

Scope: time-limited demands to settle any claim for personal injury, bodily injury or wrongful death; not demands issued within 90 days of a jury trial in a filed suit. Effective 2017 `[VERIFY: later amendments]`.

- [ ] In writing, references section 537.058, sent certified mail, return receipt requested, to the tortfeasor's liability insurer.
- [ ] Time to accept not less than 90 days from the insurer's receipt.
- [ ] Amount, or a request for policy limits.
- [ ] Date and location of loss; claim number if known; description of known injuries.
- [ ] Parties to be released and claims to be released.
- [ ] Offer of an unconditional release of the insureds (section 537.060).
- [ ] Remaining material terms in the statutory list `[VERIFY: the statute lists eight; confirm all eight]`.
- [ ] Enclosures: list of treating providers with HIPAA-compliant authorizations; if wage loss is claimed, list of employers with authorizations.

Acceptance is by written acceptance delivered or postmarked within the period. A payment period may be required but not less than ten days after the insurer receives an executed unconditional release. A non-compliant demand is not a reasonable opportunity to settle and is inadmissible in a suit for extracontractual damages.

## 5. Florida statutory bad faith (section 624.155)

- Civil remedy notice (subsection (3)): condition precedent of 60 days' written notice to the Department of Financial Services and the insurer, stating the statutory provision (with its specific language) allegedly violated, the facts, and any individual involved. Under the current text, no action lies if, within 60 days after the insurer receives notice from the department, the damages are paid or the circumstances corrected. Harper v. GEICO Gen. Ins. Co., 272 So. 3d 448 (Fla. 2d DCA 2019), read the earlier text as running the cure period from filing. Count conservatively: treat the cure period as open until 60 days after the later of filing and the insurer's receipt of the department's notice, and record which version of the statute governs the claim date.
- 90-day safe harbor (subsection (4)): no bad faith action on a liability claim if the insurer tenders the lesser of the policy limits or the amount demanded within 90 days after receiving actual notice of a claim accompanied by sufficient evidence to support its amount.
- Claimant and insured conduct (subsection (5)(b)): the insured, the claimant and their representatives have a duty to act in good faith in furnishing information, making demands, setting deadlines and attempting to settle; the duty creates no separate cause of action, but the trier of fact may reasonably reduce damages awarded against the insurer if it was breached. A short window or an unreasonable term is therefore evidence against the claimant, not only against the demand.
- Multiple claimants (subsection (6)): no liability beyond limits where, within 90 days after notice of competing claims exceeding the limits, the insurer files interpleader or makes the limits available through binding arbitration. See `references/liens-and-multiple-claimants.md`.
- Mere negligence alone is insufficient to constitute bad faith (subsection (5)(a)); subsection (5) applies to statutory and common-law bad faith actions.
- Section 627.4137: on written request, the liability insurer must disclose within 30 days, under oath, each known policy (including excess and umbrella), the insurer, each insured, the limits, coverage defenses, and a copy of the policy. Use it to confirm limits before demanding them.

## 6. Deadline computation rules

1. Identify the trigger the governing rule uses: receipt (Georgia, Missouri), transmission with a method-based floor (California), insurer's receipt of the department's notice (Florida CRN; see the Harper caveat in section 5), or none (common law).
2. Use evidence of the trigger: return receipt, carrier tracking, fax confirmation, email delivery record. If receipt is not yet known, compute from the latest plausible receipt date and state the assumption.
3. Set the stated deadline at or beyond the floor, with a buffer of several business days, as a specific date, time and time zone. Do not set it on a weekend or holiday; whether a statute rolls a weekend expiry forward is `[VERIFY]` in each state.
4. Where common law governs, state why the window is reasonable for the volume of records, the number of claimants and any pending insurer requests. A short window is evidence against the demand, and in Florida it bears on the claimant's own good-faith conduct.
5. Show the computation every time:

```
Governing rule:     <statute and subsection, or common law>
Trigger:            <receipt | transmission | DFS notice receipt> on <date> (<weekday>); evidence: <source>
Statutory floor:    <N> days; earliest permissible expiry <date> (<weekday>)
Deadline in letter: <date> (<weekday>) <time> <time zone>; buffer <N> days over floor
```

## 7. Acceptance, clarification and counteroffer

- Acceptance mechanics must be objectively measurable: who accepts, in what form, delivered where, by when. Phone acceptance is not a measurable act and conflicts with the written-acceptance rules above.
- California section 999.3: a request for clarification, additional information or an extension is not, by itself, a counteroffer or rejection; an insurer that does not accept must notify the claimant in writing of the basis of its decision before the deadline expires.
- Georgia section 9-11-67.1 (2024 text): recipients may seek clarification of the terms, the release, liens, subrogation claims, standing to release claims, medical bills, medical records and other relevant facts; a written clarification request is not a counteroffer, but an attempt to modify the material terms is not a clarification.
- Missouri and Georgia: written acceptance of the material terms within the period. Treat any additional condition the claimant tries to add outside the listed material terms as a drafting defect.
- Elsewhere, whether a clarification request is a counteroffer is `[VERIFY]`; respond to every insurer request in writing before the deadline and say whether the deadline is extended.

## 8. Litigation offers and claimant capacity

- A time-limited demand is not a statutory litigation offer. Florida section 768.79 (offer of judgment and demand for judgment) and O.C.G.A. section 9-11-68 (offer of settlement) are fee-shifting devices made in litigation, each with its own form, service and 30-day acceptance rules and fee thresholds (Florida 25 percent; Georgia 75 and 125 percent). Never label a pre-suit demand as one, and never treat a litigation offer as satisfying a time-limited demand statute; if both are wanted, draft them as separate instruments and route the litigation offer to counsel.
- The demand must come from someone who can deliver the release: a personal representative for a death or estate claim, every required wrongful-death beneficiary or the person the statute authorizes, and a guardian or conservator (with any required court approval) for a minor or incapacitated claimant `[VERIFY: state appointment and approval rules]`. If court approval is needed, the claimant obtains it after acceptance; it is not a condition the insurer must satisfy. Georgia expressly lets the recipient seek clarification of standing to release claims.
