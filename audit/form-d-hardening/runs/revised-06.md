**Date of first sale: reasoning.** The form defines the date of first sale as "the date on which the first investor is irrevocably contractually committed to invest, which, depending on the terms and conditions of the contract, could be the date on which the issuer receives the investor's subscription agreement or check" (Form D General Instructions; Item 7 instruction; Securities Act Rules C&DI 257.02). Each executed subscription agreement provides (s. 2, p. 2) that the investor "may withdraw this subscription by written notice at any time prior to the General Partner's acceptance" and that "upon acceptance by the General Partner, evidenced by its countersignature on the signature page, this subscription shall be irrevocable." LPA s. 3.3 (p. 13) matches: admission occurs upon the General Partner's acceptance evidenced by countersignature. The subscription is therefore revocable until countersignature, and the governing event is the acceptance date, not the investor's signature date and not the closing date. The earliest acceptance in the matter is the Meridian subscription, countersigned by Dana Whitcombe on September 16, 2026 (Wednesday) (06a, p. 14). The other four subscriptions were countersigned on September 18, 2026 (Friday) (06b-06e, p. 14). Capital is first called in October 2026 (Closing Memo, p. 1); payment is not the test.

Rejected candidates: (i) September 18, 2026, the Initial Closing Date used by the Closing Memo (09, p. 1) and the Cobalt register "Notes" sheet (08). Those are rank-3 sources; the executed subscription (rank 1) shows an irrevocable commitment two days earlier. Using September 18 would compute a due date of October 5, 2026 (Monday) (raw day 15 October 3, 2026 (Saturday), rolled) and would be two business days late if the September 16 date is right. (ii) September 14, 2026 (Monday), the date Meridian signed and delivered. Under s. 2 the investor could still withdraw, so it is not the date of irrevocable commitment; it would give a raw day 15 of September 29, 2026 (Tuesday). The contract is not ambiguous, so the September 14 date is not adopted as a protective deadline, but it is recorded here and in Section 4.

## 4. Judgment calls

**4.1 Date of first sale (Item 7; no clarification field).** Question: which event made the first investor irrevocably committed? Options: (A) September 16, 2026, the GP's countersignature of the Meridian subscription (s. 2 makes the subscription irrevocable on acceptance evidenced by countersignature; LPA s. 3.3 agrees); (B) September 14, 2026, receipt of Meridian's signed subscription; (C) September 18, 2026, the Initial Closing Date used by the Closing Memo and register. Recommended: (A). Under (B) the raw day 15 is September 29, 2026 (Tuesday) and the filing would already be two days later than that date on October 1; (B) is not the contractual event because the investor could still withdraw. Under (C) the due date is October 5, 2026 (Monday), but (C) rests on a closing label in rank-3 documents and the executed subscription shows commitment two days earlier; using (C) risks a late filing. Reason: the contract is explicit that acceptance by countersignature is the irrevocability event; the earliest countersignature is September 16.

**4.2 Item 11 minimum investment (no clarification field).** The four questions:
1. Stated minimum: $5,000,000 in both the LPA (s. 3.2, p. 12) and the PPM (p. 7); they agree.
2. Waiver authority: the General Partner "may, in its sole discretion, accept a Capital Commitment of a lesser amount" (LPA s. 3.2); PPM p. 7 (waivable in the GP's sole discretion). No condition, class limit or advisory-committee consent; the authority sits in the LPA and can be changed only by amending it.
3. Non-waivable floor: none in the LPA, the PPM, any side letter (none in the matter) or any written policy (none in the matter).
4. Accepted below-minimum subscriptions: one, the Whitcombe Family Trust, $1,000,000 (06e, p. 14), accepted by Priya Raghunathan as Managing Member. The trust's trustee is Dana Whitcombe, a Managing Member of the GP, and the LPA (s. 1.1) defines a family trust of a GP member as a "Larkspur Affiliate". Under the form's outside-investor exclusion (officers, directors, general partners and, per staff interpretation, their immediate family members and controlled entities), the trust is not an outside investor. Its $1,000,000 therefore does not set the Item 11 figure. Every outside investor committed $10,000,000 or more.

Readings the record supports: (i) unrestricted discretion with no floor anywhere, so under Forms C&DI 130.03 "the answer is 0 because there is effectively no minimum" `[VERIFY]` wording; (ii) $5,000,000, if the issuer confirms it applies a policy under which no outside investor below that amount will be accepted (a policy floor counts only on the issuer's confirmation). Recommended value: 0. Alternative: $5,000,000 on written confirmation from the General Partner. Not available: $1,000,000, because the only below-minimum acceptance was not from an outside investor. Confirmation required: ask Dana Whitcombe whether the GP will accept less than $5,000,000 from any outside investor and whether any such acceptance is planned for later closings. Consequence: if $5,000,000 is entered and a lower outside commitment is later accepted, a cumulative decrease over 10 percent requires a Form D/A; 0 avoids that trigger but states publicly that there is no minimum.

**4.3 Related persons beyond the form's examples (Item 3).** Question: list the Investment Manager (Larkspur Capital Management LLC) and its executive officers? Options: (A) list the GP entity and its two Managing Members only (the form's text names general partners and managing members; the GP LLC Agreement s. 4.1 says the Managing Members perform executive-officer and director functions); (B) add the Investment Manager as Promoter and/or Executive Officer under firm practice `[VERIFY]`, with its executive officers. Recommended: (A), unless the firm's established practice is (B). Under (B) each adviser officer becomes an Item 3 entry (names and addresses not yet in the record; Jordan Alvarez, CFO, is the only adviser officer identified), the Item 16 scope expands to management-fee payments, and an omitted adviser officer later added is an amendment trigger. Under either option the adviser and its officers are Rule 506(d) covered persons.
