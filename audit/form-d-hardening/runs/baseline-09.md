### 1.3 Date of first sale and the new-notice computation

The Form D instructions define the date of first sale as "the date on which the first investor is irrevocably contractually committed to invest, which, depending on the terms and conditions of the contract, could be the date on which the issuer receives the investor's subscription agreement or check." (Form D General Instructions p. 7; Item 7 instruction p. 10; Securities Act Rules C&DI 257.02.)

Contract reasoning. The earliest executed subscription is Meridian Teachers' Retirement System (06a), signed June 15, 2026 (Monday) by Carol Nakamura. Subscription Agreement s. 2 (p. 2) provides that the Investor may withdraw the subscription by written notice at any time prior to the General Partner's acceptance; upon acceptance, evidenced by the General Partner's countersignature on the signature page, the subscription becomes irrevocable and the Investor is bound to make the Capital Commitment. LPA s. 3.3 (p. 13) confirms admission occurs on acceptance evidenced by countersignature. The GP countersigned on June 17, 2026 (Wednesday) (06a p. 14, Dana Whitcombe). Meridian was therefore revocable until June 17, 2026 and irrevocably committed on that date. The investor's signature date (June 15, 2026) is not the date of first sale because the investor could still withdraw. The closing memo (doc 09) and register (doc 08) show the same June 17 date, but they are not the basis; the countersigned subscription is. Capital is first called later (LPA s. 3.1), and payment is never the test.

The prior Form D (doc 14, Item 7) reported June 17, 2026 as the date of first sale, which agrees with this analysis.

```
Date of first sale: June 17, 2026 (Wednesday)
Raw day 15:         July 2, 2026 (Thursday)
Due date:           July 2, 2026 (Thursday)   [not rolled]
```

Compared with today, September 24, 2026 (Thursday): the due date passed 84 days ago. Under Path A the July 2, 2026 filing (if confirmed) was timely, on its face, by filing on the due date; the firm has no evidence of the time of day of submission, and a submission after 5:30 p.m. Eastern would have been dated July 3, 2026 (Friday), one day late `[VERIFY]`. Under Path B the new notice is past due.

Alternative computation, not recommended, shown for completeness. If the attorney were to treat the September 18, 2026 second closing as a separate offering (Section 4, judgment call 2), the second closing's own first sale would be the GP's acceptance of the Covewood, Hallam, Sorrel Point and Whitcombe Family Trust subscriptions on September 18, 2026 (Friday) (06b-06e, p. 14 of each):

```
Date of first sale: September 18, 2026 (Friday)
Raw day 15:         October 3, 2026 (Saturday)
Due date:           October 5, 2026 (Monday)   [rolled because raw day 15 is a Saturday]
```

That reading is not recommended: the second closing was made under the same PPM, the same subscription form, and the closing window the offering documents always contemplated. The recommended reading is the earlier deadline, which is the existing offering's.

Holiday assumption: Rule 503 does not define "holiday"; federal legal holidays are treated as holidays `[VERIFY]`. No federal holiday falls on July 2, 2026 or October 5, 2026. Filing time: file before 5:30 p.m. Eastern on the filing date; a submission after that time is deemed filed the next business day, and Form D is not among the submission types dated same-day after 5:30 p.m. `[VERIFY]`.
