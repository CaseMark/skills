**Contract reasoning, Main Fund.** Subscription Agreement s. 2 (p. 2) provides that the Investor "may withdraw this subscription by written notice at any time prior to the General Partner's acceptance" and that "upon acceptance by the General Partner, evidenced by its countersignature on the signature page, this subscription shall be irrevocable". ## 4. Judgment calls

**1. One filing or two.** Options: (a) two separate new notices, one per issuer (recommended); (b) one notice with the Main Fund as primary issuer and the Feeder as an additional issuer; (c) add the Feeder to the Main Fund's offering later by amendment (not available: no notice has been filed). Recommended (a). Reasons: the issuers have different dates of first sale (September 16 and 17) and the Feeder's Items 13 and 14 need a Regulation S election and a clarification of their own; the Feeder's $20,000,000 is already inside the Main Fund's Item 13(b), so a combined Items 13 to 15 presentation would require a second set of double-count explanations in 255 characters; and some states require a notice per issuer. Under (b): one date of first sale (September 16) and one due date (October 1) for both; both CIKs and CCCs still required; one exemption and one 3(c) paragraph (both issuers use 506(b) and 3(c)(7), so this is not an obstacle); Items 13 to 15 for the offering as a whole, with the Feeder's investors counted directly `[VERIFY]` and the Feeder's own commitment not separately counted. If the Feeder's CIK issues first, (b) is no help; if the Main Fund's issues first, (a) lets the Main Fund file on time regardless of the Feeder.

**2. Feeder date of first sale.** Options: September 17, 2026 (Nordhavn, Regulation S; recommended) or September 18, 2026 (Pacific Crest, the first Rule 506(b) sale). The form's definition turns on the first investor "irrevocably contractually committed to invest" and does not distinguish by exemption; the earlier date is the defensible one for the deadline and is recommended for Item 7. Under the alternative, Item 7 would read September 18 and be internally consistent with a Rule 506(b)-only Item 13(b); the filing should still be made by October 2. The deadline consequence of choosing wrongly runs only one way: filing by October 2 is timely under either reading.

**3. Regulation S reporting on the Feeder's Items 13(b) and 14.** Options: Rule 506(b) sales only ($5,000,000 / 1) with the clarification disclosing the $15,000,000 Regulation S sales (recommended); or all sales ($20,000,000 / 3) with a clarification saying so. Reason: Rule 500(g) excludes Regulation S purchasers from the Regulation D purchaser count and aggregate offering price unless the issuer elects to rely solely on Regulation D for offshore sales, and the Feeder's documents rely on Regulation S for those sales (02o s. 12.1(a); 06l, 06m s. 9); whether Form D itself requires either presentation is not stated `[VERIFY]`. Under the alternative, the public figures are larger and match the Program sheet's Offshore total; the clarification must then say the figure includes Regulation S sales. Whichever is chosen must be followed on every amendment. Both variants appear in Schedule 3.4 and Section 3.7.

**4. Feeder Item 13(a) and (c).** Options: Indefinite (recommended); the $250,000,000 shared cap with a clarification; a per-vehicle allocation (none exists in the documents). Reason: the Feeder has no cap of its own; its capacity is whatever the Main Fund's shared cap leaves after the Main Fund's direct sales, which cannot be determined now. Under the shared-cap alternative, (c) would be $245,000,000 or $230,000,000, each overstating program capacity (true program remaining: $139,000,000), and the clarification would have to say so.

**5.
